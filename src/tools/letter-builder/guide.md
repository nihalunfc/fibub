## What it does

Pick the kind of letter you need: a formal letter, a complaint, a request, a resignation, a reference or a leave request. The tool sets out your address, the date, the person you are writing to, a subject line, a greeting, the body and a sign-off in the usual order. The body starts as plain example text with [square brackets] where your own facts go. You edit it, watch the preview change, and download Word, PDF or plain text.

Nothing is uploaded and nothing is kept: close the page and the letter is gone, so download it first. Applying for a job instead? Use [[cover-letter]]. Sending the same letter to many people? Try [[mail-merge]], and check the files you send with [[send-check]].

## How to use it

1. Choose the letter type. The subject, greeting, body and sign-off fill in.
2. Type your name, address, email and phone.
3. Type who the letter is for. If you give a name and the greeting is still the standard one, it becomes "Dear <name>,".
4. Replace every [bracketed] part of the body. The count above the preview tells you how many are left.
5. Pick the sign-off and paper size, then download.

## What each setting does

- **Letter type**: swaps in the matching example text. If you have already changed the subject or body, your words stay.
- **Greeting**: type your own, such as "Dear Ms Jones,". "Yours faithfully" goes with "Dear Sir or Madam"; "Yours sincerely" goes with a named person.
- **Paper**: US Letter or A4. **Font**: sans-serif or serif.
- **Download**: Word to keep editing, PDF to send, text to paste into an email.

## Worked example

Choose **Resignation**, enter your name, and write "Dear Mr Brown," as the greeting. Replace the brackets with your job title, company, last day and notice period. A short resignation of about 90 words fits well on one page.

## FAQs

**Is the wording legal advice?** No. These are plain examples. For disputes, contracts or notice rules, check your own agreement or an official source.

**Is my letter saved?** No. It stays in your browser until you close the page.

**Why does the PDF leave out some letters?** The PDF uses standard fonts that only draw Latin letters. The Word file keeps every character.

**Can I use my own letter type?** Yes. Pick Formal letter and write whatever you need in the body.
