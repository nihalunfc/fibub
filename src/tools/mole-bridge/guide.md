## What it does

Chemists count particles by weighing them. The mole is the bridge: one mole of anything is 6.022 × 10²³ particles, and it has a mass in grams equal to the formula mass. This page makes the bridge visible.

- **The bridge.** Type a formula (or tap one: H₂O, CO₂, NaCl …), an amount and its unit: grams, moles, particles or litres of gas. The picture shows four boxes (mass, moles, particles, gas volume) with the right operation on each link: divide by the molar mass M to go from grams to moles, multiply by Avogadro's number Nₐ to go from moles to particles, multiply by the molar volume to get litres of gas. The link you start from is highlighted, and the steps below show every sum with your numbers.
- **Empirical formula.** Enter the mass percentage of each element. The table works out the moles in 100 g, divides by the smallest and scales to whole numbers. Give a molar mass and it also finds the molecular formula.
- **Which runs out first?** Pick a reaction and the moles of each reactant. The circles show which reactant is used up (the limiting reactant), how much product forms and what is left over.
- **Level.** Class 9 uses rounded atomic masses (H 1, C 12, O 16 …) and 22.4 L per mole of gas; Class 11 uses IUPAC atomic weights and 22.7 L per mole at STP (0 °C and 1 bar).

It follows Class 9 Science (atoms and molecules: the mole concept, molar mass, number of particles) and Class 11 Chemistry (some basic concepts of chemistry: mole concept and molar masses, percentage composition, empirical and molecular formula, stoichiometry and limiting reagent). ## Worked example: 36 g of water

Formula H₂O, amount 36, unit grams, Class 9.

- Molar mass: 2 × 1 + 1 × 16 = 18 g/mol.
- Moles = 36 ÷ 18 = 2 mol.
- Particles = 2 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 1.204 × 10²⁴ molecules.
- As a gas (steam treated as an ideal gas at 0 °C, just for practice): 2 × 22.4 = 44.8 L.

Empirical formula: carbon 40.0 %, hydrogen 6.7 %, oxygen 53.3 %. In 100 g there are 40 ÷ 12 = 3.33 mol C, 6.7 ÷ 1 = 6.7 mol H and 53.3 ÷ 16 = 3.33 mol O. Dividing by 3.33 gives 1 : 2 : 1, so the empirical formula is CH₂O (formula mass 30). With a molar mass of 180 g/mol, n = 180 ÷ 30 = 6 and the molecular formula is C₆H₁₂O₆, glucose.

Limiting reactant: 5 mol H₂ and 2 mol O₂ in 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. H₂ could react 5 ÷ 2 = 2.5 times, O₂ only 2 ÷ 1 = 2 times, so oxygen runs out first. 4 mol of water form and 1 mol of hydrogen is left.

## Tips

- If ratios come out like 1 : 1.5 or 1 : 1.33, multiply all of them by 2 or 3. The table does this and says so.
- Percentages that do not add up to about 100 % usually mean an element (often oxygen) is missing.
- The gas volume only applies to gases. 22.4 L is at 1 atm (the older standard); 22.7 L is at 1 bar, the standard used in newer books.
