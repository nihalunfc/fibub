## What it does

Your blood makes two trips for every one round your body. The right side of the heart pumps oxygen-poor blood to the lungs; it comes back rich in oxygen to the left side, which pumps it out to the whole body. That is **double circulation**. This page shows it as a loop of moving blood cells: red where the blood is rich in oxygen, blue where it is low in oxygen, and purple where the two mix. One cell has a dark ring, and the list under the picture shows where it is right now. Switch to a **frog** (three chambers, so the blood mixes in its one ventricle) or a **fish** (two chambers, single circulation through the gills) to see why mammals and birds need four chambers. To slow down a single heartbeat and see the valves, open the [[cardiac-cycle]]; to see how haemoglobin picks up and drops oxygen on the way, try the [[oxygen-dissociation]]; and for where the food in the blood comes from, the [[digestion-journey]].

## Using it

1. Pick an **animal**: human, frog or fish.
2. Move the **heart rate** (beats a minute) and **stroke volume** (mL pushed out by each ventricle per beat). The cells speed up or slow down.
3. Read **the numbers**: cardiac output, the time for the blood to go round once, the laps in a day and the litres pumped in a day.
4. Follow the ringed cell and read **one trip round the body**, step by step.
5. Pick **Class 11** for the names of the valves and vessels in the picture and a note on the pulmonary artery and veins.

The picture runs ten times faster than real life, so one lap takes about 6 seconds on screen instead of about a minute.

## Worked example

At rest, a heart beating **75 times a minute** that pushes out **70 mL** a beat has a cardiac output of 75 × 70 = 5250 mL a minute, or **5.25 L a minute**. An adult has about 5 L of blood, so it goes round once in about 5 ÷ 5.25 = 0.95 minutes, which is **about 57 seconds**. In a day the heart pumps about 7560 L, and the blood goes round about 1512 times. Push the heart rate to 150 and the output doubles to 10.5 L a minute, so one lap takes about 29 seconds.

## Where this fits in school

Class 10 science (life processes) covers the human heart, why the oxygen-rich and oxygen-poor blood must be kept apart, double circulation, and the hearts of fish and amphibians. Class 11 biology (body fluids and circulation) adds the valves, the cardiac cycle and cardiac output. This page is an independent learning aid. ## Questions

**Is blood really blue?** No. Oxygen-poor blood is a darker red. Blue is the usual colour in diagrams to tell the two apart.

**Why is the left ventricle wall thicker?** It pumps blood round the whole body, which needs a much higher pressure than the short trip to the lungs.

**Are these my numbers?** No. They are typical round values for a resting adult, for teaching. This is not a medical tool.
