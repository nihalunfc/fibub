## What it does

Many job offers quote one total, such as 15,000 a month, made of a basic part and allowances for housing, transport and more. In many labour laws, end-of-service pay, leave pay and overtime are worked out on the basic part only. So two offers with the same total can leave you with very different amounts later.

This tool splits your package, shows how big the basic share is and what that basic pay drives. It gives an estimate, not legal advice. For the full end-of-service rules use the [[gratuity-calculator]]; to turn pay into hourly or yearly figures use [[hourly-salary]]; to set offers side by side use [[job-offer-compare]].

## How to use it

1. Choose to type **amounts** or **percent** of the total.
2. In amounts, type basic, housing, transport and other pay. In percent, type the total and the basic, housing and transport shares; other is what remains.
3. Read the table of parts, then the rates that depend on basic.
4. In **Compare**, type a different basic share to see how the end-of-service estimate changes for the same total.

## What each setting does

- **Daily basic:** shown two ways, basic / 30 and basic x 12 / 365, because laws and contracts differ.
- **Overtime hourly base:** basic / 30 / 8, before any overtime multiple.
- **End-of-service estimate:** basic / 30 x 21 for each year. Example rate, UAE first 5 years; rules differ, use the gratuity calculator.

## Worked example

A package of 15,000 with 60% basic is 9,000 basic. Daily basic is 300 (9,000 / 30) or 295.89 (9,000 x 12 / 365). The overtime base is 37.50 an hour. End-of-service is 9,000 / 30 x 21 = 6,300 a year. With a 40% basic share, basic is 6,000 and the estimate falls to 4,200, which is 2,100 less every year.

## FAQs

**Why does basic matter so much?** Because several payments are worked out on it and not on the total.

**Is 21 days right for me?** Only as an example. Your country, years of service and contract decide.

**Is this legal advice?** No. It is an estimate.
