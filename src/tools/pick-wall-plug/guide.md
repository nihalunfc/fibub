## What it does

A wall plug only holds if it suits the wall and the weight. A plain plastic plug that grips tightly in brick does almost nothing in hollow plasterboard, and a small anchor that is fine for a clock will pull out under a loaded shelf. This lesson explains the main kinds of fixing and when to use each one, then gives you a picker: choose your wall, type the weight and say what you are hanging, and it suggests a fixing, the drill bit and a short reason why. You also put the steps for fitting a plastic plug in order, name six fixings from a picture and take a short quiz.

It is the second lesson of the free **Wall fixings** course. Find out what your wall is made of first with [[whats-in-your-wall|What's in your wall]], then use what you pick here to [[hang-picture-mirror|hang a picture or mirror]].

## How to use it

1. Weigh the item (stand on bathroom scales holding it and take away your own weight). For shelves and cupboards, add what will go on them.
2. In the **Fixing picker**, choose your wall, type the weight in kg or lb, and choose what kind of thing it is. Tick **Tiles over the wall** if there are tiles.
3. Read the suggestion, then check the packet of the fixings you buy: its weight rating for your wall type wins.
4. Follow the steps, tick them as you go, and try the order activity and quiz.

## What each fixing does

- **Plastic wall plug:** for solid brick, dense block and concrete. The screw spreads the plug against the sides of the hole.
- **Self-drill drywall anchor:** a wide-threaded plastic or zinc anchor that screws straight into plasterboard. Quick, for light things.
- **Metal hollow-wall anchor:** a sleeve that squashes into wings behind the board as you tighten it. Good for medium loads in drywall.
- **Spring and gravity toggles:** wings or a bar that open behind a hollow wall or lath and plaster.
- **Snap toggle:** a steel bar on plastic straps that sits flat behind the board. One of the strongest hollow-wall fixings.
- **Wood screw into a stud:** the best choice in any stud wall. No plug needed, just a pilot hole.
- **Masonry screws and frame fixings:** grip brick and concrete much harder than plastic plugs, for heavier loads.
- **Resin (chemical) anchors:** glue a threaded rod into the hole, for very heavy loads or soft block.

## Worked example

You want to hang an 8 kg mirror on a plasterboard wall, and the spot is between two studs. In the picker you choose **Drywall or plasterboard, between studs**, type 8 kg and choose **Hangs flat**. It suggests a self-drill drywall anchor. You use two, so each carries about 4 kg, and the packet says each holds 10 kg in 12.5 mm board, so you are well inside the limit. Change the wall to **Solid brick** and it suggests a plastic plug with a 6 mm masonry bit, hammer on.

## FAQs

**Why does a shelf count as double?** A shelf sticks out, so its weight levers the top fixings out of the wall. The picker counts it as twice its weight to allow for that.

**What do the plug colours mean?** Many makers colour plugs by size (for example yellow, red, brown, blue), but the colours are not the same everywhere. Read the bit and screw size on the packet.

**Can I use a bigger screw to make a plug grip better?** Only up to the size the packet says. Too big and the plug splits or the brick cracks.

**What if I hit the mortar joint in a brick wall?** Mortar is soft and holds badly. Move the hole into the brick if you can, or use a longer plug or a masonry screw.
