## What it does

Condition has a big effect on what a coin is worth, and different countries use different words for the same wear. This tool lets you pick a number on the 1 to 70 scale and see what dealers in the US, UK, France and Germany would call it, with a short plain description of the wear.

Below that is a table of every band and a table of banknote terms, which use words rather than numbers. Grades are opinions; ask a dealer or a collectors' club if a grade matters for a sale. For cards, see [[card-centering]]; for a first size check on a coin, use [[coin-check]].

## How to use it

1. Choose a number from the list or drag the slider.
2. Read the words in each language and the plain description.
3. Scroll down to compare all bands, or to look up a banknote term.

## What each setting does

- **Number**: the 1 to 70 value. Only numbers in common use are listed.
- **Slider**: steps through the same numbers.

## Worked example

Number 45 sits in the Extremely Fine band (EF / XF). A UK dealer says Extremely Fine, a French dealer says SUP (Superbe), and a German dealer says VZ (Vorzüglich). The coin shows light wear on its highest points only, and most fine detail is still sharp.

## FAQs

**Is this an official grade?** No. It is a reference to the words and numbers. Only a person looking at the coin can grade it, and two people may differ by a step.

**Why do some bands cover several numbers?** Wear is a smooth change, so each word covers a range. Mint State uses 60 to 70 for marks, shine and strike.

**Do banknotes use numbers too?** Some are graded 1 to 70, but most dealers use the words in the banknote table.

**Is anything sent anywhere?** No. It all runs in your browser.
