## What it does

Creditor (mortgage life) insurance pays your lender if you die, so the cover shrinks as the loan balance shrinks. A term life policy pays your family a fixed amount, whatever you owe. This tool shows the balance and the cover each year, adds up what each costs, and gives the cost per 1,000 of cover so the two are fairly compared.

Both prices are typed by you from real quotes; the numbers shown are examples: change them to yours. To see how much cover your family needs, try [[life-cover-needed]] and [[term-fair-price]]; for the loan itself, see [[mortgage-calculator]].

## How to use it

1. Enter the loan amount, interest rate and years.
2. Choose how the creditor premium is charged: a fixed amount a month, or a percentage of the balance each year, and type it.
3. Enter the monthly term premium and the level cover it buys.
4. Read the chart, the totals and the cost per 1,000.

## What each setting does

- **Fixed a month**: the same premium every month for the whole loan.
- **% of balance a year**: the premium falls as the balance falls.
- **Term cover**: the amount paid to your family; it does not fall.

## Worked example

A loan of 100 over 2 years at 0% has balances 100 and 50 at the start of years 1 and 2. Creditor premium 10 a year, term premium 8 a year for 100 of cover. Average creditor cover is 75, so it costs 133.33 per 1,000; term costs 80 per 1,000.

## FAQs

**Is the cheaper one better?** Not always. Creditor insurance pays the lender, not your family, and some products check your health only when you claim. Check the policy for exclusions.

**Does term cover pay off the loan?** Only if your family uses the money for it.

**Are my numbers stored?** No, nothing leaves this page.

How it's worked out: balance from the monthly repayment formula; creditor cost = sum of yearly premiums; cost per 1,000 = (yearly cost ÷ average cover) × 1,000.
