## What it does

Accurate work starts with accurate marks. This lesson teaches you to read a tape measure in millimetres and in inch fractions, why the hook on the end is loose on purpose, how to mark with a V, how to draw a line square across a board, how to check a big corner with the 3-4-5 rule, and how to check that a spirit level is telling the truth. Eight illustrated steps can be ticked as you go.

Two activities let you practise. **Read the tape** draws a piece of tape with a pencil mark at a random length: type the reading in millimetres, or switch to inches and type it as a fraction such as 5 3/8. Each answer shows how to read it. The **3-4-5 square checker** takes the two sides and the diagonal you measured and tells you whether the corner is square, and which way to push it if not.

It is the third lesson of the free **Hand tools and safety** course, after [[know-hand-tools]] and [[workshop-safety]]. With your measurements in hand, plan your boards with the [[cut-list|cut list]], or size a whole deck with the [[deck-calculator]].

## How to use it

1. Read the steps with a tape measure in your hand if you have one. Tick each step when you can do it.
2. In **Read the tape**, choose metric or imperial, read where the pencil mark is and press **Check**. **New length** gives you another. Your score counts first answers only and is not saved.
3. In the **3-4-5 square checker**, type side A, side B and the diagonal, all in the same unit. The answer updates as you type.
4. Take the quiz.

## What the tape markings mean

- **Metric tape:** the large numbers are centimetres. Every small mark is 1 mm and the mid mark is 5 mm. Many tapes also print a metre marker every 100 cm.
- **Inch tape:** the numbers are inches. Between them, the longest mark is 1/2, then 1/4 and 3/4, then eighths, then the shortest sixteenths. Many tapes mark every 16 inches (a common stud spacing) in a different way.
- **The hook:** slides by its own thickness, so readings hooked over an edge and pushed against a wall both start at true zero.

## Worked example

You are building a frame with sides of 1200 mm and 900 mm and want the corner square. You measure 600 mm along one side and 800 mm along the other (3-4-5 times 200) and mark both with a V. The diagonal between the marks reads 1004 mm. The checker says it should be 1000 mm: yours is 4 mm longer, so the corner is open at about 90.5°. You push the far ends of the frame a little closer together until the diagonal reads 1000 mm, then fix it.

On an inch tape, a mark that sits 6 small marks past 5 is 5 and 6/16 inches, which simplifies to 5 3/8 in.

## FAQs

**Why is my tape's hook loose?** It is designed to be. It moves by its own thickness so that both inside and outside measurements are right. Do not bend it.

**What is the 3-4-5 rule?** Pythagoras' theorem in practice. A triangle with sides 3, 4 and 5 always has a right angle between the 3 and 4 sides. Any multiple works: 30-40-50 cm, 6-8-10 ft.

**Should I use metric or imperial?** Whichever your materials are sold in. Do not mix them in one job. Millimetres avoid fractions, which is why many woodworkers prefer them.

**How accurate do I need to be?** For rough framing, within 2 to 3 mm (1/8 in). For furniture and shelves that must fit, within 1 mm (1/32 in). Marking with a knife instead of a pencil helps.
