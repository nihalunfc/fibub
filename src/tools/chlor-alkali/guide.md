## What it does

When electricity passes through **brine** (a strong solution of sodium chloride in water), three useful chemicals come out at once: **chlorine** gas at the positive electrode (anode), **hydrogen** gas at the negative electrode (cathode), and **sodium hydroxide** solution near the cathode. Because it makes chlorine and an alkali together, this is called the **chlor-alkali process**:

**2NaCl(aq) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + Cl₂(g) + H₂(g)**

This page shows a simplified **membrane cell**. Press **Switch on** and watch the bubbles at each electrode and the Na⁺ ions crossing the membrane. The electrode equations are written under the picture.

The second card uses **Faraday's laws** to say how much is made. Type the current and the time; the page works out the charge (Q = I × t), the moles of electrons (Q ÷ F), and from them the moles, grams and gas volumes of chlorine, hydrogen and sodium hydroxide, and the salt used up.

A third card lists what each product is used for, including bleaching powder made from chlorine, and the tests for chlorine and hydrogen.

It fits the Class 10 science chapter on acids, bases and salts (chemicals from common salt) and Class 12 electrochemistry (Faraday's laws). ## How to use it

1. Press **Switch on** (and **Switch off**) to run the picture.
2. In **How much is made?** type the current in amperes and the time, and pick seconds, minutes or hours.
3. Read the three steps and the table.

## A worked example

The page starts with **2 A for 60 minutes**.

- Q = 2 × 3 600 = 7 200 C.
- Moles of electrons = 7 200 ÷ 96 485 = 0.07462 mol.
- Chlorine and hydrogen: 0.07462 ÷ 2 = 0.03731 mol each, which is 2.645 g of chlorine and 0.925 L of each gas at 25 °C.
- Sodium hydroxide: 0.07462 mol = 2.985 g.

## Tips

- Twice the current or twice the time gives twice the products: the amount depends only on the charge.
- Chlorine and hydrogen always come out in equal volumes, because each needs two electrons per molecule.
- The membrane matters: if chlorine met the sodium hydroxide, they would react and make bleach instead.

## Check yourself

Three fresh questions each time: moles of electrons from a current and a time, the mass of sodium hydroxide made, and the volume of chlorine. Nothing is saved.

## FAQ

**Are real cells 100% efficient?** No. A little of the current is lost to side reactions and heat, so real factories make slightly less than this page says.
