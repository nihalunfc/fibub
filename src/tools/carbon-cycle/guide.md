## What it does

Carbon moves round the Earth all the time. Plants take carbon dioxide (CO₂) out of the air by **photosynthesis**; animals, plants and soil microbes put it back by **respiration** and **decay**. The ocean dissolves CO₂ from the air and lets some escape again, and slowly carries it down to the deep water. Coal, oil and gas are carbon locked away for millions of years, until we burn them.

This sandbox draws those five stores as boxes and the flows as arrows whose thickness shows their size. You choose three things that people do each year:

- **burn** fossil fuels (today about 10 gigatonnes of carbon a year),
- **clear** forest (about 1 a year),
- **plant** new forest that takes carbon in.

Then you run the years and watch the CO₂ in the air, in parts per million (ppm), change on a graph. It fits the Class 9 Science chapter on the Earth as a system of energy, matter and life, and the carbon cycle in Class 11 and 12 Biology (ecosystems and environmental issues).

Carbon enters living things through the [[photosynthesis-lab|photosynthesis lab]] and passes along the chains in the [[food-web-builder]]; what more CO₂ does to the planet's temperature is in the [[energy-balance-model]].

## How to use it

1. Pick a **Level**. *Class 9* names the stores in words; *Class 11* shows the amounts in gigatonnes of carbon (GtC) and every flow in GtC per year.
2. Set the three sliders, or type a value in the box next to each.
3. Press **Run 1 year**, **Run 10 years** or **Run to 2100**. **Back to 2025** starts over.
4. Read the big line (year and CO₂) and the note under it, which says where last year's carbon went.

## A worked example

The page starts in the model's 2025 with CO₂ at about **428 ppm**. With burning at 10 and clearing at 1, press **Run 1 year** in the Class 11 level. People released 11 GtC; the air gained about 5.6 GtC (2.6 ppm), plants and soil about 2.6 GtC and the ocean about 1.8 GtC. So roughly **half** of what people released stayed in the air. Run to 2100 with the same choices and CO₂ climbs past 600 ppm.

Now press **Back to 2025**, set burning and clearing to 0 and run to 2100: CO₂ falls only to about 385 ppm. The ocean and plants soak up the extra slowly, over decades to centuries.

## Tips

- 1 ppm of CO₂ is about 2.12 GtC of carbon in the air. Divide GtC by 2.12 to get ppm.
- Planting forests helps, but a forest only takes in carbon while it is growing, and there is limited land.
- The total carbon in all five stores never changes: carbon only moves between them.

## Check yourself

Four fresh questions: GtC to ppm, the share that stays in the air, how long new forests take, and adding up a year's flows. Nothing is saved.

## FAQ

**Are these real numbers?** They are rounded teaching values chosen so the model behaves like the real cycle in outline. Real measurements have uncertainty and change with warming, which this model leaves out. It is not a forecast.

**Why does the ocean not take all the extra CO₂?** Sea water's chemistry pushes back: as it takes in carbon, the CO₂ pressure over it rises about ten times faster (the buffer factor), so it soon stops taking much more until the surface water mixes down to the deep.
