## What it does

Most hand tools in a toolbox are levers. This lesson shows the physics behind them: a lever turns about a pivot, the **fulcrum**, and its turning effect (the **moment**) is force × distance. Put a small push far from the fulcrum and it can hold a big load close to it. Six illustrated idea cards explain the three classes of lever and why a longer handle needs less push. In the sim you pick a claw hammer, a wheel brace or a wheelbarrow, slide your hand along the handle and see the push you need, drawn to scale with arrows. Then you name the parts of a lever on a hammer pulling a nail, sort nine tools into class 1, 2 and 3, and take a short quiz.

It is lesson 2 of the free **How machines work** course, after the [[simple-machines|simple machines lab]]. For balancing moments on a beam try the [[moments-lab]], and for how tight a real bolt should be see the [[bolt-torque-calculator]].

## What each class of lever means

- **Class 1, fulcrum in the middle:** the load is on one side and your effort on the other. Claw hammers, crowbars, pliers and scissors. They can give a big force (long handle side) or more speed (long load side).
- **Class 2, load in the middle:** the fulcrum is at one end and the load sits between it and you. Wheelbarrows, bottle openers and nutcrackers. The effort is always less than the load.
- **Class 3, effort in the middle:** your push is between the fulcrum and the load. Tweezers, shovels, brooms and your forearm. You push harder than the load, but the far end moves further and faster.
- **Turning tools:** a spanner or wheel brace is a lever that turns about the nut. The torque on the nut is your push × the distance to your hand.

## Worked example

A nail needs 1,200 N to pull out of a plank. The claw holds it 4 cm from where the hammer head rocks on the wood, and your hand is 30 cm from that point. Moments: effort × 0.30 = 1,200 × 0.04, so the effort is 48 ÷ 0.30 = **160 N**, about the force to hold up 16 kg. The mechanical advantage is 30 ÷ 4 = 7.5, so your hand moves 7.5 times as far as the nail. Hold the handle halfway, at 15 cm, and you need 320 N: twice as much.

A wheel nut needs 110 N·m. With a 40 cm wheel brace the push is 110 ÷ 0.40 = **275 N**. A 25 cm spanner would need 440 N.

## FAQs

**Is a longer bar always better?** For pulling nails or prying, a longer handle needs less force. For bolts with a set tightness it is risky: you cannot feel how hard you are pulling, and you can stretch or snap the bolt. Use a torque wrench.

**Why does the sim ignore friction?** To show the moments rule clearly. Real tools lose a little to friction at the pivot, so you need slightly more push than the sim shows.

**Is my arm a lever?** Yes, a class 3 one: the elbow is the fulcrum, your biceps pulls just below it, and your hand holds the load further out. That is why holding a weight with a straight arm is so hard.

**What comes next?** Belts and pulleys, which pass turning from one shaft to another, then bearings and friction.
