## What it does

A circle theorem is a fact that is true for every circle, however you place the points. Seeing it once on paper proves nothing, because you might have drawn a special case. Here you can drag the points anywhere and see the angles update at once, so the pattern shows itself.

Pick one of eight theorems. The page states it in plain words, draws the circle with the lines and angles that matter, and prints the live values. For the circle itself, measuring arcs and sectors, use [[circle-sector]], and for angles round a circle with sine and cosine, see [[unit-circle]]. To build these diagrams with compass and ruler, try [[geometry-construction]].

## How to use it

1. Choose a theorem from the menu.
2. Drag the blue dots round the circle (for "Two tangents", drag the outside point).
3. Watch the red and blue angle values. The ones the theorem links stay equal, double or add up.
4. Open "Why it works" for the reasoning.
5. Answer the quiz, which uses new numbers each time.

## What each setting does

- **Angle at the centre:** angle at the centre = 2 × angle at the circumference.
- **Same segment:** angles from the same chord on the same side are equal.
- **Semicircle:** the angle on a diameter is 90°.
- **Cyclic quadrilateral:** opposite angles add to 180°.
- **Tangent and radius:** they meet at 90°.
- **Two tangents:** the lengths from the outside point are equal.
- **Alternate segment:** the tangent-chord angle equals the angle on the other side of the chord.
- **Chord bisector:** the perpendicular from the centre cuts the chord in half.

## Worked example

A chord AB makes an angle of 35° at the circumference. The angle at the centre is 2 × 35° = 70°. In another picture, a chord is 6 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 10 cm. Half the chord is √(10² − 6²) = 8 cm, so the chord is 16 cm long.

## FAQs

**Does the angle at the centre rule work for a reflex angle?** Yes. If P is on the minor arc, the angle at the centre on P's side is the reflex angle, still exactly double.

**Why must both angles be on the same side for "same segment"?** Angles on opposite sides of the chord add up to 180°, not equal.

**Do I need to learn the proofs?** At GCSE and Grade 10 you usually need to state and use the theorems, and name them in written answers.
