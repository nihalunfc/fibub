## What it does

A flower is the part of a plant that makes seeds. This page shows a flower **cut down the middle** so you can see every part, from the outside in:

- **sepals** (green, protect the bud) and **petals** (attract insects);
- **stamens**, the male parts: an **anther** that makes pollen on a **filament**;
- the **pistil** (carpel), the female part: a sticky **stigma**, the **style**, and the **ovary** holding **ovules**.

In **Parts**, tap anything to see its name and job, or press **Find a part** and the page asks you to tap one.

In **Pollination to seed**, slide or play through the story: pollen reaches the stigma (carried by a bee from another flower, or falling from the same flower), a **pollen tube** grows down the style, a male gamete fuses with the egg in an ovule, and then the ovule becomes a **seed** while the ovary swells into a **fruit**. At **Class 12** level the page zooms into the ovule's embryo sac and shows **double fertilisation**: one male gamete + egg makes the zygote (2n), the other + two polar nuclei makes the endosperm nucleus (3n).

It follows the Class 9 science chapter *Reproduction: How Life Continues*, the Class 10 chapter on how organisms reproduce, and the Class 12 chapter *Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants*. Reproduction from one parent is in [[reproduction-modes]], and what happens to the genes in the seeds is in [[mendel-peas]].

## How to use it

1. **Parts:** tap a part, or press **Find a part** and tap the one named. The table lists every part.
2. **Pollination to seed:** choose cross- or self-pollination, then move **Stage** or press **Play**.
3. Switch the level to **Class 12** for the embryo sac.

## A worked example

Press **Find a part**; suppose it asks for the style. Tap the thin tube between the stigma and the ovary. Now switch to **Pollination to seed**, choose **Cross-pollination** and play: the bee brings pollen to the stigma; at about a quarter of the way the tube starts down the style; just past halfway the top ovule turns green (fertilised); at the end the petals fade, the ovary is bigger (fruit) and the ovules are seeds.

## Tips

- "Pistil" and "carpel" are used for the female part; a flower can have one or several carpels.
- Pollination (pollen to stigma) comes before fertilisation (gametes fuse).
- Wind-pollinated flowers are often small and dull, with lots of light pollen.

## FAQ

**Is every flower like this?** No. Many flowers have only male or only female parts, and the numbers of parts vary. This is a typical flower with both.
