## What it does

A Registered Education Savings Plan (RESP) is the main way Canadian families save for a child's college, university or trade school. The government adds money: the Canada Education Savings Grant (CESG) pays 20% on the first $2,500 you put in each year, so $500 a year, up to $7,200 per child. Lower and middle-income families get an additional CESG on the first $500, and lower-income families can get the Canada Learning Bond without putting in anything at all.

This calculator works out the grants year by year, including catch-up for years you missed, and grows the account until the child turns 17 or 18. To grow other savings, try the [[compound-interest|compound interest calculator]]; to find room in the family budget, the [[budget-calculator|budget planner]].

## How to use it

1. Enter the **child's age this year** and when the money should be ready.
2. Enter what you **put in each year**, plus any **one-time amount** now.
3. Choose a **yearly return** and your **family net income** band for 2026.
4. Tick the **Learning Bond** box if your family qualifies.
5. Read the totals, the line about the $7,200 limit and the year-by-year table.

## What each setting does

- **One-time amount:** a lump sum in the first year. Grants still follow the yearly rules, so a big lump earns at most $1,000 of CESG.
- **Family net income:** sets the additional CESG: 20% on the first $500 (up to $100) for the lowest band, 10% (up to $50) for the middle band.
- **CESG already received:** for an older child; it counts against the $7,200 and the catch-up room.

## Worked example

You start at birth and put in $2,500 a year at 5%. The CESG adds $500 a year. After 14 years it has paid $7,000, and in the year the child turns 14 it pays the last $200, so the $7,200 limit is reached (14.4 years of full grants). You put in $45,000 by age 18 and the RESP grows to $86,597.

Start at age 5 with $5,000 a year and catch-up gives $1,000 a year for five years.

## FAQs

**What if my child does not go to school?** Grants go back to the government; your own money comes back to you, and growth may be moved to an RRSP within limits.

**Do the grants count toward the $50,000 limit?** No, only your contributions do.
