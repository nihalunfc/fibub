## What it does

A **ratio** like 2 : 3 says how much of one thing there is for every so much of another. The easiest way to feel what a ratio means is to mix something. This page has two jars. You add equal parts (cups, spoons, tins) of blue and yellow paint, or of lemon syrup and water, and the jar shows the colour of the mix.

The key idea: **the colour depends only on the ratio, not on how much you make.** 2 : 3 and 4 : 6 give exactly the same green, because in both, blue is 2 out of every 5 parts. But 2 : 3 and 3 : 4 are different greens, even though one is just "one more of each".

The page also shows each ratio in **simplest form**, the share of the first ingredient as a fraction and a percentage, a **cross-multiplication** test that says which mix is bluer (or which lemonade is stronger), and how to **scale** a mix up to any amount.

It fits Class 8 Mathematics, proportional reasoning (ratios, equivalent ratios and sharing in a ratio), and is good revision for any class that uses ratios. To find the common factor that simplifies a ratio, use [[hcf-lcm-venn]]; equal ratios come back in geometry as [[similar-triangles]].

## How to use it

- Pick **Blue and yellow paint** or **Lemonade** at the top.
- Use the − and + buttons to set the parts in mix A and mix B (0 to 20 of each). The squares above each jar show every part; the jar shows the colour of the whole mix.
- The quick buttons load useful pairs: an equal pair (2 : 3 and 4 : 6), a close pair (2 : 3 and 3 : 4) and others.
- Under the picture you get each ratio in simplest form, its share as a percentage, the cross-multiplication, and a verdict.
- In **Make more of mix A**, type how much you want in all. The page finds the size of one part and the amount of each ingredient, and lists the first five multiples of the ratio.

## A worked example

The page starts with mix A = 2 blue : 3 yellow and mix B = 4 blue : 6 yellow.

- A is already in simplest form. Blue is 2/5 of it = 40%.
- B = 4 : 6 = 2 : 3 after dividing both by 2. Blue is 4/10 = 40%.
- Cross-multiply: 2 × 6 = 12 and 3 × 4 = 12. Equal, so the ratios are equal: the jars are the same colour.

Now change B to 3 : 4. Cross-multiply: 2 × 4 = 8 and 3 × 3 = 9. The 9 belongs to B, so B is bluer (3/7 ≈ 42.9% blue against 40%).

To make 30 cups of mix A: 2 + 3 = 5 parts, so one part is 30 ÷ 5 = 6 cups. Blue = 2 × 6 = 12 cups, yellow = 3 × 6 = 18 cups.

## Why cross-multiplying works

Mix A has a share a₁/(a₁ + a₂) of blue and mix B has b₁/(b₁ + b₂). Comparing those fractions is the same as comparing a₁ × b₂ with a₂ × b₁ (multiply both sides by the two positive bottoms and cancel). So you never need decimals: two multiplications decide it.

## FAQ

**Why is adding one of each not the same ratio?** Multiplying both parts by the same number keeps the share the same. Adding the same number changes it: 1 : 2 (one third blue) plus one of each is 2 : 3 (two fifths blue).

**Is the colour exactly what real paint would do?** No. Real paints mix in complicated ways. The jar uses a simple model that keeps the key fact true: the same ratio always gives the same colour.
