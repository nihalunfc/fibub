## What it shows

Benzene reacts with **electrophiles**, positive particles such as NO₂⁺ (nitration) or Br⁺ (bromination), by swapping one of its hydrogens for the new group. Once one group is on the ring, it decides where the second one goes:

- **Ortho and para directors** (positions 2 and 4): –OH, –NH₂, –OCH₃, –CH₃ and the halogens. Most of them push electrons into the ring, so they also make it react **faster** than benzene (activating).
- **Meta directors** (position 3): –NO₂, –CN, –CHO, –COOH. They pull electrons out of the ring and make it react **slower** (deactivating).
- **Halogens** are the odd ones out: they pull electrons along the bond (−I), which slows the ring, but their lone pairs (+R) still send the new group ortho and para.

The picture marks positions o, m and p, draws the products, and the second card shows the resonance structures that put extra negative charge (or a lack of electrons) at ortho and para.

It follows aromatic hydrocarbons in Class 11 chemistry, and comes back in Class 12 with haloarenes, phenols, aldehydes, acids and amines. ## How to use it

1. **Tap a group** that is already on the ring.
2. **Pick a reaction:** nitration (concentrated nitric and sulfuric acids), bromination (Br₂ with FeBr₃) or chlorination (Cl₂ with FeCl₃).
3. Read where the new group goes (green circles), how fast the ring reacts compared with benzene, and the names of the products.
4. Look at the **Why** card: the three resonance structures with the charge on carbons 2, 4 and 6.

## Worked examples

- **Toluene + nitration:** –CH₃ is a weak activator and an ortho/para director. Products: **1-methyl-2-nitrobenzene** (2-nitrotoluene) and **1-methyl-4-nitrobenzene** (4-nitrotoluene).
- **Nitrobenzene + bromination:** –NO₂ is a strong deactivator and a meta director. Product: **1-bromo-3-nitrobenzene**.
- **Chlorobenzene + chlorination:** ortho and para, but slower than benzene: **1,2-dichlorobenzene** and **1,4-dichlorobenzene**.

So to make 1-bromo-3-nitrobenzene from benzene, nitrate first (the nitro group then sends Br meta). Brominating first would give the ortho and para products.

## Check yourself

Three questions with fresh groups: where a group directs, whether it activates or deactivates, and the product with a meta director. Nothing is saved.

## FAQ

**Why do ortho/para directors give two products?** Both positions are favoured; para is often the bigger share because ortho is crowded next to the first group.

**Why is –OH ortho/para but –CHO meta, when both start with oxygen?** In –OH the oxygen's lone pair touches the ring and pushes electrons in. In –CHO a carbon is attached to the ring, and its C=O pulls electrons out.
