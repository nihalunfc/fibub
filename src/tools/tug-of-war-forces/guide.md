## What it does

A **force** is a push or a pull, and it has a direction. When several forces act on the same thing along a line, they add up: forces in the same direction add, forces in opposite directions take away. What is left is the **net force**. If the forces cancel (net force zero) they are **balanced**, and something at rest stays at rest. If they do not cancel they are **unbalanced**, and the object starts to move towards the bigger force.

This page shows it as a tug-of-war. Add children (100 N each) and adults (250 N each) to either side. The picture draws each team's total pull as an arrow and the net force as a thick arrow underneath. Press **Pull!** and the rope moves only if the forces do not balance, faster when the difference is bigger.

It follows the school topics Class 8 Science, exploring forces (balanced and unbalanced forces, net force) and Class 9 Physics, force and laws of motion. Afterwards, [[newtons-laws]] pushes a trolley with F = ma and friction, and the [[friction-ramp]] adds friction on a slope.

## How to use it

1. Add pullers with **+ Child** or **+ Adult** on each side, or **Remove one**.
2. Read each team's total and the net force, then press **Pull!**.
3. Press **Back to the start** to try another match.
4. The **Level** switch (Class 9) adds the total mass, the acceleration a = F/m, the time to reach the winning line 2 m away (from s = ½at²) and the speed.

## Worked example

Two adults on the right pull 2 × 250 = 500 N; one adult on the left pulls 250 N. Net force = 500 − 250 = 250 N to the right: unbalanced, so the rope moves right. The moving mass is three adults (3 × 65 = 195 kg) plus the 5 kg rope, 200 kg in all, so a = 250 ÷ 200 = 1.25 m/s². To move 2 m from rest takes t = √(2s/a) = √(4 ÷ 1.25) = 1.79 s.

Five children (500 N) against two adults (500 N) is a draw: the forces balance and the rope stays still, even though everyone is pulling hard.

## Tips

- Draw arrows: same direction, add; opposite, subtract.
- Balanced forces can also act on something already moving: it then keeps moving at a steady speed.
- In real tug-of-war, the team whose feet grip the ground better can win, because the ground pushes back on their feet.

## FAQ

**Why do both teams feel the same pull in the rope?** The rope pulls each team with the tension in it. What decides the winner is the net force on the whole system, including each team's push on the ground.

**Are 100 N and 250 N real?** They are rounded values for the game; real pulls vary a lot.
