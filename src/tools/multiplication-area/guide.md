## What it does

A product like 23 × 47 is the **area** of a rectangle 23 wide and 47 long. Cutting that rectangle into pieces whose areas are easy to work out is the whole secret of mental multiplication. It works because multiplication **distributes** over addition: (20 + 3) × (40 + 7) = 20 × 40 + 20 × 7 + 3 × 40 + 3 × 7.

This page draws the rectangle and cuts it two ways:

- **From a round number:** write both numbers as a round base plus or minus a little, for example 103 = 100 + 3 and 97 = 100 − 3. The picture is a big square of the base, with strips added for numbers above it and strips taken away (hatched) for numbers below it, plus a small corner. When the two numbers are the same distance either side of the base, the strips cancel: 103 × 97 = 100² − 3² = 9991.
- **Split by place value:** hundreds, tens and ones make a grid of easy products, which you add up.

It fits Class 8 Mathematics, the chapter on the distributive property and multiplication tricks (we distribute, yet things multiply), and leads straight into the algebraic identities. The same pictures with letters instead of numbers are the [[identity-tiles]]; quick percentages in your head are practised on the [[percentage-bar]].

## A worked example

**103 × 97 with base 100:**

1. 103 = 100 + 3 and 97 = 100 − 3.
2. Big square: 100 × 100 = 10 000.
3. Strip for +3: 100 × 3 = +300. Strip for −3: 100 × (−3) = −300.
4. Corner: 3 × (−3) = −9.
5. 10 000 + 300 − 300 − 9 = 9991.

**98 × 96 with base 100:** 10 000 − 200 − 400 + 8 = 9408. Both strips are taken away, and the 2 × 4 corner was taken away twice, so it is added back once.

**23 × 47 by place value:** 800 + 140 + 120 + 21 = 1081.

## Check yourself

Four fresh questions each time: a pair either side of a round number, two numbers just below 100, two just above 100, and a two-digit product. Work them out in your head, type the answer and press **Check**. Nothing is saved.

## FAQ

**Which base should I choose?** One that is easy to square and close to both numbers. 100 for numbers in the 90s and 100s, 50 for numbers near 50, 1000 for numbers near 1000.

**Why is the corner added back when both numbers are below the base?** Each hatched strip removes the corner, so it is removed twice but should only go once. (−x) × (−y) = +xy says the same thing.
