## What it does

When a card is printed, the picture should sit in the middle, with equal border on both sides. Centring is how close it is. It is written as a split such as 55/45: the thinner border is 45% of the two borders together, the wider one is 55%.

Type the four border widths for the front, and optionally the back. The tool gives the left/right and top/bottom splits and draws the card with the picture where it really sits. It never says what grade a card would get, because every grading company sets its own centring limits and also looks at corners, edges and surface. See what the grade words mean with [[grade-scale]].

## How to use it

1. Measure the border on each side of the printed picture, in mm with a ruler or in px on a straight scan or photo.
2. Type left, right, top and bottom. Fill in the back too if you want it.
3. Read the splits and look at the drawing.

## What each setting does

- **Units**: a label only; the split is a ratio, so any unit works if you keep to it.
- **Left, Right, Top, Bottom**: the border widths. Blank means not measured.
- **Back**: optional second set.

## Worked example

Left 3 and right 2: the borders add to 5, and 3 ÷ 5 = 60%, so the split is 60/40. Top 2.5 and bottom 2.5 gives 50/50, perfectly centred up and down.

## FAQs

**Which side goes first in 60/40?** The larger border always comes first, whichever side it is on. Check the drawing to see which way the picture leans.

**What split is good enough?** That depends on the company and on the grade you want. Look at the grading company's own published limits.

**How do I measure accurately?** Place the card square on a flat scan and measure on the image, or use callipers. Keep the card straight.

**Is anything sent anywhere?** No. It all runs in your browser.
