## What it does

A moment is the turning effect of a force about a point. It is the force times the distance from the pivot to the line along which the force acts, so the same weight turns a beam more the further out it sits. This lab shows a beam on a pivot with two weights you can slide about. It adds up the clockwise and anticlockwise moments, tells you which way the beam turns, and says when it is balanced.

A beam is in equilibrium when the total clockwise moment equals the total anticlockwise moment, and the upward push from the pivot equals the total weight. On the A-level setting you can also give the beam a weight of its own, acting at its centre, and move the pivot away from the middle. If you want the forces alone first, try the [[free-body-diagram]] lab, and for levers and wheels in everyday machines see [[simple-machines]] and [[balance-scale]].

## What each setting does

- **Left weight, right weight**: the downward forces on the beam, in newtons.
- **Position of each weight**: its distance from the left end of the beam, in metres. The beam is 10 m long.
- **Pivot position**: where the beam rests. Moments are taken about this point.
- **Beam weight (A-level)**: the beam's own weight, acting at its middle, 5 m from the left end.

## Worked example

A 10 N weight sits 2 m from the left end and a 10 N weight sits 8 m from the left end, with the pivot at 5 m. The left weight is 3 m from the pivot, so its anticlockwise moment is 10 × 3 = 30 N m. The right weight is also 3 m from the pivot, so its clockwise moment is 30 N m. They are equal, so the beam balances, and the pivot pushes up with 20 N. Change the right weight to 15 N and the clockwise moment becomes 45 N m, so the beam turns clockwise with a net moment of 15 N m.

## FAQs

**Why do we use the perpendicular distance?** Only the part of a force that acts at right angles to the line from the pivot makes it turn. Here every weight acts straight down on a horizontal beam, so the distance along the beam is the perpendicular distance.

**What are the units of a moment?** Newton metres (N m), written like that and not as joules, even though the units look alike, because a moment is not an energy.

**Does the pivot position matter for balance?** The beam balances about any point only if the total moment about that point is zero. If the forces balance about one point and the upward and downward forces are equal, it balances about every point.

**Why is the pivot force equal to the total weight?** For the beam not to move up or down, the upward force at the pivot must equal the sum of all the downward weights.
