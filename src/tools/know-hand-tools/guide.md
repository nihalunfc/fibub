## What it does

This lesson is a tour of a basic home tool kit: the thirteen hand tools that cover most small jobs around the house, from hanging a picture to building a simple shelf. Each **tool card** has a drawing and says three things: what the tool is for, how to hold it, and the one mistake almost everyone makes at first. Tick a card when you have the tool or feel sure you know how to use it.

Then tap the **tool wall** to learn each tool's name, press **Test me** to see if you remember them, and finish with a short quiz that asks which tool fits a job. It is the first lesson of the free **Hand tools and safety** course. Next, learn to keep yourself safe in [[workshop-safety]], then measure accurately in [[measure-and-mark]], and when you plan a project, let the [[cut-list|cut list]] work out how many boards to buy.

## How to use it

1. Read the **Stay safe** box first.
2. Work through the tool cards. Tick each one you have or know. Ticks stay on this page only, unless you tick **Remember my ticks on this device**; then they are kept in this browser and never sent anywhere. **Print checklist** gives you a shopping list to take to the shop.

4. Take the quiz. Each time you press for new questions you get a different mix.

## What each tool does

- **Claw hammer:** drives nails and pulls them out. A 16 oz (450 g) head suits most people.
- **Screwdrivers:** flat for slotted screws, Phillips and Pozidriv for cross-head screws. They look alike but are different shapes; Pozidriv heads have four small extra lines.
- **Tape measure:** a 5 m (16 ft) tape with both metric and inch markings is handy.
- **Combination square:** marks and checks 90° and 45° lines.
- **Spirit level:** checks level (horizontal) and plumb (upright).
- **Utility knife:** cuts card, carpet and plasterboard, and marks wood more finely than a pencil.
- **Combination pliers:** grip, bend and cut wire.
- **Adjustable spanner:** turns nuts and bolts of many sizes.
- **Hand saw:** cuts wood to length.
- **G-clamps:** hold the work so your hands stay clear of the blade.
- **Cordless drill:** drills holes and drives screws; the clutch stops it over-tightening.
- **Stud finder:** finds the timber or metal studs behind a hollow wall.

## Worked example

You want to put up a small shelf on a plasterboard wall. You use the **stud finder** to find a stud and mark it, the **tape measure** to set the height, and the **spirit level** to draw a level line. The **cordless drill** makes pilot holes and drives the screws, with the clutch set low. If the shelf board is too long, you mark a square line with the **combination square**, clamp the board with two **G-clamps** and cut it with the **hand saw**. Safety glasses go on before the first hole.

## FAQs

**What should I buy first?** A claw hammer, a flat and a cross-head screwdriver, a tape measure and a utility knife. Add a drill, level and saw when a job needs them.

**Are cheap tools worth it?** For a hammer or a tape, a mid-priced one lasts for years. Cheap screwdrivers and spanners often have soft tips that round off and slip, which is when hands get hurt.

**Can a stud finder find live cables?** Many have a cable mode, but none is a guarantee. Read the wall-fixings lessons for safe zones, and never drill where you are unsure. If you hit a cable, stop and call an electrician.

**What is the difference between Phillips and Pozidriv?** Both are cross-head drives. Pozidriv has straighter sides and extra small lines on the screw head, so it grips better. Use the matching driver, or the tip will slip and damage the head.
