## What it does

Before you hang anything, you need to know two things: what the wall is made of, and what is hidden inside it. This lesson shows you how to tell the common wall types apart with a knock, a magnet and a tiny test hole, how to find the wooden or metal studs in a hollow wall, and where electric cables and water pipes usually run so you can keep your drill well away from them. The wall picture lets you tap six drill spots and find out which are safe, then test yourself.

It is the first lesson of the free **Wall fixings** course. Once you know your wall, choose the fixing with [[pick-wall-plug|Pick the right wall plug]], then put it to use in [[hang-picture-mirror|Hang a picture or mirror]].

## How to use it

1. Read the **Stay safe** box. The most important rule: never drill straight above, below or beside a switch or socket.
2. Look at the wall types table, then work down the steps with the real wall in front of you. Tick each one when it is done. Ticks are not saved unless you tick **Remember my ticks on this device**.
3. In **Safe to drill here?**, tap each numbered spot to see why it is safe or dangerous. Press **Test me** to be asked for each one.
4. Take the quiz. Press **Print checklist** for a paper copy to keep with your drill.

## What each wall type means for you

- **Drywall or plasterboard on studs:** a thin board on a frame of wood or metal studs. Light things can go in the board with a hollow-wall fixing; heavy things should screw into a stud.
- **Lath and plaster:** older houses. Plaster over thin wooden strips. It can crack and crumble, so drill gently with the hammer off.
- **Solid brick:** strong. Plastic plugs hold well. Drill into the brick, not the soft mortar between bricks.
- **Concrete:** very strong but hard to drill. You need a hammer drill. Stop if you hit steel reinforcing bar.
- **Block:** dense breeze or cinder block behaves like brick, though some have hollow cores. Lightweight aircrete is soft and needs special plugs.
- **Tiles:** a layer over any of the above. Drill the tile with a tile bit first, then the wall behind.

## Worked example

You want to hang a mirror on a living room wall. You knock along it: hollow, with a solid patch every 40 cm or so. A magnet sticks to a line of screw heads 40 cm from the corner, and your stud finder agrees. So it is plasterboard on studs. There is a light switch by the door, 1 m from the corner, so you keep clear of the line straight above and below it and the line straight across at its height. The detector shows nothing around your spot 20 cm left of the second stud. You mark it on masking tape and choose a hollow-wall fixing, or move the mirror so its fixings hit the stud.

## FAQs

**Can I trust a stud finder?** Mostly, on plain drywall. Lath, foil-backed board and tiles can confuse them. Check with a magnet or a tiny test hole hidden behind where the item will go.

**Do cables really always run straight?** They should, but old or DIY wiring can go anywhere. Treat the straight lines as a minimum and scan every spot with a detector.

**What about the UK safe zones in other countries?** The 150 mm and in-line zones are a UK rule. The US and Canadian codes protect cables differently, for example with steel plates where cables pass close to the face of a stud. Check your local rules.

**How deep can cables be?** Some are just under the surface. A depth stop on the bit and a detector both help, but nothing replaces keeping away from the danger lines.
