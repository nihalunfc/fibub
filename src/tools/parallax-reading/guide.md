## What it does

When you read a scale, the thing you are measuring is usually a little way **behind** the scale marks: the pencil sits beside a ruler that has thickness, the thread of a thermometer is inside the glass, and the water in a measuring cylinder is in the middle of the cylinder while the marks are on the outside. If your eye is not straight in line, your line of sight crosses the scale at the wrong mark. This is **parallax error**. The page draws each instrument as your eye sees it, with a side view of your eye, the scale and the object. Move the **eye** slider and the reading shifts, even though nothing has moved. Type what you read and press **Check**.

## How to use it

1. Choose an instrument: **ruler**, **thermometer** or **measuring cylinder**.
2. Leave the eye **straight on (0°)**, read the scale and type your reading. Press **Check**.
3. Move the eye slider to 30° above (or to the right for the ruler) and look again: the pencil end, the thread or the water surface now lines up with a different mark.
4. Press **New object** for a new length, temperature or volume.
5. The **Level** switch (Class 9 and up) adds the least count, the uncertainty of a reading, the parallax error d·tanθ and your percentage error.

## Worked example

The water surface in the measuring cylinder is 12 mm behind the scale, and each millilitre takes 1.8 mm of the scale. With your eye 30° above the surface, the line of sight crosses the scale 12 × tan 30° = 12 × 0.577 = 6.93 mm higher, which is 6.93 ÷ 1.8 = **3.85 mL** too high. A volume of 46 mL reads as about 50 mL. The ruler is only 3 mm thick and 1 cm is 10 mm long, so at 30° the error is 3 × 0.577 ÷ 10 = 0.17 cm, nearly 2 mm.

A reading is good to about half the smallest division: ± 0.05 cm on a millimetre ruler, ± 0.5 °C on a thermometer marked in degrees, ± 1 mL on a cylinder marked every 2 mL.

## Tips

- Always bring your eye level with the mark, or straight above it for a ruler on a table.
- Read a measuring cylinder at the bottom of the curved surface (the meniscus) for water.
- Start a ruler measurement from a clear mark, not a worn end.

## FAQ

**Why does a thicker gap make it worse?** The shift is d·tanθ, so double the gap means double the error at the same angle.

**Do digital instruments have parallax error?** Not from reading a scale, but they have their own limits, such as the last digit.
