## What it does

Money you put into a UK pension gets tax relief: the government gives back the tax you paid on it. How you get it depends on your scheme. With **relief at source** you pay 80% and your provider adds 20%; higher-rate payers claim the rest themselves. With **net pay** your employer takes the pension before tax. With **salary sacrifice** you also save National Insurance. This calculator shows all three side by side for 2026/27, with Scotland's own bands, and checks your contributions against the annual allowance, the taper for high earners and carry-forward. Add your date of birth to see your State Pension age.

See your whole payslip with the [[uk-income-tax|UK tax calculator]], then plan how long the pot lasts with the [[retirement-planner]].

## How to use it

1. Choose **where you live** and type your **salary** and any **other taxable income**.
2. Type what goes **into your pot** a year, including the relief (the gross amount), and what your **employer** adds.
3. If you might go over the allowance, type any **unused allowance** from the last three years.

## What each option does

- **Relief at source**: the table shows what leaves your bank (80%), the higher-rate claim and the real cost.
- **Net pay**: relief at your top rate straight away, but nothing if you earn under the personal allowance.
- **Salary sacrifice**: as net pay, plus National Insurance saved.
- **Money purchase allowance**: if you have already drawn taxable money flexibly, only £10,000 a year can go into defined contribution pensions without a charge.

## Worked example

Priya lives in England, earns £60,000 and puts £5,000 gross into a personal pension. She pays £4,000; her provider adds £1,000. £5,000 of her income was taxed at 40%, so she claims another £1,000 back: the **real cost is £3,000** and the relief **£2,000 (40%)**. Through salary sacrifice it would cost £2,900, as she also saves £100 of NI. In Scotland on £50,000, £4,000 gross gets 42% relief: £800 added and £880 to claim.

## FAQs

**How do I claim the higher-rate relief?** Through your Self Assessment return, or by asking HMRC if you do not file one.

**What is the annual allowance charge?** Tax on savings over your allowance, at your top rate. The figure here is an estimate.

**Is anything saved?** No. Nothing you type leaves the page. Not financial or tax advice; check the official source.
