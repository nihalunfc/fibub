## What it does

A dividend is a share of a company's profit paid to its shareholders. This calculator shows the **yield** (the dividend as a percentage of today's price), the **yield on cost** (the same dividend as a percentage of what you paid) and the income you get each year. It then projects the next years, with the dividend and the price growing at rates you choose, with or without reinvesting the dividends.

Dividends can be cut or stopped, so treat the growth rates as tests, not promises. To compare with other growth, use the [[compound-interest]] calculator; to measure what you really earned, the [[return-calculator]]; to see how income is taxed, the [[canada-income-tax]], [[uk-income-tax]] or [[india-income-tax]] tools.

## How to use it

1. Type the shares you hold, the price now and your average cost per share.
2. Type the dividend per share for one year, and how fast you expect the dividend and the price to grow (use 0 for none).
3. Choose the years and whether to reinvest (DRIP).
4. Read the yield cards and the year-by-year table.

## What each setting does

- **Dividend growth:** how much the yearly dividend per share rises each year.
- **Price growth:** how much the share price rises each year.
- **DRIP:** each year's dividends buy more shares at that year's end price, so next year's income is larger.

## Worked example

100 shares at a price of 100, paying 3 a share a year, bought at an average cost of 50. Yield is 3%, yield on cost is 6%, and income is 300 a year. With no growth and DRIP on, 300 buys 3 more shares, so year 2 income is 103 x 3 = 309.

## FAQs

**Is the dividend guaranteed?** No. Companies can change or stop it.

**Are dividends taxed?** Often, at rates that depend on where you live. Not financial or tax advice.

**Quarterly dividends?** Add them up and enter the yearly total per share.
