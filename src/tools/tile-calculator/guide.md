## What it does

This tile calculator counts the tiles and boxes you need for a floor or a wall, adds a share for cuts and breakages, and estimates the grout. The **Planks** tab does the same for laminate, vinyl or wood flooring sold by area per box.

If the walls are being painted or papered at the same time, the [[paint-calculator]] and the [[wallpaper-calculator]] work out the rest of the room.

## How to use it

1. Pick **Tiles** or **Planks**, and metric or imperial (changing units loads the example in those units).
2. Type the length and width of the area. For an L-shaped room add a second rectangle; to leave out a bath or a kitchen unit, add one set to **Take away**.
3. Type the tile size and choose the layout.
4. Type what a box holds, from the label.

## What each setting does

- **Layout**: straight lays use 10% waste; diagonal and herringbone use 15% because more tiles are cut. Choosing a layout fills in the waste box.
- **Waste and cuts**: the extra share added before rounding up. Raise it for small rooms or fragile tiles.
- **Box sold by**: tiles per box, or area per box. Boxes always round up.
- **Grout gap and tile thickness**: the joint width and depth. Small tiles need more grout.
- **Grout per litre of joint**: 1.6 kg is a rule of thumb for cement grout; the bag gives the real figure.

The count uses the tile size alone and leaves out the joints, which makes it a touch generous.

## Worked example

A floor 3 m by 4 m is 12 m². A 30 × 30 cm tile covers 0.09 m², so the floor takes 133.3 tiles. Add 10%: 133.3 × 1.1 = 146.7, rounded up to **147 tiles**. Boxes of 10 means **15 boxes**. With 3 mm joints and 8 mm tiles the grout is about 3.1 kg.

For planks, the same 12 m² plus 10% is 13.2 m². Boxes of 2.2 m² make exactly **6 boxes**.

## FAQs

**Should I round the tiles before adding waste?** No. Waste is added to the exact count, then rounded once, so you are not rounding up twice.

**Why buy extra?** Tiles and planks change shade between batches. Keep spares from the same batch.

**Does it work for walls?** Yes. Use wall width times height, and take away windows.
