## What it does

A large booking looks like good news until you count the regular customers it pushes out. If a group takes half your hotel for a weekend, a caterer fills a Friday, or a wholesale order uses most of a day's output, the regular business you would have sold that day is turned away. This tool puts both sides together so the decision is about net profit rather than the headline total.

You enter what you can supply each day, what regular customers would normally buy and pay, and what the group offers: price, how much they will really use, commission, and what else they spend. The tool works out how many regular sales you would lose, the profit you lose with them, the profit the group brings, and the lowest price that still beats regular business. It then says Accept, Negotiate or Decline and suggests a counter-offer. To go deeper on the pieces, use the [[break-even]] calculator for your own costs, the [[demand-forecaster]] for what regular demand to expect, and the [[pricing-recommender]] for the regular prices.

## How to use it

1. **Pick the example** closest to you (hotel group block, private dining or catering, bulk order) and type your own numbers over it.
2. **Your business:** how many you can supply a day, the cost to serve one, and the selling cost on regular sales, such as booking-site commission or card fees.
3. **The request:** the price offered, the share they will really take up, the share of the unused part you can resell, any agent commission, and the safety margin you want.
4. **What else they spend:** up to three extras per unit, with the profit you keep on them.
5. **Day by day:** for each day, the regular demand, the regular price and how many the group wants. Paste a table if you have one.
6. **Read the verdict.** Download the quote review as a CSV to keep with the deal.

## What each setting does

- **Take-up:** groups usually use less than they reserve. If they take 88% of what they asked for, you only earn from that part, and you can resell some of the rest.
- **Resale share:** the part of the unused rooms, seats or stock you expect to sell to someone else.
- **Safety margin:** the extra above break-even you want before you say yes. At 10%, a break-even price of 100 means you ask for 110.
- **Verdict:** Accept when the price clears break-even plus your margin. Negotiate when it is within 10% below break-even. Decline when it is further below.

## Worked example

A hotel with 120 rooms has a group asking for 21 to 23 rooms a night for four nights at 110 each. Regular guests would pay 110 to 195, it costs 25 to clean and serve a room, and booking sites take 12%. The group is expected to use 88% of its rooms and will spend about 55 more a night on food, drinks and meeting rooms, keeping 32% of it as profit. The tool finds that about 48 regular room nights would be pushed out, that the break-even price is 104.78 and that the group makes a net profit of about 347. The verdict is Negotiate, because the offer is above break-even but below break-even plus 10%. It suggests a counter-offer of 120.

## FAQs

**Why not just accept anything above my cost?** Because the room, table or stock you give the group is not free: you could have sold it to someone else. The cost that matters is the profit you lose from them.

**What if my regular demand is a guess?** The result is only as good as that forecast. Try a higher and a lower number and see whether the verdict changes.

**Does it count a repeat client?** No. A group that comes back every year is worth more than one visit; add that to your own judgement.

**Is my data kept?** Only if you tick "Remember my numbers on this device". Nothing is sent anywhere.
