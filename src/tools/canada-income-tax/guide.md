## What it does

Pick your province or territory and type your yearly pay. The calculator works out your 2026 federal tax, provincial or territorial tax, Canada Pension Plan (CPP) contributions and Employment Insurance (EI) premiums, then shows what is left: your take-home pay for the year, the month and every two weeks. A bar shows where each dollar goes, and the table lists every amount. Everything is worked out in your browser.

Once you know your take-home pay, plan where it goes with the [[budget-calculator|budget planner]], see how extra payments shrink a loan with the [[loan-payoff-planner]], or compare with the [[uk-income-tax|UK tax calculator]].

## How to use it

1. Choose your **province or territory**: where you lived on 31 December.
2. Type your **employment income**: salary, wages and bonuses before anything is taken off.
3. Add **other income** such as interest, rental profit or a pension. It is taxed, but no CPP or EI is paid on it.
4. Type any **RRSP deduction** you will claim. It lowers your taxable income.

## What each part means

- **Marginal rate**: the tax on your next $100 of income. Use it to see what a raise or an RRSP contribution is worth.
- **Average rate**: all income tax divided by your total income.
- **CPP2**: the second extra CPP contribution, 4% on pay between $74,600 and $85,000.
- **Ontario**: adds the surtax, the Health Premium and the low-income tax reduction.
- **Quebec**: uses QPP instead of CPP, the lower EI rate, QPIP, the 16.5% federal abatement and Quebec's own tax and deduction for workers.

## Worked example

Priya lives in Ontario and earns $60,000. CPP is $3,361.75 and EI $978.00. Federal tax is $5,338.30. Ontario tax is $2,382.20 plus the $600 Health Premium. Her take-home pay is $47,339.75 a year, about $3,945 a month. Her marginal rate is 29.65% and her average rate 13.9%.

## FAQs

**Is this exactly what my employer takes off?** Close, but payroll spreads the year over pay periods and your refund depends on credits this tool leaves out, such as tuition, donations and medical costs.

**Why did British Columbia's rate change in July?** BC raised its lowest rate to 5.6% for 2026, so payroll used a higher rate for the second half. This tool uses the yearly figures.

**Is anything saved?** No. Nothing you type leaves the page. Not financial or tax advice; check the official source.
