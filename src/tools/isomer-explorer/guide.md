## What it does

**Isomers** have the same molecular formula but different structures. This page lists every open-chain **structural isomer** of a small formula, each with its IUPAC name and condensed structural formula (like CH₃–CH(OH)–CH₃). **Tap any two** and it tells you what kind of isomers they are:

- **Chain isomers**: a different carbon skeleton (straight or branched).
- **Position isomers**: the same skeleton, with the group or double bond in a different place.
- **Functional isomers**: different families, such as an alcohol and an ether.
- **Metamers**: ethers or esters with different carbon groups on the two sides of the oxygen.

It also marks the isomers that come as **cis and trans** forms (geometric isomers) and those with a **chiral carbon** (optical isomers). It follows the Class 11 Chemistry chapter on organic chemistry basics (isomerism: structural and stereo) and the Class 10 idea that one formula can have more than one structure (butane and isobutane). Name your own structures with the [[carbon-chain-namer]], check rings and double bonds with the [[unsaturation-calculator]], and see how one isomer can still twist into many shapes with the [[conformation-spinner]].

## How to use it

1. Choose a **Molecular formula**. The list shows which families it covers.
2. Read the count and the list. Each card shows the isomer number, name, condensed formula, family and any cis–trans or chiral mark.
3. Tap two cards. The box above the list explains their relationship. Tap a card again to unpick it.
4. Try **Check yourself**: two counting questions and two "what kind of isomers?" questions.

## Worked example: C₄H₁₀O

The page finds **7 structural isomers**: 4 alcohols (butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol, 2-methylpropan-1-ol, 2-methylpropan-2-ol) and 3 ethers (1-methoxypropane, 2-methoxypropane, ethoxyethane). Tap butan-1-ol and butan-2-ol: same skeleton, –OH moved, so **position isomers**. Butan-1-ol and 2-methylpropan-1-ol: **chain isomers**. Butan-1-ol and ethoxyethane: alcohol and ether, **functional isomers**. Ethoxyethane (2 + 2 carbons round the O) and 1-methoxypropane (3 + 1): **metamers**. Butan-2-ol is marked chiral: its carbon 2 holds H, OH, CH₃ and C₂H₅.

## Tips

- **Count carbon skeletons first.** For C₅H₁₂O, there are 3 skeletons with 5 carbons; the –OH can go on 8 different carbons, and the O can sit in 6 different C–C bonds.
- **Same name twice means the same compound.** Turning or flipping a drawing does not make a new isomer.
- Esters are named alkyl alkanoate: "1-methylethyl methanoate" is also called propan-2-yl methanoate (isopropyl formate).

## FAQ
