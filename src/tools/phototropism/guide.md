## What it does

Plants cannot walk to the light, but they can grow towards it. A growth movement towards or away from something is a **tropism**: towards light is **positive phototropism**, and growing in response to gravity is **geotropism** (shoots grow up, roots grow down). This page grows a seedling in fast motion. The tip of the shoot senses light coming from one side, and a plant hormone, **auxin**, gathers on the shaded side. Auxin makes cells grow longer, so the shaded side grows faster than the lit side and the shoot curves towards the lamp. You can repeat the famous experiments too: cover the tip with foil, or cut it off. Plants bend to light so their leaves catch more of it for the [[photosynthesis-lab]]; plants have no nerves, unlike the animal control system in the [[brain-map]].

## Using it

1. Pick a **Level**. *Class 10* explains the bending. *Class 11* adds how far the tip is from pointing at the lamp, the history of the tip experiments, and a table of the five plant hormones.
2. Choose the **light**: a lamp you can drag anywhere in the picture (or move with the lamp position slider), light from straight above, or dark.
3. Choose what happens to the **tip**: left alone, covered with a foil cap (it cannot see light), or cut off (no auxin is made).
4. Stand the **pot** upright or lay it on its side.
5. Press **Grow** to play hour by hour (up to 44 hours), or **+1 hour** to step. Move the lamp at any time and the shoot changes course. **Start again** plants a new seedling.

## Worked example

Start with the lamp in its first place, up and to the right. The light reaches the tip 48° from the way it is pointing, so the shaded side gets **72 %** of the auxin and the lit side 28 %. After **10 hours** the tip leans 36° to the right and the split is down to 59 : 41, because the tip now faces the lamp more. After 20 hours it leans 43°, where the pull of the light balances the shoot's urge to grow upright. Cover the tip and grow again: after 10 hours the shoot is just as tall but perfectly straight. Cut the tip off and it barely grows at all.

## Where this fits in school

Class 10 science (control and coordination) covers coordination in plants: tropisms, auxin and the other plant hormones, and movements like the folding of touch-me-not leaves. Class 11 biology (plant growth and development) adds the discovery of auxin and the jobs of auxins, gibberellins, cytokinins, abscisic acid and ethylene. This page is an independent learning aid. It is not made by or connected with NCERT, and it does not copy any textbook.

## Questions

**Do roots bend away from light?** A little, in many plants, but their main response is to gravity. In this model the root mostly turns down, with a small push away from the lamp.

**Is a touch-me-not plant's folding a tropism?** No. It is fast and does not involve growth: cells near the leaf base lose water and change shape.

**Is anything saved?** No. Nothing you do is stored or sent anywhere.
