## Rent or buy: the honest comparison

Buying is not just the mortgage. You also pay property tax, upkeep and insurance, and you pay a chunk to buy and again to sell. Renting costs less up front, so the money you do not spend on a home can be invested. This tool follows both paths month by month and shows what each would be worth, so you can see the year buying pulls ahead, if it ever does.

The mortgage payment comes from the [[mortgage-calculator]]. Check that you can afford either path with the [[budget-calculator]], and see what rising prices do to rent in the [[inflation-calculator]].

## How to use it

1. Enter today's **rent** and how much it rises each year.
2. Enter the home's **price**, **down payment**, **mortgage rate** and **length**.
3. Fill in the yearly running costs: **property tax**, **maintenance** and **insurance**.
4. Set how fast you think the **home price grows** and what **return** you could get by investing.
5. Add **buying** and **selling** costs (legal fees, taxes, agent fees), then pick how many years to look ahead.

## What the numbers mean

- **Net worth buying** is the home's value minus selling costs, minus what you still owe, plus any savings left over when renting would have cost more.
- **Net worth renting** is the investments: the down payment and buying costs you did not spend, plus every month's saving when owning costs more than rent.
- **Break-even** is the first year buying is ahead.

## Worked example

Rent 2,000 a month, rising 3% a year, against a 500,000 home with 20% down, a 5% mortgage over 25 years, 1% tax, 1% upkeep, 1,200 insurance, 3% growth, 5% investment return, 3% to buy and 5% to sell. Buying pulls ahead in year 16. Before that, renting and investing wins, because the costs of buying take years to earn back.

## FAQs

**Why does the answer change so much?** Small changes in home growth, rent growth and investment return move the answer a lot. Try a cautious and a hopeful case.

**Does it count tax breaks?** No. Mortgage interest deductions or capital gains rules differ by country, so they are left out. Not financial or tax advice.

**What if I will sell sooner than the break-even year?** Then buying would have cost you more than renting. Look at the table for the year you expect to move.
