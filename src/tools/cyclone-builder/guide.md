## What it does

A **cyclone** is a huge storm spinning around a centre of very low pressure. This page shows one forming, seen from above. Air rushes toward a low over the sea, but the spinning Earth turns moving air sideways: to the **right** in the northern hemisphere and to the **left** in the southern. This is the **Coriolis effect**. So the air circles the centre, **anticlockwise** in the north and **clockwise** in the south.

Over a warm sea, water vapour rises, turns to cloud and gives out heat, which keeps air rising and pressure falling. The storm feeds itself and an **eye** forms. Cool the sea, switch off the spin or move to the equator and it fades.

It follows Class 8 Science, Pressure, Winds, Storms and Cyclones, and Class 11 Geography. Before this, [[wind-from-pressure]] shows why air moves from high to low pressure, and [[standard-atmosphere]] shows air pressure itself.

## How to use it

1. Press **Start again** to begin with still air around a weak low.
2. Set the **latitude**: 15° N is about the Bay of Bengal; negative is south.
3. Set the **sea surface temperature**.
4. Untick **Earth spins** to see a planet with no Coriolis effect.
5. Read the strength bar and which way the air circles. At **Class 11** the page shows f = 2Ω sin φ.

## Worked example

At **15° N**, sea **29 °C**, spin on, the air circles **anticlockwise** and within about 20 seconds the label reads **Growing: a cyclone**. At Class 11, f = 2 × 7.292 × 10⁻⁵ × sin 15° = **3.775 × 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹**. At **15° S** f is negative and the storm turns **clockwise**. At **0°** f = 0 and the air runs straight into the low. Cool the sea to **25 °C** and it fades, as below about 26.5 °C there is too little warm, moist air.

## Tips

- The Coriolis effect is not a push; it is what straight motion looks like from a spinning Earth.
- Bay of Bengal cyclones are most common before and after the monsoon.
- In a cyclone warning, follow the official weather service.

## FAQ

**Do sinks drain the other way in the south?** Not reliably; the effect is too weak at that size.

**Are the strength numbers real?** No, they are a cartoon.
