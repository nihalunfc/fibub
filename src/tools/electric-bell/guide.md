## What it does

An electric bell has no motor. It rings because of a clever loop. When you press the switch, current flows through a coil wrapped on an iron core, and the core becomes an **electromagnet**. It pulls a springy iron **armature**, and a small hammer on the armature hits the **gong**. But as the armature moves, it leaves the **contact screw**, which breaks the circuit. The current stops, the electromagnet switches off, the spring pulls the armature back, the contact closes again and the whole thing repeats, many times a second.

This page animates that cycle. The contact between the armature and the screw glows when the circuit is closed, and the coil lights up when the electromagnet is on. It follows the Class 8 Science topic on the effects of electric current. To build the magnet yourself, try the [[electromagnet-builder]], and to see how a switch and a battery make a circuit, use the [[simple-circuit-lab]].

## How to use it

1. Watch the animation, or press **Pause** to stop it.
2. Read the line under the picture; it says whether the circuit is closed or open.
3. Slide the **Current** to change how fast the bell rings.

## What each setting does

- **Current**: more current makes the electromagnet stronger and the cycle faster. In this model the bell rings 5 times a second for every ampere. At 0 A there is no magnet, so no ring.
- **Pause / Play**: freezes the picture so you can study one moment.

## Worked example

At 1.5 A the model gives 5 × 1.5 = 7.5 rings per second, so one ring takes 1 ÷ 7.5, about 0.13 seconds. Slow down to 0.5 A and you get 2.5 rings per second.

## FAQs

**Why does the circuit have to break?** If it stayed closed, the electromagnet would keep the armature stuck against it, and the hammer would hit the gong only once.

**Why is the armature made of iron?** Iron is attracted by a magnet and switches easily, while the core loses most of its magnetism when the current stops.

**Are the numbers real?** No. The ring rate is a rounded teaching value, and the picture is simplified.
