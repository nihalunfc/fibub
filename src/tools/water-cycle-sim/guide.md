## What it does

The water on Earth goes round and round. The Sun warms the sea and water **evaporates** into the air as invisible vapour. Wind carries the moist air inland, and where it is pushed up over hills it **cools**. Cool air cannot hold as much vapour, so the extra **condenses** into tiny droplets: a **cloud**. When the droplets grow heavy they fall as **rain**. On the ground some rain **runs off** into streams and rivers and back to the sea; the rest **soaks in**, refilling the water in the ground, and plants pull some of it up and breathe it out again (**transpiration**).

This page puts all of that on one small coast. You set the **sun**, the **air temperature**, the **wind** and the kind of **ground**, press **Play** or **Run 1 day**, and watch the arrows, cloud, rain and river change. A **water budget** adds up every flow in millimetres of water over the land, and its last line checks that water is never made or lost, only moved.

It follows Class 6 to 8 Science (the water cycle, evaporation and condensation, why the Earth is a life-sustaining planet, conserving water) and Class 9 work on evaporation and humidity, with a **Class 9 to 12** level that shows the numbers behind "warm air holds more water". To see what evaporation and condensation mean for the particles, try [[states-of-matter]]; for the plants' part, the [[transpiration-lab]]; and for gases dissolving in water, the [[dissolving-lab]].

## How to use it

1. Set the four **settings**. **Sun** (0 to 100%) sets how hard the sea is heated. **T** is the air temperature at the coast (0 to 40 °C). **Wind** (0 to 100%) is how strongly air moves in from the sea. **Ground** is forest, fields or city: it decides what share of the rain runs straight off (15%, 35% or 80%).
2. Press **Run 1 day** to move on 24 hours, or **Play** to watch it go (12 model hours a second) and **Pause** to stop. **Start again** resets the water, starting with the air half full of vapour for the temperature you set.
3. Watch the picture: the evaporation arrows grow with the flow, the cloud grows with the water in it, and the river thickens after rain.
4. Read the **water budget** bars and lines. Switch the **Level** to **Class 9 to 12** for the vapour pressure and grams of water a cubic metre of air can hold.

## Worked example

Leave the settings as the page opens (sun 70%, 25 °C, wind 50%, forest) and press **Run 1 day**. About 5.8 mm of water has evaporated from the sea and 0.2 mm has come up from the plants. At 25 °C the air over the land can hold up to 30 mm of vapour, but over the hills, 9 °C colder, only 17.2 mm, so the vapour beyond that has started to form cloud, and the first 0.4 mm of rain has fallen. Press it twice more: after 3 days 11.9 mm of rain has fallen, of which 1.8 mm ran off and 10.1 mm soaked into the forest floor. Do the same with **City** and about 9.1 of the 11.4 mm runs off, leaving only 2.3 mm to refill the ground: that is why cities flood and their wells run dry.

## Check yourself

Five fresh questions: runoff from a rain shower, what soaked in, grams of water that condense when air cools, litres a pond loses to evaporation, and cubic metres of rain on a town. Nothing is saved.

## Tips

- Millimetres of rain are litres per square metre: 1 mm on 1 m² is 1 litre.
- No wind means the moist air stays over the sea, so the land gets far less rain. Mountains facing a sea wind (like the Western Ghats in the monsoon) are among the wettest places on Earth.
- Forests act like a sponge: most rain soaks in and is released slowly, so rivers keep flowing in the dry season.

## FAQ

**Is this a weather forecast?** No. It is a teaching model with a few round numbers chosen so the parts of the cycle are easy to see. Real weather has many more processes.

**Why does the river carry water even before it rains?** Water already in the ground seeps slowly into the river. Rain then adds runoff on top.
