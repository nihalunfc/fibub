## What it does

This lesson shows you how to saw a piece of wood straight and square with an ordinary handsaw, the kind of cut you need for a shelf, a frame or a box. Each of the seven steps has a drawing and a **Done** tick, so you can follow along at the bench. The kerf sim shows why it matters which side of the pencil line you cut on: pick a side and see the piece come out the right length or a little short. A put-in-order list and a short quiz check what you learned, and you can print the steps.

It is the first lesson of the free **Light carpentry** course. Next, join your cut pieces with [[drill-and-screw|drilling and screwing]], then put both skills together to [[build-simple-shelf|build a simple shelf]].

## How to use it

1. Read the **Stay safe** box. Wear safety glasses and clamp the wood before you start.
2. Work down the steps with a scrap piece of wood first. Tick each step as you do it. Ticks are not saved unless you tick **Remember my ticks on this device**.
3. Try the kerf sim: set where you marked, how wide your saw cuts and which side you cut on.
4. Put the steps in order and take the quiz.

## What each part means

- **Square line:** a pencil line at exactly 90 degrees to the edge, drawn against a try square or speed square. Carry it round onto the edge so you can see if the saw drifts.
- **Waste side:** the side of the line you will throw away. Mark it with a cross so you never forget.
- **Kerf:** the slot the saw cuts. It has width, so it eats some wood. Cut on the waste side and the kerf comes out of the waste, not your piece.
- **Cross-cut:** a cut across the grain, the most common cut at home. A cross-cut handsaw works best held at about 45 degrees to the wood.

## Worked example

You need a shelf 600 mm (about 23⅝ in) long. You measure 600 mm from the square end, make a small V mark, square a line across and put a cross on the waste side. Your saw cuts a kerf about 2 mm wide. If you saw right down the middle of the line, the piece comes out 599 mm. If you saw on the wrong side, it is 598 mm, and two shelves cut that way will not match. Saw on the waste side with the line just showing and the piece is 600 mm. From a 2400 mm board with a 3 mm kerf you get only three 600 mm pieces, not four: each cut eats 3 mm.

## FAQs

**Why does my saw wander off the line?** Usually you are pushing too hard or twisting the handle. Use long, light strokes, keep your arm, shoulder and the saw in one line, and look down on the saw so you can see both sides of the blade.

**Why does the wood split at the end of the cut?** The offcut drops and tears the last fibres. Hold or support the offcut for the last few strokes and slow down.

**Pull saw or push saw?** Both work. Japanese-style pull saws cut on the pull stroke, have a thin kerf and are easy to start. Western handsaws cut on the push. Hold either one lightly.

**How do I check the cut is square?** Hold a try square against the face and the edge of the cut end. If light shows under the blade, plane or sand the high side.
