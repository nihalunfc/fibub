## What it does

Biologists place every species in a set of nested groups called **ranks**. From the biggest to the smallest:

**Kingdom → Phylum (Division for plants) → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species**

Each step down holds fewer organisms that are more alike. The species name is a **binomial**: the genus plus one more word, such as *Panthera tigris* for the tiger or *Mangifera indica* for the mango.

This page draws that hierarchy as a ladder for two organisms at once. Where both have the same name on a rung, the rung is joined across the middle; where they part, the ladder splits in two. So you can see straight away how closely two organisms are related: a tiger and a lion stay together down to the genus *Panthera*; a house cat and a dog only to the order Carnivora; a human and a mango not even at the first rung.

It fits the Class 11 biology chapter on the living world (taxonomic categories and the hierarchy), and the Class 9 chapter on diversity. Sort organisms into kingdoms first with the [[five-kingdoms]] game, and into animal phyla with the [[animal-key]].

## How to use it

1. Pick an organism for the **Left** and **Right** columns.
2. The title names the lowest rank they share. Shared rungs sit in the shaded box in the middle; below the split, each column shows its own names.
3. The line under the ladder says how close that makes them (same order: fairly close; same family: close; same genus: very close).
4. **Put the ranks in order**: tap the seven rank names from kingdom down to species. A wrong tap tells you whether that rank is lower down or already placed. **Shuffle again** starts a new round.

## Worked example

The page opens with the **tiger** on the left and the **house cat** on the right. The title reads "Tiger and House cat share down to the family: Felidae". The shaded box covers Animalia, Chordata, Mammalia, Carnivora and Felidae; then the ladder splits: genus *Panthera* and *Felis*, species *Panthera tigris* and *Felis catus*. The note says "Same family: close relatives."

Change the right column to **Lion**: now even the genus is shared (*Panthera*), and only the species differs (*Panthera leo*).

## Tips

- A handy way to remember the order: **K**ing **P**hilip **C**ame **O**ver **F**or **G**ood **S**oup.
- Animal family names end in -idae (Felidae, Hominidae); plant family names end in -aceae (Poaceae, Anacardiaceae).
- Write a scientific name with a capital letter for the genus and a small letter for the species epithet, in italics (underline it when handwriting).

## FAQ

**Why do the plants say Dicotyledonae and Monocotyledonae?** That is the class level used in school biology. Modern plant classification (based on DNA) uses different groups above the order, but the order, family, genus and species shown here stay the same.

**Is a dog Canis familiaris or Canis lupus familiaris?** Both are used. Many biologists treat the dog as a subspecies of the wolf; school books usually write Canis familiaris.
