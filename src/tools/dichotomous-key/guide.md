## What it does

A **dichotomous key** names a living thing by asking a chain of questions that each have only two answers, yes or no ("dichotomous" means "cut in two"). Each answer sends you to the next question until only one name is left. Scientists use keys to identify plants, insects and fish in the field.

This page has eight animals drawn simply: an earthworm, a spider, a butterfly, a fish, a frog, a snake, a pigeon and a cat.

- **Follow a key**: tap an animal (or press **Random mystery**), look at its drawing, and answer each question. The key is shown as a tree with your route highlighted. If you answer wrongly, the page tells you which question went astray.
- **Build a key**: start with all eight animals. Pick a question; the page splits the group into "yes" and "no". Keep splitting the first group that still has more than one animal. Questions that don't split a group are refused, with the reason. When every animal stands alone, press **Follow my key** to test it.

It follows the Class 9 science chapter *Patterns in Life: Diversity and Classification* and the Class 11 biology chapter *The Living World*. Where these animals sit in the big tree of life is in [[classification-tree]], and a key for any group of organisms, written as text, is in [[dichotomous-key-builder]].

## How to use it

1. **Follow a key:** pick the mystery animal, then press **Yes** or **No** for each question.
2. Read the result. Tap another animal to start again.
3. **Build a key:** choose a question from the list and press **Use this question**. Use **Undo** or **Start again** as you like.
4. When it's finished, compare your longest route with the ready-made key, then press **Follow my key**.

## A worked example

Mystery: the snake. Backbone? Yes. Feathers? No. Fur? No. Legs? No. Fins? No. The key says snake, in 5 questions. The pigeon takes only 2 (backbone yes, feathers yes).

Now build: "Does it have a backbone?" splits the eight into five with backbones and three without. "Does it have legs?" then splits the five into frog, pigeon, cat and fish, snake.

## Tips

- Good questions are about things you can see for sure (wings, legs), not size or colour, which vary.
- A question that splits a group in half makes a shorter key.
- Every route must end at one name only.

## FAQ

**Is a spider an insect?** No. Insects have six legs; spiders have eight, which is why the key asks.
