## What it does

Push a drawing pin in with your thumb and it goes in easily, yet the same push from your palm leaves no mark. The difference is **pressure**: how much force lands on each square metre. This page presses two objects into pretend soft clay with the **same force**. A needle point puts it all on a tiny spot and slides in; a flat palm spreads it out and hardly marks the clay.

It shows the pressure under each object, how many times bigger one is, and the sum **P = F ÷ A** with your numbers. A graph plots pressure against area, so you can see that halving the area doubles the pressure.

It follows Class 8 Science, pressure, winds, storms and cyclones (force on an area, the pascal, sharp and broad objects), and at **Class 11** thrust and pressure in powers of ten. Next, [[liquid-pressure]] shows pressure growing with depth in water, and [[barometer-altitude]] shows air pressure falling as you climb.

## How to use it

1. Choose the **left** and **right** objects, from a needle point to a snowshoe. Pick **Your own area** on the left for any area from 0.01 mm² to 1 m².
2. Set the **force** with the slider, or drag up and down on the picture. Both objects get the same force.
3. Read the pressures. The colour shows whether the clay hardly marks, dents, or the object goes in.
4. Switch **Level** to **Class 11** for powers of ten and to make the force come from **a resting mass** (weight = mass × 9.8 m/s², the thrust).
5. Try **Check yourself** at the bottom.

## Worked example

A **needle point** (0.01 mm² = 0.00000001 m²) and a **flat palm** (100 cm² = 0.01 m²) are each pressed with **100 N**.

- Needle: P = 100 ÷ 0.00000001 = **10 GPa**. It goes in.
- Palm: P = 100 ÷ 0.01 = **10 kPa**. A dent only.

The needle gives **1,000,000 times more pressure**, because its area is a million times smaller. A 2 kg brick (thrust 19.6 N) lying flat on 200 cm² presses with 980 Pa; on its end (50 cm²) it presses with 3,920 Pa.

## FAQ

**Is a pascal big or small?** Small: one newton on a whole square metre. That is why kPa and MPa are common.

**What is the difference between thrust and pressure?** Thrust is the total force on a surface, in newtons. Pressure is that force shared over the area, in pascals.

**Are the clay limits real?** No. They are made-up values to show the idea.

**Is anything stored?** No. Everything runs in your browser.
