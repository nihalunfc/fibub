## What it does

A map is a drawing made smaller by the same amount in every direction. Its **scale** says by how much. A scale of **1 : 50 000** means 1 cm on the map stands for 50 000 cm on the ground, which is 500 m. A scale is just a ratio, so the same thinking scales a recipe up or down.

This page has three parts:

- **Measure on the map.** A made-up town is drawn on a grid of 1 cm squares. Drag the pins A and B (or pick two places from the lists) and the page measures the gap in centimetres, multiplies by the scale, and changes the answer into metres or kilometres, step by step. A scale bar in the corner changes with the scale.
- **From real distance to the map.** Type a real distance and see how long it would be on the map.
- **Recipe scaler.** A lemon rice recipe for 4 people, scaled to any number of people by multiplying every amount by the same factor.

It fits Class 8 Mathematics, proportional reasoning (scale drawings, maps and the unitary method), and links to geography map work. Mixes kept in the same ratio are shown in the [[ratio-mixer]]; a scale drawing and the real thing are [[similar-triangles|similar figures]], with every length in the same ratio.

## How to use it

- Tap a scale: **1 : 10 000**, **1 : 25 000**, **1 : 50 000** or **1 : 100 000**.
- Drag a pin, or choose places in **From** and **To**. The big line gives the result; the steps show the working.
- In the second box, type a real distance in km.
- In the recipe, move the slider or type the number of people.

## A worked example

The page starts with Home to School at 1 : 50 000.

1. The pins are 8.1 cm apart on the map.
2. Real distance = 8.1 × 50 000 = 405 000 cm.
3. 100 000 cm = 1 km, so 405 000 cm = 4.05 km.

Switch to 1 : 100 000 and the same 8.1 cm stands for 8.1 km: twice the scale number, twice the distance.

Backwards: 3 km = 300 000 cm, and at 1 : 50 000 that is 300 000 ÷ 50 000 = 6 cm on the map.

Recipe for 6 people instead of 4: the factor is 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5, so 4 cups of rice become 6 cups and 60 g of peanuts become 90 g.

## FAQ

**Is 1 : 100 000 a bigger or smaller scale than 1 : 10 000?** Smaller: things look smaller and the map covers more ground. 1 : 10 000 shows more detail of a small area.

**Why is the measured distance shorter than the walk?** The pins measure a straight line. Roads bend, so the trip along them is longer.
