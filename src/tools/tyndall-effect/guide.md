## What it does

Salt water, milk and muddy water are all "something mixed into water", yet they behave very differently. The difference is the size of the particles. This tool lets you test a mixture the way a school lab would.

Pick a mixture and the torch shines a beam through the beaker. In a **true solution** such as salt water the beam's path cannot be seen at all. In a **colloid** such as milk, starch solution or fog the path lights up, because the particles are just big enough to scatter light sideways: the **Tyndall effect**. In a **suspension** such as muddy water the liquid is cloudy and little light reaches the screen. **Leave to stand** shows which ones settle, and **Filter it** shows which leave something on the filter paper. Drag the particle size slider to move smoothly from one kind to the next.

It follows the school topics on mixtures: solutes, solvents and concentration (Class 8 Science, solutes, solvents and solutions) and true solutions, colloids and suspensions, the Tyndall effect and the types of colloid (Class 9 Science, exploring mixtures and their separation). Separate real mixtures step by step in [[separation-lab]], and see why scattering makes the sky blue in [[sky-colour]].

## How to use it

1. Choose a **Level**. Class 9 adds the colloid's dispersed phase, dispersion medium and type (sol, emulsion, aerosol and so on) and the size boundaries.
2. Pick a **Mixture**, or move the **Particle size** slider to try any size; the arrow on the scale under the beaker shows where it sits.
3. **Torch off** and **Torch on** switch the beam. **Leave to stand** waits a while; **Filter it** pours it through filter paper. The text under the pill says what you would see.
4. In **Make a solution**, set the grams of salt or sugar and of water to get the mass percentage.

## Worked example

Pick **Milk with water** (particles about 400 nm, a typical rounded value). The pill says **A colloid**: the beam shows, nothing settles and everything passes through filter paper. Its dispersed phase is a liquid (fat drops) in a liquid medium: an **emulsion**.

Switch to **Salt in water** (about 0.3 nm): a true solution. No beam, no settling, nothing on the filter. Then **Muddy water** (about 20 µm): a suspension, cloudy, it settles, and filtering leaves the mud behind.

In **Make a solution**, **20 g** of salt in **180 g** of water makes 200 g of solution: 20 ÷ 200 × 100 = **10 %** by mass.

## Tips

- A colloid can look perfectly clear and still be a colloid. The torch test tells you.
- Filter paper cannot separate a colloid; a centrifuge or special membranes can.
- The size limits are rough. Nature does not switch sharply at exactly 1 nm or 1000 nm.

## FAQ

**Why can I not see the beam in clean air or salt water?** You only see light that reaches your eye. In a true solution almost nothing sends the light sideways, so from the side the beam is invisible.

**Is milk a solution?** No. It is an emulsion, a colloid of fat drops in water, which is why it is white and shows the Tyndall effect.
