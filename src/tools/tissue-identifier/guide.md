## What it does

A **tissue** is a group of similar cells working together on one job. Under a microscope, each tissue has a few tell-tale features: thick corners on its cell walls, stripes across its fibres, rings around a canal. Learn those features and you can name a tissue at a glance.

This page has simple drawings of **15 tissues**:

- **Plant:** meristematic tissue, parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma, xylem, phloem, and epidermis with stomata.
- **Animal:** squamous and columnar epithelium, blood, bone, striated (skeletal), smooth and cardiac muscle, and nervous tissue (a neuron).

In **Explore**, pick a tissue and tap its numbered dots to see the features that give it away, where it is found and what it does. In **Identify**, the page shows a drawing with no name and four choices; after you answer, the features appear so you can see what you should have spotted. A table compares the three kinds of muscle side by side.

It follows the Class 9 science chapter *Tissues in Action* and helps with the Class 11 chapters on plant anatomy and animal tissues. Single cells and their parts are in [[cell-explorer]], water moving up through xylem is in [[transpiration-lab]], and how meristem cells divide is in [[cell-division]].

## How to use it

1. **Explore:** tap a tissue name. Tap a numbered dot on the drawing (or a feature in the list) to highlight it.
2. Read **Where it is found** and **What it does**.
3. **Identify:** press the Identify button. Choose one of the four names. A green answer is right; if you pick wrong, the right one turns green too. Press **Next drawing**.
4. Use the **Muscle types compared** table to learn striated, smooth and cardiac muscle together.

## A worked example

The page opens on **parenchyma**. Its three clues are: (1) small spaces between the round cells, (2) thin cell walls, (3) a large vacuole with the nucleus pushed to one side. Now tap **Collenchyma**: the cells fit closely and their walls are thick only at the corners (dark blobs). Tap **Sclerenchyma**: the walls are thick all round and the cells have only a tiny empty space inside, because they are dead.

So the three simple plant tissues line up as: thin walls everywhere (parenchyma), thick corners (collenchyma), thick everywhere (sclerenchyma).

## Tips

- **Plant or animal?** Plant cells have walls, so their outlines are firm and often six-sided. Animal tissues have no walls.
- **Muscles:** stripes and many edge nuclei mean striated; no stripes and spindles mean smooth; stripes with branches and dark cross lines (intercalated discs) mean cardiac.
- **Blood** is the only tissue here whose cells float in a liquid.
