## What it does

Pick the country whose labour law covers your private-sector job, type your service, pay and the days you have used, and the calculator shows the annual leave the law sets so far this leave year, the days left, the leave salary for any number of days, and what the law sets for unused days if the job ended today. It also shows the daily rate and how it was worked out.

Add this to your end-of-service pay with the [[gratuity-calculator]], or see how your pay splits into basic and allowances with [[salary-breakdown]].

## How to use it

1. Pick **Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia or the UAE**.
2. Type your **completed years** and the **months since your last work anniversary** (your leave year), or open More options and enter your start date.
3. Type your **monthly basic** and **allowances**, the **days taken** this leave year and any **days carried over**.
4. Set the **days of leave to price**.

**Include allowances** follows what the law says: Oman and Saudi Arabia pay leave on the full wage, Qatar on the basic wage, and the UAE pays leave itself on the wage but unused days at the end on the basic wage. Untick or tick it to see both.

## Worked example

In Dubai, Amal has 3 years and 8 months of service, a basic of AED 6,000 and allowances of AED 3,000. She has taken 5 days this leave year and carried 4 over. She has earned 30 × 8 ÷ 12 = **20 days**, so 20 + 4 − 5 = **19 days** are left. Ten days of leave at AED 9,000 ÷ 30 = AED 300 a day is **AED 3,000**. If the job ended now, 19 days at AED 6,000 ÷ 30 = AED 200 a day gives **AED 3,800**.

## FAQs

**Why 21 days in Qatar?** The law sets three weeks; the calculator counts a week as 7 days (28 days from 5 years).

**Can a contract give more?** Yes. The law sets the least; your contract or company rules may be better.

**Is anything saved?** No. Everything is worked out in your browser. These are estimates of what the law sets, not legal advice; check the official source.
