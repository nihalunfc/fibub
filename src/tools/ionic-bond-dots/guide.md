## What it does

When a metal reacts with a non-metal, the metal atoms **give** their outer electrons to the non-metal atoms. Both end up with a full outer shell, as **ions** of opposite charge that attract each other: an **ionic bond**. This page lets you do the transfer yourself, one electron at a time:

- pick a metal (lithium, sodium, potassium, magnesium, calcium, aluminium) and a non-metal (fluorine, chlorine, oxygen, sulfur, nitrogen),
- the outer shell of each atom is drawn with the metal's electrons as **crosses (×)** and the non-metal's as **dots (•)**,
- press **Move one electron** and an arrow shows where the next one goes,
- when an atom has given or taken all it needs, it is put in square brackets with its charge,
- the page explains the shells before and after, why the numbers of atoms are what they are, and gives the formula and the name.

It follows the Class 10 chapter on **metals and non-metals** (how metals and non-metals react, ionic compounds), and the Class 11 chapter on chemical bonding.

Once you have the ions, write the formulas of many more compounds with the [[formula-writer]]; the shells come from [[build-an-atom]], and which metals react most readily is shown in the [[reactivity-series]].

## How to use it

1. Choose the **Metal** and **Non-metal**. The right number of each atom is drawn: one sodium and one chlorine, but one magnesium and **two** chlorines.
2. Press **Move one electron** to move them one by one, or **Move them all**. **Start again** puts them back.
3. Read **What happened** for the shells of the atoms and ions and why the formula balances.

## A worked example

**Magnesium and chlorine.** Magnesium (Z = 12) has shells 2, 8, 2: two outer electrons. Chlorine (Z = 17) has 2, 8, 7: it needs one more to make 8. One chlorine can only take one electron, so a magnesium atom needs **two** chlorine atoms. Two electrons move, giving Mg²⁺ (2, 8) and two Cl⁻ (2, 8, 8). The formula is **MgCl₂**, magnesium chloride.

**Aluminium and oxygen** is the biggest: aluminium loses 3 and oxygen gains 2, so 2 Al and 3 O balance at 6 electrons: **Al₂O₃**.

## Tips

- Metal atoms lose all their outer electrons; non-metal atoms gain enough to reach 8.
- The total lost must equal the total gained. The formula uses the smallest numbers of atoms that make that true.
- Many ions end with the same shells as a noble gas: Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺, F⁻ and O²⁻ are all 2, 8, like neon.

## Check yourself

Four fresh questions each time: a formula (type digits normally, e.g. MgCl2), the number of electrons moved, the shells of an ion (like 2,8) and the charge on an ion. Nothing is saved.

## FAQ

**Are the electrons really crosses and dots?** No: all electrons are the same. The marks just show where each one came from.

**Why only elements up to calcium?** The simple 2, 8, 8 shell rule works for them; heavier atoms fill shells in a more complicated order (Class 11).
