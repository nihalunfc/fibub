## What it does

A traditional 401(k) or IRA saves tax now and is taxed when you take money out. A Roth is taxed now and comes out tax-free. Which leaves you more depends mostly on one thing: is your tax rate higher now or in retirement? This calculator grows your yearly contributions both ways and shows the after-tax value of each, the tax rate at which they tie, and a table for a range of retirement tax rates. It also shows your 2026 contribution limit.

Work out today's tax rate from your pay with the [[us-paycheck|US paycheck calculator]], plan the whole retirement with the [[retirement-planner]], or compare Canada's version of the same choice in [[rrsp-tfsa|RRSP or TFSA]].

## How to use it

1. Pick the **account** and your **age** (this sets your limit and catch-up).
2. Type what you **put in each year** before tax, the **years** until you retire and a **yearly return**.
3. Type your **tax rate now** (your top federal plus state rate) and your expected **tax rate in retirement**.
4. Choose how to **compare**.

## What each option does

- **Same cost to my take-home pay**: $1 in a traditional costs you the same as $1 minus tax in a Roth. This is the fair test below the limit.
- **Same dollars into the account**: for when you put in the full limit either way. The traditional's tax saving is invested in a normal account, whose returns are taxed each year at the rate you type.
- **Break-even tax rate later**: if your retirement rate is lower than this, traditional wins; higher, Roth wins.

## Worked example

Maria puts $6,000 a year into her 401(k) for 30 years at 6%. Her tax rate is 22% now and she expects 12% in retirement. The traditional pot grows to $502,810; after 12% tax she keeps **$442,473**. The Roth version costs the same take-home pay, so $4,680 goes in each year and grows to **$392,192**, tax-free. Traditional wins by $50,281. With the same 22% rate later, they tie exactly.

## FAQs

**What about my employer's match?** Matches go in pre-tax, whichever you choose, so they do not change the comparison.

**Should I split between both?** Many people do, because nobody knows future tax rates. The table shows how much is at stake either way.

**Is anything saved?** No. Nothing you type leaves the page. Not financial or tax advice; check the official source.
