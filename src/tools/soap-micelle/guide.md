## What it does

Oil and water do not mix, so water alone cannot wash oil or grease off a cloth. Soap can, because each soap molecule has two ends: an **ionic head** (–COO⁻ Na⁺) that likes water and a long **hydrocarbon tail** that likes oil. In water, soap molecules gather round a drop of oil with their tails inside it and their heads facing out. This ball is a **micelle**. The micelle stays in the water, so rinsing carries the oil away.

**Hard water** contains calcium (Ca²⁺) and magnesium (Mg²⁺) ions. These grab the heads of soap molecules and make an insoluble grey **scum**, so some of the soap is wasted before any cleaning starts. **Detergents** have a different head (often –SO₃⁻) that does not form scum, so they work in hard water too.

This page shows both:

- **Inside the wash.** Choose soft or hard water and soap or detergent, then add molecules two at a time. Watch them get caught as scum (in hard water with soap), then wrap the oil drop into a micelle, then sit at the surface as lather. **Rinse** carries the micelle away.
- **Lather test.** The classic school activity with three test tubes of distilled, tap and hard water. Add one drop of soap (or detergent) to each and shake, again and again, and read off how many drops each tube needs before lather appears.

It fits the Class 10 science chapter on carbon and its compounds (soaps and detergents) and the hard water work in Class 11. Soaps are salts of long-chain acids named in [[carbon-chain-namer]], and they are made from esters (fats and oils) as shown in [[esterification]].

## How to use it

1. Pick **Soft water** or **Hard water**, and **Soap** or **Detergent**.
2. Press **Add 2 molecules** several times and read what happens under the picture.
3. Press **Rinse** once the oil is wrapped up; **Start again** clears the water.
4. In the lather test, choose soap or detergent solution and press **Add a drop to each and shake** until all three tubes have lather.

## A worked example

Soap in soft water: after 4 molecules there are enough free ones to wrap the oil, so the micelle forms at once.

Soap in hard water with 5 Ca²⁺ ions: the first 10 molecules are all caught as scum (5 ions × 2 molecules each). Only from the 11th molecule on is any soap free, and the micelle forms at 14.

In the lather test with soap, distilled water foams after 3 drops, tap water after 7 and hard water after 15. With detergent all three foam after 3 drops.

## Tips

- Scum is the grey ring you see in a bucket and on clothes washed with soap in hard water.
- Because detergents do not make scum, they are used in washing machines and shampoos. Some detergents are slow to break down in the environment, which is a problem for rivers.
- In the model, numbers are kept small so you can count them; a real drop of soap holds billions of molecules.

## Check yourself

Three fresh questions each time: soap lost to scum, soap left free, and detergent in hard water. Nothing is saved.

## FAQ

**Is the lather test data real?** No. It is a counting model that follows the real chemistry (two soap anions per calcium ion) with small made-up numbers.
