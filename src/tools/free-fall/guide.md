## What it does

Free fall means falling with only gravity acting. Near Earth's surface every freely falling object speeds up by 9.8 m/s each second, whatever its mass. This page drops a stone and a feather side by side so you can see that for yourself, then turns air on to show why a real feather behaves so differently. It is a teaching model with round numbers.

You can read the speed under each object as it falls and the landing times below. To see the same ideas on a sideways throw, try [[projectile-launcher]], and to plot the fall as a graph, open [[motion-graphs]].

## How to use it

1. Leave "No air" selected and press Drop again.
2. Change the drop height and watch the landing time.
3. Switch to "With air" and drop again.
4. Answer the quiz.

## What each setting does

- **No air / With air**: with air, drag grows with speed. When drag equals weight, the speed stops rising. That speed is the terminal speed (50 m/s for the stone, 2 m/s for the feather here).
- **Drop height**: how far the objects fall. Higher means longer.
- **Class 11**: shows the formulas used when drag is included.

## Worked example

With no air, after 1 s: v = gt = 9.8 × 1 = 9.8 m/s and s = ½gt² = ½ × 9.8 × 1² = 4.9 m. From 20 m, t = √(2 × 20 ÷ 9.8) = 2.02 s for both objects.

## FAQs

**Why do both land together without air?** A heavier object has more weight, but also more inertia, so the acceleration is the same.

**Why does a feather fall slowly in air?** Air drag on it is large compared with its tiny weight, so it reaches terminal speed almost at once.

**Is g exactly 9.8?** It varies a little with place. 9.8 m/s² is the standard school value.

**What is terminal speed?** The steady speed reached when drag balances weight, so there is no more acceleration.
