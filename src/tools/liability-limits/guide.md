## What it does

Car liability cover pays other people for harm you cause: their injuries and the damage to their property. It has limits, and a bill above a limit is yours. Limits are often written as three numbers, such as 25/50/25: the most paid for one injured person, the most paid for all injuries in one accident, and the most paid for property, in thousands.

This tool takes your limits and an accident you describe, and shows what the policy pays, what is left for you, and a bar for each person and for property. Some policies have one combined single limit instead, and it handles that too. For other words on your policy see [[insurance-terms]]; for a small repair that may not be worth a claim see [[claim-or-pay]]; for whether a cover is worth its price see [[should-i-insure]].

## How to use it

1. Choose split limits or a combined single limit and type them. The numbers shown are examples: change them to yours.
2. Add one row per injured person with the cost of their injuries, then the property damage.
3. Optionally type your net worth to see how much of it a gap would be.

## What each setting does

- **Per person**: the most paid for any one injured person.
- **Per accident**: the most paid for all injuries together.
- **Property**: the most paid for damage to others' property.
- **Combined single limit**: one number for injuries and property together.

## Worked example

Limits 25/50/25, two injured people costing 30,000 and 15,000, and 10,000 of property damage. The first person is paid 25,000 (the per-person limit), the second 15,000, so injuries are 40,000, under the 50,000 accident limit. Property is paid 10,000. You owe the remaining 5,000.

## FAQs

**What if the policy limit is lower than the bill?** The rest can be claimed from you. This tool shows the gap; it does not give legal advice.

**Does it include my own car or injuries?** No, that is other cover.

**Are my numbers stored?** No, nothing leaves this page.

How it's worked out: each person is paid the smaller of their cost and the per-person limit, injuries are then held to the per-accident limit, property is paid up to its limit, and you owe cost minus paid. With a single limit, injuries and property share one cap.
