## What it does

India lets a passenger of Indian origin who has lived abroad for more than a year bring some jewellery in free of duty, and charges duty on the rest. This tool works out the duty-free part, the duty on the excess, your landed cost per gram, and how that compares with buying the same jewellery in an Indian shop.

Check the purity of your pieces first with [[gold-purity]], see how much over the spot price you paid with [[bullion-premium]], and turn a price in dirhams or dollars into rupees with [[currency-converter]].

## How to use it

1. Enter the weight in grams and pick the purity (24K, 22K or 18K).
2. Type the price per gram of 24K gold where you buy it, in rupees, and the shop price per gram of 24K in India.
3. Choose the passenger, the months you have lived abroad, and whether you hold an Indian passport or are of Indian origin.
4. Read the duty-free weight, the duty and the comparison.

## What each setting does

- **Purity:** the tool scales the 24K price by karat, so 22K is 22/24 of it.
- **Passenger:** a female passenger may bring 40 g duty-free, any other passenger 20 g, after more than one year abroad.
- **Time abroad:** over 12 months unlocks the allowance; 6 months or more allows the lower eligible-passenger rate on the excess.
- **Duty rates:** editable, because they change by notification.

## Worked example

A woman of Indian origin, 18 months abroad, brings 50 g of 22K jewellery. 24K costs ₹12,000 a gram abroad, so 22K is worth ₹11,000 a gram and the lot ₹5,50,000. 40 g is duty-free, leaving 10 g worth ₹1,10,000. Duty at 15% is ₹16,500, and IGST at 3% on ₹1,26,500 is ₹3,795: ₹20,295 in all. Landed cost is ₹5,70,295, or ₹11,405.90 a gram. At ₹13,500 a gram for 24K in India, the same jewellery costs ₹6,18,750, so she saves about ₹48,455. After only 3 months abroad, all 50 g pay the 38.5% baggage rate (₹2,11,750) and buying in India is cheaper.

## FAQs

**Is there still a rupee limit on the allowance?** No. The Baggage Rules, 2026 replaced the old ₹50,000 and ₹1,00,000 caps with weight limits only.

**Do coins or bars count?** No. The allowance is for jewellery; bars and coins pay duty and must be declared.

**Why might customs charge more?** Officers value gold at the notified tariff value, which can differ from your receipt.
