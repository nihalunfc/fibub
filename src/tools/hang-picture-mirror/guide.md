## What it does

Most crooked or oddly placed pictures come from guessing where the hook should go. This lesson shows you how to pick a height that looks right, how to choose between a single picture hook, two D-rings or a French cleat, and how to hang the piece level first time. The hook height calculator takes the picture's height, the drop from the top of the frame to the wire (or D-rings, or cleat) and the centre height you want, and tells you exactly how high to put the hook, plus where along the wall if you give the wall width.

It is the last lesson of the free **Wall fixings** course. Check what is behind the wall first with [[whats-in-your-wall|What's in your wall]] and choose a fixing for the weight with [[pick-wall-plug|Pick the right wall plug]]; if you are redecorating first, the [[paint-calculator]] works out how much paint the wall needs.

## How to use it

1. Read the **Stay safe** box, then follow the steps with the picture and wall in front of you. Tick each one when it is done (ticks are kept only if you tick **Remember my ticks on this device**).
2. In the **Hook height calculator**, choose how the picture hangs and your units. Type the picture height, the drop and the centre height. For two D-rings, also type their spacing. Type the wall width if you want the picture centred on the wall.
3. Mark the hook height (and side position) on masking tape on the wall.
4. Name the parts in the picture, put the steps in order and take the quiz.

## What each measurement means

- **Picture height:** the whole frame, top edge to bottom edge.
- **Drop:** how far below the top of the frame the picture rests on the hook. For wire, pull it up taut first. For D-rings, measure to the top of the ring; for a cleat, to the top of the frame's cleat.
- **Centre height:** where the middle of the picture will be, measured from the floor. 145 cm (57 in) is a good start.
- **D-ring spacing:** the distance between the two D-rings, centre to centre. Your two hooks go the same distance apart.
- **Wall width:** optional. The calculator centres the picture on the wall and gives the hook positions from the left end.

## Worked example

A frame is 60 cm tall. With the wire pulled up taut, the top of the wire is 10 cm below the top of the frame. You want its centre at 145 cm. The top of the frame will be 145 + 60 ÷ 2 = 175 cm from the floor, so the hook goes 10 cm lower, at **165 cm**. The bottom of the frame will be at 115 cm. If it hangs on two D-rings 40 cm apart instead, centred on a 300 cm wall, the two hooks go at 165 cm high, 130 cm and 170 cm from the left end of the wall.

## FAQs

**Should I measure to the bottom of the hook or the nail?** To the point where the wire or ring will rest: the lowest point of the hook's curve.

**Why does my picture tilt forward?** The wire is fixed too high on the back, or it is too loose. Move the D-rings or screw eyes down to about a third of the way from the top, or shorten the wire.

**Is a French cleat hard to make?** No. Cut a strip of wood along its length at 45°. Screw one half to the wall (bevel up, facing the wall) and the other to the frame (bevel down), so they hook together.

**What about a group of pictures?** Treat the whole group as one big picture: put the group's centre at 145 cm and space the frames 5 to 8 cm (2 to 3 in) apart.
