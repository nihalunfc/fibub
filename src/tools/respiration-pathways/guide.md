## What it does

Every living cell releases energy from food by **respiration**. It always starts the same way: in the cytoplasm, one glucose molecule (6 carbons) is split into two pyruvate molecules (3 carbons each), which gives a little energy as 2 ATP. What happens next depends on oxygen. **With oxygen** (aerobic respiration), pyruvate goes into the mitochondria and is broken down completely to carbon dioxide and water, giving much more energy: about 38 ATP per glucose in all. **Without oxygen** (anaerobic respiration), the pyruvate is changed in the cytoplasm instead: **yeast** makes ethanol and carbon dioxide (fermentation), and **your muscles**, when short of oxygen, make lactic acid. Either way only the first 2 ATP are gained. Photosynthesis makes the glucose and oxygen that respiration uses, so it is worth a look in the [[photosynthesis-lab]]; the glucose itself comes from food broken down on the [[digestion-journey]].

## Using it

1. Pick a **Level**. *Class 10* uses word equations. *Class 11* adds the chemical equations, the name glycolysis, and a mass check.
2. Pick the **cell**: yeast or your muscle. It decides what the anaerobic way makes.
3. Move the **oxygen supply**. At 100 % every pyruvate goes the aerobic way; at 0 % none does. In between, both happen, and the arrows' widths show the share.
4. Set the **glucose** in moles to see the products in moles and grams in the table.

## Worked example

Take **1 mol of glucose** (180.16 g) in yeast with plenty of oxygen. It uses 6 mol of oxygen (192 g) and gives 6 mol of carbon dioxide (264.06 g) and 6 mol of water (108.12 g): 372.16 g in and 372.18 g out, equal to rounding. Each glucose gives **38 ATP**. Now slide the oxygen to 0 %: the yeast ferments, making 2 mol of ethanol (92.14 g) and 2 mol of carbon dioxide (88.02 g), and only **2 ATP** per glucose. To make 76 ATP, the aerobic way needs 2 glucose molecules, the anaerobic way 38. That is why yeast without oxygen gets through sugar so fast.

## Where this fits in school

Class 10 science (life processes) covers respiration, the breakdown of glucose by different pathways, and lactic acid in muscles and fermentation in yeast. Class 11 biology (respiration in plants) adds glycolysis, fermentation and aerobic respiration in detail with the ATP count. This page is an independent learning aid. It is not made by or connected with NCERT, and it does not copy any textbook.

## Questions

**Is it 38 ATP or 30?** School books count 38 (36 in some). Careful measurements in real cells give about 30 to 32, because some energy is used moving molecules across the mitochondrial membranes. The idea is the same: aerobic gives many times more energy.

**Do plants respire?** Yes, all the time, in every living cell, day and night.

**Is anything saved?** No. Nothing you choose is stored or sent anywhere.
