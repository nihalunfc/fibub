## What it does

A one-hour meeting with six people is six hours of work, and those hours have a price. This tool shows that price from the number of people, their average pay and the length, and it can run a live meter while the meeting is on. If the meeting repeats, it also shows what the series costs over a year.

It is not a reason to cancel every meeting. It helps you ask whether a regular meeting is worth the time, or whether fewer people or a shorter slot would do. To see where an hourly figure comes from, use the [[hourly-salary|salary converter]]; to price your own time, see the [[freelance-rate]] calculator.

## How to use it

1. Type the number of people and the length in minutes.
2. Choose whether you know their average hourly pay or yearly salary, and enter it.
3. Add an extra percentage if you want to count benefits and space (many teams use 20 to 30).
4. Choose how often it repeats and read the yearly cost.
5. During the meeting, press Start on the live meter.

## What each setting does

- **Extra on top of pay:** the real cost of an employee is more than the salary. 0 uses pay only.
- **Working hours a year:** turns a yearly salary into an hourly figure. 2,080 means 40 hours for 52 weeks.
- **How often it repeats:** every day counts 5 a week; every month counts about one in four weeks.
- **Weeks held a year:** use less than 52 for holidays and weeks off.

## Worked example

Six people on 50 an hour in a one-hour meeting cost 6 x 50 = 300. If the meeting is held every week for 48 weeks, the year costs 300 x 48 = 14,400, which is 288 person-hours.

## FAQs

**Is this money really spent?** No. Salaries are paid anyway. The figure shows what the time is worth, so you can decide if the meeting is a good use of it.

**What if people earn different amounts?** Use the average. For a mix of very different pay levels, run the tool once per group and add the results.

**Does the meter keep running if I close the page?** No. The meter and all your entries stay on this page only and nothing is saved.
