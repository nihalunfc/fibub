## What it does

An **alloy** is a mixture of a metal with other metals, or with a non-metal such as carbon. Mixing changes the properties: pure iron is soft and rusts, but iron with a little carbon (steel) is strong, and iron with chromium and nickel (stainless steel) does not rust. Pure gold is too soft for jewellery, so it is mixed with copper or silver.

On this page you type the percentage of each metal, or start from a known alloy, and the page tells you:

- **which alloy** your mix is (brass, bronze, tin–lead solder, lead-free solder, carbon steel, cast iron, stainless steel, nichrome, duralumin, magnalium, 22- or 18-carat gold), using typical school composition ranges,
- **what it is made of, why it is used and what for**,
- the **melting point** of the main pure metal for comparison,
- the **grams of each metal** in a piece of any mass,
- for gold, its **purity in carats** (24 carat is pure gold).

A **lattice picture** shows why alloys are harder: push the top rows of atoms in a pure metal and they slide; in an alloy, atoms of a different size jam the rows.

It fits the Class 10 science chapter on metals and non-metals (alloys, corrosion) and Class 8 work on metals. ## How to use it

1. Pick a starting alloy, or **Clear all** and type your own percentages. The total must be 100%.
2. Read **What have you made?** If the mix is not a known alloy, change the percentages.
3. Type the **mass of the piece** to get the grams of each metal.
4. In **Why alloys are harder**, switch between pure metal and alloy and press **Push the top rows**.

## A worked example

Start from **brass**: 70% copper and 30% zinc. The page names it brass, harder than copper, does not rust, used for taps and instruments. A 250 g brass tap holds 175 g of copper and 75 g of zinc.

Now change it to 88% copper and 12% tin: the page calls it **bronze**. Set 75% gold, 15% copper and 10% silver: **18-carat gold**, because 24 × 75/100 = 18.

## Tips

- Steel is mostly iron: even 1% carbon changes it a lot.
- Stainless steel needs at least about 10.5% chromium to stop rusting.
- Solder melts lower than either of its metals, which is why it is good for joining wires without melting them.
