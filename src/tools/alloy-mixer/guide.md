## What it does

An **alloy** is a metal mixed with other elements, melted together so the atoms mix. Most metal things around you are alloys, because pure metals are often too soft, rust too easily or melt too high. This page lets you mix your own:

- choose one of ten alloys: **brass**, **bronze**, old and lead-free **solder**, **steel**, **stainless steel**, **duralumin**, **magnalium**, **22 carat gold** and dental **amalgam**,
- read the usual recipe, what it is used for and why,
- change the recipe with a slider for each element; numbers that don't add to 100 % are scaled,
- set a **batch mass** to get the grams of each metal, the **density** by the mixing rule, the volume, and for gold the **carat**,
- **push the top layers** of atoms in a picture to see why a pure metal bends easily but an alloy resists,
- turn **carats** into per cent gold and grams of gold in a piece of jewellery.

It follows the Class 10 chapter on **metals and non-metals** (alloys) and the Class 8 topic of materials.

How readily each metal reacts, and so how it is extracted, is in the [[reactivity-series]]; look up each element in the [[periodic-table]], and see a thin layer of one metal put on another in the [[electroplating-calculator]].

## How to use it

1. Choose the **Alloy** and type the **Batch mass** in grams.
2. Move the sliders (or type per cent by mass). **Back to the usual recipe** resets them.
3. Read the masses, the density working and, for gold, the carat.
4. In **Why alloys are harder**, choose **Pure metal** or **Alloy** and press **Push the top layers**.
5. In **Gold carats**, type a carat number and the mass of the jewellery.

## A worked example

**Brass**, 65 % copper and 35 % zinc, batch 500 g:

- copper 0.65 × 500 = 325 g, zinc 0.35 × 500 = **175 g**,
- density by the mixing rule: 1 ÷ (0.65 ÷ 8.96 + 0.35 ÷ 7.13) = **8.22 g/cm³**, between copper (8.96) and zinc (7.13),
- the 500 g batch takes up about 60.8 cm³.

**Gold**: 22 carat means 22 parts gold in 24, which is 91.67 % (often stamped 917). A 10 g chain of it holds 9.167 g of pure gold.

## Tips

- An alloy's density is always between the densities of its metals (by this rule).
- Steel is iron with only a little carbon, yet much harder: the small carbon atoms sit in the gaps between iron atoms.
- Solder melts at a lower temperature than either tin or lead alone, which is why it is good for joining wires.
