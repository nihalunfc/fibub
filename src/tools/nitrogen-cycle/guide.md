## What it does

Every protein and every strand of DNA in your body contains nitrogen. The air is nearly four-fifths nitrogen gas (N₂), but plants and animals cannot use N₂ directly. Nitrogen reaches them, and returns to the air, through a cycle of processes, most of them carried out by bacteria.

This page draws the cycle as six stores, the **air**, **plants**, **animals**, **dead matter and wastes**, **soil ammonium** and **soil nitrate**, joined by eleven numbered arrows. There are two games:

- **Follow an atom**: your nitrogen atom (the red N) starts in one store, and you are given a goal (a green dashed box), or asked to take it all the way round and back. Press the process that moves it. If you pick one that does not start where the atom is, the page says where that process does start and which ones you can use. At the end it tells you whether you used the fewest steps.
- **Name the arrow**: one arrow lights up; choose its name from four.

The **Class 8–9** level describes each process in plain words; the **Class 11** level adds the bacteria (Rhizobium, Nitrosomonas, Nitrobacter, Pseudomonas), the enzyme nitrogenase and the chemical forms NH₄⁺, NO₂⁻ and NO₃⁻. It fits the Class 9 Science chapter on the Earth as a system of energy, matter and life, the Class 8 chapters on microorganisms and crop production, and nitrogen metabolism in Class 11 Biology.

Carbon moves round a similar loop in the [[carbon-cycle]]; nitrogen passes up the food chains in the [[food-web-builder]], and plants build their proteins with energy captured in the [[photosynthesis-lab]].

## How to use it

1. Read the challenge above the picture.
2. Press a process button. The atom moves along the arrow, which lights up, and the step is added to your path.
3. **New challenge** gives a fresh start and goal.
4. Switch to **Name the arrow** for a naming quiz; your score for this visit is shown with each answer.
5. The list under the picture explains every process at the level you chose.

## A worked example

The first challenge is: atom in the **air**, goal **animals**. Press *Lightning* and the atom drops into soil nitrate; press *Uptake of nitrate* and it is in a plant; press *Feeding* and it reaches an animal. That took 3 steps, which the page confirms is the fewest. Another 3-step route is fixation, uptake of ammonium, feeding.

Going back takes longer: from an animal to the air needs 4 steps (death and excretion, decay, nitrification, denitrification), because only denitrification returns nitrogen to the air.

## Tips

- Only two kinds of arrow leave the air in nature: fixation by bacteria and lightning. People add a third, the fertiliser factory (dashed arrow).
- Only one arrow returns nitrogen to the air: denitrification, which happens where soil has little oxygen.
- Legumes (peas, beans, gram) carry nitrogen-fixing bacteria in their root nodules, which is why farmers grow them to enrich the soil.

## Check yourself

Four fresh questions: three ask you to choose the process that fits a description (type the number of your choice), and one asks the fewest processes between two stores. Nothing is saved.

## FAQ

**Is this the whole nitrogen cycle?** No: it is a teaching model. The real cycle also has ammonia escaping from soil and dung to the air, nitrate washing into rivers, and nitrogen locked in rocks.

**Why can't we breathe in nitrogen and use it?** The two atoms in N₂ are held by a very strong triple bond. Only the enzyme nitrogenase, lightning, or a factory at high temperature and pressure can break it.

**Is this from the NCERT book?** It covers the same topic with fibub's own words and pictures. fibub is not part of NCERT.
