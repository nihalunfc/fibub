## What it does

Most new freelancers pick a rate by copying a salary, and then find they earn less than they hoped. A salary pays you for holidays, sick days, admin and slow weeks. A freelance rate has to cover all of that, plus tax and your own costs, using only the hours you can actually invoice.

Enter the take-home pay you want in a year, your business costs, the share you set aside for tax, the weeks you will not work and the billable hours you expect in a week. The tool gives an hourly and day rate. To compare it with an employee's pay use [[hourly-salary]], and once you have a rate, send the bill with the [[invoice-maker]].

## How to use it

1. Enter the **take-home pay** you want in a year, after tax.
2. Add yearly **business costs**: software, insurance, equipment, a co-working desk.
3. Enter the **tax and savings** share of your income to set aside. Ask your tax authority or accountant if you are unsure.
4. Enter **weeks off** and your **billable hours a week**. Be honest: most freelancers bill 20 to 30 hours in a 40-hour week.
5. Read the rates, then check the table to see what more or fewer hours would mean.

## What each setting does

- **Tax and savings:** the percentage of your billing you keep aside, so the take-home is what is left.
- **Weeks off:** holidays, sick time and quiet weeks. They are not billed.
- **Billable hours:** only hours you charge for. Admin and looking for work do not count.
- **Days per week:** turns the hourly rate into a day rate.

## Worked example

You want 60,000 a year to take home, with 6,000 of business costs and 20% set aside for tax. You need to bill 60,000 / 0.8 + 6,000 = 81,000. With 4 weeks off you work 48 weeks, and at 30 billable hours that is 1,440 hours. The hourly rate is 81,000 / 1,440 = 56.25, and a 6-hour billable day is 337.50. Percentages are easy to check with the [[percentage-calculator]], and the [[budget-calculator]] helps you decide what take-home you need.

## FAQs

**Why is my rate so much higher than a salary equivalent?** You pay for your own holidays, tools, tax and unbillable time.

**Which tax rate should I use?** Your best estimate of income tax plus social contributions on your profit. Check the official source for your country. Not financial or tax advice.

**Should I add a margin?** Many people add 10 to 20% for slow months and late payers.
