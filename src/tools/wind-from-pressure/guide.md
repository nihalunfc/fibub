## What it does

Wind is air moving because of differences in **pressure**. This page shows land on the left and sea on the right, with dots for parcels of air. Warm one side and the air above it expands, gets lighter and **rises**, leaving **low pressure** (L). Over the cooler side the air sinks: **high pressure** (H). Air is pushed from high to low, so wind blows along the ground from the cool side to the warm side, and high up the air drifts back in a loop.

Four scenes show it: a **sea breeze** by day, a **land breeze** at night, and the **summer** and **winter monsoons** over India.

It follows Class 8 Science, Pressure, Winds, Storms and Cyclones, with more for Class 11 Geography. Before this, [[standard-atmosphere]] shows how air pressure falls with height; next, [[cyclone-builder]] shows how the Earth's spin twists wind into a storm.

## How to use it

1. Choose a **Scene**, or set the **land** and **sea** temperatures with the sliders. Tap the land or sea to warm it by 2 °C.
2. Watch the dots: orange is warm air, blue is cooled air.
3. Read which side is warmer, where air rises, where pressure is low and which way the wind blows.
4. Switch **Level** to **Class 11** for air density and the pressure 1 km up.
5. **Pause** stops the motion.

## Worked example

On the **sunny afternoon** the land is 34 °C and the sea 27 °C, so the page shows a **sea breeze**: L over the land, H over the sea. At Class 11 the air over the land has density 101,325 ÷ (287.05 × 307.15) = **1.1492 kg/m³**, over the sea **1.1760 kg/m³**, so land air is 2.33% lighter. Switch to the **clear night**: the land is now cooler than the sea and the loop turns round, a land breeze.

## Tips

- Land heats and cools faster than water, so breezes change direction between day and night.
- A bigger temperature difference gives a stronger wind.
- The monsoon is a sea breeze lasting a season.

## FAQ

**Is this a real weather model?** No. It is one simple loop to show the idea.

**Why low pressure over warm ground?** Warm air rises and spreads out high up, so less air presses down below.

**Is anything stored?** No.
