## What it does

Wind is air on the move, and it moves because of differences in **pressure**. This page shows land on the left and sea on the right, with dots for parcels of air. When one side is warmer, it heats the air above it. Warm air expands, becomes lighter and **rises**, so less air presses down there: **low pressure** (L). Over the cooler side the air is heavier and sinks: **high pressure** (H). Air is pushed from high to low pressure, so a wind blows along the ground from the cool side to the warm side, while high up the air drifts back. The dots go round and round in a loop.

Four scenes show the same idea at different sizes: a **sea breeze** on a sunny afternoon, a **land breeze** on a clear night, and the **summer** and **winter monsoons** over India, where the land and the Indian Ocean take turns being the warmer side for months at a time.

It follows the school topics on air and wind: Class 8 Science, pressure, winds, storms and cyclones (air moves from high to low pressure, uneven heating of land and water, sea and land breezes, monsoon winds), with more detail for Class 11 Geography, atmospheric circulation (pressure differences, why warm air rises, the monsoon). ## How to use it

1. Choose a **Scene**, or set the **land** and **sea** temperatures yourself with the sliders. Tap the land or the sea in the picture to warm it by 2 °C.
2. Watch the dots. Orange dots are warm air that has touched the warm ground; blue dots have been cooled over the cooler side or high up.
3. Read what the air does: which side is warmer, where it rises, where the pressure is low, and which way the wind blows, with a word for how strong it is.
4. Switch the **Level** to **Class 11** to see the density of the air over each side and the pressure 1 km up, worked out with the numbers.
5. **Pause** stops the motion.

## Worked example

On the **sunny afternoon** the land is at 34 °C and the sea at 27 °C. The land is 7 °C warmer, so the page shows **a moderate wind from the sea to the land (sea breeze)**, with L over the land and H over the sea.

At Class 11 the air over the land has density 101,325 ÷ (287.05 × 307.15) = **1.1492 kg/m³**, and over the sea **1.1760 kg/m³**: the land air is **2.33% lighter**. One kilometre up, the pressure is **90,656 Pa** over the land and **90,421 Pa** over the sea, 235 Pa apart, so up there the air flows from the land toward the sea. That leaves less air above the land, the ground pressure there falls, and the ground wind blows in from the sea.

Switch to the **clear night**: the land (21 °C) is now cooler than the sea (26 °C) and the loop turns round, giving a land breeze.

## Tips

- Land warms up and cools down much faster than water. That is why the breeze changes direction between day and night.
- The bigger the temperature difference, the bigger the pressure difference and the stronger the wind.
- The monsoon is like a sea breeze that lasts a whole season and stretches across a whole subcontinent.

## FAQ

**Is the moving air a real weather model?** No. It is one simple loop of air whose speed grows with the temperature difference, to show the idea. Real winds also depend on hills, the Earth's spin and the weather systems passing by.

**Why does the warm side get low pressure if warm air expands?** The warm air rises and spreads out sideways high up. That takes air away from above the warm ground, so less air presses down on it.
