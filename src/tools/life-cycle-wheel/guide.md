## What it does

Plants live a double life. One generation, the **sporophyte**, is diploid (2n: two sets of chromosomes) and makes spores by **meiosis**. The spores grow into the other generation, the **gametophyte**, which is haploid (n) and makes gametes. Two gametes join at **fertilisation** to make a diploid zygote, which grows into the next sporophyte. This is **alternation of generations**.

What changes from algae to flowering plants is how big and independent each generation is. This page draws each group's life cycle as a wheel: blue segments are haploid, orange are diploid, and markers show where meiosis (M) and fertilisation (F) happen. Step round with **Next stage**, or tap a segment, to read what each stage is and why it has that ploidy.

- **Green alga** (Chlamydomonas, Spirogyra): **haplontic**. Only the zygote is diploid.
- **Bryophyte** (moss): **haplo-diplontic**, and the green plant is the gametophyte; the sporophyte lives on it.
- **Pteridophyte** (fern): haplo-diplontic, but the big plant is the sporophyte; the tiny prothallus lives on its own.
- **Gymnosperm** (pine) and **angiosperm** (flowering plant): the sporophyte is everything you see; the gametophytes shrink to a few cells in the pollen and the ovule. School books call this pattern **diplontic**.
- **Human**: truly diplontic: meiosis makes the gametes directly.

It fits the Class 11 biology chapter on the plant kingdom (algae to angiosperms, and plant life cycles), and links to sexual reproduction in flowering plants in Class 12. Revise meiosis with [[cell-division]] first; the flower that makes the pollen and the ovule is in the [[floral-formula]] maker.

## Worked example

The page opens on the **bryophyte** (moss). Stage 1 is the leafy gametophyte, haploid (n): "The green moss plant you see is the haploid gametophyte." Step on: sperm and egg (n), then the F marker: fertilisation, n + n → 2n. Stage 3, the zygote, and stage 4, the sporophyte (foot, seta, capsule), are orange (2n), and the note says the sporophyte depends on the gametophyte for food. After the M marker, meiosis in the capsule gives haploid spores (stage 5), which grow into the protonema (stage 6) and a new leafy plant. The facts say: haplo-diplontic, main body gametophyte (n), water needed, wheel share n 74 %, 2n 26 %.

Switch to **Angiosperm**: the wheel is now mostly orange (2n 86 %), the gametophytes are a pollen grain and an embryo sac, and the note under the wheel adds that endosperm is triploid (3n).

## FAQ

**Why are seed plants called diplontic when they still have gametophytes?** Their gametophytes are reduced to a few cells inside the sporophyte's tissues and never live on their own, so the life cycle looks like the diplontic one of animals. Strictly, they still alternate generations, which is why the wheel shows a thin haploid stage.

**Do the wheel shares mean time?** No. They are rough, to show which generation dominates. A fern prothallus may live for weeks; the fern for years.
