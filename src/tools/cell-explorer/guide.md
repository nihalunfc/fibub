## What it does

Every living thing is made of cells, and each cell is full of smaller parts called **organelles**, each with its own job. This explorer draws three cells you meet at school:

- a **plant cell**, with its cell wall, large vacuole and green chloroplasts;
- an **animal cell**, with no wall, small vacuoles and lysosomes;
- a **bacterium**, a much smaller cell with no nucleus: its DNA lies loose in the cytoplasm.

Tap any part to see its name, what it does and which cells have it. **Zoom in on it** brings that part up close. The **compare table** puts the three cells side by side, and **How much bigger?** shows how much the drawing is magnified on your own screen. It fits **Class 9 Science: the fundamental unit of life** (cell membrane, cell wall, nucleus, cytoplasm, organelles, prokaryotes and eukaryotes) and **Class 11 Biology: cell, the unit of life**, which adds the nucleolus, centrioles, plasmodesmata and the parts of a bacterium. ## How to use it

1. **Pick the level.** *Class 9* shows the main parts with a one-line job each. *Class 11* adds more parts and a longer explanation of each.
2. **Pick a cell**: plant, animal or bacterium.
3. **Tap a part.** It lights up and the box below tells you about it. On a computer you can also move with the Tab key and press Enter.
4. **Zoom in on it** to look closer; press again to zoom out. The scale bar changes as you zoom.
5. **Compare the cells.** Tap a row to jump to that part in the drawing. Download the table as a CSV file, or the drawing and table as an image, PDF or Word file.
6. **How much bigger?** Shows the magnification of the drawing on your screen and works out the total magnification of a light microscope from its two lenses.
7. **Check yourself**: fresh questions each time, with the working shown.

## Worked example

A typical plant cell in this explorer is 50 µm (0.05 mm) across. If the drawing on your screen is 90 mm wide, the magnification is 90 mm ÷ 0.05 mm = **×1800**. Under a light microscope with a ×10 eyepiece and a ×40 objective, the total magnification is 10 × 40 = **×400**, so the same cell looks 0.05 × 400 = **20 mm** across. A bacterium only 2 µm long would look 0.8 mm across at ×400, which is why bacteria need about ×1000 to be seen clearly.

## Plant, animal and bacterial cells in one line each

- **Plant cells** have a cellulose wall, one large vacuole and chloroplasts, as well as everything an animal cell has (except, usually, centrioles and lysosomes).
- **Animal cells** have no wall, so their shape is more rounded or irregular, and they burst in plain water.
- **Bacteria** are **prokaryotes**: no nucleus and no membrane-bound organelles such as mitochondria; smaller 70S ribosomes; often a flagellum and plasmids.

## FAQ

**Why are the parts so spread out?** To make them easy to tap. Real cells are packed with organelles, and the ER and ribosomes are everywhere.

**Do plant cells have mitochondria?** Yes. Plants respire all the time, day and night, so they need mitochondria as well as chloroplasts.

**Why do the sizes say "about"?** Cells vary a lot: some plant cells are 10 times bigger than others. The sizes here are round values for the sums.
