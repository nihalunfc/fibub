## What it does

The animal kingdom has millions of species, but a few questions about body plan sort them into a handful of large groups called **phyla**. This page turns those questions into a branching key, drawn as a tree:

1. **Level of organisation:** are the cells only loosely together (sponges), or do they form tissues and organs?
2. **Symmetry:** radial (like a wheel) or bilateral (left and right halves)?
3. **Germ layers:** two (diploblastic) or three (triploblastic)?
4. **Body cavity:** none, a false cavity (pseudocoelom) or a true coelom?
5. **Notochord:** a stiff rod along the back, at least in the embryo?
6. **Jointed legs** with a hard exoskeleton, and **ring-like segments**.

The answers lead to 11 phyla: Porifera, Cnidaria, Ctenophora, Platyhelminthes, Aschelminthes, Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Hemichordata and Chordata. Each ends with a one-line summary of its main features.

It fits the Class 11 biology chapter on the animal kingdom, and the Class 9 chapter on diversity. Start one level up with the [[five-kingdoms]] sorter; to write a key of your own for any group, use the [[dichotomous-key-builder]], and follow single species from kingdom to species on the [[taxonomy-ladder]].

## How to use it

1. **Answer the questions yourself.** Each card gives the question, a short explanation of the words in it, and the possible answers. Tap one; the next question appears and the tree lights up your path. **Back one step** undoes the last answer, **Start again** clears them all.
2. **Pick an animal** from the list (20 examples, from a bath sponge to a human). The key walks it all the way, lists each answer in words and names the phylum.
3. In the tree, numbered circles are the questions (Q1 to Q8), the orange one is the question you are on, and the blue line is your path. The phyla sit on the right.

## Worked example

Pick **Starfish**. The path reads:

1. How are the cells organised? Tissues or organs.
2. What symmetry does the adult body have? Radial.
3. How many germ layers form the body? Three (triploblastic).

The title says **Phylum Echinodermata**, and the summary explains: spiny skin, radial adults with bilateral larvae, a water vascular system with tube feet, all marine. The jellyfish shares the first two answers but has only two germ layers, which sends it to Cnidaria instead.

Now pick **Earthworm**: tissues, bilateral, true coelom, no notochord, no jointed legs, ring segments: **Annelida**. The roundworm Ascaris stops earlier, at the false body cavity: **Aschelminthes**.

## Tips

- Symmetry is about the adult. Starfish larvae are bilateral, which is one clue that echinoderms are closer to us than to jellyfish.
- "Worm" is not a phylum: flatworms, roundworms, segmented worms and acorn worms sit in four different phyla.
- Chordates (and hemichordates) are often taught as a separate group of the key; this page keeps them in the same tree so you can see where they branch off.

## FAQ

**Why is the order of questions this one?** The early questions separate the biggest, oldest splits in animal body plans; later ones split closer relatives. Other keys use a different order and reach the same phyla.

**Where are insects, crabs and spiders?** All in Arthropoda, the largest phylum. Snails, octopuses and mussels are in Mollusca.
