## What it does

Before decimal money, British coins counted in pounds, shillings and pence (£sd) and Indian coins in rupees, annas, pice and pies. Old price tags, bills, coin catalogues and family papers still use them. This tool adds up an old amount and shows it in decimal money, and it works the other way too.

The British side takes pounds, shillings, pence and farthings and gives the total in old pence, decimal pounds and new pence. The Indian side does the same for rupees, annas, pice and pies and gives decimal rupees and naye paise. A table lists the old British coins in old and new pence. Look up the year on a coin with [[coin-dates]], and see what its metal is worth with [[melt-value]].

## How to use it

1. Pick British or Indian money.
2. Type the amounts you have. Leave a box at 0 if there is none.
3. Read the total and its decimal value.
4. To go back, type old pence (British) or decimal rupees (Indian) in the "Or start from" box.

## What each setting does

- **Farthings**: each is a quarter of an old penny, so 2 farthings is a halfpenny.
- **Old pence**: the single unit that everything is added up in. 240 of them make a pound.
- **Pies, pice, annas**: 3 pies make a pice, 4 pice an anna, 16 annas a rupee.

## Worked example

£2 2s 6d is 2 × 240 + 2 × 12 + 6 = 510 old pence. That is £2.125 in decimal, or 212.5 new pence. For India, 1 rupee 8 annas is 192 + 96 = 288 pies, which is 1.5 rupees or 150 naye paise.

## FAQs

**What would this buy today?** That needs an inflation figure for the year and place. This tool converts units only.

**Is a guinea 21 shillings?** Yes, 252 old pence. It stopped being a coin long ago but prices were still quoted in guineas.

**Why do some Indian coins show a different split?** Older coins before the anna system used other divisions. This tool covers the rupee, anna, pice and pie as above.

**Is anything stored?** No. The sums run in your browser.
