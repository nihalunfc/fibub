## What it does

A flat (plane) mirror follows one simple rule, the **law of reflection**: the angle at which light hits it (the **angle of incidence**, *i*) equals the angle at which it leaves (the **angle of reflection**, *r*). Both are measured from the **normal**, a line at right angles to the mirror, and the incident ray, the normal and the reflected ray all lie in one flat plane.

This lab has three views:

- **Reflection**: drag a laser round a mirror and read *i* and *r* off the protractor arcs. The laser's **image** sits straight behind the mirror, as far behind as the laser is in front, and the reflected ray seems to come from it.
- **Two mirrors**: set two mirrors at any angle θ, like the inside of a **kaleidoscope**, and see every image of an F-shaped object. The count follows **360 ÷ θ − 1** when 360 ÷ θ is even; the page also shows the odd and in-between cases by tracing each reflection.
- **Periscope**: two parallel mirrors in a tube. Tilt them and see that the light only gets through near 45°.

It follows Class 6 to 8 Science on light (reflection, the image in a plane mirror, lateral inversion, the kaleidoscope and periscope) and the start of Class 10 work on reflection of light. Next, curved mirrors and where their images form are in the [[mirror-ray-diagram]]; light bending as it enters glass is in [[snell-law]]; and lenses are in the [[lens-ray-diagram]].

## How to use it

1. Pick **Reflection**, **Two mirrors** or **Periscope** at the top.
2. **Reflection**: drag the laser dot anywhere above the mirror (left or right of the normal), or set the **angle of incidence** *i* from 0° to 80° with the slider or box.
3. **Two mirrors**: set the angle **θ** between them (30° to 180°, or tap a preset) and **pos**, where the object sits across the gap (50% is on the middle line). Faded copies are the images.
4. **Periscope**: set the **tilt** of both mirrors from 30° to 60° and watch the light's path.

## Worked example

The page opens with the laser at *i* = 35°. The reflected ray leaves at *r* = 35° on the other side of the normal. Both rays make 90° − 35° = 55° with the mirror surface. The laser is 12.3 cm in front of the mirror on the drawing, and its image is 12.3 cm behind it.

Open **Two mirrors**: at θ = 60°, 360 ÷ 60 = 6 is even, so you see 6 − 1 = **5 images**, arranged round a circle like a kaleidoscope pattern. Try **72°**: 360 ÷ 72 = 5 is odd, so you see **5 images** with the object off the middle line, but **4** when it sits exactly on it (pos = 50), because two of the images then land in the same place.

In the **Periscope**, at 45° the top mirror turns the light through 90° straight down the tube, the bottom mirror turns it back level, and you see the object the right way up. At 40° the light goes down 10° off straight and hits the tube wall.

## Check yourself

Five fresh questions: the angle of reflection, the angle of incidence from the angle with the surface, images between two mirrors, the distance to your own image, and the angle that gives a number of images. Nothing is saved.

## FAQ

**Why does my image swap left and right?** A plane mirror reverses front and back, which makes a right hand look like a left hand: **lateral inversion**. Two reflections (as in a periscope, or the even-numbered images in the two-mirror view) undo it.

**Why can a kaleidoscope show so many images?** Its mirrors are usually at 60°, and the images of images fill the circle. Three mirrors in a triangle repeat the pattern forever.
