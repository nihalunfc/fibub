## What it does

Sleep comes in cycles of light, deep and dream sleep that last about 90 minutes each. Waking at the end of a cycle, when sleep is light, often feels easier than an alarm in the middle of deep sleep. This calculator counts back from your wake-up time, or forward from your bedtime, in whole cycles, and adds the time it takes to fall asleep. A table shows how many hours of sleep experts recommend for each age.

It is a planning aid, not medical advice. If you are planning around a pregnancy or your cycle, see the [[due-date-calculator]] and the [[ovulation-calendar]].

## How to use it

1. Pick a tab: **I wake up at**, **I go to bed at**, or **Going to bed now**.
2. Set the time. The results update straight away.
3. Choose one of the three times. The highlighted ones (5 or 6 cycles) suit most adults.

## What each setting does

- **Wake-up time / Bedtime**: the time you know. Hidden in "Going to bed now", which uses your device's clock.
- **Minutes to fall asleep**: added before the first cycle. 15 minutes is typical; change it if you fall asleep faster or slower.
- **Cycle length**: 90 minutes by default. Real cycles run from about 70 to 120 minutes, so adjust it if a time works better for you.
- **Clock**: shows times in 24-hour or 12-hour (AM/PM) style.
- **Reset**: back to the example.

## Worked example

You need to wake at 07:00. Six cycles are 9 hours, plus 15 minutes to fall asleep, so go to bed at 21:45. Five cycles (7 h 30 min) give 23:15, and four cycles (6 h) give 00:45. In 12-hour style the first one reads 9:45 PM.

## FAQs

**How much sleep do adults need?** At least 7 hours a night, by the American Academy of Sleep Medicine. The National Sleep Foundation says 7 to 9 for adults and 7 to 8 from age 65.

**Is the 90-minute rule exact?** No. It is an average. Use the times as a starting point and change the cycle length to suit you.

**Do naps count for children?** Yes. The ranges for children under 6 include naps.
