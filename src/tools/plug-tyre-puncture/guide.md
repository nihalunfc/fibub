## What it does

This lesson shows you how to plug a small puncture in a **tubeless** car tyre with a string plug kit, the kind sold in car shops for a few pounds or dollars. A nail or screw in the tread is the most common puncture, and a plug can get you home or to a tyre shop without fitting the spare. Each of the eight steps has a drawing and a **Done** tick. Before you start, the **Can this be plugged?** checker asks where the hole is, how big it is and what happened, and tells you whether a plug is sensible. The cut-through tyre picture shows which parts can be repaired and which cannot, then a put-in-order activity and a quiz check that it has stuck.

Be honest with yourself about what a plug is. Many tyre makers, and the UK repair standard BS AU 159, treat a string plug pushed in from outside as a **temporary** fix. A proper repair is a plug-patch fitted from inside, by a tyre shop, after they have taken the tyre off the rim and checked the inside for hidden damage.

It is the second lesson of the free **Tyres and punctures** course. If the hole cannot be plugged, fit the spare with [[change-flat-tyre|the change a flat tyre lesson]], then set the pressure with [[tyre-pressure]].

## How to use it

1. Read **Stay safe**. Work away from traffic, with the car on firm, flat ground.
2. Use the **Can this be plugged?** checker. If it says no, stop and fit the spare.
3. Follow the steps and tick each one. Ticks are kept only if you tick **Remember my ticks on this device**.
4. Tap the parts of the cut-through tyre, then press **Test me**. Put the steps in order and take the quiz.

## What each part means

- **Middle of the tread:** the repair zone. A round hole up to about 6 mm (1/4 in) can be plugged here.
- **Shoulder:** the curved edge where tread meets side. It flexes too much: no repairs.
- **Sidewall:** the side of the tyre. A hole, cut or bulge here means a new tyre.
- **Bead:** the stiff edge that seals on the rim. Damage here means a new tyre.
- **Inner liner:** the airtight skin inside, where a tyre shop fits a plug-patch.
- **Reamer and needle:** the reamer cleans and roughens the hole; the needle carries the plug in.

## Worked example

You find a screw in the middle of the front-right tread. The checker says: middle of the tread, about 4 mm, not driven flat, not a run-flat, so yes. You pull the screw out at the angle it went in, ream the hole six times, thread a plug halfway through the needle and push it in until about a third is still showing. You pull the needle out in one quick move, trim the ends, and pump the tyre to the 2.3 bar (33 psi) on the door label. Soapy water shows no bubbles. Next morning the pressure is still 2.3 bar, and you book a tyre shop to check it and fit a plug-patch.

## FAQs

**Is it legal to drive on a plugged tyre?** Rules differ by country. In the UK, repairs to car tyres are expected to meet BS AU 159, which an outside string plug alone does not. Treat it as a way to get to a tyre shop and check your local rules.

**Can I plug a tyre with an inner tube?** No. Plug kits are for tubeless tyres only. A tube needs a patch (see the bike puncture lesson for how patching works).

**How fast can I drive on a plug?** Gently and at normal speeds until it is checked. Avoid long, fast motorway runs.

**Can a tyre have more than one repair?** Sometimes, if the holes are far enough apart. A tyre shop decides; the rules set a minimum gap.
