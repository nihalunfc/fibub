## What it does

Everything is made of particles, and the kind of particles decides what sort of matter it is. This page deals you everyday things such as air, table salt, copper wire, rust or brass, and three bins: **Element**, **Compound** and **Mixture**. Drag each thing into its bin, or tap it and then tap a bin. Tap a thing to see a **particle picture**: coloured balls for atoms, joined into molecules where they are joined.

- An **element** has one kind of particle, made of one kind of atom (oxygen gas is O₂, still only oxygen).
- A **compound** has one kind of particle with two or more kinds of atom joined in a fixed ratio (water is always H₂O).
- A **mixture** has two or more kinds of particle mixed but not joined (air).

It follows Class 8 Science, Nature of Matter. Before this, [[states-of-matter]] shows how particles move; after it, [[build-an-atom]] and [[formula-writer]] go inside the atom and the formula.

## How to use it

1. Choose a **Level**. Class 8 has three bins. Class 9 splits Mixture into homogeneous (same all through) and heterogeneous (not the same all through).
2. Drag a thing into a bin, or tap it and then a bin. A right answer gets a tick; a wrong one is counted and stays in the tray.
3. Use the particle picture to decide.
4. **New set** deals fresh things. Answer the **Check yourself** questions.

## Worked example

Take **pure water**, **tap water** and **iron sulphide**. Pure water shows only H₂O: one kind of particle with two kinds of atom joined, so a **compound**. Tap water adds a few dissolved calcium carbonate particles: two kinds of particle, so a **mixture** (a homogeneous one at Class 9). Iron filings stirred with sulphur are a mixture, and a magnet pulls the iron out. Heat them and they join into FeS, a compound, and the magnet no longer works.

## Tips

- "Pure" on a shop label is not the same as pure in science, which means one kind of particle.
- An alloy such as brass is a mixture, because the amounts of each metal can change.

## FAQ

**Why is air a mixture when oxygen is an element?** Air holds several kinds of particle, none joined to the others, in changing amounts.

**Is anything stored?** No. Everything stays on the page.
