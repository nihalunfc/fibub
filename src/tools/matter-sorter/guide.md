## What it does

Everything around you is made of particles, and the kind of particles decides what sort of matter it is. This page deals you nine everyday things, such as air, table salt, a copper wire, rust, brass or muddy water, and three bins: **Element**, **Compound** and **Mixture**. Drag each thing into its bin, or tap it and then tap a bin. Tap any thing to see a **particle picture** of it: coloured balls for the atoms, joined into molecules where they are joined.

The picture makes the rule easy to see:

- an **element** has one kind of particle made of one kind of atom (oxygen gas is O₂ molecules, still only oxygen atoms);
- a **compound** has one kind of particle, but each particle has two or more kinds of atom joined in a fixed ratio (water is always H₂O);
- a **mixture** has two or more kinds of particle that are mixed but not joined (air is nitrogen, oxygen and a little argon and carbon dioxide).

It follows the school topics on the nature of matter: Class 8 Science, elements, compounds and mixtures (pure substances, metals and non-metals, symbols and formulae), and Class 9 Science, mixtures and their separation (homogeneous and heterogeneous mixtures, solutions and alloys). Before this, [[states-of-matter]] shows how particles move in solids, liquids and gases; after it, [[build-an-atom]] shows what is inside one atom and [[formula-writer]] shows how formulas like NaCl are written.

## How to use it

1. Choose a **Level**. Class 8 has three bins. Class 9 splits Mixture into **same all through** (homogeneous, like a solution or an alloy) and **not the same all through** (heterogeneous, with parts you can see or that settle).
2. Drag a thing into a bin, or tap it (it turns coloured) and then tap a bin. A right answer moves it into the bin with a tick; a wrong one is counted and stays in the tray.
3. Look at the particle picture below to decide. Once a thing is sorted, the page explains why it belongs there; tap a ticked thing in a bin to see it again.
4. **New set** deals nine fresh things with every bin used. **Use all things** puts all 32 in the tray.
5. Answer the **Check yourself** questions; they change every time.

## Worked example

Take **pure water**, **tap water** and **iron sulphide**.

- Pure water shows only H₂O molecules: one kind of particle, but with two kinds of atom (H and O) joined. It is a **compound**.
- Tap water shows mostly H₂O and a few particles of dissolved calcium carbonate: two kinds of particle, so it is a **mixture**. At Class 9 it is a homogeneous one, because it looks the same all through.
- Iron filings stirred with sulphur are a **mixture**: you see iron atoms and S₈ rings side by side, and a magnet pulls the iron out. Heat them and they join into iron sulphide, FeS, which is a **compound**: one kind of particle and a magnet no longer works.

## Tips

- "Pure" in a shop (pure ghee, pure juice) is not the same as pure in science. A science pure substance has only one kind of particle.
- Two atoms of the same element joined together (O₂, N₂, S₈) still make an element.
- An alloy such as brass or steel is a mixture: you can change how much of each metal is in it.

## FAQ

**Why is air a mixture when oxygen is an element?** Air has several kinds of particle (nitrogen, oxygen, argon, carbon dioxide). None of them is joined to the others, and the amounts can change.

**Are the particle pictures to scale?** No. Atoms are far smaller and far more numerous; a few are drawn big so you can count the kinds. Big molecules like sugar and oil are simplified to a few atoms.

**Is anything stored?** No. Your sorting and answers stay on the page and are gone when you leave.
