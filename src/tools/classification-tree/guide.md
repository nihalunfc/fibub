## What it does

Biologists sort living things into groups inside groups, called **ranks**: kingdom, phylum (called division for plants), class, order, family, genus and species. Each species has a two-part scientific name, its **binomial name**, made of its genus and its species word, such as *Panthera tigris* for the tiger.

This page has three parts:

- **Walk the tree**: start at Whittaker's five kingdoms (Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia) and tap a group to go one rank down, until you reach a species and see all seven of its ranks. The breadcrumbs at the top take you back up.
- **Compare two living things**: choose two species and see their ranks side by side. Shared ranks are shaded, and the page says the lowest rank they share. A tiger and a lion share everything down to the genus *Panthera*; a tiger and a human part ways at the order.
- **Check a binomial name**: type a scientific name in any capitals and the page writes it correctly, gives the short form (*P. tigris*), and lists what was wrong.

It follows the Class 9 science chapter *Patterns in Life: Diversity and Classification* and the Class 11 biology chapter *The Living World*. ## How to use it

1. In **Walk the tree**, tap a kingdom, then keep tapping groups. The small number under each group is how many of this page's species are in it.
2. In **Compare**, pick a first and a second living thing. Read the shaded rows and the sentence below the table.
3. In **Check a binomial name**, type a name. Try "homo Sapiens" or "Panthera tigris tigris".
4. Answer the four questions at the bottom to check yourself.

## Worked example

Compare the **potato** and the **brinjal**. Both are Plantae, Angiospermae, Dicotyledonae, order Solanales, family Solanaceae and genus *Solanum*. They differ only at the species: *Solanum tuberosum* and *Solanum melongena*. So they are close relatives, which is why their flowers look so alike. Now compare the potato and **wheat**: they share only the kingdom and division, then split at class (dicot against monocot).

## Writing scientific names

- Two words: the genus, then the species word.
- The genus starts with a capital letter; the species word is all small letters.
- Print it in *italics*; when handwritten, underline each word separately.
- After the first full use, the genus may be shortened to its first letter: *E. coli*.
- A third small word names a subspecies (*Panthera tigris tigris*); a name after it in capitals is the person who first described it, and is not in italics.

## FAQs

**Why do some books say Polemoniales for potato?** Older classifications used that order; Solanales is the name used today. The page notes this.

**Are there really only five kingdoms?** Five is the school model. Many scientists now use three domains (Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya) above the kingdoms, and split Monera and Protista further.
