## What it does

This is a project lesson: you build one real wooden shelf from a single board and two metal brackets, and put it up on a wall. It brings together the two skills from earlier in the course. Nine illustrated steps take you from planning to a level, loaded shelf, each with a **Done** tick so you can follow along over an afternoon. Tap the picture to learn the parts of a shelf, put the steps in order, and take the short quiz before you start drilling.

It is the last lesson of the free **Light carpentry** course. Cut the board with [[saw-straight|a straight saw cut]], fix the brackets as in [[drill-and-screw|drilling and screwing wood]], and check the screws in the wall can take the weight with [[shelf-load]].

## What each part does

- **Shelf board:** solid pine or oak, or a faced board such as plywood or MDF, usually 18 to 22 mm (about ¾ to ⅞ in) thick. Thicker and shorter spans sag less under books.
- **Brackets:** carry the board and pass its weight to the wall. Longer vertical arms grip the wall better, because the top screw has a longer lever.
- **Screws:** short ones up into the board (shorter than the board is thick, so they do not poke through), and longer ones into the wall.
- **Wall plug:** a plastic or metal sleeve that lets a screw grip brick, block or plasterboard. Into a timber stud you use a wood screw with no plug.
- **Stud:** an upright timber or metal post inside a hollow wall. Screwing into a stud is the strongest fixing on a hollow wall.
- **Spirit level:** the bubble sits between the lines when the shelf is level.

## Worked example

You want a 800 mm (about 31½ in) shelf, 200 mm (8 in) deep, for paperbacks. Books weigh roughly 20 to 25 kg per metre of shelf, so about 20 kg here. You buy an 18 mm pine board, two 200 mm brackets with 150 mm upright arms and a pack of 16 mm screws for the board. You cut the board to 800 mm on the waste side of the line and sand it. You set the brackets 130 mm in from each end (about a sixth of the length), drill 2 mm pilots and screw them on. After two coats of oil you mark a level line, check for cables and pipes, find a stud for one bracket, use the right plugs for the other, and screw it up. With about 21.5 kg on it (books plus the board) and the wall screws 120 mm apart on each upright arm, the shelf load tool shows each top screw is pulled out with about 9 kg, well within a good fixing.

## FAQs

**How far apart should brackets be?** For an 18 mm board with books, about 400 to 600 mm (16 to 24 in) apart, set in from each end by about a sixth of the length. Heavier loads or thinner boards need them closer.

**Can I use the screws that came with the brackets?** For the board, often yes. For the wall, packet screws and plugs are often too short or too weak: pick the fixing for your wall type.

**Why fix the brackets to the board first?** It is easier to get them square and in line on the bench. Then you only need to level one thing on the wall. Some people fix the brackets to the wall first and screw the board down after; both work.

**What wall fixings should I use?** That depends on the wall. The Wall fixings lessons [[whats-in-your-wall]] and [[pick-wall-plug]] cover it.
