## What it does

Gratuity (end-of-service pay) is a lump sum the law sets for a worker who leaves after years of service. Pick Bahrain, India, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia or the UAE, type your last pay and how long you worked, and the calculator applies that country's rule. For India it also splits the amount into the tax-free part and the part taxed as salary. Everything is worked out in your browser.

Add pay for unused holiday with [[leave-pay]], see what the taxable part costs you with the [[india-income-tax|India tax calculator]], then plan the lump sum with the [[budget-calculator|budget planner]].

## How to use it

1. Pick the **country** whose law covers your job.
2. Type your **last monthly pay**: basic plus dearness allowance (India), basic wage (Oman, Qatar, UAE), basic plus social allowance (Bahrain) or your full wage (Saudi Arabia).
3. Type your **years and months** of service.

**More options:** enter your date of joining and last working day and the years, months and extra days are filled in for you (the last day counts as worked; every Gulf country except Saudi Arabia also uses the extra days). A table under the result shows the gratuity for 1 to 30 whole years of service with the same pay, so you can see what an extra year is worth. A line at the top gives the answer in one sentence, or tells you what is missing.

## The rules

- **India, covered by the Act** (10 or more staff): 15 ÷ 26 × last monthly pay × years. A part year of more than 6 months counts as a full year. You need 5 years, or 1 year as a fixed-term employee since November 2025. The Act pays at most Rs 20 lakh. Since the Labour Codes came into force on 21 November 2025, "wages" must be at least half of your total pay: if your allowances (HRA, conveyance, special allowance and the like) come to more than 50 % of the total, the amount over 50 % is added back to basic plus DA before the formula. Example: total pay Rs 1,00,000 with basic plus DA Rs 40,000; allowances are Rs 60,000, Rs 10,000 over half, so gratuity is worked out on Rs 50,000.
- **India, not covered**: half a month's average pay for each completed year.
- **India tax**: tax-free up to the least of the formula, Rs 20 lakh and what you were paid. Government employees pay no tax on it.
- **UAE**: 21 days' basic salary a year for the first 5 years, 30 days a year after that, part years pro rata, at least 1 year, never more than 2 years' basic.
- **UAE option**: since 2023 an employer may instead join a voluntary savings scheme (Cabinet Resolution 96 of 2023), paying monthly into a fund; this calculator shows the standard rule.
- **Saudi Arabia**: half a month's wage a year for the first 5 years, a full month after. If you resign: nothing under 2 years, a third for 2 to 5 years, two thirds under 10 years, all from 10 years.
- **Qatar**: at least 3 weeks' (21 days') basic wage a year, part years pro rata, after at least 1 year.
- **Oman** (workers outside the Social Protection Law): at least one month's basic wage a year, part years pro rata, until that law's savings system starts.
- **Bahrain** (non-Bahraini workers): half a month's wage a year for the first 3 years, a month after, part years pro rata. Since 1 March 2024 the employer pays monthly into the Social Insurance Organization, which pays the gratuity.

## Worked example

Ravi earns Rs 50,000 basic plus DA and leaves after 10 years: 15 ÷ 26 × 50,000 × 10 = **Rs 2,88,462**, all tax-free. In Dubai, Sara's basic is AED 10,000 after 7 years: 5 × 21 + 2 × 30 = 165 days at AED 333.33 = **AED 55,000**. In Riyadh, an 8,000 riyal wage after 7 years gives SAR 36,000, or SAR 24,000 on resignation. In Doha, QAR 10,000 basic after 7 years gives 7 × 21 days at QAR 333.33 = **QAR 49,000**.

## FAQs

**Why not Kuwait?** We could not open the current official text of its labour law with all amendments, so it is left out for now.

**Is anything saved?** No. Nothing you type leaves the page. These are estimates of what the law sets, not legal, financial or tax advice; check the official source.
