## What it does

Pick the meat and type its weight, and this page gives a roasting time range, the oven setting, how long to rest it and the temperature the thickest part must reach. For a whole turkey it also says how long thawing takes in the fridge or in cold water.

Times are only a guide; the safe temperature is what matters, so the page shows both the USDA FSIS and the Health Canada figures. To change the oven setting to gas mark or a fan oven, use [[oven-temperature]]; to fit the roast into the week, use the [[meal-planner]].

## How to use it

1. Choose the meat: whole turkey, whole chicken, or a beef, pork or lamb roast.
2. Type the weight and pick kg or lb. Switching the unit converts the number you typed.
3. For turkey, tick Stuffed if the stuffing cooks inside the bird. For beef and lamb, choose how done you like it.
4. Start checking with a thermometer at the start of the time range.

## What each setting does

- **Meat**: sets the oven temperature, the time rule and the safe temperature.
- **Weight, kg or lb**: the raw weight on the label.
- **Stuffed** (turkey only): uses the USDA chart for stuffed birds, which takes longer.
- **How done** (beef and lamb): medium-rare 63 °C, medium 71 °C or well done 77 °C, as Health Canada lists them.
- **Minutes per kg, from and to** (not turkey): fibub's rule of thumb, filled in for you. Edit it if your recipe or oven differs.
- **Reset**: goes back to the 6 kg turkey example.

## Worked example

A 6 kg turkey, not stuffed, is 13.2 lb, so it falls in the USDA 12 to 14 lb row: 3 h to 3 h 45 min at 325 °F (about 165 °C). It is done at 74 °C (165 °F) in the thigh and breast by USDA (Health Canada says 82 °C for a whole bird), then rests at least 20 minutes. Thawing in the fridge takes about 3 days (63 to 79 hours); in cold water about 6½ hours.

## FAQs

**Why do USDA and Health Canada differ?** They set their own advice. Health Canada asks for more heat on whole birds (82 °C) and pork (71 °C).

**Is rare beef listed?** No. Both agencies set 63 °C as the lowest safe point for whole cuts.
