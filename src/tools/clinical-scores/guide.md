## What it does

Two of the best-known scores in medicine are simple sums, but students and new staff often mix up the wording or the order. This page lays each one out as a short list of plain descriptions. Tap the one that fits and the page adds it up.

- **APGAR** describes a newborn baby a minute or a few minutes after birth: skin colour (Appearance), heart rate (Pulse), response to stimulation (Grimace), muscle tone (Activity) and breathing (Respiration). Each scores 0, 1 or 2, so the total is 0 to 10. Virginia Apgar proposed it in 1953.
- The **Glasgow Coma Scale** (GCS) describes how awake and responsive a person is, from three parts: eye opening (E, 1 to 4), the best verbal response (V, 1 to 5) and the best motor response (M, 1 to 6). The total runs from 3 to 15. Graham Teasdale and Bryan Jennett published it in 1974; the 15-point form with "abnormal flexion" as its own step followed in 1976.

The descriptions on this page are written in our own words. They are not the official charts, which come with training on how to give the stimulus and judge the response.

For blood gas results in the same patient, use the [[acid-base-interpreter]]; to compare two people's scores, [[reliability-analysis|reliability and agreement]].

## How to use it

1. **APGAR:** choose the minute (1, 5 or 10) and tap one line for each of the five signs. The total appears with the band commonly used: 7 to 10 reassuring, 4 to 6 moderately abnormal, 0 to 3 low.
2. **GCS:** choose the wording. **Adult and older child** is for anyone who can talk and follow a command. **Young child** swaps the verbal and motor lines for ones that suit a baby or toddler (coos and babbles, cries, moves on their own); eye opening stays the same.
3. Tap the best response for eyes, verbal and motor. The result is written the standard way, such as **E3V4M5 = 12**, with the band often used after head injury: 13 to 15 mild, 9 to 12 moderate, 3 to 8 severe.
4. If a part cannot be tested, such as speech with a breathing tube in place or eyes swollen shut, choose **Not testable**. The part is written NT and no total is given, because adding up a missing part would be misleading.
5. **Download** saves the scores as an image, a PDF or a Word file for notes or a practice log.

## Worked example

The page opens with a baby whose body is pink but hands and feet blue (1), heart rate over 100 (2), who grimaces when stimulated (1), moves actively (2) and cries well (2). It shows **APGAR at 1 minute: 8 / 10**, in the 7 to 10 band.

The GCS example is a person whose eyes open when spoken to (E3), who talks in muddled sentences (V4) and moves a hand towards a firm squeeze (M5). The page shows **E3V4M5 = 12**, in the 9 to 12 band. If that person then had a breathing tube, choosing Not testable for verbal gives **E3VNTM5** and no total.

## Tips

- Always pass on the three GCS parts, not only the total. Two people with a total of 8 can be in very different states.
- Score the best response you see. For motor, use the better side.
- An APGAR score describes the baby at that moment. Resuscitation does not wait for the 1-minute score.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is the lowest GCS 3 and not 0?** Each part starts at 1 for no response, so a person with no response at all scores E1V1M1 = 3.

**Is the paediatric GCS a different scale?** It keeps the same numbers and adds age-suitable wording for the verbal and motor parts. Several versions exist; check the one your service uses.

**Does the page store anything?** No. Nothing you tap is stored or sent anywhere.
