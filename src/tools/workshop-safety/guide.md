## What it does

This lesson covers the safety habits that prevent most DIY injuries at home: protecting your eyes, ears and lungs, knowing when gloves help and when they are dangerous, keeping the floor clear, clamping work, cutting away from your body, setting up a ladder, and dealing with a small cut. There are nine illustrated steps you can tick, a **spot the hazards** workshop picture with seven things wrong in it, a put-in-order activity for the routine before a job, and a short quiz.

It is the second lesson of the free **Hand tools and safety** course. If you are new to the tools themselves, start with [[know-hand-tools]]; next, learn to measure accurately in [[measure-and-mark]], then plan your cuts with the [[cut-list|cut list]].

## What each piece of protection does

- **Safety glasses:** stop chips and dust hitting your eyes. Look for a standard mark such as EN 166 (Europe, UK) or ANSI Z87.1 (US).
- **Ear defenders or plugs:** for hammer drills, saws, grinders and long spells of loud work. Hearing damage builds up and does not come back.
- **Dust mask (FFP2 or N95):** for sanding, cutting MDF and sweeping up. A thin paper mask does very little.
- **Gloves:** for carrying rough or sharp material. Never near a spinning tool.
- **Sturdy closed shoes:** protect your feet from dropped tools and timber.

## Worked example

You are about to drill holes for a shelf in the garage. Before you start you clear the off-cuts from the floor, run the extension lead along the wall and send the dog indoors. You check the drill's lead for damage, roll up your sleeves and take off your watch, then put on safety glasses and a dust mask. The shelf bracket hole is above head height, so you set the stepladder fully open on level ground rather than stretching. You clamp the shelf board to the bench to drill it, unplug the drill to change bits, and tidy up when you finish.

## FAQs

**Are normal glasses good enough?** No. They are not made to stop impacts and leave the sides open. Wear safety glasses over them, or get prescription safety glasses.

**Why not wear gloves when drilling?** A glove can catch on the spinning chuck or bit and wind your hand in faster than you can let go. Bare, dry hands are safer with spinning tools.

**What is the 1 in 4 rule?** For a leaning ladder, the foot should be 1 unit out from the wall for every 4 units of height up to where it rests. That makes an angle of about 75°.

**When does a cut need a doctor?** If it is deep or gaping, will not stop bleeding after 10 minutes of firm pressure, has something stuck in it, or came from a dirty or rusty object and your tetanus jabs are not up to date. Anything in the eye needs medical help. In an emergency call your local emergency number.
