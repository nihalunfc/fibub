## What it does

Two quantities are in **direct proportion** when doubling one doubles the other: twice as many pencils cost twice as much. They are in **inverse proportion** when doubling one halves the other: twice as many workers finish a job in half the days.

This page gives you four everyday situations, two of each kind. For each one it draws the graph and puts a dot on it that you drag left and right. As you drag, the page writes out the sum with your numbers, and a table of five rows shows the test:

- **Direct:** y ÷ x is the same in every row (the constant k), and the graph is a straight line through 0.
- **Inverse:** x × y is the same in every row (k again), and the graph is a curve that gets close to both axes but never touches them.

A slider changes k: the price of one pencil, the speed of the car, the total work in worker-days or the length of the trip.

It fits Class 8 Mathematics, proportional reasoning (direct and inverse proportion and the unitary method), and links forward to graphs of motion in Class 9 Science. Ratios themselves are mixed and compared in the [[ratio-mixer]]; distance against time for a moving object is drawn live in [[motion-graphs]].

## How to use it

- Tap a situation: **Pencils and cost** or **Time and distance** (direct), **Workers and days** or **Speed and time** (inverse).
- Move the **k** slider, or type a value in the box, to change the constant.
- Drag the dot along the graph. The big line shows the pair of values; the line under it shows the sum.
- The highlighted column in the table is the one that stays the same.

## A worked example

**Workers and days** with k = 24 worker-days (the job takes one worker 24 days):

- 4 workers: 24 ÷ 4 = 6 days.
- 8 workers: 24 ÷ 8 = 3 days. Twice the workers, half the days.
- The table rows 2 → 12, 4 → 6, 6 → 4, 8 → 3, 12 → 2 all have x × y = 24.

**Pencils and cost** with k = ₹5: 6 pencils cost 5 × 6 = ₹30, and every row has cost ÷ pencils = 5.

Unitary method for an inverse problem: 6 workers take 10 days, so the job is 6 × 10 = 60 worker-days, and 4 workers take 60 ÷ 4 = 15 days.

## FAQ

**Is "as one goes up, the other goes up" enough for direct proportion?** No. y = x + 3 goes up with x, but y ÷ x changes, so it is not direct proportion. Its graph is a line that does not pass through 0.

**Why does the inverse curve never touch the axes?** x × y = k with k above 0, so neither x nor y can ever be 0. Zero workers would need endless days.
