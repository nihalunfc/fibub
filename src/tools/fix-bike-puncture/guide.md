## What it does

This lesson shows you how to mend a punctured bicycle inner tube with a patch, the job every rider needs sooner or later. A puncture kit costs very little, fits in a saddle bag, and a patched tube is as good as new. The nine steps each have a drawing and a **Done** tick, so you can follow along with the bike in front of you. The wheel picture teaches the names of the parts (tyre, inner tube, valve, rim, spokes, hub, quick release, patch and tyre lever), and you can press **Test me** to check you remember them. A put-in-order activity and a short quiz make sure the order sticks, and **Print checklist** gives you a paper copy for the shed.

It is the third lesson of the free **Tyres and punctures** course. For a car, see [[plug-tyre-puncture|plugging a tubeless car tyre]] or [[change-flat-tyre|changing a flat car tyre]], and use [[tyre-pressure]] to convert psi and bar.

## How to use it

1. Read **Stay safe** and get the bike out of the way of traffic.
2. Work down the steps and tick each one when it is done. Ticks are kept only if you tick **Remember my ticks on this device**, and **Clear ticks** removes them.
3. Tap the parts of the wheel picture to learn their names, then press **Test me**.
4. Put the steps in order, then take the quiz.

## What each part does

- **Tyre:** the outer rubber case that grips the road. Its side shows the size and the pressure range.
- **Inner tube:** the rubber ring inside that holds the air. This is what you patch.
- **Valve:** **Presta** is thin, with a small tip you unscrew before pumping; **Schrader** is the wider car-type valve. Your pump must fit yours (many pumps fit both).
- **Rim:** the metal hoop the tyre sits on. **Spokes** hold it true; the **hub** turns on bearings in the middle.
- **Quick release:** a lever that clamps the wheel in the frame. Some bikes have axle nuts or a thru-axle instead.
- **Tyre lever:** a flat plastic lever that lifts the tyre edge off the rim.

## Worked example

Your back tyre goes soft on the way home. You change to the smallest sprocket, open the quick release and lift the wheel out. You let the rest of the air out, hook two levers under the tyre opposite the valve, and run one round the rim. Pumping the tube a little, you hear a hiss and mark the hole. Lining the tube up with the tyre, you find a small thorn in the tread and pull it out. You sand round the hole, spread thin glue, wait four minutes until it goes dull, and press the patch on hard for a minute. With a little air in the tube you tuck it in and thumb the tyre back on, finishing at the valve. You pump to 60 psi, inside the 50 to 85 psi printed on the tyre, refit the wheel, and check the brakes.

## FAQs

**Can I ride on it straight away?** Yes, once the patch has been pressed on firmly. Glued patches bond more fully over the next hour.

**Should I just fit a new tube?** On the roadside it is quicker. Patch the old one at home and carry it as your spare.

**Why does my tyre keep going flat?** Usually something is still stuck in the tyre, a spoke end is poking through the rim tape, or the tube was pinched while refitting.

**What about tubeless bike tyres?** They are sealed with liquid sealant and mended with plugs, more like a car tyre. Follow the tyre maker's instructions.
