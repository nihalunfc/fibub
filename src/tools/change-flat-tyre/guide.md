## What it does

This lesson walks you through changing a flat tyre on a car, the way you would do it at the side of a quiet road or in a car park. Each of the eight steps has a drawing and a **Done** tick, so you can follow along with the car in front of you. The picture of a wheel lets you tap each part to learn its name, then test yourself. A put-in-order activity and a short quiz check that the order sticks, and you can print the whole list to keep in the glovebox.

It is the first lesson of the free **Tyres and punctures** course. When the spare is on, work out how tight the nuts should be with wheel nut torque, check the spare's pressure with [[tyre-pressure]], and see if the flat can be mended in the puncture plugging lesson.

## How to use it

1. Read the **Stay safe** box first. If the car is on a fast or busy road, do not change the tyre: get everyone out on the side away from traffic and call for help.
2. Work down the steps and tick each one when it is done. Ticks are not saved unless you tick **Remember my ticks on this device**.
3. Tap the parts of the wheel picture, then press **Test me** to see if you remember them.
4. Put the steps in order and take the quiz. Press **Print checklist** for a paper copy.

## What each part does

- **Wheel nuts (or bolts):** hold the wheel on the hub. Many cars have one locking nut that needs a special key, usually kept in the glovebox or with the jack.
- **Wheel brace:** the L-shaped spanner for the nuts. A longer handle gives more turning force for the same push.
- **Jack:** lifts the car at a strong, marked **jacking point** on the sill. Anywhere else can bend the body or let the car slip.
- **Valve:** where air goes in. Check the spare's pressure here as soon as you can.

## Worked example

You have a flat front-left tyre in a car park. You switch on the hazard lights, pull the parking brake, put the car in Park (or first gear) and chock the rear-right wheel with a brick. You take a quarter to half turn off each of the five nuts with the wheel still on the ground, then jack at the front-left jacking point until the tyre is about 3 cm clear. You take the nuts off, swap the wheels, do the nuts up by hand in a star pattern (1, 3, 5, 2, 4), lower the car and tighten them firmly in the same star order. The handbook says 110 N·m: with a 40 cm brace that is a push of about 275 N, roughly your body weight on one foot pushing down. After 50 km you check the nuts again.

## FAQs

**Which way do wheel nuts undo?** Anticlockwise, as you look at them ("lefty loosey"). A few old vehicles used left-hand threads on one side; the handbook will say.

**Why loosen the nuts before jacking?** With the tyre on the ground, friction stops the wheel turning while you push. In the air, the wheel just spins, and heavy pushing could rock the car off the jack.

**How fast can I drive on a space-saver spare?** Usually no faster than 80 km/h (50 mph) and only as far as a tyre shop. The limit is printed on the spare.

**Should I tighten the nuts as hard as I can?** No. Too tight can stretch the studs or warp the brake disc; too loose and the wheel can come off. Firm in a star pattern, then the right torque with a torque wrench as soon as you can.
