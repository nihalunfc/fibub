## What it does

If your employer pays you a house rent allowance (HRA) and you live in a rented home, part of that HRA can be tax-free in India's old tax regime. The tax-free part is the smallest of three amounts: the HRA you receive, the rent you pay minus 10% of your salary, and 50% of your salary in the listed cities or 40% elsewhere. This calculator works out all three, marks the lowest and shows the tax-free and taxable HRA a month and for the year.

From FY 2026-27, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad get the 50% limit too, alongside Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata and Chennai. To see whether the old regime, with HRA, beats the new one for you, use the [[india-income-tax|India income tax calculator]].

## How to use it

1. Enter your monthly Basic + DA, the HRA on your payslip and the rent you pay.
2. Choose the city where you rent and the tax year.
3. If you rented for only part of the year, enter the number of months.
4. Read the tax-free and taxable HRA, and the three limits below them.

## What each setting does

- **Basic + DA:** "salary" for HRA means Basic plus DA that counts for retirement benefits, plus any commission paid as a fixed % of sales. Not your gross pay.
- **HRA received:** the allowance on your payslip.
- **Rent you pay:** the rent for the home you live in.
- **City:** the eight listed cities get 50% of salary, everywhere else 40%.
- **Tax year:** before FY 2026-27, only the four older metros got 50%.
- **Months:** for months without rent, all HRA is taxable.

## Worked example

Basic + DA ₹50,000 a month, HRA ₹20,000, rent ₹25,000, in Delhi. The three limits are ₹20,000 (HRA), ₹25,000 − ₹5,000 = ₹20,000 (rent minus 10%), and ₹25,000 (50% of salary). The lowest is **₹20,000**, so all the HRA is tax-free: ₹2,40,000 for the year.

## FAQs

**Can I claim HRA in the new regime?** No. HRA is fully taxable there.

**I pay rent to my parents. Is that allowed?** Generally yes, if the rent is real, paid and they show it as income. Your employer may ask for your landlord's PAN when the rent is over ₹1 lakh a year.

**Is this my final tax?** No. Not financial or tax advice; check the official source.
