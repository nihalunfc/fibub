## What it does

A leaf does not use every colour of sunlight equally. Its pigments soak up some colours and let others bounce off. This page draws the **absorption spectrum** of the three main groups of leaf pigments, **chlorophyll a**, **chlorophyll b** and the **carotenoids**, against the colours of the rainbow from 400 nm (violet) to 700 nm (red), and on top of them the **action spectrum**: how fast photosynthesis goes in each colour.

Slide or drag the marker across the graph to pick a colour. The page tells you how strongly each pigment absorbs it and how fast photosynthesis would go, as a percentage of the best colour. At Class 11 level it also works out the energy of one photon of that colour and replays **Engelmann's experiment**, where bacteria that love oxygen crowd around the parts of an alga lit by blue and red light.

It fits Class 10 Science (life processes: photosynthesis and chlorophyll) and Class 11 Biology (photosynthesis in higher plants: the pigments, absorption and action spectra). See how light, carbon dioxide and temperature limit the rate in [[photosynthesis-lab]], follow what the absorbed light does next in [[z-scheme]], and see how white light splits into these colours in [[prism-dispersion]].

## How to use it

1. Choose **Class 10** for the main idea, or **Class 11** for the photon energy line and Engelmann's experiment.
2. Drag across the graph, or use the **Colour of light** slider.
3. Tick or untick each curve to compare them one at a time. The dashed black curve is the rate of photosynthesis.
4. Read the big line and the four numbers under it.
5. At Class 11, watch the bacteria gather, then press **Scatter the bacteria** to start them again.

## Worked example

Put the marker at **450 nm** (blue). Chlorophyll b is near its tallest peak, the carotenoids absorb strongly and chlorophyll a absorbs a fair amount, so the page says the light is strongly absorbed, with photosynthesis at about 66 % of its best. One photon of 450 nm light carries E = hc/λ = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ × 2.998 × 10⁸)/(450 × 10⁻⁹) = 4.41 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, which is **2.76 eV**.

Now slide to **550 nm** (green). Every pigment curve is close to zero, the light is hardly absorbed and mostly reflected, and photosynthesis drops to about 9 % of its best. That reflected green light is what you see when you look at a leaf.

At **662 nm** (red) chlorophyll a reaches its red peak and the rate climbs back to about 69 %. A red photon there carries only 1.87 eV, less than the blue one, but it still drives photosynthesis.

## Tips

- An absorption spectrum belongs to one pigment; an action spectrum belongs to the whole process. When the two match, it is good evidence that those pigments do the work.
- Chlorophyll a is the main pigment; b and the carotenoids are **accessory pigments** that catch extra colours and pass the energy on.
- The rate never quite reaches zero in green, because light bounces around inside a real leaf and some of it is caught in the end.

## FAQ

**Are these real measured curves?** No. They are smooth teaching curves built from bell shapes at the usual peak positions, so the shapes are right but the exact heights are rounded.

**Why are autumn leaves yellow and orange?** In autumn many trees break down their chlorophyll; the carotenoids, which absorb only blue, are left, so the leaves reflect yellow, orange and red.

**What did Engelmann's bacteria show?** They gathered where the alga gave off the most oxygen, in blue and red light, which drew an action spectrum without any instruments.
