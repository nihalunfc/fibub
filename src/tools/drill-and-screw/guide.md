## What it does

This lesson shows you how to screw one piece of wood to another so the joint pulls up tight and the wood does not split. Eight illustrated steps take you from choosing the screw to driving it flush, each with a **Done** tick. The **screw picker** suggests a screw length and thickness for your two pieces, with the clearance and pilot drill sizes for softwood or hardwood. ## What each hole does

- **Clearance hole:** drilled through the top piece, as wide as the screw's smooth shank. The screw slides through without gripping, so its head can pull the top piece down tight. Without it, the threads grip both pieces and can hold them apart (a gap you cannot close).
- **Pilot hole:** a thinner hole in the base, about as wide as the screw's core (the solid middle without the thread). It makes room so the wood is not wedged apart and split, and it guides the screw in straight. Hardwood needs a slightly bigger pilot than softwood.
- **Countersink:** a cone-shaped recess at the top so a countersunk (flat) head sits flush instead of sticking up or crushing the surface.
- **Clutch:** the numbered ring on a drill driver. It slips at a set twist, so the screw stops when it is home. Start low and go up a step at a time.

## Worked example

You are fixing an 18 mm (¾ in) pine board onto a 45 mm (1¾ in) batten. About 2.5 times 18 mm is 45 mm, and the tip stays 13 mm inside the batten, so a 45 mm screw works. That length is usually 4.0 mm thick (#8). Drill a 4.5 mm clearance hole through the board, a 2.5 mm pilot hole about 27 mm deep into the batten (as deep as the screw will go) (3 mm for oak or other hardwood), countersink, then drive the screw with a PZ2 bit and the clutch on a low setting. The screw goes 27 mm into the batten, 60% of its length, which grips well.

## FAQs

**Do I always need a pilot hole?** Modern "wood screws" with sharp, cutting tips often go into softwood without one, away from the ends. Always drill a pilot in hardwood, within about 25 mm (1 in) of an end, and in thin or narrow pieces.

**PZ or PH bit?** Pozidriv (PZ) heads have extra small lines between the arms of the cross; Phillips (PH) do not. A PH bit in a PZ screw slips and chews the head. Most wood screws in the UK and Europe are PZ2; in North America many are Phillips, square (Robertson) or Torx.

**Why did the screw head snap?** Too much force in hard wood without a big enough pilot. Use a larger pilot, a little wax on the thread and a lower clutch setting.

**How far from the end should a screw go?** As a rough guide, at least 25 mm (1 in) from an end and 10 mm from an edge, with a pilot hole. Closer than that splits easily.
