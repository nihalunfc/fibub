## What it does

Winter tyres are made from rubber that stays soft in the cold, with grooves and slits that bite into snow. This tool checks a set of tyres in four quick steps: which mark is on the sidewall, how deep the tread is, how old the tyres are and whether you have checked the pressure when cold. Then it gives one verdict: Ready, Check these or Not ready, and lists the reasons.

It also shows the winter tyre rules for Canada, the USA and the UK as we last checked them. Find the age with [[tyre-age]], measure the grooves with [[tread-depth]], and fix the pressure with [[tyre-pressure]].

## How to use it

1. Pick your country.
2. Tap the mark you find on the sidewall: the mountain snowflake, M+S only, or neither.
3. Type the tread depth in the main groove, and the date code if you know it.
4. Tick the box once you have checked the pressure with cold tyres.
5. Read the verdict and fix any red or amber line.

## What each setting does

- **Mountain snowflake (3PMSF)**: the tyre passed a snow grip test. This is the best mark for winter.
- **M+S**: the maker says it is for mud and snow. There is no grip test, and many all-season tyres carry it.
- **Tread**: winter tyres want 4 mm (5/32 inch) or more. Below that, snow grip drops sharply.
- **Age**: from the date code. Over 6 years, have them checked; at 10, replace them.
- **Pressure**: cold air makes pressure fall, about 1 psi for every 5 to 6 degrees C.

## Worked example

Snowflake mark, 6 mm tread, made 3 years ago and pressure checked: Ready. The same car with M+S only and 3 mm tread: Not ready, because the tread is under 4 mm and the mark is a weaker one.

## FAQs

**What is the 7 degrees rule?** Below about 7 C, winter-rated tyres grip better than summer ones even on dry roads, because the rubber stays soft. It is a guide, not a law of nature.

**Are all-season tyres winter tyres?** Not unless they carry the snowflake mark. M+S alone is not a snow test.

**Do I need four winter tyres?** Fit all four. Two winter and two summer tyres can make the car slide.

Not safety advice. When in doubt, ask a tyre shop.
