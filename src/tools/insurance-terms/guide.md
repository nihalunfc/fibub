## What it does

Insurance papers are full of words that sound alike: deductible, excess, copay, coinsurance, sub-limit. This glossary explains about 40 of them in one or two plain sentences, with a tiny number example, so you can see what each one does to a real bill.

Cards carry a badge where a word means something different in India, Canada, the US or the UK. Use it before you read a policy, and keep it open beside the wording. To test a promise in a real policy, try [[policy-fine-print]]; to decide if cover is worth its price, try [[should-i-insure]].

## How to use it

1. Type a word in the search box. The cards filter as you type.
2. Pick a **Country** to show words that matter there, and a **Type of cover** (health, dental, car, life, home, general).
3. Read the example on each card. All numbers are made-up examples.

## What each filter does

- **Country:** "All" shows everything. A country shows general words plus the ones special to it.
- **Type of cover:** keeps only the words used in that kind of policy.
- **Search:** matches the word, its short name and the meaning.

## Worked example

A health plan has a deductible of 1,000 and a claim of 5,000. You pay the first 1,000 and the plan pays 4,000. If the plan also has 20% coinsurance, you pay 20% of the remaining 4,000 (800), so you pay 1,800 in all.

## FAQs

**Is this the wording of my policy?** No. Your policy document decides what is covered. This only explains common words.

**Why do countries differ?** Rules and names differ. For example, a "deductible" in the US is often called an "excess" in the UK.

**Are these recommendations?** No. No insurer or product is named, and nothing here is advice.
