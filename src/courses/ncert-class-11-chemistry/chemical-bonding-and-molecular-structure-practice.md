<div class="bigidea">Bonding questions follow one chain: <b>count electrons → count electron pairs → shape → hybridisation → polarity</b>. Do it in that order and most questions answer themselves.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For a Lewis structure, show the electron count first, then the structure with lone pairs.
2. For shape, write the electron-pair count, then the lone-pair count, then the name of the shape.
3. For MO questions, write the filled configuration and then bond order = (N_b − N_a)/2.

## 1-mark questions

1. State the octet rule.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Atoms tend to gain, lose or share electrons until they have eight electrons in their outermost shell (two for hydrogen and helium).</details>

2. Which has the higher boiling point, HF or HCl, and why?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>HF, because its molecules are held together by hydrogen bonds. HCl has only weaker dipole attractions.</details>

3. Name the shape of XeF₂.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Linear (five electron pairs: two bond pairs and three lone pairs on the equator of a trigonal bipyramid).</details>

4. How many σ and π bonds are there in ethyne, C₂H₂?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>3 σ bonds (one C–C and two C–H) and 2 π bonds.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

5. Why is the bond angle in H₂O (104.5°) smaller than that in CH₄ (109.5°)?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both have four electron pairs, pointing to the corners of a tetrahedron. In H₂O two of them are lone pairs, which repel the bond pairs more strongly than bond pairs repel each other (lp–lp > lp–bp > bp–bp), squeezing the H–O–H angle to 104.5°.</details>

6. CO₂ has polar bonds but its dipole moment is zero. Explain.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>CO₂ is linear (O=C=O). The two C=O bond dipoles are equal and act in opposite directions, so they cancel and the net dipole moment is zero.</details>

7. Draw the Lewis structure of the nitrate ion NO₃⁻ and give the formal charges.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Valence electrons: 5 + 18 + 1 = 24. N is in the middle with one N=O and two N–O⁻ bonds, every O with an octet. Formal charges: N = 5 − 0 − 4 = +1; the double-bonded O = 6 − 4 − 2 = 0; each single-bonded O = 6 − 6 − 1 = −1. Total = +1 + 0 − 1 − 1 = −1. ✓ There are three equivalent resonance forms, so the three N–O bonds are equal in the real ion.

</details>

8. Using MO theory, compare the bond order and magnetic behaviour of N₂ and O₂⁺.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

N₂ (10 valence electrons): σ2s² σ*2s² π2p⁴ σ2pz². N_b = 8, N_a = 2, bond order 3, diamagnetic.

O₂⁺ (11 valence electrons): σ2s² σ*2s² σ2pz² π2p⁴ π*2p¹. N_b = 8, N_a = 3, bond order 2.5, one unpaired electron, paramagnetic.

</details>

9. What is hybridisation? Give the hybridisation and shape of the central atoms in BF₃ and NH₃.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Hybridisation is the mixing of atomic orbitals of one atom with similar energies to form the same number of equivalent hybrid orbitals. BF₃: sp², trigonal planar, 120°. NH₃: sp³, with one lone pair, so the shape is trigonal pyramidal, about 107°.</details>

10. Why does ice float on water?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>In ice each water molecule forms four hydrogen bonds, holding the molecules in an open hexagonal cage with empty space. On melting, some bonds break and the molecules pack closer, so liquid water is denser than ice.</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

11. (a) Explain the shapes of SF₄ and XeF₄ using VSEPR. (b) Predict the hybridisation of S and Xe.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**SF₄:** S has 6 valence electrons; four go to bonds, so there are 4 bond pairs and 1 lone pair = 5 pairs, trigonal bipyramidal arrangement. The lone pair takes an equatorial place (more room), so the shape is **see-saw**. Hybridisation sp³d.

**XeF₄:** Xe has 8 valence electrons; four go to bonds, leaving 2 lone pairs = 6 pairs, octahedral arrangement. The two lone pairs go opposite each other (to be as far apart as possible), so the four F atoms lie in a plane: **square planar**. Hybridisation sp³d².

</details>

12. (a) State the rules for filling molecular orbitals. (b) Write the MO configuration of O₂ and find its bond order. (c) Explain why O₂ is paramagnetic.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Aufbau (lowest energy first), Pauli (at most two electrons, opposite spins) and Hund (one electron in each degenerate orbital before pairing).

(b) 12 valence electrons: σ2s² σ*2s² σ2pz² π2p⁴ π*2p². N_b = 8, N_a = 4, so bond order = (8 − 4)/2 = 2.

(c) The last two electrons enter the two degenerate π*2p orbitals one each (Hund), so O₂ has two unpaired electrons and is attracted by a magnetic field.

</details>

13. Explain the formation of a double bond in ethene using orbital overlap, and say why rotation about it is restricted.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Each carbon is sp² hybridised: three sp² orbitals make three σ bonds (one to the other C, two to H) in one plane at 120°. The unhybridised p orbital on each carbon stands perpendicular to this plane. The two p orbitals overlap **sideways**, above and below the plane, to make one π bond. The C=C bond is therefore 1σ + 1π.

To rotate one CH₂ group, the p orbitals would have to turn out of alignment and the π bond would break, which costs a lot of energy. So rotation is restricted.

</details>

## Case-based question

Hydrogen bonds decide many properties of water and DNA. The boiling points (°C) of some hydrides are: CH₄ −161, SiH₄ −112, NH₃ −33, PH₃ −88, H₂O 100, H₂S −60, HF 20, HCl −85.

14. (a) Why does the boiling point rise from CH₄ to SiH₄? (b) Why are NH₃, H₂O and HF out of line? (c) Which of H₂O or HF forms more hydrogen bonds per molecule in the liquid, and why? (d) What would you expect the boiling point of H₂O to be if there were no hydrogen bonding?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) SiH₄ is larger with more electrons, so the weak London forces between molecules are stronger.

(b) N, O and F are small and highly electronegative, so their hydrides form hydrogen bonds, which need extra energy to break.

(c) H₂O: each molecule has two H atoms and two lone pairs, so it can form up to four hydrogen bonds. HF has one H and three lone pairs but can use only one H for donating, so it forms about two per molecule, in chains.

(d) Extrapolating the trend of H₂S, H₂Se and H₂Te gives about −80 °C.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

Options: (a) both true and R explains A; (b) both true but R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

15. **A:** BF₃ is a non-polar molecule although the B–F bond is polar. **R:** BF₃ is trigonal planar so the bond dipoles cancel.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). Three equal dipoles at 120° add to zero.</details>

16. **A:** The bond length in O₂⁺ is shorter than in O₂. **R:** O₂⁺ has a higher bond order than O₂.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). Bond order is 2.5 for O₂⁺ and 2 for O₂; a higher bond order means a shorter, stronger bond.</details>

17. **A:** The central atom of PCl₅ obeys the octet rule. **R:** Phosphorus has empty d orbitals.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(d). A is false (P has 10 electrons, an expanded octet). R is true and explains why the expansion is possible.</details>
