**Practice · Class 11 Chemistry · Chapter 9.** Read the chapter lesson, [Hydrocarbons](/learn/courses/ncert-class-11-chemistry/hydrocarbons/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer. Marks go for equations with conditions, the reasoning, the mechanism arrows and the final answer.

<div class="bigidea">Four ideas solve most questions: alkanes substitute by radicals; alkenes and alkynes add by electrophiles (Markovnikov); benzene substitutes by electrophiles; and a group on the ring decides whether the next group goes ortho–para or meta.</div>

## How to set out an answer

1. Write the balanced equation and the conditions (catalyst, temperature, light).
2. For an addition, identify the more stable intermediate (carbocation or radical).
3. For a ring, say whether the group on it donates (o/p) or withdraws (m).
4. For a number, write the formula, substitute with units and give the answer with a unit.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the alkane formed by the Wurtz reaction of methyl bromide.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Ethane (2CH₃Br + 2Na → CH₃CH₃ + 2NaBr).</details>

2. What is the hybridisation of carbon in ethyne?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>sp, with a bond angle of 180°.</details>

3. Which test shows a hydrocarbon is unsaturated?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It decolourises bromine water (or the pink/purple colour of cold dilute alkaline KMnO₄).</details>

4. What is the electrophile in the Friedel–Crafts alkylation of benzene with CH₃Cl and AlCl₃?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The methyl carbocation CH₃⁺ (or the polarised CH₃–Cl···AlCl₃ complex).</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

5. (2) Why does the boiling point of an alkane fall when the chain is branched? Compare n-pentane with neopentane.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A branched molecule is more compact, so it has less surface area in contact with its neighbours and weaker London forces. n-Pentane boils at 309 K, neopentane at about 283 K.</details>

6. (2) State Markovnikov’s rule and apply it to HCl and 2-methylpropene.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>In the addition of HX to an unsymmetrical alkene, the negative part (X) goes to the carbon that bears fewer hydrogens, so the more stable carbocation forms. Here H adds to the CH₂ carbon, giving the tertiary cation (CH₃)₃C⁺, and the product is 2-chloro-2-methylpropane.</details>

7. (2) How would you distinguish between ethane, ethene and ethyne?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Ethane does nothing with bromine water or KMnO₄. Ethene and ethyne both decolourise bromine water and Baeyer’s reagent. Only ethyne gives a white precipitate with ammoniacal silver nitrate.</details>

8. (3) Write the equations for the free-radical chlorination of methane: initiation, two propagation steps and one termination step.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Initiation: `$\text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{h\nu} 2\text{Cl}\cdot$`.

Propagation: `$\text{Cl}\cdot + \text{CH}_4 \to \text{HCl} + \cdot\text{CH}_3$`; `$\cdot\text{CH}_3 + \text{Cl}_2 \to \text{CH}_3\text{Cl} + \text{Cl}\cdot$`.

Termination: `$\cdot\text{CH}_3 + \cdot\text{CH}_3 \to \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_3$` (or Cl· + Cl· → Cl₂).

</details>

9. (3) Convert (a) ethyne to benzene, (b) ethene to ethane-1,2-diol, (c) ethyne to ethanal. Give the reagents.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Pass ethyne through a red-hot iron tube at 873 K (cyclic polymerisation, 3 C₂H₂ → C₆H₆). (b) Cold, dilute, alkaline KMnO₄ (Baeyer’s reagent). (c) Dilute H₂SO₄ with HgSO₄ at 333 K (hydration via the enol).</details>

10. (3) Calculate the volume of CO₂ at STP formed by burning 5.8 g of butane, C₄H₁₀ (C = 12, H = 1).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Moles of butane = 5.8 ÷ 58 = 0.10 mol. `$\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10} + 6.5\,\text{O}_2 \to 4\text{CO}_2 + 5\text{H}_2\text{O}$`, so moles of CO₂ = 4 × 0.10 = 0.40 mol. Volume = 0.40 × 22.4 = **8.96 L**.

</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

11. (5) Explain the mechanism of electrophilic addition of HBr to propene. Why is 2-bromopropane the main product, and what happens when a peroxide is present?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Step 1: the π electrons of propene attack the δ+ hydrogen of HBr: `$\text{CH}_3\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{H}^+ \to \text{CH}_3\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{HCH}_3$` (secondary carbocation) or `$\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2$` (primary).

The secondary cation is more stable (more +I effect and hyperconjugation: 6 α C–H against 3), so it forms faster.

Step 2: Br⁻ attacks the carbocation to give CH₃CHBrCH₃, 2-bromopropane (Markovnikov product).

With a peroxide the reaction is a free-radical chain: Br· adds first to the CH₂ end to give the more stable secondary radical, which takes an H from HBr. The product is 1-bromopropane (anti-Markovnikov; Kharasch effect). It works only for HBr.

</details>

12. (5) What is aromaticity? Apply the Hückel rule to benzene, cyclobutadiene, cyclooctatetraene and the cyclopentadienyl anion.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

A species is aromatic if it is cyclic, planar, fully conjugated and has (4n + 2) π electrons.

Benzene: 6 π electrons, n = 1: aromatic. Cyclobutadiene: 4 π electrons (4n, n = 1): antiaromatic, unstable. Cyclooctatetraene: 8 π electrons but tub-shaped, so not planar: non-aromatic. Cyclopentadienyl anion: planar, all five carbons sp² with 6 π electrons (4 from two double bonds, 2 from the carbanion pair): aromatic.

</details>

13. (5) Write the equations and conditions for the nitration, halogenation, sulphonation and Friedel–Crafts alkylation of benzene, and outline the mechanism of nitration.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Nitration: `$\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{HNO}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4,\ 330\text{ K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$`.

Halogenation: `$\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{FeCl}_3} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + \text{HCl}$`.

Sulphonation: `$\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4\ (\text{fuming}) \to \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_3\text{H} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$`.

Friedel–Crafts: `$\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{CH}_3\text{Cl} \xrightarrow{\text{AlCl}_3} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_3 + \text{HCl}$`.

Mechanism of nitration: (1) HNO₃ + 2H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + H₃O⁺ + 2HSO₄⁻. (2) The ring’s π cloud attacks NO₂⁺ to give the arenium ion. (3) The arenium ion loses H⁺ and the ring becomes aromatic again, giving nitrobenzene.

</details>

## Case-based question

14. **From the gas stove to the plastic bag.** A household LPG cylinder contains mainly butane. A shopkeeper says the plastic bag he gave you is “polythene, made from ethene”. A student then notices that bromine water added to a sample of the bag’s monomer gas loses its orange colour at once, but nothing happens when butane is bubbled through it.

(a) (1) Write the equation for the complete combustion of butane.

(b) (1) Why does butane not react with bromine water?

(c) (1) Write the reaction that forms polythene from ethene.

(d) (1) What type of reaction is the decolourisation of bromine water by ethene?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) `$2\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10} + 13\text{O}_2 \to 8\text{CO}_2 + 10\text{H}_2\text{O}$`.

(b) Butane is saturated: it has only σ bonds, so there is no π bond for bromine to add to.

(c) `$n\,\text{CH}_2{=}\text{CH}_2 \to (-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2-)_n$` (addition polymerisation).

(d) Electrophilic addition, giving 1,2-dibromoethane.

</details>

## Assertion-reason questions

Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

15. **A:** Benzene undergoes electrophilic substitution reactions easily. **R:** Benzene has a delocalised cloud of 6 π electrons.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A: the π cloud is electron-rich and attacks electrophiles, while substitution (not addition) preserves the ring’s stability.</details>

16. **A:** Nitrobenzene is nitrated at the meta position. **R:** The –NO₂ group is an electron-donating group.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(C) A is true; R is false: –NO₂ is strongly electron-withdrawing, which is why it deactivates the ring and directs meta.</details>

17. **A:** The eclipsed conformation of ethane is less stable than the staggered form. **R:** There is greater repulsion between C–H bond pairs in the eclipsed form.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A: eclipsed ethane is about 12 kJ/mol higher in energy because of torsional strain.</details>
