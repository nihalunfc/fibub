## How to set out answers

1. Name the law or formula first (for example ΔH° = ΣΔH_f°(products) − ΣΔH_f°(reactants)).
2. Substitute with units and keep the signs of heat and work.
3. State the answer with a sign and unit, and say what the sign means (exothermic, spontaneous, and so on).

## 1-mark questions

1. Is a closed system able to exchange matter with its surroundings?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>No. A closed system exchanges only energy (heat and work), not matter.</details>

2. Give the mathematical form of the first law of thermodynamics.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>ΔU = q + w.</details>

3. What is the enthalpy of formation of an element in its standard state?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Zero, by definition.</details>

4. Is the sign of ΔS positive or negative for the evaporation of water?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Positive: a liquid turns into a gas with far more freedom.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

5. State Hess's law. Why is it true?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The total enthalpy change of a reaction is the same whether it takes place in one step or in several. It is true because enthalpy is a state function: ΔH depends only on the initial and final states, not on the path.</details>

6. Why is ΔH different from ΔU for the reaction N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) → 2NH₃(g)? Calculate ΔH − ΔU at 298 K.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ΔH = ΔU + Δn_g RT, and the moles of gas change: Δn_g = 2 − 4 = −2.

ΔH − ΔU = −2 × 8.314 × 298 = −4955 J ≈ −4.96 kJ. So ΔH is 4.96 kJ more negative than ΔU: the gas shrinks and the surroundings do work on the system.

</details>

7. Two moles of an ideal gas expand isothermally and reversibly from 5 L to 20 L at 300 K. Calculate w and q. (R = 8.314 J/(K mol), log 4 = 0.602)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

w = −2.303 nRT log(V₂/V₁) = −2.303 × 2 × 8.314 × 300 × 0.602 = −6916 J ≈ −6.9 kJ.

For an ideal gas at constant temperature ΔU = 0, so q = −w = +6.9 kJ. The gas absorbs 6.9 kJ of heat and does 6.9 kJ of work.

</details>

8. Explain with ΔH and ΔS why ice melts spontaneously at 5 °C but not at −5 °C.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Melting has ΔH = +6.0 kJ/mol (heat absorbed) and ΔS = +22 J/(K mol) (more disorder). ΔG = ΔH − TΔS. At 278 K: 6010 − 278 × 22 = −106 J (negative, spontaneous). At 268 K: 6010 − 268 × 22 = +114 J (positive, not spontaneous). The switch is at T = ΔH/ΔS = 273 K.

</details>

9. Calculate ΔH° for C₂H₄(g) + H₂(g) → C₂H₆(g), given ΔH_f°: C₂H₄ = +52.3, C₂H₆ = −84.7 kJ/mol.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>ΔH° = (−84.7) − (+52.3 + 0) = −137.0 kJ. Exothermic.</details>

10. Predict the sign of ΔS for (a) 2SO₂ + O₂ → 2SO₃, (b) dissolving sugar in water.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Negative: three gas moles become two. (b) Positive: the crystal's ordered molecules spread through the water.</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

11. (a) Define enthalpy and show that ΔH = ΔU + Δn_g RT. (b) When does q_p equal q_v?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) H = U + pV. At constant pressure ΔH = ΔU + pΔV. For ideal gases pV = nRT, so at constant T and p, pΔV = Δn_g RT, giving ΔH = ΔU + Δn_g RT. Here Δn_g is the gas moles of products minus gas moles of reactants.

(b) q_p = ΔH and q_v = ΔU, so they are equal when Δn_g = 0 (no change in gas moles) or when only solids and liquids take part.

</details>

12. From the data, find the enthalpy of formation of CO(g): C(graphite) + O₂ → CO₂, ΔH = −393.5 kJ; CO(g) + ½O₂ → CO₂, ΔH = −283.0 kJ. Explain why it cannot be measured directly.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Target: C + ½O₂ → CO.

Add equation 1 to the reverse of equation 2: C + O₂ → CO₂ (−393.5) and CO₂ → CO + ½O₂ (+283.0). The CO₂ cancels, giving C + ½O₂ → CO with ΔH = −393.5 + 283.0 = **−110.5 kJ/mol**.

It cannot be measured directly because burning carbon in a limited supply of oxygen always gives a mixture of CO and CO₂, so the heat measured is not for one clean reaction.

</details>

13. (a) State the criterion for spontaneity in terms of ΔG. (b) A reaction has ΔH = −92 kJ and ΔS = −199 J/K. Find the temperature below which it is spontaneous. (c) Which of the two terms wins at high temperature?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) ΔG = ΔH − TΔS < 0.

(b) ΔG = 0 at T = ΔH/ΔS = (−92 000)/(−199) = 462 K. Below 462 K the −ΔH term wins, so ΔG is negative.

(c) At high temperature the TΔS term is large. Since ΔS is negative, −TΔS is positive and ΔG becomes positive: the reaction is not spontaneous above 462 K.

</details>

## Case-based question

A company makes quicklime (CaO) by heating limestone in a kiln: CaCO₃(s) → CaO(s) + CO₂(g). Data at 298 K: ΔH° = +178 kJ/mol, ΔS° = +161 J/(K mol).

14. (a) Is the reaction endothermic or exothermic? (b) Why is ΔS positive? (c) Calculate ΔG° at 298 K. (d) At what minimum temperature does the decomposition become spontaneous?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Endothermic, because ΔH is positive.

(b) A gas (CO₂) is produced from solids, so there is more freedom and more disorder.

(c) ΔG° = 178 − 298 × 0.161 = 178 − 48.0 = +130 kJ/mol. It is not spontaneous at room temperature, which is why limestone stays as limestone.

(d) T = ΔH/ΔS = 178 000/161 = 1105 K (about 830 °C). Kilns run above this.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

Options: (a) both true and R explains A; (b) both true but R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

15. **A:** The enthalpy of formation of O₂(g) is zero. **R:** Enthalpy of formation is defined for the formation of a compound from its elements in their standard states.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). O₂ is itself an element in its standard state, so nothing is formed and ΔH_f° is zero by definition.</details>

16. **A:** The entropy of a gas increases when it expands into a vacuum. **R:** The gas does work during free expansion.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(c). A is true: the gas has more volume to spread into, so more arrangements. R is false: p_ext = 0, so w = 0.</details>

17. **A:** Heat and work are not state functions. **R:** Their values depend on the route taken between the same two states.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). Heat and work are path functions: different routes between the same states give different q and w, even though ΔU = q + w is the same.</details>
