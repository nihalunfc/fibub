**Practice · Class 11 Chemistry · Chapter 3.** Read the chapter lesson, [Classification of Elements and Periodicity in Properties](/learn/courses/ncert-class-11-chemistry/classification-of-elements-and-periodicity-in/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">For every trend question ask two things: <b>how many protons pull</b> (nuclear charge) and <b>how far away</b> is the electron (the number of shells). Across a period the pull grows and the shell stays the same; down a group the shell count grows. Almost every answer follows.</div>

## How to answer a trend question

1. Write the configurations or positions of the elements (period and group).
2. Say which factor dominates: nuclear charge (same period), number of shells (same group) or number of electrons for the same Z (isoelectronic).
3. Give the order with a one-line reason and mention any exception (for example half-filled or full subshell).

## 1-mark questions

1. State the modern periodic law.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers.</details>

2. Which block does the element with configuration [Ne] 3s² 3p³ belong to?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The p-block (it is phosphorus, group 15, period 3).</details>

3. Name two elements that Mendeleev predicted.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Eka-aluminium (gallium) and eka-silicon (germanium); eka-boron (scandium) is a third.</details>

4. Which element has the highest electronegativity?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Fluorine (3.98 on the Pauling scale).</details>

5. Write the systematic name of the element with Z = 115.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Ununpentium (symbol Uup); today named moscovium (Mc).</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

6. Why was ordering by atomic mass replaced by ordering by atomic number in the modern table?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Some pairs, like Ar–K and Te–I, would be in the wrong group if arranged by mass. Moseley showed that atomic number is the fundamental property, and ordering by Z puts every element in its correct group without exceptions.</details>

7. Explain why the radius of a cation is smaller than that of its atom and that of an anion is larger.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A cation has lost electrons (often a whole outer shell) while the nuclear charge is unchanged, so the remaining electrons are pulled in more strongly. An anion has more electrons repelling each other but the same nuclear charge, so the cloud expands.</details>

8. Arrange N³⁻, O²⁻, F⁻, Na⁺ and Mg²⁺ in order of increasing radius and explain.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Mg²⁺ < Na⁺ < F⁻ < O²⁻ < N³⁻. All have 10 electrons; the nuclear charge (12, 11, 9, 8, 7) falls from left to right, so the pull on the same 10 electrons falls and the radius rises.</details>

9. The first four ionisation enthalpies of an element are 578, 1817, 2745 and 11577 kJ/mol. Find its group and justify.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The big jump comes after the third value (2745 to 11577), so there are 3 valence electrons: it is in group 13 (aluminium).</details>

10. Why is the electron gain enthalpy of chlorine more negative than that of fluorine?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Fluorine’s 2p subshell is very small, so electrons already there repel the incoming electron strongly. Chlorine’s larger 3p subshell has more room, so the extra electron is accommodated with a greater release of energy (−349 against −328 kJ/mol).</details>

11. Why is the first ionisation enthalpy of nitrogen greater than that of oxygen?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Nitrogen (2p³) has a half-filled, stable subshell with three unpaired electrons. Oxygen (2p⁴) has one paired p electron, and the repulsion from its partner makes it easier to remove, even though oxygen has a higher nuclear charge.</details>

12. What is meant by an amphoteric oxide? Give an example with equations.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

One that reacts with both acids and bases to form salts and water. Al₂O₃ + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂O and Al₂O₃ + 2NaOH + 3H₂O → 2Na[Al(OH)₄].

</details>

## 5-mark questions

13. Describe how and why the atomic radius, first ionisation enthalpy and electronegativity change (a) across period 3 and (b) down group 1.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Across period 3 the nuclear charge rises from +11 to +17 while electrons enter the same shell and shield poorly. Z_eff increases, so the radius falls (Na 166 pm to Cl 102 pm), while ionisation enthalpy (496 to 1251 kJ/mol, with small dips at Al and S) and electronegativity (0.93 to 3.16) rise. (b) Down group 1 each element has an extra shell. The outer electron is farther away and better shielded, so the radius rises (Li 128 to Cs 244 pm), while ionisation enthalpy (520 to 376 kJ/mol) and electronegativity (0.98 to 0.79) fall.</details>

14. Write the electronic configurations and say the period, group and block of elements with Z = 12, 17, 26 and 38.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Z = 12 (Mg): [Ne] 3s², period 3, group 2, s-block.

Z = 17 (Cl): [Ne] 3s² 3p⁵, period 3, group 17, p-block.

Z = 26 (Fe): [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s², period 4, group 8, d-block.

Z = 38 (Sr): [Kr] 5s², period 5, group 2, s-block.

</details>

15. Explain the variation of the nature of oxides across period 3 with the reactions of Na₂O, Al₂O₃ and SO₃.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Metal oxides are basic and ionic, non-metal oxides acidic and covalent, with an amphoteric oxide in between. Na₂O + H₂O → 2NaOH (strongly basic). Al₂O₃ reacts with acids and bases (amphoteric). SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄ (strongly acidic). The cause is the fall in metallic character and the rise in electronegativity of the element across the period.</details>

16. Calculate the energy required to remove one electron from every atom in 3.9 g of potassium (39 g/mol, ΔᵢH₁ = 419 kJ/mol) and say how much would be needed to remove a second electron from the same atoms if ΔᵢH₂ = 3052 kJ/mol. Why is it so much greater?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Moles = 3.9/39 = 0.10 mol. First electrons: 0.10 × 419 = 41.9 kJ. Second electrons: 0.10 × 3052 = 305 kJ. The second electron comes from an inner, completed 3p shell (argon core), closer to the nucleus and not shielded in the same way, so it takes seven times more energy.

</details>

## Case-based question

Arun’s teacher gives him a card with data about three unknown elements X, Y and Z of period 3. X forms a basic oxide that dissolves in water to give a strong alkali; Y has an amphoteric oxide and a first ionisation enthalpy of 578 kJ/mol; Z forms an acidic oxide with water giving a strong acid, and has an electronegativity of 3.16.

17. (a) Identify X, Y and Z. (b) Arrange them in order of atomic radius. (c) Which has the highest electron gain enthalpy (most negative)? (d) Write the formula of Y’s oxide.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) X = sodium (Na₂O + H₂O → 2NaOH); Y = aluminium (amphoteric Al₂O₃, IE₁ 578); Z = chlorine (Cl₂O₇, HClO₄, EN 3.16).

(b) Na > Al > Cl (the radius falls across the period).

(c) Chlorine (−349 kJ/mol).

(d) Al₂O₃.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

18. **A:** The second ionisation enthalpy of an element is always greater than its first. **R:** After one electron is removed, the remaining electrons are held more strongly by the same nuclear charge.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A: the positive ion has a smaller cloud and the same nuclear charge on fewer electrons.</details>

19. **A:** Noble gases have large positive electron gain enthalpies. **R:** They have completely filled outer shells, so an added electron must start a new shell.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A.</details>

20. **A:** Atomic radius increases from left to right in a period. **R:** The nuclear charge increases as the atomic number increases.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(D) A is false (the radius decreases across a period); R is true, and it is the reason the radius decreases, not increases.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Period = highest n; p-block group = 10 + outer electrons; block = last subshell filled.
- Across a period: radius falls; ionisation enthalpy, electronegativity and the (negative) electron gain enthalpy rise. Down a group: the opposite for each.
- Exceptions: Be > B and N > O in ionisation enthalpy; Cl more negative than F in electron gain enthalpy.
- Isoelectronic ions: the greater the nuclear charge, the smaller the ion.
- A huge jump in successive ionisation enthalpies shows the number of valence electrons.
- Oxides: basic (Na₂O) to amphoteric (Al₂O₃) to acidic (Cl₂O₇) across period 3.

</div>
