<div class="bigidea">Nearly every equilibrium question is one of four moves: <b>write K, compare Q with K, build an ICE table, or take a logarithm for pH</b>. Name the move first.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Write the balanced equation and the expression for K (leave out solids and pure liquids).
2. For numerical work, draw the ICE table, then substitute into K.
3. For pH, say whether the species is strong or weak before choosing the formula, and check the final answer is sensible.

## 1-mark questions

1. Write Kc for CaCO₃(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO₂(g).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Kc = [CO₂]. Solids are left out.</details>

2. What is the conjugate base of HSO₄⁻?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>SO₄²⁻ (remove one proton).</details>

3. State the effect of a catalyst on the position of equilibrium.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>None. It speeds up the forward and reverse reactions equally, so equilibrium is reached sooner but at the same position.</details>

4. What is the pH of pure water at 25 °C?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>7.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

5. State Le Chatelier's principle. Predict the effect of adding more N₂ to N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>If a system at equilibrium is disturbed, it shifts to oppose the disturbance. Adding N₂ raises a reactant concentration, so Q < K and the equilibrium shifts forward, using up some N₂ and making more NH₃ (K stays the same).</details>

6. For N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g), Kc = 0.0057 at 300 K. Find Kp at this temperature. (R = 0.0821 L atm K⁻¹ mol⁻¹)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Δn = 2 − 1 = 1. Kp = Kc (RT)^Δn = 0.0057 × 0.0821 × 300 = 0.140 atm.</details>

7. Explain why the pH of a solution of ammonium chloride is below 7.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>NH₄Cl is the salt of a weak base (NH₃) and a strong acid (HCl). Cl⁻ is a spectator, but NH₄⁺ is the conjugate acid of a weak base and reacts with water: NH₄⁺ + H₂O ⇌ NH₃ + H₃O⁺. The extra H₃O⁺ makes the solution acidic.</details>

8. What happens to the percent ionisation of 0.1 M ethanoic acid when it is diluted tenfold? Give the reason.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It increases (by about √10 = 3.2 times). Since α ≈ √(Ka/c), lowering the concentration raises α (Ostwald's dilution law). Dilution shifts the equilibrium toward the side with more particles (the ions).</details>

9. The solubility product of AgCl is 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰. Will a precipitate form when 100 mL of 2 × 10⁻⁵ M AgNO₃ is mixed with 100 mL of 2 × 10⁻⁵ M NaCl?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>After mixing, the volume doubles so each concentration halves: [Ag⁺] = [Cl⁻] = 1 × 10⁻⁵ M. The ionic product = (10⁻⁵)² = 1 × 10⁻¹⁰, which is **less** than Ksp = 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰, so no precipitate forms.</details>

10. A reaction has Q = 0.2 and K = 5. Which way will it go?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Q < K, so there are too few products. The reaction goes in the forward direction until Q = 5.</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

11. 1.0 mol each of H₂ and I₂ are placed in a 1.0 L vessel at a temperature where K = 49 for H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI. Calculate the equilibrium concentrations.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Let x mol/L react. [H₂] = [I₂] = 1 − x and [HI] = 2x.

K = (2x)² / (1 − x)² = 49, so 2x / (1 − x) = 7.

2x = 7 − 7x, so x = 7/9 = 0.778.

[H₂] = [I₂] = 0.222 mol/L and [HI] = 1.556 mol/L. Check: 1.556²/0.222² = 49. ✓

</details>

12. (a) What is a buffer? (b) Calculate the pH of a buffer containing 0.10 M ethanoic acid (pKa = 4.76) and 0.20 M sodium ethanoate. (c) Show how it resists a small addition of HCl.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) A solution that resists changes in pH on adding small amounts of acid or base: a weak acid with its conjugate base (or the reverse).

(b) pH = pKa + log([salt]/[acid]) = 4.76 + log 2 = 4.76 + 0.30 = **5.06**.

(c) Added H⁺ reacts with the ethanoate ion: CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺ → CH₃COOH. The salt falls a little and the acid rises a little, so the ratio hardly changes and the pH barely moves.

</details>

13. For the reaction 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃, ΔH = −198 kJ. (a) Write Kc. (b) State and explain the effect of (i) raising the pressure, (ii) raising the temperature, (iii) adding a catalyst. (c) Which conditions give the best yield?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Kc = [SO₃]² / ([SO₂]²[O₂]).

(b) (i) Pressure: 3 gas moles go to 2, so higher pressure shifts forward and increases the yield. (ii) Temperature: the forward reaction is exothermic, so heating shifts it backward and K falls. (iii) A catalyst does not change the position, it only makes equilibrium arrive sooner.

(c) High pressure and low temperature give the best yield, but low temperature is slow. In practice about 450 °C with a V₂O₅ catalyst and a moderate pressure is used.

</details>

## Case-based question

Hard water leaves a white scale of CaCO₃ in a kettle. The solubility equilibrium is CaCO₃(s) ⇌ Ca²⁺(aq) + CO₃²⁻(aq), with Ksp = 4.5 × 10⁻⁹ at 25 °C. Vinegar (ethanoic acid) dissolves the scale.

14. (a) Write the Ksp expression. (b) Find the solubility of CaCO₃ in pure water. (c) Suggest why the scale is easier to remove with acid. (d) What would happen to the solubility in a solution that already contains Na₂CO₃?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Ksp = [Ca²⁺][CO₃²⁻].

(b) s = √Ksp = √(4.5 × 10⁻⁹) = 6.7 × 10⁻⁵ mol/L.

(c) CO₃²⁻ reacts with H⁺ from the acid to give HCO₃⁻ and then CO₂ + H₂O. This removes CO₃²⁻ from the equilibrium, so (Le Chatelier) more CaCO₃ dissolves until it is all gone.

(d) The solubility falls because of the common ion effect from CO₃²⁻.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

Options: (a) both true and R explains A; (b) both true but R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

15. **A:** Adding an inert gas at constant volume does not change the position of equilibrium. **R:** The partial pressures and concentrations of the reacting gases remain the same.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). With volume fixed, the concentrations do not change, so Q still equals K.</details>

16. **A:** A solution of sodium ethanoate is basic. **R:** The ethanoate ion is the conjugate base of a weak acid and reacts with water to give OH⁻.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). CH₃COO⁻ + H₂O ⇌ CH₃COOH + OH⁻.</details>

17. **A:** The equilibrium constant of an exothermic reaction increases when the temperature is raised. **R:** Heating favours the endothermic direction.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(d). A is false: heating an exothermic reaction shifts it backward and K falls. R is true.</details>
