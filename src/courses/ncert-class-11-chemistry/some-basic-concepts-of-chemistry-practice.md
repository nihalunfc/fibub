## How to set out a numerical

1. Write what is given with units and what is asked.
2. Write the balanced equation if there is a reaction.
3. Convert every mass to moles first.
4. Use the mole ratio (or divide by the coefficient to find the limiting reagent).
5. Convert to the unit asked and round to the right significant figures.

## 1-mark questions

1. State the law of conservation of mass.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>In a chemical reaction the total mass of the reactants equals the total mass of the products; matter is neither created nor destroyed.</details>

2. How many significant figures are in 5.020 × 10⁻³?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Four: 5, 0, 2 and the trailing 0 (it is after the decimal point).</details>

3. Define one mole.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The amount of a substance that contains 6.022 × 10²³ elementary entities (the Avogadro constant), the same as the number of atoms in exactly 12 g of carbon-12.</details>

4. What is the SI unit of amount of substance?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The mole (mol).</details>

5. Is brass an element, compound or mixture?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A homogeneous mixture (a solid solution) of copper and zinc.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

6. Distinguish between precision and accuracy with an example.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Precision is how close repeated measurements are to each other; accuracy is how close a measurement is to the true value. Example: a true length of 2.00 cm measured as 2.31, 2.30, 2.32 cm is precise but not accurate.</details>

7. Two oxides of nitrogen contain 7 g and 14 g of nitrogen per 8 g of oxygen. Which law do they show? Explain.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The law of multiple proportions: with 8 g of oxygen fixed, the masses of nitrogen are 7 : 14 = 1 : 2, a small whole-number ratio.</details>

8. Calculate the mass of 3.011 × 10²³ molecules of CO₂ (44 g/mol).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Moles = 3.011 × 10²³ / 6.022 × 10²³ = 0.5 mol. Mass = 0.5 × 44 = 22 g.

</details>

9. A compound has 85.7 % carbon and 14.3 % hydrogen and a molar mass of 42 g/mol. Find its molecular formula.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Moles in 100 g: C = 85.7/12 = 7.14, H = 14.3/1 = 14.3. Dividing by 7.14: C : H = 1 : 2, so the empirical formula is CH₂ (mass 14). 42/14 = 3, so the molecular formula is C₃H₆.

</details>

10. Calculate the molarity of a solution made by dissolving 4.0 g of NaOH (40 g/mol) in water to make 500 mL.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Moles = 4.0/40 = 0.10 mol; volume = 0.500 L; M = 0.10/0.500 = 0.20 mol/L.

</details>

11. Why is the atomic mass of chlorine 35.45 u and not a whole number?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It is the weighted average of its isotopes ³⁵Cl (about 75.8 %) and ³⁷Cl (about 24.2 %). No single atom weighs 35.45 u.</details>

12. Balance and name the type of reaction: Al + O₂ → Al₂O₃.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃ (check: 4 Al and 6 O on each side). It is a combination (oxidation) reaction.

</details>

## 5-mark questions

13. 5.0 g of calcium carbonate is heated: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂. (a) How many moles of CaCO₃ is this? (b) What mass of CaO forms? (c) What volume of CO₂ at 273.15 K and 1 bar forms? (Ca 40, C 12, O 16; molar volume 22.7 L)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) n = 5.0/100 = 0.050 mol. (b) 1 mol CaCO₃ gives 1 mol CaO, so n(CaO) = 0.050 mol and mass = 0.050 × 56 = 2.8 g. (c) n(CO₂) = 0.050 mol, volume = 0.050 × 22.7 = 1.14 L. Check the mass: 2.8 g + 0.050 × 44 = 2.2 g gives 5.0 g, conserved.

</details>

14. 6.0 g of hydrogen and 6.0 g of nitrogen react: N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃. Find the limiting reagent, the mass of ammonia formed and the mass of the excess reagent left.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

n(H₂) = 6.0/2 = 3.0 mol; n(N₂) = 6.0/28 = 0.214 mol. Divide by the coefficient: H₂ 3.0/3 = 1.0; N₂ 0.214/1 = 0.214. Nitrogen is limiting. NH₃ formed = 2 × 0.214 = 0.429 mol = 7.3 g. H₂ used = 3 × 0.214 = 0.643 mol, left = 3.0 − 0.643 = 2.36 mol = 4.7 g.

</details>

15. Concentrated nitric acid is 70 % HNO₃ by mass and has a density of 1.41 g/mL. Find its molarity, molality and the mole fraction of HNO₃ (HNO₃ = 63 g/mol, H₂O = 18 g/mol).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Take 100 g of solution: 70 g HNO₃ = 1.111 mol; 30 g water = 0.030 kg = 1.667 mol. Volume = 100/1.41 = 70.9 mL = 0.0709 L.

Molarity = 1.111/0.0709 = 15.7 mol/L. Molality = 1.111/0.030 = 37.0 mol/kg. Mole fraction = 1.111/(1.111 + 1.667) = 0.40.

</details>

16. Describe how the laws of chemical combination led to Dalton’s atomic theory, and state any two postulates of the theory that are no longer fully correct.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Conservation of mass suggests atoms are not made or destroyed; constant proportions suggests compounds form from fixed numbers of atoms; multiple proportions suggests whole atoms (never fractions) combine. Dalton explained all three with indivisible atoms joining in simple whole-number ratios. Today we know atoms are divisible (electrons, protons, neutrons), and atoms of the same element can differ in mass (isotopes).</details>

## Case-based question

Ravi, a Class 11 student, studies a fertiliser factory. Urea, CO(NH₂)₂ (60 g/mol), is made from ammonia and carbon dioxide: 2NH₃ + CO₂ → CO(NH₂)₂ + H₂O. The factory mixes 34 kg of NH₃ (17 g/mol) with 44 kg of CO₂ (44 g/mol) and collects 48 kg of urea.

17. (a) How many moles of NH₃ and of CO₂ are used? (b) Which is limiting? (c) What is the theoretical yield of urea in kg? (d) Find the percentage yield.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) n(NH₃) = 34 000/17 = 2000 mol; n(CO₂) = 44 000/44 = 1000 mol.

(b) Divide by the coefficients: NH₃ 2000/2 = 1000; CO₂ 1000/1 = 1000. They are in exactly the stoichiometric ratio, so neither is in excess.

(c) 1000 mol of urea = 1000 × 60 = 60 000 g = 60 kg.

(d) Percentage yield = 48/60 × 100 = 80 %.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

18. **A:** 1 mol of O₂ and 1 mol of O₃ contain the same number of molecules. **R:** One mole of any substance contains 6.022 × 10²³ elementary entities.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A: a mole counts entities, whatever their size or mass. The two samples have different masses (32 g and 48 g) and different numbers of atoms, but the same number of molecules.</details>

19. **A:** Molality of a solution does not change with temperature. **R:** Molality is the moles of solute per litre of solution.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(C) A is true; R is false. Molality is moles per kilogram of <i>solvent</i>, and masses do not change on heating. Molarity (per litre of solution) is the one that changes.</details>

20. **A:** The reactant present in the smaller mass is always the limiting reagent. **R:** Limiting reagent is decided by moles divided by the coefficient.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(D) A is false, R is true. Mass depends on molar mass as well; only moles divided by the coefficient tell which reactant runs out first.</details>
