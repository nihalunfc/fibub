**Practice · Class 10 Maths · Chapter 6.** Read the chapter lesson, [Triangles](/learn/courses/ncert-class-10-maths/triangles/), first. Try each question on paper before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Two tools solve nearly every question here: the basic proportionality theorem `$\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}$` when a line is parallel to a side, and the equal ratio of corresponding sides when two triangles are similar. Draw a clear figure, mark equal angles, and write the similarity in the right order.</div>

## How to set out an answer

1. Draw the figure and mark what is given (parallel lines, equal angles, lengths).
2. State the reason in words: “DE ∥ BC, so by the basic proportionality theorem” or “by AA, ABC ∼ PQR”.
3. Write corresponding sides in the same order, then substitute and solve.
4. Write the answer with its unit.

## 1-mark questions

1. State the converse of the basic proportionality theorem.
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

If a line divides two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, then it is parallel to the third side.

</details>

2. Are any two squares similar? Give a reason.
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Yes. All angles are 90°, and the sides of each square are equal, so the ratio of corresponding sides is the same for all four.

</details>

3. If `$\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$` and `$\angle A = 50^\circ$`, `$\angle E = 60^\circ$`, find `$\angle C$`.
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

`$\angle D = 50^\circ$` and `$\angle B = \angle E = 60^\circ$`, so `$\angle C = 180^\circ - 110^\circ = 70^\circ$`.

</details>

4. In `$\triangle ABC$`, `$DE \parallel BC$`, `$AD = 2$` cm, `$AB = 6$` cm and `$AE = 3$` cm. Find `$AC$`.
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

`$AE/AC = AD/AB$`, so `$3/AC = 2/6$` and `$AC = 9$` cm.

</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. In `$\triangle ABC$`, `$DE \parallel BC$` with `$AD = 4x - 3$`, `$DB = 3x - 1$`, `$AE = 8x - 7$` and `$EC = 5x - 3$`. Find `$x$`. *(3 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

`$\dfrac{4x - 3}{3x - 1} = \dfrac{8x - 7}{5x - 3}$`. Cross multiply: `$(4x-3)(5x-3) = (8x-7)(3x-1)$`.

`$20x^2 - 27x + 9 = 24x^2 - 29x + 7$`, so `$0 = 4x^2 - 2x - 2$`, i.e. `$2x^2 - x - 1 = 0$`, `$(2x + 1)(x - 1) = 0$`.

`$x = 1$` or `$x = -\tfrac12$`. For `$x = -\tfrac12$` the lengths `$4x - 3 = -5$` are negative, so reject it. `$x = 1$` gives AD = 1, DB = 2, AE = 1, EC = 2: valid.

</details>

6. A 1.6 m tall boy casts a shadow of 2.4 m. At the same time a flagpole casts a shadow of 18 m. How tall is the pole? *(2 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The sun’s rays make equal angles, so the two right triangles are similar (AA): `$\dfrac{h}{18} = \dfrac{1.6}{2.4}$`, `$h = 18 \times \dfrac23 = 12$` m.

</details>

7. Two triangles have sides 6 cm, 8 cm, 12 cm and 9 cm, 12 cm, 18 cm. Are they similar? Name the test. *(2 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

`$6/9 = 8/12 = 12/18 = 2/3$`. All three ratios are equal, so they are similar by SSS.

</details>

8. In `$\triangle ABC$`, D is on AB and E is on AC with `$\angle ADE = \angle ABC$`. Prove `$\triangle ADE \sim \triangle ABC$`. *(3 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

In triangles ADE and ABC: `$\angle A$` is common, and `$\angle ADE = \angle ABC$` (given). By AA, `$\triangle ADE \sim \triangle ABC$`. (It also follows that DE ∥ BC, because the corresponding angles ADE and ABC are equal.)

</details>

9. The diagonals of a trapezium ABCD with AB ∥ DC meet at O. Show that `$\dfrac{OA}{OC} = \dfrac{OB}{OD}$`. *(3 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

In triangles OAB and OCD: `$\angle AOB = \angle COD$` (vertically opposite angles) and `$\angle OAB = \angle OCD$` (alternate angles, since AB ∥ DC). By AA, `$\triangle OAB \sim \triangle OCD$`, so `$\dfrac{OA}{OC} = \dfrac{OB}{OD}$`.

</details>

## 5-mark questions (with model answers)

10. Prove the basic proportionality theorem: if a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, it divides them in the same ratio. *(5 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Given: in `$\triangle ABC$`, DE ∥ BC with D on AB and E on AC. To prove: `$\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}$`.

Construction: join BE and CD, and draw `$EM \perp AB$` and `$DN \perp AC$`.

`$\dfrac{ar(ADE)}{ar(BDE)} = \dfrac{\tfrac12 \times AD \times EM}{\tfrac12 \times DB \times EM} = \dfrac{AD}{DB}$` … (1)

`$\dfrac{ar(ADE)}{ar(CDE)} = \dfrac{\tfrac12 \times AE \times DN}{\tfrac12 \times EC \times DN} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}$` … (2)

Triangles BDE and CDE have the same base DE and lie between the same parallels DE and BC, so `$ar(BDE) = ar(CDE)$` … (3)

From (1), (2), (3): `$\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}$`. Hence proved.

</details>

11. In `$\triangle ABC$`, D is a point on AB with `$AD : DB = 3 : 2$`. A line through D parallel to BC meets AC at E. The line through E parallel to AB meets BC at F. Find the ratio `$BF : FC$`. *(5 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

DE ∥ BC, so `$\dfrac{AE}{EC} = \dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac32$`.

In `$\triangle ABC$`, EF ∥ AB (F on BC, E on AC). By the theorem, `$\dfrac{CE}{EA} = \dfrac{CF}{FB}$`. So `$\dfrac{CF}{FB} = \dfrac23$`, that is, `$BF : FC = 3 : 2$`.

</details>

12. A tower casts a shadow 28 m long on level ground. At the same time a 2 m vertical rod casts a shadow of 1.6 m. Find the height of the tower. If the angle of the sun at that time is `$\theta$`, find `$\tan\theta$`. *(5 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The rod and tower with their shadows form similar right triangles (same sun angle, both vertical, AA). `$\dfrac{H}{28} = \dfrac{2}{1.6}$`, so `$H = 28 \times 1.25 = 35$` m.

`$\tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{height}}{\text{shadow}} = \dfrac{2}{1.6} = 1.25$` (the same for both, which is exactly why they are similar).

</details>

## Case-based question

**The map of a plot.** A farmer in Wayanad draws the triangular plot ABC on paper. A straight irrigation channel DE runs across it from D on side AB to E on side AC. On the plot, AD = 60 m, DB = 40 m and AE = 90 m. The surveyor says that EC = 60 m.

(a) Is DE parallel to BC? Give a reason.
(b) The side BC is 150 m. Find DE if it is parallel to BC.
(c) The farmer draws a map in which the side BC is 15 cm. What is the scale of the map?
(d) Where would E have to be (find EC) if the channel were exactly parallel to BC and AD, DB stayed the same, with AE = 90?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

(a) `$AD/DB = 60/40 = 1.5$` and `$AE/EC = 90/60 = 1.5$`. The ratios are equal, so by the converse of the basic proportionality theorem DE ∥ BC.

(b) Triangles ADE and ABC are similar (DE ∥ BC), so `$\dfrac{DE}{BC} = \dfrac{AD}{AB} = \dfrac{60}{100}$`, giving `$DE = 0.6 \times 150 = 90$` m.

(c) 150 m on the ground is 15 cm on the map, so 15 cm : 15 000 cm = 1 : 1000.

(d) For DE parallel to BC we need `$AE/EC = AD/DB = 1.5$`, so `$EC = 90/1.5 = 60$` m. That matches the surveyor’s figure, so the channel really is parallel.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

13. **Assertion:** Any two rhombuses are similar. **Reason:** The sides of a rhombus are all equal.
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

(D). The reason is true but the assertion is false: two rhombuses can have different angles (a thin one and a nearly square one), so they are not similar.

</details>

14. **Assertion:** In `$\triangle ABC$`, if `$AD/DB = AE/EC$` then `$DE \parallel BC$`. **Reason:** This is the converse of the basic proportionality theorem.
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

(A). Both are true, and the converse is exactly the statement in A.

</details>

15. **Assertion:** Two triangles with sides 3, 4, 5 and 6, 8, 11 are similar. **Reason:** Two triangles are similar if their sides are in proportion.
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

(D). `$3/6 = 4/8 = 1/2$` but `$5/11 \neq 1/2$`, so the sides are not all in proportion and A is false. R is a true statement of SSS similarity.

</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Parallel line in a triangle: `$\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}$`; equal ratios prove the line is parallel.
- Similar triangles: AA, SSS (three ratios) or SAS (equal included angle and two proportional sides).
- Write the similarity in order so that the pairs of corresponding sides are easy to read off.
- To prove similarity of two triangles in a figure, look for common angles, vertically opposite angles and alternate angles from parallel lines.
- Shadow and height problems use similar right triangles made by the sun.
- Check algebra answers against the story: lengths cannot be negative.

</div>
