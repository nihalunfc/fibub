**Practice · Class 10 Maths · Chapter 10.** Read the chapter lesson, [Circles](/learn/courses/ncert-class-10-maths/circles/), first. Try each question on paper before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Every tangent question starts the same way: join the centre to each point of contact and mark the right angles. After that you only need Pythagoras, the angle sum of a triangle or quadrilateral, and "tangents from a point are equal".</div>

## How to set out an answer

1. Draw a neat figure. Mark the centre O, every point of contact and every given length.
2. Write each fact with its reason in brackets: "∠OAP = 90° (tangent ⟂ radius)", "PA = PB (tangents from P)".
3. Use the facts step by step (Pythagoras, angle sum, congruence).
4. Write the answer with its unit, and check that it makes sense.

## 1-mark questions

1. How many tangents can be drawn to a circle from a point on the circle?
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Exactly one: the tangent at that point.

</details>

2. A tangent PQ at P to a circle of centre O makes what angle with OP?
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

`$90^\circ$`, since the tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.

</details>

3. From a point 25 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 7 cm, a tangent is drawn. Find its length.
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

`$PT = \sqrt{25^2 - 7^2} = \sqrt{576} = 24$` cm.

</details>

4. Can a tangent to a circle pass through its centre? Give a reason.
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

No. A line through the centre meets the circle at two points (the ends of a diameter), so it is a secant, not a tangent.

</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. PA and PB are tangents from P to a circle with centre O and `$\angle APB = 64^\circ$`. Find `$\angle AOB$`. *(2 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

`$\angle OAP = \angle OBP = 90^\circ$` (tangent ⟂ radius). In quadrilateral OAPB the angles add up to `$360^\circ$`, so `$\angle AOB = 360^\circ - 90^\circ - 90^\circ - 64^\circ = 116^\circ$`.

</details>

6. Two concentric circles have radii 10 cm and 6 cm. Find the length of the chord of the larger circle that touches the smaller one. *(3 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Let the chord touch the smaller circle at M. Then OM ⟂ chord (tangent ⟂ radius), so M is the midpoint of the chord. Half the chord `$= \sqrt{10^2 - 6^2} = 8$`. The chord is **16 cm**.

</details>

7. Prove that the tangents drawn at the ends of a diameter of a circle are parallel. *(2 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Let AB be a diameter. The tangent at A is perpendicular to the radius OA, which lies along AB. The tangent at B is also perpendicular to OB, along AB. Two lines perpendicular to the same line AB are parallel to each other.

</details>

8. A circle touches the sides of triangle ABC at D (on BC), E (on CA) and F (on AB). If AB = 10 cm, BC = 12 cm and CA = 8 cm, find AF, BD and CE. *(3 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Let AF = AE = x, BF = BD = y, CD = CE = z (equal tangents). Then `$x + y = 10$`, `$y + z = 12$`, `$z + x = 8$`. Adding: `$x + y + z = 15$`.

So `$x = 15 - 12 = 3$`, `$y = 15 - 8 = 7$`, `$z = 15 - 10 = 5$`. Hence **AF = 3 cm, BD = 7 cm, CE = 5 cm**. Check: AB = 3 + 7 = 10.

</details>

9. A quadrilateral ABCD is drawn round a circle. Prove that `$AB + CD = BC + DA$`. *(3 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Let the circle touch AB, BC, CD, DA at P, Q, R, S. Tangents from a point are equal: AP = AS, BP = BQ, CQ = CR, DR = DS.

Add: `$AB + CD = (AP + BP) + (CR + DR) = (AS + BQ) + (CQ + DS) = (BQ + CQ) + (DS + AS) = BC + DA$`.

</details>

10. Tangents PA and PB from an outside point P make `$\angle APB = 80^\circ$`. Find `$\angle PAB$`. *(3 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

PA = PB (equal tangents), so triangle PAB is isosceles. The base angles are equal: `$\angle PAB = \angle PBA = \tfrac12(180^\circ - 80^\circ) = 50^\circ$`.

</details>

## 5-mark questions (with model answers)

11. **Prove that the tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.** *(5 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Let a tangent XY touch the circle (centre O) at P. Take any other point Q on XY and join OQ.

Since the tangent touches the circle at P only, Q lies outside the circle, so `$OQ > OP$`. This is true for every point Q of XY other than P.

So OP is shorter than every other line from O to XY: OP is the shortest distance from O to the line XY. The shortest distance from a point to a line is the perpendicular. Hence `$OP \perp XY$`. **Proved.**

</details>

12. **Prove that the lengths of the two tangents from an external point to a circle are equal.** *(5 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Let PA and PB be tangents from P to a circle with centre O. Join OA, OB, OP.

`$\angle OAP = \angle OBP = 90^\circ$` (tangent ⟂ radius). `$OA = OB$` (radii). `$OP = OP$` (common).

So triangle OAP is congruent to triangle OBP (RHS). Therefore `$PA = PB$` (corresponding sides). **Proved.**

</details>

13. **From a point P, 13 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 5 cm, two tangents PA and PB are drawn. Find the length of the tangent, the length of AB, and the area of quadrilateral OAPB.** *(5 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

(a) `$PA = \sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = 12$` cm.

(b) OP is the axis of symmetry and meets AB at its midpoint M at right angles. In right triangle OAP, the area is `$\tfrac12 \times 5 \times 12 = 30$`, which also equals `$\tfrac12 \times OP \times AM = \tfrac12 \times 13 \times AM$`. So `$AM = 60/13$` and `$AB = 2 \times 60/13 = 120/13 \approx 9.23$` cm.

(c) Quadrilateral OAPB is made of two congruent triangles OAP and OBP: area `$= 2 \times 30 = 60$` cm².

</details>

## Case-based question

**The pulley.** A rope is stretched from a point P outside a round pulley of radius 6 cm and wound over it. The rope leaves the pulley along two straight pieces PA and PB, each touching the pulley at A and B. The centre of the pulley is O and OP = 10 cm.

(a) What is the angle between the rope PA and the radius OA?
(b) Find the length of rope PA.
(c) Find `$\angle APB$` approximately, given that `$\sin \angle APO = 0.6$` (which means `$\angle APO \approx 37^\circ$`).
(d) Find the total length of the two straight pieces of rope.
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

(a) The rope touches the pulley, so it is a tangent: the angle with OA is **90°**.

(b) `$PA = \sqrt{10^2 - 6^2} = 8$` cm.

(c) OP bisects `$\angle APB$`, so `$\angle APB = 2 \times 37^\circ = 74^\circ$`. (Here `$\sin \angle APO = OA/OP = 6/10 = 0.6$`.)

(d) PB = PA = 8 cm (equal tangents), so the two pieces total **16 cm**.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

14. **Assertion:** From a point 5 cm away from the centre of a circle of radius 3 cm, two tangents of length 4 cm can be drawn. **Reason:** The length of a tangent from a point at distance d is `$\sqrt{d^2 - r^2}$`.
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

(A). `$\sqrt{25 - 9} = 4$` and there are two tangents because the point is outside. The formula in R is the reason.

</details>

15. **Assertion:** The tangent at the point P of a circle is parallel to the radius OP. **Reason:** A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

(D). A is false: the tangent is perpendicular to OP, not parallel to it. R is true.

</details>

16. **Assertion:** If two tangents from P make `$60^\circ$` with each other, the radii to the points of contact make `$120^\circ$`. **Reason:** The angles at the two points of contact are each `$90^\circ$`.
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

(A). Quadrilateral OAPB has angles 90°, 90°, 60° and the fourth angle `$= 360^\circ - 240^\circ = 120^\circ$`. R is exactly why.

</details>
