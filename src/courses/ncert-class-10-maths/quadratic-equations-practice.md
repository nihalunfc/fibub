**Practice · Class 10 Maths · Chapter 4.** Read the chapter lesson, [Quadratic Equations](/learn/courses/ncert-class-10-maths/quadratic-equations/), first. Try each question on paper before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Most quadratic questions reduce to three moves: get to standard form `$ax^2+bx+c=0$`, decide whether to factorise or use the formula (check D first), and check each root against the story.</div>

## How to set out an answer

1. For a word problem write “Let the ... be x” and say what x means with its unit.
2. Write the equation, then standard form.
3. Solve (show the factorising or the formula with the values of a, b, c).
4. Reject any root that does not fit (a negative length) and write the answer in words with its unit.
5. Check by putting it back into the story.

## 1-mark questions

1. Write `$2x^2 = 3x - 5$` in standard form.
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

`$2x^2 - 3x + 5 = 0$`.

</details>

2. Find the discriminant of `$x^2 - 8x + 16 = 0$` and say what it tells you.
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

`$D = 64 - 64 = 0$`: the roots are real and equal (`$x = 4$` twice).

</details>

3. Is `$(x - 2)^2 + 1 = x^2 - 3$` a quadratic equation?
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

No. It expands to `$x^2 - 4x + 5 = x^2 - 3$`, which simplifies to `$-4x = -8$`: linear.

</details>

4. Find the roots of `$x^2 - 49 = 0$`.
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

`$x^2 = 49$`, so `$x = 7$` or `$x = -7$`.

</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Solve `$x^2 + 5x - 14 = 0$` by factorisation. *(2 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Product `$-14$`, sum 5: the numbers are 7 and `$-2$`. `$(x + 7)(x - 2) = 0$`, so `$x = -7$` or `$x = 2$`.

</details>

6. Solve `$3x^2 - 5x - 2 = 0$` by splitting the middle term. *(3 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

`$a \times c = -6$`, `$b = -5$`: the numbers are `$-6$` and 1. `$3x^2 - 6x + x - 2 = 3x(x - 2) + 1(x - 2) = (3x + 1)(x - 2) = 0$`. So `$x = -\tfrac13$` or `$x = 2$`.

</details>

7. For what values of `$k$` does `$kx^2 + 6x + 1 = 0$` have equal roots? *(2 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Equal roots need `$D = 0$`: `$36 - 4k = 0$`, so `$k = 9$`.

</details>

8. Without solving, say whether `$2x^2 - 6x + 3 = 0$` has real roots. If so, find them. *(3 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

`$D = 36 - 24 = 12 > 0$`: two distinct real roots. `$x = \dfrac{6 \pm \sqrt{12}}{4} = \dfrac{3 \pm \sqrt3}{2}$`, about 2.37 and 0.63.

</details>

9. The sum of two numbers is 9 and the sum of their squares is 45. Find the numbers. *(3 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Let one be `$x$`, the other `$9 - x$`. `$x^2 + (9-x)^2 = 45$` gives `$2x^2 - 18x + 81 = 45$`, so `$x^2 - 9x + 18 = 0$`, `$(x-3)(x-6)=0$`. The numbers are 3 and 6.

</details>

## 5-mark questions (with model answers)

10. **A rectangular park has a perimeter of 56 m and an area of 192 m². Find its length and breadth.** *(5 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Perimeter 56 m means length + breadth = 28. Let the breadth be `$x$` m, so the length is `$28 - x$` m.

Area: `$x(28 - x) = 192$`, so `$x^2 - 28x + 192 = 0$`.

Factorise: numbers with product 192 and sum 28 are 12 and 16, so `$(x - 12)(x - 16) = 0$`, giving `$x = 12$` or `$16$`.

If the breadth is 12 m, the length is 16 m (the breadth is the shorter side). The park is **16 m by 12 m**. Check: 2(16 + 12) = 56 and 16 × 12 = 192.

</details>

11. A motor boat goes 24 km upstream and returns downstream in 5 hours in total. The stream flows at 2 km/h. Find the speed of the boat in still water. *(5 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Let the boat's speed be `$x$` km/h. Upstream speed `$x - 2$`, downstream `$x + 2$`.

Time: `$\dfrac{24}{x-2} + \dfrac{24}{x+2} = 5$`. Multiply by `$(x-2)(x+2)$`: `$24(x+2) + 24(x-2) = 5(x^2 - 4)$`, so `$48x = 5x^2 - 20$`.

`$5x^2 - 48x - 20 = 0$`: `$a \times c = -100$`, split `$-48x = -50x + 2x$`: `$5x(x - 10) + 2(x - 10) = (5x + 2)(x - 10) = 0$`.

`$x = 10$` or `$x = -0.4$`. A speed cannot be negative, so the boat's speed is **10 km/h**. Check: 24/8 + 24/12 = 3 + 2 = 5 h.

</details>

12. Prove that `$x^2 - 2x + 3 = 0$` has no real roots, and find the least value of `$x^2 - 2x + 3$`. *(5 marks)*
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

`$D = 4 - 12 = -8 < 0$`, so there are no real roots: the parabola never meets the x-axis.

By completing the square, `$x^2 - 2x + 3 = (x - 1)^2 + 2$`. A square is never negative, so the expression is at least 2, with the least value 2 at `$x = 1$`. Since it is never 0, there is no root.

</details>

## Case-based question

**The cricket ball.** A fielder throws a ball straight up from the boundary. Its height in metres after `$t$` seconds is `$h = 24t - 5t^2$`.

(a) Write the equation to find when the ball is at height 28 m.
(b) Find the discriminant and say what it tells you.
(c) Find the times at which the ball is at 28 m.
(d) Does the ball ever reach 40 m? Explain.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

(a) `$24t - 5t^2 = 28$`, so `$5t^2 - 24t + 28 = 0$`.

(b) `$D = 576 - 560 = 16 > 0$`: two distinct real times, so the ball is at 28 m twice (going up and coming down).

(c) `$t = \dfrac{24 \pm 4}{10}$`, so `$t = 2$` or `$t = 2.8$`. So at 2 s and 2.8 s.

(d) Set `$5t^2 - 24t + 40 = 0$`: `$D = 576 - 800 = -224 < 0$`. No real root, so the ball never reaches 40 m (its highest point is 28.8 m).

</details>

## Assertion and reason

Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

13. **Assertion:** `$x^2 + 4x + 5 = 0$` has no real roots. **Reason:** Its discriminant is negative.
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

(A). `$D = 16 - 20 = -4 < 0$`, which is exactly why there are no real roots.

</details>

14. **Assertion:** The roots of `$ax^2 + bx + c = 0$` are always real. **Reason:** The discriminant is `$b^2 - 4ac$`.
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

(C). R is true, but the roots are real only when `$D \ge 0$`. A is false.

</details>

15. **Assertion:** If `$a$` and `$c$` have opposite signs, `$ax^2 + bx + c = 0$` always has two distinct real roots. **Reason:** Then `$-4ac$` is positive, so `$D = b^2 - 4ac > 0$`.
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

(A). If `$a$` and `$c$` have opposite signs, `$ac < 0$`, so `$-4ac > 0$` and `$D > b^2 \ge 0$`. Two distinct real roots, and R explains it.

</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Standard form first; then check D = b² − 4ac to know how many real roots to expect.
- Factorise when the numbers are friendly, otherwise use `$x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{D}}{2a}$`.
- Equal roots: D = 0. Real and distinct: D > 0. No real roots: D < 0. Questions with k ask you to set D that way.
- In word problems, reject roots that do not fit (negative lengths, speeds, ages) and always check in the story.
- Never divide both sides by an expression with x in it: you lose a root.

</div>
