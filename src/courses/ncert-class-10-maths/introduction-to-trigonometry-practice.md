**Practice · Class 10 Maths · Chapter 8.** Read the chapter lesson, [Introduction to Trigonometry](/learn/courses/ncert-class-10-maths/introduction-to-trigonometry/), first. Try each question on paper before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Three habits solve almost every question here: draw the triangle and label opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse for the right angle; use Pythagoras for a missing side; and use sin² + cos² = 1 (with its two cousins) to swap one ratio for another.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Draw a right triangle and mark the angle you are working with.
2. Write the ratio in words first (for example sin A = opposite/hypotenuse), then put in the numbers.
3. For proofs, say “LHS = …” and work on that side only; end with “= RHS”.
4. Write special values in exact form (√3/2), not as rounded decimals, unless the question asks for decimals.

## 1-mark questions

1. In a right triangle ABC, right-angled at B, AB = 8 and BC = 6. Find tan A.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Opposite to A is BC = 6 and adjacent is AB = 8. tan A = 6/8 = 3/4.</details>

2. Write the value of sin 60° cos 30° + cos 60° sin 30°.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(√3/2)(√3/2) + (1/2)(1/2) = 3/4 + 1/4 = 1.</details>

3. If sin θ = cos θ and θ is acute, what is θ?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>θ = 45°, the only acute angle where the opposite and adjacent sides are equal.</details>

4. Simplify (1 + tan²θ) cos²θ.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>1 + tan²θ = sec²θ, and sec²θ × cos²θ = 1.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. If cos A = 12/13, find sin A and tan A. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Adjacent 12, hypotenuse 13, so opposite = √(169 − 144) = 5. sin A = 5/13 and tan A = 5/12.</details>

6. Evaluate: (2 tan 45° + cos 60°) / (sin 30° + 4 cos² 45°). *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Top: 2 × 1 + 1/2 = 5/2. Bottom: 1/2 + 4 × 1/2 = 5/2. The value is 1.</details>

7. A triangle has sides 7 cm and 24 cm about the right angle. Write the six ratios of the angle opposite the 7 cm side. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Hypotenuse = √(49 + 576) = 25. sin = 7/25, cos = 24/25, tan = 7/24, cosec = 25/7, sec = 25/24, cot = 24/7.</details>

8. Prove that (1 − cos²θ) cosec θ = sin θ. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>LHS = sin²θ × (1/sin θ) = sin θ = RHS.</details>

## 5-mark questions

9. Prove that (sin θ + cosec θ)² + (cos θ + sec θ)² = 7 + tan²θ + cot²θ. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>LHS = sin²θ + 2 sin θ cosec θ + cosec²θ + cos²θ + 2 cos θ sec θ + sec²θ. Now sin θ cosec θ = 1 and cos θ sec θ = 1, so LHS = (sin²θ + cos²θ) + 2 + 2 + cosec²θ + sec²θ = 1 + 4 + (1 + cot²θ) + (1 + tan²θ) = 7 + tan²θ + cot²θ = RHS.</details>

10. Prove that (1 + cos θ)/sin θ + sin θ/(1 + cos θ) = 2 cosec θ. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>LHS = [(1 + cos θ)² + sin²θ] / [sin θ (1 + cos θ)] = [1 + 2cos θ + cos²θ + sin²θ] / [sin θ (1 + cos θ)] = [2 + 2cos θ] / [sin θ (1 + cos θ)] = 2(1 + cos θ) / [sin θ (1 + cos θ)] = 2/sin θ = 2 cosec θ.</details>

11. If tan θ = 3/4, find the value of (4 sin θ − cos θ)/(4 sin θ + cos θ). *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>Divide the top and bottom by cos θ: (4 tan θ − 1)/(4 tan θ + 1) = (4 × 3/4 − 1)/(4 × 3/4 + 1) = (3 − 1)/(3 + 1) = 2/4 = 1/2.</details>

12. Show that sec θ − tan θ = 1/(sec θ + tan θ), and use it to find sec θ − tan θ when sec θ + tan θ = 5. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>(sec θ − tan θ)(sec θ + tan θ) = sec²θ − tan²θ = 1. So sec θ − tan θ = 1/(sec θ + tan θ). When sec θ + tan θ = 5, sec θ − tan θ = 1/5.</details>

## Case-based question

Meera is making a triangular roof frame for a model house from three sticks. The frame is an isosceles triangle with two sloping sticks of 10 cm each and a base of 12 cm. She drops a vertical post from the top of the roof to the middle of the base.

(i) Find the length of the post. (ii) Find sin, cos and tan of the angle between a sloping stick and the base. (iii) Is that angle more than 45° or less? Give a reason using tan. (iv) Check that sin²A + cos²A = 1 for this angle.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(i) The post splits the base into 6 and 6. Post = √(100 − 36) = 8 cm. (ii) For the angle A at the base: opposite = 8, adjacent = 6, hypotenuse = 10. sin A = 4/5, cos A = 3/5, tan A = 4/3. (iii) tan A = 4/3 is more than 1 = tan 45°, so A is more than 45°. (iv) (4/5)² + (3/5)² = 16/25 + 9/25 = 1. ✓</details>

## Assertion and reason

Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

13. **Assertion:** sin θ = 3/2 is not possible for an acute angle. **Reason:** The hypotenuse is the longest side of a right triangle, so opposite/hypotenuse is less than 1.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). 3/2 is more than 1, which sin cannot be, and the reason explains why.</details>

14. **Assertion:** tan 30° × tan 60° = 1. **Reason:** tan 30° = 1/√3 and tan 60° = √3.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). The product (1/√3) × √3 = 1, which uses exactly the two values given in the reason.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- sin = opp/hyp, cos = adj/hyp, tan = opp/adj; reciprocals cosec, sec, cot.
- Given one ratio, draw the triangle, find the third side by Pythagoras and read off the others.
- Special values: sin 30° = 1/2, sin 45° = 1/√2, sin 60° = √3/2; cos is the same list reversed; tan 45° = 1.
- Identities: sin² + cos² = 1; 1 + tan² = sec²; 1 + cot² = cosec².
- Prove identities by changing one side only; convert to sin and cos if stuck.
