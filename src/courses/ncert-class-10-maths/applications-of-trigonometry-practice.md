**Practice · Class 10 Maths · Chapter 9.** Read the chapter lesson, [Some Applications of Trigonometry](/learn/courses/ncert-class-10-maths/applications-of-trigonometry/), first. Try each question on paper before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Every question is a right triangle in disguise. Draw the picture first, mark the angle from the horizontal, name the sides (opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse) and choose sin, cos or tan. With two angles, write one equation for each triangle and solve them together.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Draw a neat figure and mark every known length and angle; label the unknown with a letter.
2. State which right triangle you are using and which ratio, for example “In triangle ABC, tan 60° = AB/BC”.
3. Use exact values (√3) and give the final answer in a simple form; take √3 = 1.73 only if asked.
4. Write the unit (m or cm) in the answer. Add the observer’s eye height if it is given.

## 1-mark questions

1. The shadow of a vertical pole is √3 times its height. What is the sun’s altitude?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>tan θ = height / shadow = 1/√3, so θ = 30°.</details>

2. A ladder 10 m long reaches 5 m up a wall. What angle does it make with the ground?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>sin θ = 5/10 = 1/2, so θ = 30°.</details>

3. From the top of a building the angle of depression of a bike on the road is 60°. What is the angle of elevation of the top of the building from the bike?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>60°, because the angles are alternate (equal).</details>

4. If the height of a tower and its distance from you are equal, what is the angle of elevation?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>tan θ = 1, so θ = 45°.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. A tree casts a shadow 18 m long when the sun’s altitude is 30°. Find the height of the tree. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>tan 30° = height/18, so height = 18/√3 = 6√3 ≈ 10.4 m.</details>

6. A 14 m ladder leans against a wall and makes 60° with the ground. Find the distance of its foot from the wall and the height it reaches. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Foot: 14 cos 60° = 7 m. Height: 14 sin 60° = 7√3 ≈ 12.1 m.</details>

7. From a point on level ground 30 m from the foot of a tower, the angle of elevation of the top is 30°. The observer’s eyes are 1.5 m above the ground. Find the height of the tower. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Above the eye: 30 tan 30° = 30/√3 = 10√3 ≈ 17.32 m. Tower ≈ 17.32 + 1.5 = 18.82 m.</details>

8. From the top of a 40 m cliff the angle of depression of a boat is 30°. How far is the boat from the foot of the cliff? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>tan 30° = 40/d, so d = 40/tan 30° = 40√3 ≈ 69.3 m.</details>

## 5-mark questions

9. From a point on the ground, the angles of elevation of the top of a tower are 45° from B and 30° from A, where A is 60 m farther from the tower than B (all in a straight line). Find the height of the tower. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>Let the tower be h and B be x from the foot. tan 45° = h/x, so x = h. tan 30° = h/(x + 60) gives √3 h = x + 60 = h + 60. So (√3 − 1) h = 60 and h = 60/(√3 − 1) = 30(√3 + 1) ≈ 82 m.</details>

10. From the top of a 30 m building, the angle of depression of the foot of a tower is 45° and the angle of elevation of its top is 60°. Find the height of the tower and its distance from the building. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>Distance: tan 45° = 30/d, so d = 30 m. The part of the tower above the building: tan 60° = x/30, so x = 30√3 ≈ 51.96 m. Tower = 30 + 30√3 = 30(1 + √3) ≈ 82 m. It is 30 m from the building.</details>

11. A boy flies a kite with the string 90 m long, kept straight, making 30° with the ground. His hand is 1.5 m above the ground. Find the height of the kite above the ground. If the wind pulls the kite higher so that the string makes 60° and the length is unchanged, how much higher is it now? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>At 30°: 90 sin 30° = 45 m above the hand, so 46.5 m above the ground. At 60°: 90 sin 60° = 45√3 ≈ 77.94 m above the hand, so ≈ 79.44 m above the ground. It rises 45√3 − 45 = 45(√3 − 1) ≈ 32.9 m.</details>

12. Two poles of heights 8 m and 14 m stand on level ground. A taut wire connects their tops and makes an angle of 30° with the horizontal. Find the length of the wire and the distance between the poles. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>The difference in height is 14 − 8 = 6 m. In the right triangle with the wire as the hypotenuse: sin 30° = 6/L, so L = 12 m. The distance between the poles: cos 30° = d/12, so d = 6√3 ≈ 10.4 m.</details>

## Case-based question

A school wants to find the height of its flagpole. Riya stands in a line with the pole, 24 m from its base, and measures the angle of elevation of the top as 30°. Her eyes are 1.5 m above the ground. Then she walks straight towards the pole until the angle of elevation is 60°, and stops at that point.

(i) Find the height of the pole from her first reading. (ii) How far from the base of the pole is she when the angle is 60°? (iii) How far did she walk between the two readings? (iv) Why can the shadow method (shadow equal to height when the sun is at 45°) not be used around noon in Delhi in June?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(i) Above her eye: 24 tan 30° = 24/√3 = 8√3 ≈ 13.86 m. The pole is about 13.86 + 1.5 = 15.36 m high. (ii) The part above her eye is still 8√3 m, so tan 60° = 8√3/x gives x = 8√3/√3 = 8 m from the base. (iii) She walked 24 − 8 = 16 m. (iv) Around noon in June the sun in Delhi is nearly overhead (altitude about 85°), so shadows are very short; the 45° sun happens only in the morning and afternoon.</details>

## Assertion and reason

Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

13. **Assertion:** The angle of elevation of the Sun is 30° when a pole’s shadow is √3 times its height. **Reason:** tan 30° = 1/√3.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). tan θ = height/shadow = 1/√3, and that is tan 30°, which is exactly the reason given.</details>

14. **Assertion:** As you walk towards a tower on level ground, the angle of elevation of its top increases. **Reason:** The angle of elevation is measured from the vertical line through the eye.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(C). The assertion is true (tan θ = height/distance grows as the distance falls). The reason is false: the angle is measured from the horizontal.</details>
