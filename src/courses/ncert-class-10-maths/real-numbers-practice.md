**Practice · Class 10 Maths · Chapter 1.** Read the chapter lesson, [Real Numbers](/learn/courses/ncert-class-10-maths/real-numbers/), first. Try each question on paper before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Almost every Real Numbers question is one of four moves: factorise into primes, take the smallest or greatest powers for HCF or LCM, use HCF × LCM = a × b, or test the denominator for only 2s and 5s. Do the prime factorisation neatly and the rest is reading it off.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Write the prime factorisation of each number in index form (for example 144 = 2⁴ × 3²).
2. Say what you are taking: “common primes, smallest powers” for HCF; “all primes, greatest powers” for LCM.
3. For proofs of irrationality, write: “Suppose, to the contrary, that … is rational”, then reach a contradiction, then state the conclusion.
4. Check with HCF × LCM = product when you can.

## 1-mark questions

1. Express 84 as a product of primes.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>84 = 2² × 3 × 7.</details>

2. Is 7 × 11 × 13 + 13 a prime or a composite number?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Composite: 13 × (7 × 11 + 1) = 13 × 78, so it has factors other than 1 and itself.</details>

3. Without dividing, say whether 9/80 has a terminating decimal.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Yes. 80 = 2⁴ × 5 has only the primes 2 and 5.</details>

4. State whether 5 − √3 is rational or irrational.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Irrational. A rational number minus an irrational number is irrational.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Find the HCF and LCM of 96 and 360 by prime factorisation. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>96 = 2⁵ × 3 and 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5. HCF = 2³ × 3 = 24. LCM = 2⁵ × 3² × 5 = 1440. Check: 24 × 1440 = 34 560 = 96 × 360.</details>

6. The HCF of two numbers is 9 and their LCM is 270. One number is 54. Find the other. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>HCF × LCM = product, so the other number = (9 × 270) ÷ 54 = 45.</details>

7. Prove that √5 is irrational. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Suppose √5 = a/b in lowest terms. Then a² = 5b², so 5 divides a², hence 5 divides a. Write a = 5c: 25c² = 5b², so b² = 5c² and 5 divides b. Now 5 divides both a and b, contradicting lowest terms. So √5 is irrational.</details>

8. Show that 6 + 3√2 is irrational, given that √2 is irrational. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Suppose 6 + 3√2 = r is rational. Then √2 = (r − 6)/3, which is rational (integers and r combined by subtraction and division). That contradicts √2 being irrational. So 6 + 3√2 is irrational.</details>

## 5-mark questions

9. A sweet shop has 420 laddus and 130 barfis. It wants to pack them in identical boxes, each box holding the same number of laddus and the same number of barfis, with none left over. (a) What is the largest number of boxes it can fill? (b) How many laddus and barfis go in each box? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>The number of boxes must divide both 420 and 130, and to have the largest number of boxes we need the HCF. 420 = 2² × 3 × 5 × 7 and 130 = 2 × 5 × 13. HCF = 2 × 5 = 10. (a) At most 10 boxes. (b) Each box has 420 ÷ 10 = 42 laddus and 130 ÷ 10 = 13 barfis.</details>

10. Without long division, say which of 17/8, 13/12, 29/343 and 19/625 have terminating decimal expansions, and find the decimal for each that does. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>17/8: 8 = 2³, terminates: 2.125. 13/12: 12 = 2² × 3, has 3, repeats. 29/343: 343 = 7³, has 7, repeats. 19/625: 625 = 5⁴, terminates: 19 × 16 / 10000 = 304/10000 = 0.0304.</details>

## Case-based question

Three friends practise at a gym. Asha does a full circuit every 12 minutes, Bilal every 18 minutes and Chandan every 30 minutes. They all start together at 6:00 a.m. The gym floor is a rectangle of 24 m by 18 m, and the owner wants to cover it with the largest square mats possible, with none cut.

(i) Write 12, 18 and 30 as products of primes. (ii) When will all three finish a circuit together again? (iii) Find the side of the largest square mat. (iv) How many mats are needed?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(i) 12 = 2² × 3, 18 = 2 × 3², 30 = 2 × 3 × 5. (ii) LCM = 2² × 3² × 5 = 180 minutes, so at 9:00 a.m. (iii) HCF(24, 18) = 6 m. (iv) (24 ÷ 6) × (18 ÷ 6) = 4 × 3 = 12 mats.</details>

## Assertion and reason

Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

11. **Assertion:** 4<sup>n</sup> can never end in the digit 0 for any natural number n. **Reason:** 4<sup>n</sup> = 2<sup>2n</sup>, whose only prime factor is 2.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). For a number to end in 0 it must have both 2 and 5 as factors. 4ⁿ has no 5, so the reason explains the assertion.</details>

12. **Assertion:** √3 + √3 is irrational. **Reason:** The sum of two irrational numbers is always irrational.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(C). √3 + √3 = 2√3, which is irrational, so A is true. R is false: for example √2 + (−√2) = 0 is rational.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Factorise first: HCF uses common primes with smallest powers, LCM uses all primes with greatest powers.
- HCF × LCM = a × b for two numbers; use it to find a missing number.
- HCF for equal pieces; LCM for repeating events.
- To prove a root is irrational, assume it is p/q in lowest terms and find a common factor.
- A fraction in lowest terms terminates only if its denominator has just the primes 2 and 5.
