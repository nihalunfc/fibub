**Practice · Class 10 Maths · Chapter 3.** Read the chapter lesson, [Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables](/learn/courses/ncert-class-10-maths/pair-of-linear-equations/), first. Try each question on paper before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Every question in this chapter is one of three jobs: decide how many solutions from the ratios, solve by substitution or elimination, or turn a story into two equations. Always check the answer in both original equations.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Number the equations (1) and (2).
2. State the method and show every step: the multiplication, the addition or subtraction, and the value found.
3. Find the second variable by putting the first in an original equation.
4. Verify in the other equation. For word problems, write the answer in a sentence with units.

## 1-mark questions

1. Write the general form of a linear equation in two variables.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>ax + by = c, where a and b are not both zero.</details>

2. If the lines of a pair are parallel, how many solutions does the pair have?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>None. Parallel lines never meet.</details>

3. For the pair 3x + 2y = 7 and 6x + 4y = 14, what are the ratios a₁/a₂, b₁/b₂, c₁/c₂?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>1/2, 1/2 and 1/2. They are all equal, so the lines coincide.</details>

4. Is (1, 2) a solution of 3x − y = 1?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>3(1) − 2 = 1, yes.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Without solving, say whether x − 2y = 3 and 3x − 6y = 10 has one solution, none or infinitely many. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>a₁/a₂ = 1/3, b₁/b₂ = −2/−6 = 1/3, c₁/c₂ = 3/10. The first two are equal but the third is different, so the lines are parallel: no solution.</details>

6. Find k so that 2x + ky = 6 and 4x + 8y = 12 has infinitely many solutions. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>2/4 = k/8 = 6/12 = 1/2, so k = 4.</details>

7. Solve by substitution: 2x + y = 10 and x − y = 2. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>From (2) x = y + 2. Put in (1): 2(y + 2) + y = 10, so 3y + 4 = 10, y = 2 and x = 4. Check (1): 8 + 2 = 10.</details>

8. Solve by elimination: 5x + 3y = 21 and 2x − 3y = 0. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The y terms are +3y and −3y, so add: 7x = 21, x = 3. From (2): 6 = 3y, so y = 2. Check (1): 15 + 6 = 21.</details>

Fresh numbers every time. Try until you get three right in a row.

## 5-mark questions

9. Solve graphically: x + y = 6 and x − y = 2. Write the three points you plotted for each line and the coordinates of the meeting point. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>Line 1: (0, 6), (3, 3), (6, 0). Line 2: (0, −2), (2, 0), (4, 2). Plot both on the same axes. The lines cross at (4, 2). Check: 4 + 2 = 6 and 4 − 2 = 2. So x = 4 and y = 2.</details>

10. A boat goes 24 km downstream in 2 hours and 16 km upstream in 2 hours. Find the speed of the boat in still water and the speed of the stream. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>Let the boat be x km/h and the stream y km/h. Downstream speed = 24/2 = 12, so x + y = 12. Upstream speed = 16/2 = 8, so x − y = 8. Add: 2x = 20, x = 10. Then y = 2. Boat 10 km/h, stream 2 km/h.</details>

## Case-based question

A school canteen sells samosas and chai. On Monday, 3 samosas and 2 cups of chai cost ₹70. On Tuesday, 2 samosas and 3 cups of chai cost ₹65.

(i) Write the pair of equations if a samosa costs x and a chai y rupees. (ii) Are the lines parallel, intersecting or coincident? (iii) Find x and y. (iv) What will 5 samosas and 4 chai cost?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(i) 3x + 2y = 70 and 2x + 3y = 65. (ii) 3/2 is not equal to 2/3, so the lines intersect: one solution. (iii) Multiply (1) by 3 and (2) by 2: 9x + 6y = 210 and 4x + 6y = 130. Subtract: 5x = 80, x = 16. Then 2y = 70 − 48 = 22, y = 11. Samosa ₹16, chai ₹11. (iv) 5(16) + 4(11) = 80 + 44 = ₹124.</details>

## Assertion and reason

Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

11. **Assertion:** The pair x + 2y = 4 and 2x + 4y = 9 is inconsistent. **Reason:** The lines represented by the pair are parallel.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). 1/2 = 2/4 but 4/9 is different, so the lines are parallel and have no common point.</details>

12. **Assertion:** A pair of linear equations in two variables always has at least one solution. **Reason:** Two straight lines in a plane can be parallel.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(D). A is false: a parallel pair has no solution. R is true, and it is exactly why A fails.</details>
