**Practice · Class 10 Maths · Chapter 2.** Read the chapter lesson, [Polynomials](/learn/courses/ncert-class-10-maths/polynomials/), first. Try each question on paper before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Two moves solve almost everything here: factorise to find the zeros (split the middle term), or skip the zeros and use α + β = −b/a and αβ = c/a. Check the sign of b before you write −b/a.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Write the polynomial and name a, b and c, with their signs.
2. To find zeros: set p(x) = 0, factorise, set each factor to 0.
3. To check: sum of zeros against −b/a, product against c/a.
4. For a graph question, say how many times the curve meets the x-axis and what that means.

## 1-mark questions

1. What is the degree of 5x³ − 2x + 9?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>3 (the highest power of x).</details>

2. Find the zero of the polynomial 3x − 12.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>3x − 12 = 0 gives x = 4.</details>

3. The graph of y = p(x) cuts the x-axis at 3 points. How many zeros does p(x) have?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>3 zeros (each meeting point is a zero).</details>

4. If the sum of the zeros of a quadratic is −3, what is the coefficient b when a = 2?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Sum = −b/a, so −3 = −b/2 and b = 6.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Find the zeros of x² − 7x + 10 and check the relation between zeros and coefficients. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>x² − 5x − 2x + 10 = (x − 5)(x − 2), so the zeros are 5 and 2. Sum = 7 = −(−7)/1 and product = 10 = 10/1.</details>

6. Find a quadratic polynomial whose zeros are 4 and −1. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Sum = 3 and product = −4, so x² − 3x − 4. (Any non-zero multiple also works.)</details>

7. Find the value of k if −2 is a zero of p(x) = x² + 3x + k. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>p(−2) = 4 − 6 + k = 0, so k = 2.</details>

8. Without finding the zeros, find the sum and product of the zeros of 3x² + 5x − 2. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>a = 3, b = 5, c = −2. Sum = −b/a = −5/3. Product = c/a = −2/3.</details>

## 5-mark questions

9. The graph of y = x² − 4x + k is drawn for different values of k. (a) For what value of k does it touch the x-axis at one point? (b) For which k does it have two zeros? (c) For which k does it have none? Explain with the graph. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>The lowest point of the parabola is at x = 2, where y = 4 − 8 + k = k − 4. (a) It touches the axis when the lowest point is on it: k − 4 = 0, so k = 4 (zero at x = 2). (b) Two zeros when the lowest point is below the axis: k < 4. (c) No zeros when the lowest point is above the axis: k > 4.</details>

10. If α and β are the zeros of x² − 6x + 8, find (a) α + β, (b) αβ, (c) α² + β², and (d) a polynomial whose zeros are 2α and 2β. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>(a) α + β = 6. (b) αβ = 8. (c) α² + β² = (α + β)² − 2αβ = 36 − 16 = 20. (d) New sum = 12, new product = 4 × 8 = 32, so x² − 12x + 32.</details>

## Case-based question

A cricketer hits a ball and its height in metres after t seconds is modelled by h(t) = −5t² + 20t, starting from the bat at ground level.

(i) What is the degree of h(t)? (ii) Find the zeros of h(t). (iii) What do the zeros mean in the story? (iv) Find the height at t = 1.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(i) 2. (ii) −5t(t − 4) = 0, so t = 0 or t = 4. (iii) t = 0 is when the ball leaves the bat and t = 4 s is when it returns to the ground. (iv) h(1) = −5 + 20 = 15 m.</details>

## Assertion and reason

Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

11. **Assertion:** The polynomial x² + 4 has no real zeros. **Reason:** The graph of y = x² + 4 lies above the x-axis for every x.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). x² + 4 is at least 4, so it is never 0, and the graph never meets the axis. R is the graph picture of A.</details>

12. **Assertion:** A cubic polynomial has exactly three zeros. **Reason:** A polynomial of degree n has at most n zeros.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(D). A is false: a cubic has at most three; x³ has one zero and x³ + x has one. R is true.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Degree is the highest power; zeros are where the graph meets the x-axis; at most n zeros for degree n.
- For ax² + bx + c: sum of zeros = −b/a, product = c/a.
- A quadratic with sum S and product P: x² − Sx + P.
- Put a zero into the polynomial to find an unknown coefficient.
- Check signs: the sum is −b/a, not b/a.
