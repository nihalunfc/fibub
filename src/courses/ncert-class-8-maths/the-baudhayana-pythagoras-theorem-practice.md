**Practice · Class 8 Maths · Chapter 9.** Read the lesson, [The Baudhayana-Pythagoras Theorem](/learn/courses/ncert-class-8-maths/the-baudhayana-pythagoras-theorem/), first. Try each question on paper before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Draw the triangle, mark the right angle, and write c on the side opposite it. Add the squares of the legs to get c², or subtract a leg's square from c² to get the other leg. Then take the square root and check that c is the longest side.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Draw a neat sketch and mark the right angle and the hypotenuse.
2. Write the theorem for this triangle, for example AC² = AB² + BC².
3. Substitute, simplify and take the square root.
4. Write the answer with its unit and check that the hypotenuse is the longest side.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the longest side of a right triangle.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The hypotenuse, opposite the right angle.</details>

2. Is 5, 12, 13 a Pythagorean triple?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Yes: 25 + 144 = 169 = 13².</details>

3. Find the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs 6 cm and 8 cm.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>√(36 + 64) = √100 = 10 cm.</details>

4. Which triple comes from m = 3 and n = 2?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>a = 9 − 4 = 5, b = 2 × 3 × 2 = 12, c = 9 + 4 = 13: the triple 5, 12, 13.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. A 10 m ladder reaches 8 m up a wall. How far is its foot from the wall? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Foot² = 100 − 64 = 36, so the foot is 6 m from the wall.</details>

6. Find the diagonal of a rectangle 12 m by 5 m. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>d² = 144 + 25 = 169, so d = 13 m.</details>

7. Is a triangle with sides 7, 24, 25 right-angled? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>7² + 24² = 49 + 576 = 625 = 25². Yes, the right angle is opposite the side 25.</details>

8. Show that 2, 3, 4 do not make a right triangle and say whether it is acute or obtuse. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>2² + 3² = 13 and 4² = 16. They are not equal, so it is not right-angled. 13 < 16, so the triangle is obtuse.</details>

9. Find the distance between (1, 2) and (4, 6). *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Across 3, up 4. d² = 9 + 16 = 25, so d = 5.</details>

## 5-mark questions

10. A 15 m pole stands on level ground. A wire 17 m long runs from the top to a point on the ground. (a) How far is the point from the foot of the pole? (b) Another 25 m wire runs from the top of a 24 m pole. How far from its foot does it reach the ground? (c) Which of the two wires is anchored farther from its pole? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) d² = 17² − 15² = 289 − 225 = 64, so d = 8 m.

(b) d² = 25² − 24² = 625 − 576 = 49, so d = 7 m.

(c) The first wire (8 m) is anchored farther.

</details>

11. Prove with the area picture that c² = a² + b² for a right triangle, using a square of side a + b divided into four copies of the triangle and a square. Then check with a = 5, b = 12. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Area of the big square = (a + b)². It is made of four triangles of area ½ab each (total 2ab) and the middle square c². So (a + b)² = 2ab + c², giving a² + 2ab + b² = 2ab + c² and a² + b² = c². Check: 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169 = 13², so c = 13.

</details>

## Case-based question

**The rope and the plot.** A mason is marking the corner of a rectangular plot using a rope with knots at equal gaps of 1 m. He takes 3 m along one side and 4 m along the other and ties a rope between them.

(a) How long must the rope between the two marks be for the corner to be a right angle? *(1)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>5 m, since 3² + 4² = 25 = 5².</details>

(b) The plot is 30 m by 40 m. How long is its diagonal? *(1)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The 3-4-5 triple multiplied by 10 gives 30-40-50, so the diagonal is 50 m.</details>

(c) He measures the diagonal and finds 52 m. Is the corner a right angle? *(1)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>No. A right angle needs 50 m. 30² + 40² = 2500 but 52² = 2704, so the corner is more than 90°.</details>

(d) Name the old Indian text where a rule like this is written. *(1)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The Baudhayana Sulba Sutra (about 800 BCE).</details>

## Assertion and reason

Options: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

12. **A:** A triangle with sides 8, 15 and 17 is right-angled. **R:** 8² + 15² = 17².
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). 64 + 225 = 289. Both true and R gives the reason.</details>

13. **A:** A triangle with sides 2, 3 and 4 is right-angled. **R:** The converse says: if a² + b² = c² then the triangle is right-angled.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(D). A is false because 4 + 9 ≠ 16. R is a true statement but does not apply to 2, 3, 4.</details>

14. **A:** The sides 6, 8 and 10 form a Pythagorean triple. **R:** 3, 4, 5 is a triple and 6, 8, 10 is every side of it multiplied by 2.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). Scaling by the same number keeps a² + b² = c².</details>
