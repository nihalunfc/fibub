**Practice · Class 8 Maths · Chapter 2.** Read the lesson, [Power Play](/learn/courses/ncert-class-8-maths/power-play/), first. Try each question on paper before you open its answer.

<div class="bigidea">Check the base before using a law. Same base: add or subtract powers. Power of a power: multiply. Negative power: flip. Standard form: count the places the point moves.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Write each step with the law you use, such as “same base, add powers”.
2. Turn negative powers into fractions only at the end if you can.
3. For standard form, write the number between 1 and 10 first, then the power of 10.
4. Check with a small case (for example, put a = 2).

## 1-mark questions

1. Write 5 × 5 × 5 × 5 as a power.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>5⁴.</details>

2. Find the value of 7⁰ + 3⁰.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>1 + 1 = 2.</details>

3. Write 3 × 10⁶ in the usual way.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>3 000 000 (thirty lakh).</details>

4. Find 10⁻².
<details><summary>Answer</summary>1/100 = 0.01.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

5. Simplify 4⁷ ÷ 4⁴ × 4². *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Subtract then add: 4⁷⁻⁴ × 4² = 4³ × 4² = 4⁵ = 1024.</details>

6. Find the value of (2/3)⁻³. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Flip the fraction: (3/2)³ = 27/8 = 3.375.</details>

7. Write 0.000 000 82 and 6 400 000 000 in standard form. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>0.000 000 82: move the point 7 places right, 8.2 × 10⁻⁷. 6 400 000 000: move the point 9 places left, 6.4 × 10⁹.</details>

8. Which is greater, 2⁸ or 8³? Show your working. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>8³ = (2³)³ = 2⁹. Since 2⁹ is more than 2⁸, 8³ is greater (512 against 256).</details>

9. Simplify (5⁴ × 5⁻²) ÷ 5⁰ and give the answer as a number. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>5⁴ × 5⁻² = 5² = 25, and dividing by 5⁰ = 1 leaves 25.</details>

Fresh numbers every time. Try until you get three right in a row.

## 5-mark questions

10. The speed of light is about 3 × 10⁸ m/s. How far does light travel in one year (365 days)? Give your answer in standard form in metres. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>One year = 365 × 24 × 60 × 60 = 31 536 000 s ≈ 3.15 × 10⁷ s. Distance = 3 × 10⁸ × 3.15 × 10⁷ = 9.46 × 10¹⁵ m (about 9.5 × 10¹⁵ m, one light-year). Powers added: 8 + 7 = 15, and 3 × 3.15 = 9.45 so the front number stays below 10.</details>

11. A bacterium splits in two every 20 minutes. Starting with 1 bacterium, how many are there after 4 hours, and what is that in standard form? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>4 hours = 240 minutes = 12 doublings. After 12 doublings there are 2¹² = 4096 bacteria. In standard form 4096 = 4.096 × 10³.</details>

## Case-based question

An astronomy club learns that the Sun’s mass is about 2 × 10³⁰ kg, the Earth’s mass is about 6 × 10²⁴ kg, and a hydrogen atom has a mass of about 1.7 × 10⁻²⁷ kg. The Earth is 1.5 × 10¹¹ m from the Sun.

12. (a) Which is heavier, the Sun or the Earth, and by about what power of ten? (b) Write the Earth–Sun distance in kilometres. (c) Write the mass of a hydrogen atom as a decimal with the first non-zero digit after how many zeros? (d) How many times heavier is the Sun than the Earth, roughly?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) The Sun is heavier, by a factor of about 3.3 × 10⁵ (the powers of ten differ by 6, and 2 ÷ 6 ≈ 0.33). (b) 1.5 × 10¹¹ m = 1.5 × 10⁸ km (divide by 10³). (c) 1.7 × 10⁻²⁷ = 0.000…0017 with 26 zeros after the decimal point before the digit 1. (d) 2 × 10³⁰ ÷ 6 × 10²⁴ = (2/6) × 10⁶ ≈ 3.3 × 10⁵ times.</details>

## Assertion and reason

13. **Assertion (A):** 2⁻³ is a negative number. **Reason (R):** A negative power makes a number negative.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both are false. 2⁻³ = 1/8, which is positive. A negative power means reciprocal, not negative.</details>

14. **Assertion (A):** 3⁵ × 3² = 3⁷. **Reason (R):** For the same base, powers are added when multiplying.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both are true and R explains A.</details>

15. **Assertion (A):** Every non-zero number raised to the power 0 equals 1. **Reason (R):** Each step down in the power divides by the base, and 5¹ ÷ 5 = 1.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both are true and R explains A; the pattern a⁰ = a¹ ÷ a = 1 works for any non-zero a.</details>
