**Practice · Class 8 Maths · Chapter 6.** Read the lesson, [We Distribute, Yet Things Multiply](/learn/courses/ncert-class-8-maths/we-distribute-yet-things-multiply/), first. Try each question on paper before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Name the pattern before you write anything: a common factor, a perfect square, a difference of squares or (x + a)(x + b). Say what a and b are, put them in the formula in brackets, then check with a small number.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Write the identity you use, for example (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b².
2. Say what a and b are (for example a = 3x, b = 2y).
3. Substitute with brackets, simplify, give the answer.
4. Check by putting x = 1 into the question and into your answer.

## 1-mark questions

1. Expand 4(x + 6).
<details><summary>Answer</summary>4x + 24.</details>

2. Write 99 × 15 as 100 × 15 − 15 and find it.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>1500 − 15 = 1485.</details>

3. Expand (x + 6)².
<details><summary>Answer</summary>x² + 12x + 36.</details>

4. What is the coefficient of x in (x + 4)(x − 9)?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>4 + (−9) = −5.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Expand (3x − 2y)². *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(3x)² − 2(3x)(2y) + (2y)² = 9x² − 12xy + 4y².</details>

6. Use an identity to find 104 × 96. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(100 + 4)(100 − 4) = 10000 − 16 = 9984.</details>

7. Factorise 9x² − 16. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(3x)² − 4² = (3x + 4)(3x − 4).</details>

8. Factorise 6x² + 15x. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The common factor is 3x: 6x² + 15x = 3x(2x + 5). Check: 3x · 2x = 6x² and 3x · 5 = 15x. ✓</details>

9. If x + y = 9 and xy = 20, find x² + y². *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>x² + y² = (x + y)² − 2xy = 81 − 40 = 41.</details>

## 5-mark questions

10. Expand and simplify (x + 3)(x + 5) − (x + 1)(x + 7). Show every step. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(x + 3)(x + 5) = x² + 8x + 15. (x + 1)(x + 7) = x² + 8x + 7. The difference is x² + 8x + 15 − x² − 8x − 7 = 8. The answer is 8, whatever x is.

</details>

11. (a) Use (a + b)² to find 62². (b) Use (a + b)(a − b) to find 62 × 58. (c) Find 62² − 58² quickly. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) (60 + 2)² = 3600 + 240 + 4 = 3844.

(b) 62 × 58 = (60 + 2)(60 − 2) = 3600 − 4 = 3596.

(c) 62² − 58² = (62 + 58)(62 − 58) = 120 × 4 = 480.

</details>

## Case-based question

**The calendar trick.** In the month of November, Arjun draws a 2 × 2 square on the calendar. The top-left date is x. A week has 7 days.

(a) Write the other three dates in terms of x. *(1)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>x + 1, x + 7 and x + 8.</details>

(b) Find the sum of the four dates in terms of x. *(1)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>x + (x + 1) + (x + 7) + (x + 8) = 4x + 16.</details>

(c) If the top-left date is 9, what is the sum? *(1)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>4 × 9 + 16 = 52 (9 + 10 + 16 + 17 = 52).</details>

(d) Arjun says the sum is 58. Can he be right? *(1)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>4x + 16 = 58 gives x = 10.5, not a whole date, so no. (The sum is always a multiple of 4.)</details>

## Assertion and reason

Options: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

12. **A:** (x + 6)² = x² + 36 is a wrong expansion. **R:** The expansion of (a + b)² has a middle term 2ab.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). The missing term here is 12x.</details>

13. **A:** 47 × 53 = 2491. **R:** (50 − 3)(50 + 3) = 50² − 3².
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). 2500 − 9 = 2491. Both are true and R gives the working.</details>

14. **A:** (a − b)² = a² − b². **R:** (a − b)(a + b) = a² − b².
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(D). A is false because (a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b². R is true but is a different expression.</details>
