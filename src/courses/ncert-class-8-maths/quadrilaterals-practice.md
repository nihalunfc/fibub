**Practice · Class 8 Maths · Chapter 4.** Read the lesson, [Quadrilaterals](/learn/courses/ncert-class-8-maths/quadrilaterals/), first. Try each question on paper before you open its answer.

<div class="bigidea">Draw the shape, mark what is given, and ask: which family does it belong to, and what extra rules come with that? Angles then add to 360°, neighbouring angles to 180°, and diagonals tell the rest.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Draw a neat figure and mark equal sides, equal angles and parallel lines.
2. Name the property you use, such as “opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal”.
3. Show the sum or equation, then the answer with degrees or units.
4. Check that the four angles add up to 360°.

## 1-mark questions

1. State the sum of the angles of a quadrilateral.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>360°.</details>

2. Which quadrilateral has exactly one pair of parallel sides?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A trapezium (in the usual class-8 sense).</details>

3. What do the diagonals of a rectangle have in common?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>They are equal in length (and bisect each other).</details>

4. Is every square a rhombus? Answer yes or no.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Yes: a square has four equal sides.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

5. Three angles of a quadrilateral are 75°, 105° and 95°. Find the fourth angle. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>75 + 105 + 95 = 275, so 360 − 275 = 85°.</details>

6. In a parallelogram two neighbouring angles are (2x + 10)° and (3x − 20)°. Find x and all four angles. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>They add up to 180°: 5x − 10 = 180, so x = 38. The angles are 86°, 94°, 86°, 94°.</details>

7. The diagonals of a parallelogram are 18 cm and 12 cm. They meet at O. Find OA and OB, taking AC = 18. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The diagonals bisect each other: OA = 9 cm and OB = 6 cm.</details>

8. One side of a rhombus is 9 cm. Find its perimeter, and one angle if its neighbouring angle is 110°. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Perimeter = 4 × 9 = 36 cm. Neighbouring angles add up to 180°, so the angle is 70°.</details>

9. A kite has angles 100° at A, 70° at B and 70° at D (AB = AD). Find the fourth angle at C. Why are B and D equal? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>C = 360 − (100 + 70 + 70) = 120°. B and D are equal because the kite is symmetric about the diagonal AC.</details>

Fresh numbers every time. Try until you get three right in a row.

## 5-mark questions

10. Prove that the opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal using the fact that neighbouring angles add up to 180°. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Let ABCD be a parallelogram. Then ∠A + ∠B = 180° and ∠B + ∠C = 180° (neighbouring angles between parallel lines). Subtract: ∠A − ∠C = 0, so ∠A = ∠C. In the same way ∠B + ∠C = 180° and ∠C + ∠D = 180°, so ∠B = ∠D. Opposite angles are equal.</details>

11. The diagonals of a quadrilateral cross at right angles and bisect each other. The diagonals are 16 cm and 30 cm. Name the quadrilateral and find its area and the length of each side. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Diagonals that bisect each other make a parallelogram, and at right angles it is a rhombus. Area = ½ × 16 × 30 = 240 cm². Each side is the long side of a right triangle with sides 8 and 15: side² = 64 + 225 = 289, so each side is 17 cm.</details>

## Case-based question

A carpenter is making a window frame ABCD from four wooden strips. The strips AB and DC are each 60 cm, and BC and AD are each 40 cm. He finds that the diagonals AC and BD are both 72 cm. A second frame PQRS has PQ = QR = RS = SP = 50 cm, and one of its diagonals measures 80 cm (use the rule that the sides and half-diagonals of a rhombus form a right triangle).

12. (a) What kind of quadrilateral is ABCD? (b) Why? (c) What is the perimeter of ABCD? (d) What kind of shape is PQRS, and is it a square?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) A rectangle. (b) Opposite sides are equal, so it is a parallelogram, and its diagonals are equal, so it is a rectangle. (c) 2 × (60 + 40) = 200 cm. (d) PQRS has four equal sides, so it is a rhombus. With a diagonal of 80 cm the half-diagonal is 40 cm, and the other half-diagonal is √(50² − 40²) = 30 cm (the diagonals meet at 90°), so the other diagonal is 60 cm. The diagonals are not equal, so it is not a square.</details>

## Assertion and reason

13. **Assertion (A):** Every rectangle is a parallelogram. **Reason (R):** Both pairs of opposite sides of a rectangle are parallel.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both are true and R explains A.</details>

14. **Assertion (A):** The diagonals of every parallelogram are equal. **Reason (R):** The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false (only for a rectangle). R is true. So A is false and R is true.</details>

15. **Assertion (A):** The diagonals of a kite bisect each other. **Reason (R):** A kite has two pairs of equal neighbouring sides.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false: in a kite only one diagonal bisects the other. R is true. So A is false and R is true.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Four angles of a quadrilateral: 360°. Parallelogram neighbours: 180°, opposite angles equal.
- Diagonals: parallelogram bisect; rectangle equal; rhombus perpendicular; square all three; kite perpendicular with one bisected.
- Every square is a rectangle and a rhombus; every rectangle and rhombus is a parallelogram.
- Rhombus area is half the product of its diagonals.
