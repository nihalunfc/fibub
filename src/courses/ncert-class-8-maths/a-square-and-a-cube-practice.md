**Practice · Class 8 Maths · Chapter 1.** Read the lesson, [A Square and a Cube](/learn/courses/ncert-class-8-maths/a-square-and-a-cube/), first. Try each question on paper before you open its answer.

<div class="bigidea">Most questions here are one of three jobs: spot a square or cube using the last digit, make pairs or triples of prime factors, or use the odd-number pattern. Choose the job first, then calculate.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Write the number as a product of primes when asked for a root or a multiplier.
2. Show the grouping (pairs for squares, triples for cubes) in brackets.
3. Check by multiplying the root back.
4. Give units (cm, m², cm³) for word problems.

## 1-mark questions

1. Find 17².
<details><summary>Answer</summary>289.</details>

2. Without calculating, say why 4 837 is not a perfect square.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>It ends in 7, and no square ends in 2, 3, 7 or 8.</details>

3. How many numbers lie between 20² and 21²?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>2 × 20 = 40 numbers.</details>

4. Find the cube root of 343.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>7, because 7 × 7 × 7 = 343.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

5. Find the square root of 2025 by prime factors. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>2025 = 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 × 5 × 5 = (3 × 3)(3 × 3)(5 × 5). Take one from each pair: 3 × 3 × 5 = 45. So √2025 = 45.</details>

6. Find the smallest number by which 180 must be multiplied to make a perfect square. Also say what the square root is. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>180 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5. The 5 is alone, so multiply by 5: 900 = 30². The square root is 30.</details>

7. Find the smallest number by which 1323 must be divided to give a perfect cube. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>1323 = 3 × 3 × 3 × 7 × 7. The three 3’s make a group, but the two 7’s do not fill a triple. Divide by 7 × 7 = 49 to get 27 = 3³. (Dividing by 49 removes the lonely primes.)</details>

8. Without adding, find 1 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 9 + 11 + 13 + 15 + 17. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>There are 9 odd numbers, so the sum is 9² = 81.</details>

9. Is 3267 a perfect square? Show by repeated subtraction of odd numbers, or by estimating. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>57² = 3249 and 58² = 3364. Since 3249 &lt; 3267 &lt; 3364, the number 3267 lies between two consecutive squares, so it is not a perfect square.</details>

Fresh numbers every time. Try until you get three right in a row.

## 5-mark questions

10. Find the square root of 7056 by the prime factor method and check the answer. Then find the smallest number to be added to 7056 so that it becomes the next perfect square. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>7056 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 7 × 7 = (2 × 2)(2 × 2)(3 × 3)(7 × 7), so √7056 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 7 = 84. Check: 84 × 84 = 7056. The next square is 85² = 7225, so we must add 7225 − 7056 = 169.</details>

11. Show that 1728 is a perfect cube by prime factors and find the side of a cube-shaped tank whose volume is 1728 litres, in dm (1 litre = 1 dm³). *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>1728 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 3 = (2 × 2 × 2)(2 × 2 × 2)(3 × 3 × 3). All primes fall into triples, so it is a perfect cube. Its cube root is 2 × 2 × 3 = 12. The side of the tank is 12 dm = 1.2 m. Check: 12 × 12 × 12 = 1728.</details>

## Case-based question

A school is making a square flower garden with square tiles. The gardener first lays 1 tile, then adds an L-shaped border of 3 tiles, then a border of 5 tiles, and keeps adding the next odd number. The garden finally has 14 borders. A second, cube-shaped sandpit of side 6 m is built next to it.

12. (a) How many tiles are in the finished garden? (b) How many tiles were in the 14th border? (c) The headmaster says the garden has 200 tiles. Can he be right? (d) How many cubic metres of sand fill the sandpit?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) The sum of the first 14 odd numbers is 14² = 196 tiles. (b) The 14th odd number is 2 × 14 − 1 = 27 tiles. (c) No. 196 is the number of tiles for 14 borders and the next would be 225; 200 is between 14² and 15², not a square. (d) 6 × 6 × 6 = 216 m³.</details>

## Assertion and reason

13. **Assertion (A):** 4 761 is a perfect square. **Reason (R):** Its last digit is 1.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is true (69 × 69 = 4761). R is a true statement about the number, but a last digit of 1 does not prove that a number is a square, so it does not explain A. Both are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.</details>

14. **Assertion (A):** The cube of an odd number is odd. **Reason (R):** The product of odd numbers is odd.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both are true and R explains A: n × n × n of an odd number is a product of odd numbers.</details>

15. **Assertion (A):** There are 30 natural numbers between 15² and 16². **Reason (R):** Between n² and (n + 1)² there are 2n numbers.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both are true and R explains A: 2 × 15 = 30.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Squares end only in 0, 1, 4, 5, 6, 9; the sum of the first n odd numbers is n²; there are 2n numbers between n² and (n + 1)².
- Square root: pair the prime factors. Cube root: group them in threes.
- To make a perfect square or cube, multiply by the lonely primes; to turn a number into one, divide by them.
- Check every root by multiplying it back.
