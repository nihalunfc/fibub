**Practice · Class 8 Maths · Chapter 11.** Read the lesson, [Exploring Some Geometric Themes](/learn/courses/ncert-class-8-maths/exploring-some-geometric-themes/), first. Try each question on paper before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">For symmetry, use two tests: the mirror (lines) and the turn (order). For tiling, add the corners round a point to see if they make 360°. For solids, count V, E and F, then check V − E + F = 2.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Draw a rough sketch and mark what you count (lines, corners, faces).
2. State the rule you use: n lines for a regular n-gon, corner `180 − 360/n`, or V − E + F = 2.
3. Substitute and give the answer with its unit (degrees, number of faces).
4. Check that the answer makes sense (for example, the corners round a point cannot exceed 360°).

## 1-mark questions

1. How many lines of symmetry does an equilateral triangle have?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>3.</details>

2. What is the order of rotational symmetry of a rectangle that is not a square?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>2 (it fits after a half-turn of 180°).</details>

3. Name the three regular polygons that tessellate on their own.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The equilateral triangle, the square and the regular hexagon.</details>

4. How many faces, edges and vertices does a triangular prism have?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>5 faces, 9 edges, 6 vertices (check: 6 − 9 + 5 = 2).</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Find the smallest angle through which a regular octagon can be turned about its centre to fit onto itself. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Order 8, so 360° ÷ 8 = 45°.</details>

6. Why do regular pentagons not tessellate? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Each corner is 108°. Three corners make 324° and leave a gap of 36°; four would make 432°, which is more than 360°. The corners can never add up to exactly 360°.</details>

7. A solid has 9 vertices and 16 edges. How many faces does it have? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

F = 2 − V + E = 2 − 9 + 16 = 9.

</details>

8. A Koch curve starts as a line 9 cm long. Find its length after 2 steps. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Each step multiplies the length by 4/3. Length = 9 × (4/3)² = 9 × 16/9 = 16 cm.

</details>

9. Is a row of six squares a net of a cube? Explain. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

No. If we roll a cube along the row, after four squares the bottom face returns to the first face, so the fifth square gets the same face as the first. Two squares cannot both be the same face.

</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

10. (a) Find the corner angle of a regular hexagon and of a regular octagon. (b) Show that a regular octagon and a square can tile together by looking at one point. (c) How many squares and octagons meet at the point? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Hexagon: 180° − 360° ÷ 6 = 120°. Octagon: 180° − 360° ÷ 8 = 135°. (b) At a point, 2 octagons and 1 square: 135° + 135° + 90° = 360°, so no gap. (c) 2 octagons and 1 square.

</details>

11. A Sierpinski triangle is built in steps. (a) How many filled triangles are there after 3 steps? (b) What fraction of the original area is filled after 3 steps? (c) Does the filled area grow or shrink with more steps? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 triangles. (b) Each step keeps 3/4, so (3/4)³ = 27/64. (c) It shrinks: each step multiplies the filled area by 3/4, which is less than 1.

</details>

## Case-based question

12. A tile shop shows floor patterns. Pattern A uses regular hexagons, pattern B uses regular pentagons, pattern C uses squares and regular octagons together.

(a) Which pattern leaves gaps? Why? *(2 marks)*
(b) Find the angle at which two octagons and a square meet, and say whether C works. *(2 marks)*
(c) How many lines of symmetry does one tile of pattern A have? *(1 mark)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Pattern B: the pentagon’s corner 108° does not divide 360°, so gaps (36° at each three-tile meeting) are left. (b) 135° + 135° + 90° = 360°, so C works. (c) 6 lines of symmetry.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

For each, choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

13. **A:** Regular pentagons cannot tile a floor on their own. **R:** Each corner of a regular pentagon is 108°, which does not divide 360° exactly.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). Both are true, and R is the reason.</details>

14. **A:** A cube and an octahedron both have 12 edges. **R:** For both, V − E + F = 2.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(B). Both true (cube 8, 12, 6; octahedron 6, 12, 8), but Euler’s rule does not explain why the edge counts happen to be equal.</details>
