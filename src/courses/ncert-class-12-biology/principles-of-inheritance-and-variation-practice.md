**Practice · Class 12 Biology · Chapter 4.** Read the chapter lesson, [Principles of Inheritance and Variation](/learn/courses/ncert-class-12-biology/principles-of-inheritance-and-variation/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Every genetics question follows the same four steps: write the parents’ genotypes, write their gametes, fill the Punnett square (or multiply probabilities), and read the ratios, both genotype and phenotype. Never skip the gametes step.</div>

## How to set out a cross

1. Choose letters (capital for dominant, small for recessive) and say what they stand for.
2. Write the genotypes of the parents, then the gametes each makes.
3. Draw the Punnett square and fill it in.
4. State the genotype ratio, the phenotype ratio and the answer to the question asked.

## 1-mark questions

1. What is a test cross?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A cross of an individual of unknown genotype with a homozygous recessive individual, to find out the genotype.</details>

2. Name the chromosomal disorder caused by an extra X chromosome in a male.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Klinefelter syndrome (XXY).</details>

3. Who proposed the chromosomal theory of inheritance?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Sutton and Boveri.</details>

4. Which blood group has the genotype IAIB?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Group AB (codominance).</details>

5. What is a recessive allele?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>An allele that shows its effect only when there is no dominant allele, that is, in the homozygous state.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

6. (2) State Mendel’s law of segregation.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The two alleles of a gene separate at gamete formation so that each gamete carries only one allele, and they are unaltered. They come together again at random at fertilisation.</details>

7. (2) How does incomplete dominance differ from codominance? One example of each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>In incomplete dominance the heterozygote is intermediate (red × white snapdragon gives pink). In codominance both alleles are fully expressed in the heterozygote (the IAIB blood group AB).</details>

8. (2) Why are fruit flies a good material for genetic studies?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>They are small, easy to grow in the laboratory, have a short life cycle (about two weeks), produce many offspring from one mating, have clear male and female differences and only four pairs of chromosomes.</details>

9. (3) A man of blood group A whose mother was group O marries a woman of group B whose father was group O. List the possible blood groups of their children with the genotypes.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The man is IAi (his mother gave him i) and the woman is IBi. Gametes: IA, i and IB, i. Children: IAIB (AB), IAi (A), IBi (B), ii (O), each 1/4.</details>

10. (3) Explain why a colour-blind man and a normal homozygous woman have no colour-blind sons but all their daughters are carriers.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Colour blindness is X-linked recessive. The father (XcY) gives his Y to sons and his Xc to daughters. The mother (XX) gives a normal X to all. Sons are XY (normal) and daughters XXc (carriers).</details>

11. (3) Distinguish between the sex determination in humans and in birds.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>In humans the female is XX and the male XY; the sperm (X or Y) decides the sex of the child. In birds the female is ZW and the male ZZ; the egg (Z or W) decides the sex of the chick.</details>

## 5-mark questions

12. (5) Describe Mendel’s dihybrid cross of round yellow and wrinkled green seeds up to F2, with the Punnett square result, and state the law it shows.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>P: RRYY × rryy. Gametes RY and ry. F1: all RrYy, round yellow. F1 selfed: each parent makes RY, Ry, rY, ry in equal numbers, giving 16 combinations. F2: 9 round yellow (R_Y_), 3 round green (R_yy), 3 wrinkled yellow (rrY_), 1 wrinkled green (rryy). Each trait on its own gives 3 : 1, so the two traits are inherited independently: the law of independent assortment.</details>

13. (5) What is linkage? How did Morgan explain recombinants, and how does recombination frequency show the distance between genes?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Linkage is the tendency of genes on the same chromosome to be inherited together. Morgan found that the genes for yellow body and white eyes stayed together in Drosophila crosses, so they are linked. A few new combinations (recombinants) appear because of crossing over between homologous chromosomes in prophase I. The farther apart two genes are, the more likely a crossover falls between them, so recombination frequency is higher. 1 % recombination is 1 map unit; for example 1.3 % for yellow and white (close) and 37.2 % for white and miniature (far).</details>

14. (5) Explain how non-disjunction causes Down syndrome, Klinefelter syndrome and Turner syndrome.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Non-disjunction is the failure of a pair of chromosomes to separate in meiosis, so some gametes get an extra chromosome (n + 1) and others lack one (n − 1). If an n + 1 gamete with an extra chromosome 21 fuses with a normal gamete, the child has trisomy 21: Down syndrome (47 chromosomes). A gamete with XX (or XY) joining a normal Y (or X) gives XXY: Klinefelter syndrome. A gamete lacking a sex chromosome joining a normal X gives XO (45 chromosomes): Turner syndrome.</details>

## Case-based question

In a family a rare disorder appears. Two healthy parents, I-1 and I-2, have four children: two healthy sons, one affected son and one healthy daughter. The affected son’s mother has a healthy brother and an affected uncle on her side.

15. (a) Is the disorder dominant or recessive? (b) Is it likely to be X-linked? Why? (c) What is the genotype of the mother if it is X-linked recessive? (d) What is the chance that another son of these parents is affected?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Recessive, because healthy parents have an affected child. (b) Yes, likely X-linked: only a son is affected and an affected maternal uncle shows that the allele travels through females. (c) She is a carrier, XHXh. (d) 1/2, since the son gets Xh from her with probability 1/2 and the father gives Y.</details>

## Assertion and reason

Choose: (a) both true and R explains A; (b) both true but R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

16. **A:** In a dihybrid test cross the ratio is 1 : 1 : 1 : 1. **R:** The recessive parent makes only one type of gamete.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). The recessive parent (rryy) makes only ry gametes, so the four types of gamete of the dihybrid appear in the offspring in equal numbers.</details>

17. **A:** A haemophilic father cannot pass the disease to his son. **R:** The son gets his X chromosome from his mother.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). The son gets Y from the father, and the allele is on the X, which comes from the mother.</details>

18. **A:** Genes that are close together on a chromosome show a high percentage of recombination. **R:** Crossing over is more likely between genes that are closer together.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both are false, so none of (a) to (c) fits and the answer is "both A and R are false". Closely linked genes show a low percentage of recombination, because a crossover is less likely to fall between genes that are close together.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Genotype → gametes → Punnett square → ratios. Monohybrid 3 : 1 and 1 : 2 : 1; dihybrid 9 : 3 : 3 : 1; test cross 1 : 1 or 1 : 1 : 1 : 1.
- A son gets his X from his mother: X-linked recessive traits pass from carrier mothers to half their sons.
- Recombination frequency is the percentage of recombinants and equals the distance in map units.
- Non-disjunction gives trisomy (Down, XXY) or monosomy (Turner, XO).
