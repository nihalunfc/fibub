**Biology · Class 12 · Chapter 2 · about 90 minutes.** By the end you will be able to name the parts of the male and female systems and say what each does, follow a sperm and an egg from their stem cells to the zygote, read a menstrual cycle graph, explain fertilisation and implantation, and describe pregnancy, birth and lactation.

<div class="bigidea">Humans reproduce sexually. A male makes millions of tiny sperm all the time from puberty, a female releases about one large egg a month, and the two meet inside her body. Hormones from the brain, the pituitary and the gonads run the whole programme, and the cycle in a woman is a feedback loop that prepares a nest each month for an embryo that may or may not come.</div>

## 1. The male reproductive system

The male system sits in the pelvis and in a pouch of skin hanging outside it. Its parts are the **testes**, the **ducts** that carry sperm, the **accessory glands** and the **penis**.

### Testes

The two **testes** lie in a bag of skin, the **scrotum**, outside the abdomen. This is not a design flaw: sperm production needs a temperature about **2 to 2.5 °C lower** than the body, and the scrotum keeps the testes cooler.

Each testis is oval, about 4 to 5 cm long, and is divided into some 250 **compartments (lobules)**. Each lobule holds one to three very coiled **seminiferous tubules**, where sperm are made. Two kinds of cells matter.

- **Sertoli cells** line the tubules and nourish the developing sperm.
- **Leydig (interstitial) cells** lie in the spaces between tubules and make **androgens**, mainly testosterone.

Tubule cells that divide to make sperm are the **spermatogonia** (the male germ cells).

### Ducts and glands

Sperm leave the testis along a path you should be able to write from memory: seminiferous tubules → **rete testis** → **vasa efferentia** → **epididymis** (sperm mature and are stored here) → **vas deferens** (rises into the abdomen and loops over the bladder) → **ejaculatory duct** (formed where the vas deferens meets the duct of the seminal vesicle) → **urethra** → the opening at the tip of the penis (urethral meatus). The urethra carries both urine and semen, but never at the same time.

The **accessory glands** make the fluid part of semen, the **seminal plasma**: the paired **seminal vesicles** (rich in fructose, an energy source for sperm), the **prostate** (alkaline, milky fluid) and the paired **bulbourethral glands** (mucus that lubricates). The seminal plasma also contains calcium and some enzymes.

The **penis** is the male copulatory organ. Its tip, the **glans**, is covered by a loose fold of skin, the **foreskin**. The shaft has erectile tissue that fills with blood to make the penis firm for copulation.

## 2. The female reproductive system

The female system is also mostly in the pelvis: two **ovaries**, two **fallopian tubes**, the **uterus**, the **vagina** and the external genitalia, plus the **mammary glands**.

- **Ovaries** are about 2 to 4 cm long. They make the **ovum** (egg) and the hormones oestrogen and progesterone. Inside an ovary, the stroma is packed with **follicles**, each with one developing egg.
- **Fallopian tubes (oviducts)** are about 10 to 12 cm long. The end near the ovary is a funnel, the **infundibulum**, with finger-like **fimbriae** that collect the egg at ovulation. Then come the wide **ampulla** (where fertilisation takes place) and the narrow **isthmus** that joins the uterus.
- The **uterus** (womb) is shaped like an inverted pear. It has three layers: the thin outer **perimetrium**, the thick muscular **myometrium** (which contracts strongly during birth) and the inner glandular **endometrium**, which changes through the menstrual cycle and is where the embryo implants. The narrow lower end is the **cervix**; the **cervical canal** and the **vagina** together form the **birth canal**.
- **External genitalia**: the mons pubis, labia majora and minora, hymen and clitoris. The hymen is often torn by a first sexual intercourse, but it can also be torn by sport or other activity, so it is not a reliable sign of anything.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c12b-hrep-female"><figcaption>The female system. Watch the egg travel from ovary to uterus; switch to Fertilised to see sperm, fertilisation and the early embryo.</figcaption></figure>

The **mammary glands** are paired structures in the breast that make milk after birth. Each has 15 to 20 **lobes** made of clusters of cells called **alveoli**; the alveoli secrete milk, which goes by mammary tubules into the **mammary ampulla** and then out through the **lactiferous duct** at the nipple.

## 3. Gametogenesis

**Gametogenesis** is the making of gametes in the gonads: spermatogenesis in the testes and oogenesis in the ovaries. Both end in haploid cells (n = 23 in humans, from 2n = 46), formed by meiosis.

### Spermatogenesis

It begins at puberty. Rising **GnRH** from the hypothalamus makes the pituitary release **LH** and **FSH**. LH acts on Leydig cells, which make androgens; FSH acts on Sertoli cells; together they run spermatogenesis. The steps are:

1. A **spermatogonium** (2n) divides by mitosis, so there is always a supply of stem cells.
2. Some grow into a **primary spermatocyte** (2n) and start meiosis I.
3. Meiosis I gives two **secondary spermatocytes** (n). They are haploid, with each chromosome still made of two chromatids.
4. Meiosis II gives four **spermatids** (n).
5. In **spermiogenesis** a spermatid grows a tail and becomes a **sperm**. The release of sperm into the tubule is **spermiation**.

<div class="problem" data-problem="c12b-hrep-sperm"></div>

A sperm has four parts: a **head** (the nucleus, capped by the **acrosome** that holds enzymes to dissolve the egg's coverings), a short **neck**, a **middle piece** packed with mitochondria that provide the energy for swimming, and a **tail** that moves the sperm. A man releases 200 to 300 million sperm in one ejaculate. For normal fertility, at least about 60 % of them should have a normal shape and at least 40 % should move vigorously.

<div class="problem" data-problem="c12b-hrep-semen"></div>

### Oogenesis

Oogenesis starts **before birth**, which is a big difference from spermatogenesis.

1. In the foetus, **oogonia** (2n) divide by mitosis, then each becomes a **primary oocyte** that starts meiosis I and **stops in prophase I**. Each is surrounded by a layer of cells, forming a **primary follicle**.
2. At birth each ovary holds about 1 to 2 million primary oocytes; many degenerate, so by puberty only about **60,000 to 80,000** are left in each ovary.
3. After puberty, a few follicles grow every month: primary → **secondary follicle** → **tertiary follicle**, which has a fluid-filled cavity (the antrum). Just before ovulation the follicle is a **Graafian follicle**, and the primary oocyte finishes meiosis I, forming a large **secondary oocyte** (n) and a tiny **first polar body**.
4. At **ovulation** the follicle bursts and the secondary oocyte, arrested at **metaphase II**, is released.
5. Only if a **sperm enters** does it finish meiosis II, forming the **ovum** and the **second polar body**.

Also, **a man’s sperm are made all the time** from puberty, but a woman’s oocytes are not renewed.

## 4. The menstrual cycle

The first cycle (**menarche**) begins at puberty, at about 10 to 14 years, and cycles stop at **menopause** at about 50 years. The cycle is the repeating pattern of changes in the ovary and the uterus, and it lasts about 28 days on average. It is controlled by four hormones: **FSH** and **LH** from the pituitary, **oestrogen** and **progesterone** from the ovary. Slide through the cycle and watch what each does.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c12b-hrep-cycle"><figcaption>Schematic hormone curves for one cycle. Move the day, change the cycle length, or switch to Fertilised to see why pregnancy stops periods.</figcaption></figure>

1. **Menstrual phase** (days 1 to 5): the endometrium breaks down and leaves with blood because the egg was not fertilised.
2. **Follicular phase** (days 6 to 13): rising **FSH** makes a follicle grow; the follicle makes more and more **oestrogen**, which rebuilds the endometrium (this is the proliferative phase of the uterus).
3. **Ovulation** (about day 14): high oestrogen causes a surge of **LH** (and a smaller one of FSH). The follicle bursts and releases the secondary oocyte.
4. **Luteal phase** (days 15 to 28): the empty follicle becomes the **corpus luteum**, which makes **progesterone**; the endometrium becomes thick, rich in blood vessels and glands, ready for an embryo (secretory phase).
5. If there is no embryo, the corpus luteum degenerates after about 10 days, progesterone falls, and the next menstrual phase begins. If an embryo implants, the corpus luteum is kept alive by **hCG** and there is **no menstruation**.

The luteal phase lasts about 14 days in nearly every woman, while the follicular phase is what varies. So ovulation falls about 14 days before the end of the cycle, not always on day 14.

Menstruation is a normal sign of a healthy cycle, and women should be able to manage it with clean, safe products. Periods can vary in length and flow, and missed or very painful periods should be checked by a doctor.

## 5. Fertilisation and implantation

During **copulation** (coitus) semen is deposited in the vagina (insemination). The sperm swim through the cervix and the uterus into the fallopian tube. The egg is released only once per cycle and lives about 24 hours, and sperm live up to a few days, so fertilisation is only possible if sperm and egg are in the tube around the same time. It happens in the **ampulla**.

1. The sperm touches the **zona pellucida**, the jelly layer around the egg. This triggers the **acrosomal reaction**: acrosome enzymes digest a path through the zona.
2. One sperm enters the cytoplasm of the egg. At once the egg changes the zona pellucida so that **no other sperm can enter** (this blocks polyspermy).
3. The entry of the sperm makes the secondary oocyte complete meiosis II, forming the **ovum** and the **second polar body**.
4. The sperm nucleus (n) and the ovum nucleus (n) fuse: a **zygote** (2n) is made. This is **fertilisation**.

The sex of the child depends on the sperm: an **X-bearing** sperm gives a girl (XX) and a **Y-bearing** sperm a boy (XY). The egg always carries an X.

### From zygote to implantation

While it moves to the uterus the zygote divides by mitosis, **cleavage**: 2, 4, 8 and 16 cells (**blastomeres**), with no growth in size, so the cells get smaller. A solid ball of 8 to 16 cells is the **morula**. The morula continues to divide and forms the **blastocyst**, a hollow ball with an outer layer, the **trophoblast**, and a clump of cells inside, the **inner cell mass**. The trophoblast attaches to the endometrium and the blastocyst sinks into it: **implantation**, about 7 days after fertilisation. The inner cell mass becomes the embryo.

## 6. Pregnancy and embryonic development

After implantation, finger-like **chorionic villi** grow out of the trophoblast and interlock with the uterine tissue. Together the villi and the uterine tissue form the **placenta**, a disc that joins the embryo and the mother. The placenta:

- brings **oxygen and nutrients** to the embryo and takes away **carbon dioxide and wastes**; the blood of the mother and of the foetus do not mix;
- lets protective **antibodies** (IgG) pass to the foetus;
- works as an **endocrine gland**, making **hCG** (human chorionic gonadotropin), **hPL** (human placental lactogen), oestrogens and progesterone, and in the later stage **relaxin**. hCG, hPL and relaxin are made only in pregnancy. Progesterone from the placenta is also needed to keep the pregnancy.

The placenta is joined to the embryo by the **umbilical cord**, which carries blood between them. Pregnancy tests detect **hCG** in urine.

The **inner cell mass** has stem cells that can become any tissue. It soon forms three layers, the **ectoderm**, **mesoderm** and **endoderm**, from which all the organs develop. Development goes on in this order (see the slider): the **heart** forms and begins beating by the end of the first month; by the end of the second month the **limbs and digits** are developed; by the end of **12 weeks** (the first trimester) most major organ systems and the external genitalia are formed; the **first movements** and **hair** appear in the fifth month; by about 24 weeks the eyelids separate and eyelashes form; and the foetus is ready for birth by about the ninth month.

## 7. Parturition and lactation

**Parturition** is the birth of the baby. Signals from the fully developed foetus and the placenta (a rise in cortisol, among others) make the uterus start weak contractions, the **foetal ejection reflex**. The stretch of the cervix makes the mother's pituitary release **oxytocin**; oxytocin makes the uterus contract harder, which stretches the cervix more, and so on. This is **positive feedback**: the loop gets stronger every time until the baby is born. After birth the placenta is expelled, as the afterbirth.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c12b-hrep-oxytocin"><figcaption>Positive feedback (birth) against negative feedback (a thermostat). Slide the loop strength to see when the loop grows and when it fades.</figcaption></figure>

**Lactation** is milk production. Under the hormone **prolactin**, the mammary glands begin to make milk at the end of pregnancy. The first milk, **colostrum**, is yellowish and rich in **antibodies (IgA)**, so it gives the newborn **passive immunity**. Breast-feeding during the first few months is strongly recommended by doctors because the baby gets nutrients and protection against infections.

<div class="keypoints">

- Male: testes in the scrotum (2 to 2.5 °C cooler), seminiferous tubules (spermatogonia, Sertoli cells), Leydig cells (testosterone). Path: tubules, rete testis, epididymis, vas deferens, ejaculatory duct, urethra. Glands: seminal vesicles, prostate, bulbourethral.
- Female: ovaries, fallopian tubes (fimbriae, ampulla, isthmus), uterus (perimetrium, myometrium, endometrium), cervix, vagina. Mammary glands: lobes, alveoli, lactiferous ducts.
- Spermatogenesis: spermatogonium → primary spermatocyte → 2 secondary → 4 spermatids → 4 sperm. Oogenesis: oogonium → primary oocyte (paused in prophase I until puberty) → secondary oocyte + first polar body (ovulation at metaphase II) → ovum + second polar body (only if a sperm enters).
- Cycle of about 28 days: menstrual, follicular, ovulation (LH surge, about 14 days before the cycle ends), luteal. FSH grows the follicle, oestrogen rebuilds the lining, progesterone maintains it.
- Fertilisation in the ampulla: acrosomal reaction, entry of one sperm, zona pellucida block, meiosis II, zygote (2n). Cleavage → morula (8 to 16 cells) → blastocyst (trophoblast + inner cell mass) → implantation about day 7.
- Placenta: chorionic villi + uterine tissue. It exchanges gases and nutrients, and makes hCG, hPL, oestrogens, progesterone and relaxin. Pregnancy about 40 weeks from the last period.
- Birth: oxytocin and cervical stretch form a positive feedback loop. Colostrum (IgA) gives passive immunity.

</div>
