**Practice · Class 12 Biology · Chapter 2.** Read the chapter lesson, [Human Reproduction](/learn/courses/ncert-class-12-biology/human-reproduction/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Most marks come from three skills: giving the exact order of structures or stages (sperm path, gametogenesis, cleavage to implantation), reading a hormone graph day by day, and counting cells and chromosomes. Draw the sequence on rough paper before you write the answer.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For a "sequence" question, write the stages in order joined by arrows, then add a few words for each.
2. For a hormone question, name the gland, then the hormone, then the effect on its target.
3. For a diagram question, give a title, draw large, label with straight lines and write the ploidy (n or 2n) where asked.
4. In a numerical, show the multiplication (for example 4 sperm per cell) before the answer.

## 1-mark questions

1. Which cells of the testis produce testosterone?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The Leydig (interstitial) cells, which lie between the seminiferous tubules.</details>

2. Name the structure where fertilisation takes place in humans.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The ampulla of the fallopian tube.</details>

3. What is the function of the corpus luteum?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It secretes progesterone (and some oestrogen), which maintains the endometrium for implantation and pregnancy.</details>

4. Name the hormone that is detected in a urine pregnancy test.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin).</details>

5. What is the name of the first milk, and why is it important?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Colostrum. It is rich in antibodies (IgA) and gives the newborn passive immunity.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

6. (2) Why are the testes in the scrotum outside the abdominal cavity?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Spermatogenesis needs a temperature about 2 to 2.5 °C lower than the normal body temperature. The scrotum keeps the testes cooler than the abdomen.</details>

7. (2) Name the three accessory glands of the male system and one secretion of each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Seminal vesicles: fructose-rich fluid. Prostate: alkaline fluid. Bulbourethral glands: mucus for lubrication.</details>

8. (2) Distinguish between a primary and a secondary oocyte.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The primary oocyte (2n) is paused in prophase I of meiosis I, and exists from birth. The secondary oocyte (n) forms just before ovulation when meiosis I finishes, with a first polar body; it is paused at metaphase II.</details>

9. (3) Explain the role of the zona pellucida and the acrosome in fertilisation.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The zona pellucida is the layer around the egg to which the sperm bind; this triggers the acrosomal reaction, in which acrosome enzymes digest a path through the zona. After one sperm enters, the zona changes so that other sperm cannot enter (no polyspermy).</details>

10. (3) How many sperm and how many ova can be formed from 20 primary spermatocytes and 20 primary oocytes? Explain.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>80 sperm and 20 ova. Each primary spermatocyte gives 2 secondary spermatocytes and 4 spermatids (4 sperm). Each primary oocyte gives one large ovum and polar bodies, because the divisions are unequal.</details>

11. (3) Describe the changes in the uterus during one menstrual cycle.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Menstrual phase (days 1 to 5): the endometrium is shed with blood. Proliferative phase: rising oestrogen thickens and rebuilds the endometrium. Secretory phase (after ovulation): progesterone makes the lining thick, glandular and rich in blood vessels. If there is no embryo, hormone levels fall and the lining is shed.</details>

## 5-mark questions

12. (5) Describe spermatogenesis, naming the cell and the ploidy at each stage, and the hormones that control it.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Spermatogonium (2n) divides by mitosis. Some become primary spermatocytes (2n) and undergo meiosis I to give two secondary spermatocytes (n). Meiosis II gives four spermatids (n). Spermiogenesis turns spermatids into sperm, which are released into the tubule lumen (spermiation). Control: GnRH from the hypothalamus makes the anterior pituitary release LH and FSH. LH acts on Leydig cells, which produce androgens; FSH acts on Sertoli cells, which help spermiogenesis. Androgens are needed for the process.</details>

13. (5) Explain the events of the menstrual cycle with reference to the hormones FSH, LH, oestrogen and progesterone.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Days 1 to 5: menstruation; FSH begins to rise. Follicular phase: FSH stimulates follicle growth; the follicle makes oestrogen, which thickens the endometrium. About day 14: high oestrogen triggers an LH surge (with a small FSH peak) that causes ovulation. Luteal phase: LH converts the ruptured follicle into the corpus luteum, which makes progesterone (and oestrogen) to maintain the endometrium. If there is no fertilisation, the corpus luteum degenerates, progesterone falls and menstruation begins. If implantation occurs, hCG keeps the corpus luteum active and the period does not come.</details>

14. (5) Trace the development of the zygote up to implantation, naming each stage and where it occurs.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Fertilisation in the ampulla gives a zygote (2n). Cleavage divisions (mitotic, with no growth) occur as the zygote moves along the tube to give 2, 4, 8 and 16 blastomeres. The 8 to 16-celled solid ball is the morula, which reaches the uterus. It becomes a blastocyst with an outer trophoblast and an inner cell mass. The trophoblast attaches to the endometrium and the blastocyst sinks in (implantation), about the seventh day. The inner cell mass becomes the embryo.</details>

## Case-based question

Meera’s cycle is regular and lasts 30 days. Her last period began on 3 March. She and her husband are trying for a child and are told that the fertile days are around ovulation.

15. (a) On which day of the cycle is ovulation most likely? (b) Name the hormone that peaks just before it. (c) Which structure forms from the follicle after ovulation, and which hormone does it make? (d) Why does her period not come if she becomes pregnant?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) About day 16 (30 − 14). (b) LH (the LH surge, with high oestrogen). (c) The corpus luteum, which makes progesterone. (d) The embryo makes hCG, which keeps the corpus luteum active, so progesterone stays high and the endometrium is not shed.</details>

## Assertion and reason

Choose: (a) both true and R explains A; (b) both true but R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

16. **A:** A woman releases only about 400 eggs in her lifetime although she has 1 to 2 million primary oocytes at birth. **R:** Most follicles degenerate and only one is ovulated per cycle.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). Most oocytes are lost before puberty or in follicles that do not mature; about one ovulation per cycle over some 35 years gives about 400.</details>

17. **A:** Colostrum is very useful to the newborn. **R:** It is rich in antibodies.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). The antibodies (IgA) give passive immunity.</details>

18. **A:** Sperm can fertilise the egg only in the uterus. **R:** The sperm reach the uterus first.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(d). A is false: fertilisation occurs in the ampulla of the fallopian tube. R is true as a statement of travel, but it does not support A.</details>
