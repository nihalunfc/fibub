**Practice · Class 12 Biology · Chapter 13.** Read the chapter lesson, [Biodiversity and Conservation](/learn/courses/ncert-class-12-biology/biodiversity-and-conservation/), first. Try each question on paper before you open its answer.

<div class="bigidea">Biodiversity answers score when you name the example exactly (Nile perch, Western Ghats, Z = 0.1 to 0.2, Tilman, Ehrlich) and keep the lists in the right order: three levels, three reasons to conserve, four causes of loss.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For lists (the evil quartet, in situ methods), give a numbered list with one example per point.
2. For a species-area problem, write S = C × A^Z or the log form, then substitute.
3. For a graph, label both axes and say whether the scale is linear or log.
4. For conservation questions, always say whether the method is in situ or ex situ.

## 1-mark questions

**1.** Name the three levels of biodiversity.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Genetic diversity, species diversity and ecological diversity.</details>

**2.** What does the term "endemic" mean?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A species that is found only in one particular region and nowhere else.</details>

**3.** Who proposed the rivet-popper hypothesis?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Paul Ehrlich.</details>

**4.** Write the equation of the species-area relationship in the log form.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>log S = log C + Z log A.</details>

**5.** Name the three biodiversity hotspots that include parts of India.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The Western Ghats and Sri Lanka, the Himalaya and Indo-Burma.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

**6.** (2 marks) State the three reasons for the high species diversity of the tropics.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Tropical regions stayed undisturbed by glaciation for a long time, giving long evolutionary time; the climate is stable and predictable, which promotes niche specialisation; and there is more solar energy, so higher productivity.</details>

**7.** (2 marks) What are the values of Z in the species-area relationship for small and for very large areas?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>For areas of similar size within a region the slope is about 0.1 to 0.2 for all taxa; for very large areas such as continents it is steeper, 0.6 to 1.2.</details>

**8.** (3 marks) What is meant by co-extinction? Give an example.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>When a species goes extinct, the species that depend on it also become extinct. Example: a host-specific parasite that lives on only one host dies when the host goes extinct; a plant that has one pollinator can die when the pollinator is lost.</details>

**9.** (3 marks) Differentiate between in situ and ex situ conservation with two examples each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>In situ conservation protects species in their natural habitats: national parks, sanctuaries, biosphere reserves, sacred groves. Ex situ conservation protects them outside the natural habitat: zoos, botanical gardens, seed banks, cryopreservation of gametes. In situ keeps the whole ecosystem and lets species keep evolving; ex situ is for species that are too threatened to survive in the wild.</details>

**10.** (3 marks) Describe Tilman's experiment and what it showed.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>David Tilman grew plots with different numbers of grassland species and measured total biomass over years. Plots with more species had higher biomass and less year-to-year variation in it. So more diverse communities are more productive and more stable.</details>

## 5-mark questions

**11.** (5 marks) Describe the four major causes of biodiversity loss ("the evil quartet"), giving an example of each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. **Habitat loss and fragmentation:** the most important cause. Tropical rain forests once covered about 14 % of land and now cover under 6 %. Fragmentation breaks the habitat into small patches that cannot hold animals with large ranges.
2. **Over-exploitation:** the over-use of resources, as by hunting and over-fishing. The passenger pigeon is an example.
3. **Alien species invasions:** introduced species harm natives. The Nile perch in Lake Victoria wiped out over 200 cichlid species; the water hyacinth clogs Indian water bodies.
4. **Co-extinctions:** when a species becomes extinct, the species that depend on it, such as a parasite or a pollinated plant, also die.

</details>

**12.** (5 marks) What are biodiversity hotspots? Describe the in situ methods used to conserve biodiversity.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Hotspots are regions with very high species richness, high endemism and a large loss of their habitat. They are chosen so that limited effort protects the most species. India has parts of the Western Ghats and Sri Lanka, the Himalaya and Indo-Burma hotspots.

In situ methods protect species in their natural habitat: national parks, wildlife sanctuaries and biosphere reserves (with core, buffer and transition zones), and sacred groves protected by local tradition.

</details>

## Case-based question

**Case.** A survey of birds in forest patches gives these data: a 100 km² patch has 40 species, and a 10 000 km² patch has 100 species. A road is planned through a 10 000 km² forest which will leave only 1000 km² in the large block.

**13a.** Calculate the value of Z from the data.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Z = (log 100 − log 40) ÷ (log 10 000 − log 100) = (2 − 1.602) ÷ (4 − 2) = 0.398 ÷ 2 = **0.2**.

</details>

**13b.** What is the expected number of species in the 1000 km² block?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

S = 40 × (1000/100)^0.2 = 40 × 10^0.2 = 40 × 1.585 = about **63** species.

</details>

**13c.** Which cause of biodiversity loss is this?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Habitat loss and fragmentation.</details>

**13d.** Suggest one in situ measure to reduce the harm.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Protect the remaining forest as a national park or wildlife sanctuary, and keep a wildlife corridor across the road so that animals can move between patches.</details>

## Assertion and reason

Options: (a) both A and R are true and R explains A; (b) both are true but R does not explain A; (c) A is true, R is false; (d) A is false, R is true.

**14.** **Assertion (A):** Species diversity falls from the equator to the poles. **Reason (R):** The tropics received more solar energy and stayed climatically stable for a long time.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Both true and R explains A.</details>

**15.** **Assertion (A):** The species-area relationship is a straight line on a log-log graph. **Reason (R):** S = C·A^Z, so log S is linear in log A.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Both true and R explains A.</details>

**16.** **Assertion (A):** Seed banks are an ex situ method of conservation. **Reason (R):** Seeds are stored in a cold store outside their natural habitat.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Both true and R explains A: ex situ means off site, and a seed bank holds seeds away from where the plants grow.</details>
