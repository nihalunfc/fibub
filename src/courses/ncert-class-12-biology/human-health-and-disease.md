**Biology · Class 12 · Chapter 7 · about 85 minutes.** By the end you will be able to name the common diseases of humans with their pathogens, explain how the body fights infection (innate and acquired immunity), say how vaccines work, describe allergy, autoimmunity, AIDS and cancer, and explain the harm of drugs and alcohol.

<div class="bigidea">Health is more than not being ill: it is a state of physical, mental and social well-being. Germs that cause disease (pathogens) face barriers and then a trained army of white blood cells with a memory. A vaccine trains that army without the disease. When the defences fail, as in AIDS, or turn on the body itself, as in autoimmune disease, or lose control of growth, as in cancer, health fails.</div>

## 1. Health and disease

**Health** is a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being. It depends on **genetic disorders** (born with it), **infections** (germs) and **lifestyle** (food, exercise, sleep, drugs). Good health means a balanced diet, regular exercise, awareness of diseases, personal hygiene, control of vectors, vaccination, proper disposal of waste and clean water.

**Disease** is any condition that stops the body working properly. **Infectious diseases** are caused by **pathogens** and spread from person to person; **non-infectious diseases** (like most cancers and many heart diseases) do not spread. The pathogens are viruses, bacteria, fungi, protozoa and worms (helminths). Which kind causes which disease matters, since antibiotics work on bacteria but not on viruses.

## 2. Common diseases in humans

| Disease | Pathogen | How it spreads | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typhoid | bacterium *Salmonella typhi* | contaminated food and water | enters the small intestine, then blood and other organs; sustained high fever (39-40 °C), weakness, stomach pain, headache; confirmed by the **Widal test** |
| Pneumonia | bacteria *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae* | droplets, shared glasses and utensils | infects the **alveoli** of the lungs, which fill with fluid; fever, chills, cough; lips and nails turn bluish in severe cases |
| Common cold | **rhinoviruses** | droplets from coughs and sneezes, contaminated objects | infects the nose and throat, not the lungs: blocked nose, running nose, sore throat, cough, headache; lasts 3 to 7 days |
| Dengue, chikungunya | viruses | bite of *Aedes* mosquito (breeds in clean stagnant water) | high fever, severe body pain; in dengue the platelet count may fall |
| Malaria | protozoan *Plasmodium* (*P. vivax*, *P. malariae*, *P. falciparum*) | bite of female *Anopheles* mosquito | parasites in the liver and red blood cells; chills and high fever recurring every 3 to 4 days; *P. falciparum* causes the most serious "malignant" malaria |
| Amoebiasis (amoebic dysentery) | protozoan *Entamoeba histolytica* | cysts in food and water, carried by houseflies | lives in the large intestine: constipation, cramps, stools with mucus and blood |
| Ascariasis | roundworm *Ascaris* | eggs in soil, water, vegetables from faeces | intestinal parasite: internal bleeding, anaemia, blocked intestine, fever |
| Filariasis | worms *Wuchereria bancrofti*, *W. malayi* | bite of female *Culex* mosquito | worms live in the **lymphatic vessels** of the legs and genitals: chronic swelling, **elephantiasis** |
| Ringworm | fungi *Microsporum*, *Trichophyton*, *Epidermophyton* | contact, shared towels, damp clothes | dry, scaly, itchy patches on skin, scalp, nails (not a worm!) |

<div class="myth">

**“Ringworm is caused by a worm” and “antibiotics cure a cold”.** Ringworm is a fungal infection, and the common cold is viral, so antibiotics do not help. Taking them only breeds resistant bacteria.

</div>

### The malaria cycle

Malaria shows how a pathogen can need two hosts. Sporozoites enter the blood from the mosquito bite, go to the liver cells and multiply, burst into the red blood cells, and multiply again. When the red cells burst together a toxin called **haemozoin** is released, which causes the chill and fever that returns every 48 or 72 hours depending on the species. Some parasites become **gametocytes**; a mosquito that bites picks them up, they fuse in its gut and sporozoites form in its salivary glands.

### Prevention and control

- **Personal hygiene:** clean body, wash hands, boil or filter drinking water, eat clean food (against typhoid, amoebiasis, ascariasis).
- **Public hygiene:** proper disposal of waste and excreta, periodic cleaning of water tanks, standard practices for water supply and food.
- **Vector control** (for malaria, dengue, chikungunya, filariasis): no stagnant water around the home (coolers, tyres, pots), use of mosquito nets and repellents, spraying insecticides, introducing the fish *Gambusia* into ponds to eat larvae, and closing doors and windows.
- **Vaccination** and early treatment.

## 3. Immunity

**Immunity** is the body's ability to fight disease-causing organisms. It has two kinds.

### Innate immunity

Innate immunity is present from birth and works against any germ. It has four types of barriers.

1. **Physical:** the skin; mucus coating the lining of the respiratory, gut and urogenital tracts, which traps microbes.
2. **Physiological:** stomach acid, saliva, tears and sweat.
3. **Cellular:** white blood cells (neutrophils, monocytes) and natural killer lymphocytes, and macrophages, which engulf germs (phagocytosis).
4. **Cytokine barriers:** virus-infected cells secrete **interferons**, protein signals that protect other cells.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c12b-health-layers"><figcaption>Switch the four barriers on or off. With all four on, almost no germs get through; remove the skin (a cut or burn) and the number jumps. (The numbers are illustrative.)</figcaption></figure>

### Acquired immunity

Acquired immunity is **specific** to a pathogen and has **memory**. The first encounter gives a slow, weak response, the **primary response**; a second encounter gives a quick, strong **secondary (anamnestic) response**. The two main cell types are **B lymphocytes**, which make antibodies (proteins) into the blood, and **T lymphocytes**, which do not make antibodies but help B cells and kill infected cells.

- **Humoral (antibody-mediated) immunity:** antibodies in the body fluids ("humors").
- **Cell-mediated immunity (CMI):** T cells; it is the cause of graft rejection (a transplanted organ is seen as foreign).

An **antibody** is a Y-shaped protein with **two heavy and two light chains** (H₂L₂). Different classes: **IgA, IgM, IgE, IgG**. Each fits one antigen.

### Active and passive immunity

- **Active immunity:** your own body makes antibodies after meeting an antigen, by infection or by a vaccine. It is slow (needs time) but long-lasting.
- **Passive immunity:** ready-made antibodies are given to you. The baby gets **IgG through the placenta** and **IgA in the mother's first milk, colostrum**. Antivenom for snakebite and antitoxin for tetanus are also passive. It is quick but short-lived.

### Vaccination and immunisation

A vaccine contains a **harmless form of the antigen**: killed or weakened germs, or just a piece (the hepatitis B vaccine is made from yeast by recombinant DNA). It causes a primary response and makes memory cells; if the real germ comes later, the secondary response is fast. **Edward Jenner** in 1796 used cowpox material to protect a boy from smallpox; the disease was declared eradicated in 1980. Common childhood vaccines protect from polio, diphtheria, pertussis (whooping cough), tetanus, measles, hepatitis B and others.

### Herd immunity

When most people in a community are immune, a disease finds it hard to spread and even the unvaccinated are partly protected. If one case infects `$R_0$` others in a fully susceptible population, spread stops once the immune fraction is more than `$1 - 1/R_0$`. Measles, with `$R_0$` of about 12, needs over 90 % coverage.

### Allergies and autoimmunity

**Allergy** is an exaggerated response of the immune system to certain antigens in the environment (**allergens**: dust, pollen, some foods, animal fur). The antibodies are **IgE**, which make mast cells release **histamine** and serotonin: sneezing, watery eyes, a runny nose, or in severe cases a drop in blood pressure. Treatment uses anti-histamines, adrenaline and steroids. **Autoimmunity** is when the body attacks its own cells (self vs non-self is confused), as in **rheumatoid arthritis**.

### The immune system and its organs

Lymphoid organs are where lymphocytes form and act. **Primary** organs (where they mature): the **bone marrow** (all blood cells, B cells mature) and the **thymus** (T cells mature; shrinks with age). **Secondary** organs (where they meet antigens): **spleen** (filters blood), **lymph nodes** (filter lymph), **tonsils**, **Peyer's patches** of the small intestine, the appendix, and the **mucosal-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT)** lining the airways, gut and urogenital tracts, about 50 % of the lymphoid tissue.

## 4. AIDS

**AIDS (acquired immuno deficiency syndrome)** is caused by **HIV**, the human immunodeficiency virus, a **retrovirus**: an envelope with an RNA genome and the enzyme reverse transcriptase.

**It spreads by:** sexual contact with an infected person; transfusion of contaminated blood and blood products; sharing contaminated needles and syringes (especially among drug users); from an infected mother to her child through the placenta. **It does not spread** by touching, shaking hands, hugging, sharing food, mosquitoes or toilets. People infected by HIV deserve care and respect, not fear.

<div class="step">HIV attaches to the CD4 receptor of a helper T cell (or macrophage).</div>
<div class="step">The envelope fuses with the cell membrane and the RNA enters.</div>
<div class="step">Reverse transcriptase makes DNA from the viral RNA.</div>
<div class="step">The DNA joins the host DNA (provirus).</div>
<div class="step">The cell makes viral RNA and proteins.</div>
<div class="step">New viruses assemble and bud out, damaging the cell and going on to infect more.</div>
</div>

After infection there is a period of weeks to years with few signs while the virus multiplies in macrophages and helper T cells and the **helper T cell count falls**. The person gets repeated infections (fever, diarrhoea, weight loss) that the body cannot fight off, and is open to infections by bacteria such as *Mycobacterium* and viruses and fungi: these are the AIDS stage. Diagnosis is by the **ELISA** test (an antibody test). **Antiretroviral drugs** (ART) control the virus and let people live long, healthy lives, but they do not remove it.

**Prevention** (spread of awareness by NACO, the National AIDS Control Organisation of India, WHO and NGOs): use blood from tested blood banks; use disposable needles and syringes; use safe sex (condoms), have one faithful partner; stop injecting drugs; test pregnant women who are at risk and treat them to protect the baby; counsel the public.

## 5. Cancer

Normal cells grow and divide in a controlled way. **Contact inhibition** stops them when they touch. **Cancer** cells have lost it: they divide again and again, forming a lump of cells called a **tumour**. A **benign** tumour stays in its place and is usually not dangerous. A **malignant** tumour is made of cells that **invade** nearby tissue and, by **metastasis**, cells break off, travel through blood or lymph and start new tumours elsewhere. Run it:

**Causes of cancer.** Cancer-causing agents are **carcinogens**. **Physical:** ionising radiation (X-rays, gamma rays), UV light (skin cancer). **Chemical:** tobacco smoke (lung cancer), vinyl chloride. **Biological:** **oncogenic viruses** carry oncogenes. Normal cells have **proto-oncogenes** (cellular oncogenes) which, if changed (mutated) or turned on too much, can turn a cell cancerous. Smoking is the biggest preventable cause: tobacco use is linked to cancers of the lung, mouth and throat.

**Detection and diagnosis.** A **biopsy** (a thin piece of tissue is stained and looked at under a microscope, histopathology), blood and bone-marrow tests for leukaemia, **radiography** (X-ray), **CT** (computed tomography) and **MRI** (magnetic resonance imaging) scans. Blood tests and molecular tests look for genes or antigens. Early detection means better cure.

**Treatment.** **Surgery** removes the tumour, **radiotherapy** kills cells with radiation (tumour cells are damaged more than normal ones), **chemotherapy** uses drugs that stop cell division (with side effects such as hair loss and anaemia because normal cells that divide quickly are hit too), and **immunotherapy** helps the immune system, for example by giving **α-interferon** or biological response modifiers.

## 6. Drugs and alcohol abuse

**Drugs** are chemicals that change how the body works. Used under a doctor's advice they help; used for pleasure, in the wrong dose, or without need, they are **drug abuse**. Common abused drugs:

| Group | Source | Main effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Opioids** (morphine, heroin or "smack") | morphine from the latex of the poppy *Papaver somniferum*; heroin is made from morphine | depress the nervous system by binding opioid receptors in the brain and gut; used as painkillers by doctors |
| **Cannabinoids** (marijuana, hashish, charas, ganja) | flowers and resin of *Cannabis sativa* | act on cannabinoid receptors, mostly in the brain; affect the heart and circulation |
| **Coca alkaloids** (cocaine) | coca plant *Erythroxylum coca* | stimulate the nervous system by interfering with dopamine transport: excitement and energy, high doses cause hallucinations |
| Other plants | *Atropa belladonna*, *Datura* | hallucinogens |
| Others | barbiturates, amphetamines, LSD, benzodiazepines | sedatives, stimulants and hallucinogens, some are used as medicine for depression and sleep problems |

**Tobacco** (nicotine) is smoked, chewed or sniffed: it raises the blood pressure and heart rate, and causes cancer, bronchitis, emphysema, coronary heart disease and gastric ulcers. Smoking also reduces the oxygen-carrying capacity of blood (carbon monoxide).

**Why do young people take drugs?** Curiosity, the wish to experiment, peer pressure, stress from study or family, easy availability and the idea that drugs are fashionable. **Adolescence** (12 to 18 years) is a period of fast physical and psychological change, so it is the time of greatest risk.

**Addiction** is a psychological attachment to the effect of a drug: the person needs it again and again. **Dependence** is the body's tolerance and the **withdrawal syndrome** (anxiety, shakiness, nausea, sweating) if the drug stops suddenly, which can be life-threatening and may push people to go back to the drug.

**Effects of abuse.** Reckless behaviour, vandalism and violence, depression, loss of interest in studies, an overdose (breathing failure, heart failure, coma, death), and spread of **AIDS and hepatitis B** through shared needles. Alcohol damages the liver (**cirrhosis**) and nervous system, causes accidents, and in pregnancy harms the baby. Some sportspersons take **anabolic steroids** and other drugs ("doping") to improve performance; they can cause liver damage, mood swings, high blood pressure, and in women a deeper voice, and in men reduced sperm production and breast development; doping is banned.

**Prevention and control.** Avoid undue peer pressure; get education and counselling; look for warning signs (sudden change in behaviour, loss of interest, drop in marks); talk to parents, teachers and friends; seek help from professionals, doctors and **de-addiction centres**; help the person find a healthy way of coping with stress, for instance sport, hobbies and friends.

<div class="keypoints">

- Health is physical, mental and social well-being. Infectious pathogens: virus, bacteria, fungi, protozoa, helminths.
- Typhoid (*Salmonella typhi*, Widal), pneumonia, common cold (rhinovirus), malaria (*Plasmodium*, female *Anopheles*, haemozoin), amoebiasis (*Entamoeba*), ascariasis, filariasis (*Wuchereria*, *Culex*), ringworm (fungi), dengue and chikungunya (*Aedes*).
- Innate immunity: physical, physiological, cellular and cytokine barriers. Acquired: specific, with memory; primary and secondary response; B cells (antibodies, humoral), T cells (CMI).
- Antibody: 2 heavy + 2 light chains; IgA, IgM, IgE, IgG. Active (own antibodies) and passive (colostrum, IgG, antivenom) immunity. Vaccines give memory.
- Allergy: IgE, histamine. Autoimmunity: rheumatoid arthritis. Primary organs: bone marrow, thymus; secondary: spleen, lymph nodes, MALT.
- AIDS: HIV is a retrovirus (reverse transcriptase) that kills helper T cells; ELISA test; spread by body fluids, blood, needles, mother to child.
- Cancer: loss of contact inhibition, benign vs malignant, metastasis; carcinogens; biopsy, imaging; surgery, radiation, chemo, immunotherapy.
- Opioids, cannabinoids, coca alkaloids; tobacco; addiction and dependence; prevention by education, counselling and help.

</div>
