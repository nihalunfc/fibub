## How to set out answers

1. For a "name the microbe" question, give the scientific name in full and the product.
2. For sewage treatment, name the stages in order (primary, secondary, digester) and what happens in each.
3. For biogas, say which bacteria, which raw material, which gas and what is left over.
4. For numerical questions, show the doubling formula N = N₀ × 2ⁿ or the percentage formula, with units.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the bacterium that makes curd from milk.

<details><summary>Answer</summary><i>Lactobacillus</i>, a lactic acid bacterium.</details>

2. Which fungus is the source of penicillin?

<details><summary>Answer</summary><i>Penicillium notatum</i>.</details>

3. Name the bacterium whose spores are used as a biological insecticide.

<details><summary>Answer</summary><i>Bacillus thuringiensis</i> (Bt).</details>

4. What are flocs?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Masses of bacteria associated with fungal filaments, formed in the aeration tank of a sewage plant.</details>

5. Name a free-living nitrogen-fixing bacterium.

<details><summary>Answer</summary><i>Azotobacter</i> (or <i>Azospirillum</i>).</details>

## 2-mark questions

6. Why does milk take longer to set into curd in winter?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The lactic acid bacteria grow slowly at low temperature, so lactic acid builds up slowly and the pH takes longer to reach the value at which casein coagulates.</details>

7. Explain what BOD is. What does a high BOD show?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>BOD (biochemical oxygen demand) is the amount of oxygen used by bacteria to break down the organic matter in one litre of water. A high BOD shows that the water has much organic matter and is highly polluted.</details>

8. What is the role of the yeast in the making of bread? Which gas is produced?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Baker's yeast ferments the sugar in the dough, releasing CO₂, which gets trapped and makes the dough rise and become soft and light.</details>

9. Why should antibiotics not be used for the common cold?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The cold is caused by viruses, and antibiotics do not act on viruses. Using them needlessly selects for resistant bacteria.</details>

10. State two advantages of biofertilisers over chemical fertilisers.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>They do not pollute the soil and water, are cheaper, improve soil structure and fertility over time and are a part of organic farming.</details>

## 3-mark questions

11. Describe the role of microbes in the production of biogas. Name the bacteria and the main gas.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Cattle dung is mixed with water in a closed digester. Anaerobic bacteria break the cellulose and other substances into small molecules and then methanogens (such as <i>Methanobacterium</i>) make methane. Biogas contains mostly methane (about 55 to 65 %) and CO₂. The gas is a fuel and the leftover slurry is manure.</details>

12. List the chemicals and the microbes that produce them: citric acid, acetic acid, butyric acid, lactic acid.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Citric acid: <i>Aspergillus niger</i>. Acetic acid: <i>Acetobacter aceti</i>. Butyric acid: <i>Clostridium butylicum</i>. Lactic acid: <i>Lactobacillus</i>.</details>

13. What are statins, streptokinase and cyclosporin A? Give the microbe and the use of each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Statins (<i>Monascus purpureus</i>): lower blood cholesterol. Streptokinase (<i>Streptococcus</i>): dissolves blood clots after a heart attack. Cyclosporin A (<i>Trichoderma polysporum</i>): immunosuppressant used in organ transplants.</details>

14. How do mycorrhiza and cyanobacteria help plants?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Mycorrhiza (such as <i>Glomus</i>) absorb phosphorus from the soil and give it to the plant and also give resistance to root pathogens and drought. Cyanobacteria (<i>Anabaena</i>, <i>Nostoc</i>) fix atmospheric nitrogen and are used as a biofertiliser in paddy fields; they add organic matter too.</details>

## 5-mark questions

15. Describe the treatment of sewage with its stages, naming the microbes involved.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Primary treatment is physical: screens remove floating debris, grit settles, and the solids settle as primary sludge, leaving the primary effluent. Secondary treatment is biological: the effluent goes into aeration tanks where air is pumped in, and aerobic bacteria grow as flocs (with fungal filaments) and digest the organic matter, so the BOD falls greatly. The effluent then goes to a settling tank where the flocs settle as activated sludge. Part of it is returned to the aeration tank; the rest goes to anaerobic digesters where anaerobic bacteria digest it, producing biogas (methane, H₂S and CO₂). The clear effluent is released into a river.

</details>

16. What is biocontrol? Describe three biocontrol agents and why they are better than chemical pesticides.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Biocontrol is the use of living organisms to control pests. Examples: the ladybird (eats aphids); <i>Bacillus thuringiensis</i> (Bt), whose toxin kills caterpillars; baculoviruses, which are species-specific and have no effect on non-target animals; <i>Trichoderma</i>, which controls plant diseases. Chemical pesticides are toxic, kill useful insects, pollute soil and water, leave residues in food and the pests become resistant, so biocontrol is safer and long-lasting.

</details>

## Case-based question

**Read this.** A farmer has six cattle. He builds a biogas plant, feeding it 60 kg of dung a day, and uses the gas for cooking. He then applies the slurry coming out of the plant as manure to his field of peas, whose root nodules he finds full of pink tissue. At the end of the year he notes that he buys much less LPG and fertiliser.

17. (i) Which bacteria produce the gas, and what is the main gas? (ii) Why is cattle dung a good raw material? (iii) If 1 kg of dung gives 0.036 m³ of gas, how much gas does the plant make per day? (iv) Name the nitrogen-fixing bacterium in the pea nodules.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(i) Methanogens such as <i>Methanobacterium</i>; the gas is methane. (ii) The dung already contains methanogens from the rumen and plenty of organic matter. (iii) 60 × 0.036 = 2.16 m³ per day. (iv) <i>Rhizobium</i>.

</details>

## Assertion-reason

Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

18. **A:** Aeration tanks in sewage plants have air pumped into them. **R:** The microbes that digest the organic matter there are aerobic.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A.</details>

19. **A:** Biogas can be made in a plant with no oxygen. **R:** Methanogens are strict anaerobes.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A: oxygen would stop the methanogens.</details>

20. **A:** Baculoviruses are used in integrated pest management. **R:** They harm many kinds of animals and plants.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(C) A is true. R is false: baculoviruses are species-specific and narrow-spectrum, with no harm to plants, mammals, birds or fish.</details>
