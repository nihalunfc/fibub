## How to set out answers

1. Give the cause (name the pathogen), the route of spread, the effect, and one way of prevention.
2. For immunity, name the cell and what it does: macrophage (engulfs, presents), helper T (activates), B/plasma (antibodies), killer T (destroys infected cells), memory cells (faster second response).
3. For a flow of events (HIV, vaccine), number the steps.
4. Use neat labelled diagrams: the antibody (2 heavy + 2 light chains), the HIV life cycle, the malaria cycle.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the test used to confirm typhoid.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The Widal test.</details>

2. Which toxin released from red blood cells causes the fever in malaria?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Haemozoin.</details>

3. Name the immunoglobulin that gives the baby passive immunity through colostrum.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>IgA.</details>

4. What is the full form of ELISA?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay.</details>

5. Name the plant source of morphine.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The opium poppy, <i>Papaver somniferum</i>.</details>

6. Name the vector of filariasis.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The female <i>Culex</i> mosquito.</details>

## 2-mark questions

7. Differentiate between active and passive immunity.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Active: the body makes its own antibodies after meeting an antigen (infection or vaccine); slow and long-lasting. Passive: ready-made antibodies are given (colostrum, antivenom); quick but short-lived.</details>

8. Why does a second dose or second infection produce a stronger antibody response?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The first exposure forms memory B and T cells. On the second meeting these respond faster and in larger numbers, the secondary response.</details>

9. What is the function of the thymus and the bone marrow in immunity?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>They are the primary lymphoid organs: B cells (and all blood cells) form and mature in the bone marrow; T cells mature in the thymus.</details>

10. State two ways in which HIV is NOT transmitted.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>By touching, shaking hands, hugging, sharing food, toilets, or mosquito bites.</details>

11. What is an allergen? Name the antibody and the chemical involved in an allergic reaction.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>An allergen is a substance that causes an exaggerated immune response (dust, pollen). IgE antibodies on mast cells cause the release of histamine.</details>

## 3-mark questions

12. How do vaccines work? Why is a booster dose given?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A vaccine has a harmless form of the antigen (killed or weakened pathogen, or a protein piece). It causes a primary response and memory cells, without the disease. If the real pathogen enters, memory cells give a quick strong response. A booster gives a second, bigger response and keeps the memory cell numbers high.</details>

13. Describe how HIV multiplies in the body.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>HIV enters a macrophage or helper T cell by the CD4 receptor. Its RNA is released and reverse transcriptase makes DNA from it. The DNA joins the host DNA. The cell makes viral RNA and proteins, which assemble into new viruses that bud out and infect other cells. The helper T cell count falls and immunity is lost.</details>

14. What are carcinogens? Give one physical, one chemical and one biological example.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Carcinogens are agents that can cause cancer. Physical: X-rays or UV. Chemical: tobacco smoke. Biological: oncogenic viruses, which carry oncogenes.</details>

15. List the ways to prevent drug abuse among adolescents.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Avoid undue peer pressure, education and counselling, spend free time in sports and hobbies, talk to parents and teachers, look for warning signs, seek medical help and de-addiction centres.</details>

## 5-mark questions

16. Explain the primary and secondary immune response with a diagram of antibody level against time. Add the roles of B cells, helper T cells and memory cells.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

On the first exposure there is a lag, then a slow, small rise in antibody (primary response). The antigen is shown by macrophages to helper T cells, which activate B cells; B cells become plasma cells (antibodies) and memory cells. On the second exposure memory cells react quickly: the antibody level rises sooner, higher and stays longer (secondary response). A graph shows a low first peak and a high second peak.

</details>

17. Describe the life cycle of Plasmodium in the human body and in the mosquito.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Sporozoites enter the blood from the bite of a female Anopheles and go to the liver, multiply, then infect the red blood cells where they multiply and burst the cells, releasing haemozoin (fever every 48 to 72 hours). Some form gametocytes. A mosquito takes gametocytes in a blood meal; they fuse in its gut to form zygotes, which become sporozoites that go to the salivary glands, ready to infect the next person.

</details>

## Case-based question

**Read this.** In a village a number of people have fever and chills every second day. The doctor finds parasites in red blood cells. Near the houses there are open water tanks, and mosquitoes breed there. A health worker suggests nets, removing stagnant water and stocking the pond with the fish *Gambusia*.

18. (i) Name the disease and the likely pathogen. (ii) Name the vector. (iii) Why do the symptoms recur every second day? (iv) How does *Gambusia* help?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(i) Malaria, caused by Plasmodium (for example P. vivax). (ii) The female Anopheles mosquito. (iii) The parasites multiply in red blood cells and burst them together every 48 hours, releasing haemozoin, which causes chills and fever. (iv) The fish eat mosquito larvae, so the number of mosquitoes falls (biological vector control).

</details>

## Assertion-reason

Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

19. **A:** A person with AIDS suffers many infections. **R:** HIV reduces the number of helper T cells.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A: without helper T cells, B and killer T cells are not properly activated.</details>

20. **A:** Antibiotics cure dengue. **R:** Dengue is caused by a virus.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(D) A is false: antibiotics do not act on viruses. R is true.</details>

21. **A:** Cancer cells form tumours. **R:** They have lost contact inhibition and keep dividing.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A.</details>

- Know each disease with its pathogen, route and organ. Widal (typhoid), haemozoin (malaria fever), Culex (filariasis), Aedes (dengue), Anopheles (malaria).
- Innate (4 barriers) and acquired (specific, memory). B cells make antibodies (H₂L₂); T cells help and kill. Active vs passive.
- Vaccines create memory cells; a second dose gives a bigger, faster response. Herd immunity threshold is 1 − 1/R₀.
- HIV: retrovirus, reverse transcriptase, kills helper T cells, ELISA test, spreads by body fluids only.
- Cancer: no contact inhibition, tumour, metastasis; carcinogens; biopsy, scans, surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy.
- Drugs: opioids, cannabinoids, coca alkaloids; tobacco; addiction, dependence, withdrawal; prevention is education, counselling, avoiding peer pressure.
