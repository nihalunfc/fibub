**Practice · Class 12 Biology · Chapter 3.** Read the chapter lesson, [Reproductive Health](/learn/courses/ncert-class-12-biology/reproductive-health/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Most questions are about naming a method and saying how it works, or about a number: growth rate, doubling time, fertility rate or sex ratio. For methods, learn the pair "name and mechanism". For numbers, write the formula first.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For a list question ("name three...") give names first, then a few words each.
2. For a mechanism question, say what the method stops: ovulation, fertilisation or implantation.
3. For a numerical, write the formula, substitute, and give the unit.
4. Keep language neutral and factual in questions about sexual health.

## 1-mark questions

1. Expand RCH.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Reproductive and Child Health (care programmes).</details>

2. Name the surgical method of birth control in females.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Tubectomy (cutting and tying the fallopian tubes).</details>

3. Name a non-steroidal contraceptive pill developed in India.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Saheli.</details>

4. Which STI can be prevented by a vaccine?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Hepatitis B.</details>

5. What does ICSI stand for?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Intra-cytoplasmic sperm injection.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

6. (2) Why is amniocentesis banned for finding the sex of a foetus?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It was misused to find the sex of the foetus so that female foetuses were aborted, which lowered the sex ratio. It is allowed only to detect genetic disorders of the foetus.</details>

7. (2) What are the features of an ideal contraceptive?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>User-friendly, easily available, effective, reversible, with no or few side effects, and not interfering with sexual drive or the act.</details>

8. (2) Give two reasons for the population explosion.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A fall in death rate (better health care, food and sanitation) and a fall in infant and maternal mortality, while the birth rate stayed high.</details>

9. (3) Explain how the oral contraceptive pill prevents pregnancy.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It contains progestogen alone or with oestrogen. These keep FSH and LH low, so there is no LH surge and no ovulation. They also thicken the cervical mucus so sperm cannot pass, and make the uterus unsuitable for implantation.</details>

10. (3) Differentiate between ZIFT and GIFT.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>ZIFT: a zygote or an embryo of up to 8 blastomeres, formed in the laboratory (in vitro), is placed in the fallopian tube. GIFT: eggs (from a donor or the woman) are placed in the fallopian tube and fertilisation takes place inside the body.</details>

11. (3) Why do many STIs remain untreated and what problems can they cause?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Early symptoms are often absent or mild (itching, a discharge), and some people are embarrassed to see a doctor. Untreated, STIs can cause pelvic inflammatory disease, infertility, ectopic pregnancy, and infection of the baby.</details>

## 5-mark questions

12. (5) Describe the main types of contraceptive methods with one example of each and how it acts.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Natural: periodic abstinence, avoids intercourse near ovulation. Barrier: condom, stops sperm reaching the egg and lowers STI risk. IUDs: CuT, copper ions reduce sperm motility and fertilising ability. Hormonal: oral pills or implants, stop ovulation and implantation and thicken cervical mucus. Surgical: vasectomy (cut and tie the vas deferens) or tubectomy (cut and tie the fallopian tubes); permanent.</details>

13. (5) Explain the statement "Population is growing fast although the birth rate has fallen". Suggest measures to control the growth.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A fall in the death rate and a large number of young people entering the child-bearing age mean that births still exceed deaths, so the population keeps growing. Measures: awareness and education (especially of girls), easily available contraception, raising the age of marriage, encouraging small families with incentives, and safe health care so that parents expect their children to survive.</details>

14. (5) Describe the steps of IVF and embryo transfer and say when the embryo is placed in the fallopian tube and when in the uterus.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The ovaries are stimulated and eggs are collected; sperm are collected; egg and sperm are mixed in a culture dish for fertilisation in vitro; the zygote divides to an early embryo. A zygote or an embryo with up to 8 blastomeres is transferred into the fallopian tube (ZIFT); an embryo with more than 8 blastomeres into the uterus (IUT). The embryo implants and grows in the mother as in a natural pregnancy.</details>

## Case-based question

In a town the male population is 40 000 and the sex ratio was 920 ten years ago. A health worker finds that, in a clinic, couples were learning the sex of the unborn child and ending pregnancies when it was a girl.

15. (a) How many females lived in the town ten years ago? (b) What is the term for the test used by the clinic, and for what is it meant? (c) Name the law that bans this use. (d) Suggest two steps the community can take.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) 40 000 × 920 ÷ 1000 = 36 800 females. (b) Amniocentesis (or other prenatal sex determination); it is meant only to detect genetic disorders. (c) The PCPNDT Act (the law bans sex determination before birth). (d) Awareness about the equal value of girls and boys; reporting illegal tests; education and incentives for girls.</details>

## Assertion and reason

Choose: (a) both true and R explains A; (b) both true but R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

16. **A:** Condoms help to reduce the risk of STIs. **R:** They form a barrier that stops contact with body fluids.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a).</details>

17. **A:** Copper IUDs prevent fertilisation. **R:** Copper ions increase the movement of sperm.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(c). A is true. R is false: copper ions reduce sperm motility and fertilising ability.</details>

18. **A:** MTP is safest in the first trimester. **R:** MTP is a regular method of birth control.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(c). A is true. R is false: MTP is for specific medical and legal reasons, not a regular method of birth control.</details>

- MTP is legal under the MTP Act and safest in the first trimester. Sex determination for choosing a child’s sex is banned.
- ART: IVF-ET, ZIFT (up to 8 cells into the tube), IUT (more than 8 into the uterus), GIFT, ICSI, AI/IUI.
