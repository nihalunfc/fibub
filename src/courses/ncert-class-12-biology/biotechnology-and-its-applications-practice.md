1. For a mechanism (Bt toxin, RNAi, gene therapy), write the steps in order with the key word of each in bold.
2. For "uses of" questions, give a number of separate points, each with an example.
3. For ethical questions, give a balanced view: benefit and risk, and who decides (GEAC).
4. Draw a flow diagram when it saves words.

## 1-mark questions

**1.** What is a GMO?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A genetically modified organism: a plant, animal, bacterium or fungus whose genes have been altered by gene manipulation.</details>

**2.** Name the two Bt genes that control cotton bollworms.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>cryIAc and cryIIAb.</details>

**3.** How many amino acids are in chain A and chain B of human insulin?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Chain A has 21 and chain B has 30.</details>

**4.** Name the vector commonly used to carry a gene into lymphocytes in ADA gene therapy.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A disarmed retrovirus (retroviral vector).</details>

**5.** What does ELISA stand for?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

**6.** (2 marks) Why do Bt toxin crystals harm an insect but not the person who eats Bt cotton oil or food?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The crystal is an inactive protoxin that dissolves only in the alkaline pH of the insect gut, where it is activated. The toxin then binds to receptors on insect gut cells. The human gut is acidic and has no such receptors, so the protein is not activated or toxic to us.</details>

**7.** (2 marks) What is meant by RNA interference?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A cellular defence in which double-stranded RNA that is complementary to a particular mRNA binds to it and silences it, so the mRNA is not translated into protein.</details>

**8.** (3 marks) Why was making human insulin in *E. coli* difficult, and how was the problem overcome in 1983?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Human insulin is made as proinsulin with a C peptide that is removed to give the mature A and B chains. Bacteria cannot do this maturation. Eli Lilly prepared DNA for chains A and B separately, put them in plasmids of <i>E. coli</i>, made each chain in a different culture, extracted them and joined them by disulphide bonds.</details>

**9.** (3 marks) Give three uses of transgenic animals with an example for each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Disease models (mice for cancer or Alzheimer's); biological products (human alpha-1-antitrypsin for emphysema, or Rosie's milk with human alpha-lactalbumin); vaccine safety (transgenic mice to test the polio vaccine); chemical safety testing (animals more sensitive to toxins).</details>

**10.** (3 marks) What is biopiracy? Name one case from India.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Biopiracy is the use of bioresources and the traditional knowledge about them by companies or organisations of other countries without proper authorisation and without paying the people or country that hold them. Examples are the patents granted on basmati rice, turmeric and neem to foreign firms, which were challenged.</details>

## 5-mark questions

**11.** (5 marks) Explain how ADA deficiency is treated by gene therapy. Why is it not a permanent cure, and how could it be made permanent?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ADA (adenosine deaminase) is needed for a working immune system; a child without it has severe immune deficiency.

1. Lymphocytes are taken from the patient's blood and grown in a culture.
2. A functional ADA cDNA is introduced into the lymphocytes with a retroviral vector.
3. The modified lymphocytes are returned to the patient.

Lymphocytes are not immortal; they die after some time, so the patient needs periodic infusions. A permanent cure would need the working gene to be put into the early embryonic cells or bone-marrow cells that make lymphocytes, so that new lymphocytes always carry it.

</details>

**12.** (5 marks) Describe the mechanism of RNAi used to protect tobacco plants from the nematode *Meloidogyne incognita*.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. The nematode infects the roots and reduces yield.
2. Using Agrobacterium vectors, genes were introduced into tobacco that make sense and antisense RNA of a gene of the nematode.
3. The two RNAs, being complementary, pair to form double-stranded RNA (dsRNA).
4. The dsRNA silences the nematode's specific mRNA that enters the host cell (RNA interference), so the protein cannot be made.
5. The parasite cannot survive in the transgenic host, so the plant is resistant.

</details>

## Case-based question

**Case.** A cotton farmer in Maharashtra plants Bt cotton. Over the season, bollworm damage stays low and he sprays only twice instead of eight times. A neighbour says the Bt seed is "poison for people". At the village meeting, an agriculture officer explains that the seeds were approved by GEAC.

**13a.** What is Bt cotton?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A GM cotton in which a cry gene from <i>Bacillus thuringiensis</i> has been put, so the plant makes a protein toxic to bollworms.</details>

**13b.** Why does the toxin not harm people?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It is an inactive protoxin that needs the alkaline gut and specific receptors of insects to work.</details>

**13c.** What is GEAC?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee of the Government of India, which decides the safety of GM research and of putting GM organisms into use.</details>

**13d.** Give one benefit and one possible risk of Bt cotton.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Benefit: less insecticide, lower cost. Risk: pests may become resistant over time, or the gene may spread to wild relatives, so monitoring and refuge plantings are needed.</details>

## Assertion and reason

Options: (a) both A and R are true and R explains A; (b) both are true but R does not explain A; (c) A is true, R is false; (d) A is false, R is true.

**14.** **Assertion (A):** PCR is useful for early detection of HIV. **Reason (R):** PCR can amplify the nucleic acid of the virus even when only a few copies are present.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Both true and R explains A.</details>

**15.** **Assertion (A):** Insulin from *E. coli* needs the A and B chains to be made separately in the first method. **Reason (R):** Bacteria cannot remove the C peptide from proinsulin.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Both true and R explains A: the chains were made separately because the bacterium could not carry out the maturation.</details>

**16.** **Assertion (A):** Rosie's milk was a more balanced food for babies. **Reason (R):** Rosie's milk lacked protein.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(c) A is true, R is false. Rosie's milk was enriched with human alpha-lactalbumin.</details>
