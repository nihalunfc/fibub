## 1-mark questions

1. What is the function of the tapetum?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It nourishes the developing pollen grains and supplies material for their walls.</details>

2. Name the structure through which the pollen tube comes out of the pollen grain.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A germ pore, a thin place in the exine.</details>

3. Which cells of the embryo sac help to guide the pollen tube?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The two synergids, through the filiform apparatus.</details>

4. What is the ploidy of the primary endosperm nucleus?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Triploid (3n).</details>

5. Name a plant in which polyembryony is common.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Citrus (orange, lemon) or mango.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

6. (2) Differentiate between geitonogamy and xenogamy.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Geitonogamy: pollen goes to another flower of the <b>same plant</b>; genetically it is self-pollination. Xenogamy: pollen goes to a flower of a <b>different plant</b>; the only kind that brings new genes.</details>

7. (2) Why is the exine of a pollen grain made of sporopollenin useful to scientists?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Sporopollenin resists acids, alkalis and enzymes, so pollen is preserved as fossils. Its patterns identify plants, which helps to study past vegetation and climate.</details>

8. (3) A flowering plant has 2n = 24. Give the number of chromosomes in the pollen mother cell, microspore, egg and endosperm cell. Explain the endosperm number.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Pollen mother cell 24, microspore 12, egg 12, endosperm 36. The endosperm is 3n because it forms from one male gamete (12) fusing with two polar nuclei (12 + 12).</details>

9. (3) Name three outbreeding devices and explain how each stops self-pollination.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(i) Dichogamy: pollen and stigma ripen at different times. (ii) Herkogamy: anther and stigma are in different positions. (iii) Self-incompatibility: the pistil stops the tube of its own pollen. (Also unisexual flowers, especially dioecious plants.)</details>

10. (3) Why does a farmer growing hybrid maize have to buy new seed every year, and how could apomixis help?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Hybrid seeds give plants with high yield only in the first generation; in the next generation the genes are reshuffled and the good combination is lost. Apomictic seeds are clones of the mother, so the hybrid would be copied each year and the seeds could be saved and sown again.</details>

## 5-mark questions

11. (5) Describe the development of a mature embryo sac from the megaspore mother cell. State the number of cells and nuclei at the end.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The megaspore mother cell (2n) in the nucellus divides by meiosis into four haploid megaspores; three degenerate and one is functional. The functional megaspore divides by mitosis three times (1 → 2 → 4 → 8 nuclei), with 4 nuclei at each pole. Walls form: three cells at the micropylar end (egg and two synergids, the egg apparatus), three antipodal cells at the chalazal end, and a large central cell with two polar nuclei. The sac has <b>7 cells and 8 nuclei</b>.</details>

12. (5) Explain double fertilisation. Why is it called "double", and what are its products?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The pollen tube enters through the micropyle into a synergid and releases two male gametes (n). One fuses with the egg (syngamy) to form the diploid zygote. The other fuses with the two polar nuclei (triple fusion) to form the triploid primary endosperm nucleus. Two fusions occur in one embryo sac, so it is double fertilisation, found only in angiosperms. Products: zygote → embryo; PEN → endosperm. Afterwards the ovule becomes the seed and the ovary the fruit.</details>

13. (5) Describe the steps of artificial hybridisation and give the reason for each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(1) Emasculation: remove the anthers of the female parent in the bud stage so self-pollen cannot fall on its stigma. (2) Bagging: cover with a paper or muslin bag to keep out foreign pollen. (3) When the stigma is ripe, dust it with pollen from the chosen male parent to ensure the desired cross. (4) Rebag to protect from stray pollen until the fruit sets. If the female plant has only female flowers, emasculation is not needed.</details>

## Case-based question

A student cuts open a coconut. The hollow centre holds a watery liquid, and a white layer lines the shell. She also cuts open a pea pod: the seeds have no white food layer, only two big green halves.

14. (a) What is the watery liquid in the coconut? (b) What is the white layer? (c) What is the name for seeds like the pea that lose their endosperm? (d) Where is the food stored in a pea seed?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Free-nuclear endosperm (tender coconut water), with many free nuclei. (b) Cellular endosperm, formed later when walls appear. (c) Non-albuminous (ex-albuminous) seeds. (d) In the two cotyledons, since the endosperm was used up while the seed developed.</details>

## Assertion and reason

Choose: (a) both true and R explains A; (b) both true but R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

15. **A:** Pollen grains can be found as fossils. **R:** The exine is made of sporopollenin, which is highly resistant.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). Sporopollenin does not break down, so the grains are preserved.</details>

16. **A:** Cleistogamy ensures seed set even in the absence of pollinators. **R:** Cleistogamous flowers promote cross-pollination.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(c). A is true: the pollen falls on the stigma inside the closed flower. R is false: cleistogamy is always self-pollination.</details>

17. **A:** The embryo sac has seven cells but eight nuclei. **R:** The two polar nuclei lie in a single central cell.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). The central cell is one cell with two nuclei, so cells = 7 and nuclei = 8.</details>
