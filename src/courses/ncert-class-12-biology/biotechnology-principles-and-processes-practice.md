**Practice · Class 12 Biology · Chapter 9.** Read the chapter lesson, [Biotechnology: Principles and Processes](/learn/courses/ncert-class-12-biology/biotechnology-principles-and-processes/), first. Try each question on paper before you open its answer.

<div class="bigidea">Three habits win marks here: name the enzyme or step exactly (EcoRI, Taq, ligase, lysozyme), give the temperature and role for each PCR step, and for any plasmid question say which gene was broken and what the cell can and cannot grow on.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For a definition, give the term, then one line of what it does or means, then an example.
2. For a process (PCR, cloning, bioreactor), write numbered steps in the correct order with the key word of each step in bold.
3. For a numerical, write the formula (N = N₀ × 2ⁿ, sites = L / 4ⁿ), substitute, and give the unit.
4. Draw a neat labelled diagram when asked: a plasmid, a PCR cycle or a bioreactor. Label ori, the resistance genes and the cloning site.

## 1-mark questions

**1.** Name the bacterium from which the heat-stable DNA polymerase used in PCR is obtained.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>*Thermus aquaticus*, a bacterium of hot springs. Its enzyme is called Taq polymerase.</details>

**2.** What is a palindromic sequence in DNA? Give one example.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A sequence of base pairs that reads the same on both strands when each is read from its 5′ end, for example GAATTC (EcoRI site).</details>

**3.** Why is DNA seen as orange bands in a gel stained with ethidium bromide?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Ethidium bromide inserts between the stacked bases of DNA and fluoresces orange when exposed to UV light.</details>

**4.** State the function of DNA ligase in recombinant DNA technology.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It joins the sugar-phosphate backbones of the vector and the insert (forming phosphodiester bonds), sealing the nicks left after the sticky ends have paired.</details>

**5.** Which enzyme is used to remove RNA when isolating DNA?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Ribonuclease (RNase).</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

**6.** (2 marks) Why is the cloning vector's origin of replication essential?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Without an origin of replication (ori) the vector, and the foreign gene attached to it, cannot be copied in the host. Ori is the start point of replication and also decides the copy number: a high-copy ori gives many copies of the gene per cell.</details>

**7.** (2 marks) Distinguish exonucleases from endonucleases.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Exonucleases remove nucleotides from the free ends of a DNA strand. Endonucleases cut the DNA at a position inside the strand. Restriction enzymes are endonucleases that cut at a specific recognition site.</details>

**8.** (3 marks) How is a bacterial cell made competent to take up a recombinant plasmid?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The cells are treated with a divalent cation such as calcium chloride, which makes pores in the cell wall and increases the chance of DNA entry. They are then incubated on ice with the recombinant DNA, given a heat shock at 42 °C for a short time, and returned to ice. The temperature change opens the pores briefly so the DNA enters.</details>

**9.** (3 marks) A circular plasmid of 5000 bp is cut with EcoRI and gives two fragments of 1500 bp and 3500 bp. How many EcoRI sites does it have? Explain.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Two sites. A circular DNA has no free ends, so *n* cuts give *n* fragments. Two fragments mean two cuts. Check: 1500 + 3500 = 5000 bp, the whole plasmid.

</details>

**10.** (3 marks) Write three requirements of a good cloning vector and explain one.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>An origin of replication, a selectable marker (such as an antibiotic-resistance gene) and a unique cloning site for a restriction enzyme. The selectable marker lets you pick out the cells that have taken up the vector, because only they survive on the antibiotic.</details>

## 5-mark questions

**11.** (5 marks) Describe the steps of one PCR cycle, giving the temperature and the role of each. How many copies result from 8 cycles starting with one DNA molecule?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. **Denaturation (about 94 °C):** heat breaks the hydrogen bonds so the two template strands separate.
2. **Annealing (about 54 °C):** the mixture is cooled and the two primers (short single-stranded DNA) bind to the complementary ends of the target on each strand.
3. **Extension (72 °C):** heat-stable Taq polymerase adds nucleotides to the 3′ end of each primer and builds the new strand using the template.

One cycle changes one double strand into two. After 8 cycles the number is 2⁸ = **256** copies (taking perfect doubling).

</details>

**12.** (5 marks) Explain insertional inactivation with pBR322. How would you pick out a recombinant plasmid?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

pBR322 has two resistance genes: ampicillin (ampR) and tetracycline (tetR). The BamHI site lies inside tetR. When a foreign gene is ligated into BamHI, the tetR gene is broken: this is **insertional inactivation**.

To select: grow the transformed bacteria on ampicillin. Colonies that grow carry a plasmid (empty or recombinant). Then replica-plate them onto tetracycline: colonies with an empty vector still grow, but recombinants, whose tetR gene is broken, do not. The colonies that grow on ampicillin and not on tetracycline are the recombinants. Cells without any plasmid fail on both antibiotics.

</details>

## Case-based question

**Case.** A student isolates plant DNA, cuts it and the plasmid pBR322 with BamHI, mixes them with DNA ligase and transforms *E. coli*. She plates the cells on three plates: no antibiotic, ampicillin, and tetracycline. The first two plates have colonies. The tetracycline plate has none for the cells she believes carry the insert.

**13a.** Why did she use ampicillin?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Ampicillin lets only the cells that took up a plasmid (with a working ampR gene) grow. Cells without a plasmid die.</details>

**13b.** Why no growth on tetracycline for recombinants?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>BamHI cuts inside the tetR gene. The insert broke the gene (insertional inactivation), so the recombinant cells are sensitive to tetracycline.</details>

**13c.** How could the plant gene be amplified before cloning?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>By PCR, with two primers that match the ends of the gene and Taq polymerase.</details>

**13d.** Name the enzyme that would join the plant DNA to the plasmid.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>DNA ligase.</details>

## Assertion and reason

Options: (a) both A and R are true and R explains A; (b) both are true but R does not explain A; (c) A is true, R is false; (d) A is false, R is true.

**14.** **Assertion (A):** Fragments of DNA move toward the anode during gel electrophoresis. **Reason (R):** DNA is negatively charged because of its phosphate groups.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true and R explains A (option a).</details>

**15.** **Assertion (A):** Taq polymerase is used in PCR instead of an ordinary DNA polymerase. **Reason (R):** Taq polymerase is not denatured at 94 °C.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true and R explains A (option a): the enzyme must survive the repeated denaturation steps.</details>

**16.** **Assertion (A):** A restriction enzyme cuts only at its recognition site. **Reason (R):** Restriction enzymes cut every DNA at random positions.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is true, R is false (option c). The enzyme cuts only at a specific palindromic sequence.</details>

- PCR: 94 °C, 54 °C, 72 °C; copies = N₀ × 2ⁿ.
- Circular DNA: n cuts give n pieces. Linear DNA: n cuts give n + 1 pieces.
