<div class="bigidea">Three habits win marks here: write strands with their 5′ and 3′ ends, pair bases carefully (A-T or A-U, G-C), and name the enzyme or the experiment for each step. For a diagram, label the parts and give one line on what each does.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For a sequence question, write the template, then the pairing base by base. Mark 5′ and 3′ ends.
2. For an experiment, give the aim, what was done, what was seen and what it showed.
3. For a process (replication, transcription, translation), list the steps in order with the enzyme for each.
4. Draw neat, labelled diagrams: a nucleotide, a transcription unit, the lac operon (on and off).

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the sugar present in RNA.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Ribose.</details>

2. What is the bond between two nucleotides in a strand called?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A phosphodiester bond (between the 3′-OH of one sugar and the 5′ phosphate of the next).</details>

3. Which enzyme joins Okazaki fragments?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>DNA ligase.</details>

4. Name the codon that acts as the start signal.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>AUG (it also codes for methionine).</details>

5. What is a polysome?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Several ribosomes reading one mRNA at the same time, making many copies of the protein.</details>

6. Expand VNTR.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Variable number of tandem repeats.</details>

## 2-mark questions

7. State two differences between DNA and RNA.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>DNA has deoxyribose and thymine and is usually a double strand; RNA has ribose and uracil and is usually single stranded.</details>

8. Why did Hershey and Chase use 32P and 35S as labels?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>DNA contains phosphorus but no sulphur, protein contains sulphur but almost no phosphorus. So each label marks only one of the two molecules and shows which one enters the bacterium.</details>

9. What does it mean to say the genetic code is degenerate? Give one effect.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Most amino acids are coded by more than one codon. A change in the third base often leaves the amino acid unchanged, which protects the protein from some mutations.</details>

10. Why is replication on the lagging strand discontinuous?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>DNA polymerase can add nucleotides only to a 3′-OH end, so it builds 5′ to 3′. The lagging template runs the wrong way for continuous copying as the fork opens, so short Okazaki fragments are made, each from its own primer.</details>

11. A bacterium grows well on glucose but not on lactose. When lactose alone is given it starts making β-galactosidase. Explain in one or two lines.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Lactose acts as the inducer: it binds the repressor, which leaves the operator, so the lac genes (including z, which codes for β-galactosidase) are transcribed.</details>

## 3-mark questions

12. Describe the Meselson-Stahl experiment and its result in brief.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>E. coli was grown for many generations in 15N medium, so its DNA was heavy. The cells were moved to 14N medium. DNA from samples taken every generation was spun in a caesium chloride density gradient. After one generation all the DNA was of intermediate density; after two generations half was intermediate and half light. This fits only semi-conservative replication, where each daughter has one old and one new strand.</details>

13. Write the stages of translation with one line for each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Charging: each amino acid is joined to its tRNA. Initiation: the small subunit and initiator tRNA bind AUG and the large subunit joins. Elongation: tRNAs enter the A site and peptide bonds form; the ribosome moves along (translocation). Termination: a stop codon is reached and a release factor frees the polypeptide.</details>

14. A template strand reads 3′-TACGGCATT-5′. Write the mRNA and the amino acids it codes for (AUG Met, CCG Pro, UAA stop).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>mRNA: 5′-AUGCCGUAA-3′. Reading: AUG = Met, CCG = Pro, UAA = stop. The chain is Met-Pro.</details>

15. What are the main uses of DNA fingerprinting? Name the repeat sequences it relies on.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It relies on VNTRs, which differ in repeat number between people. Uses: forensic matching of crime-scene samples, settling paternity, identifying remains and studying genetic diversity of populations.</details>

## 5-mark questions

16. Describe the Watson-Crick model of DNA. Draw a labelled diagram of a short stretch.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Two polynucleotide chains form a right-handed double helix. The sugar-phosphate backbones are on the outside and the bases inside. The chains are antiparallel (5′ to 3′ and 3′ to 5′). A pairs with T by two hydrogen bonds and G with C by three, so a purine always faces a pyrimidine and the width stays 2 nm. One turn is 3.4 nm and holds 10 base pairs, which are 0.34 nm apart. The chains are complementary, so each can serve as a template.

</details>

17. Explain transcription in a bacterium and the extra steps in a eukaryote.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

A transcription unit has a promoter, a structural gene and a terminator. RNA polymerase binds the promoter, opens the DNA and reads the template strand 3′ to 5′, adding ribonucleotides (A, U, G, C) to the 3′ end of the mRNA. At the terminator the RNA is released. In a eukaryote the first RNA (hnRNA) has exons and introns. Introns are removed by splicing, a cap (methyl guanosine) is added at the 5′ end and a poly-A tail at the 3′ end. The mature mRNA leaves the nucleus.

</details>

18. Explain the working of the lac operon in the absence and presence of lactose, with a diagram.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The lac operon has the structural genes z, y and a, with a promoter and an operator, and a separate gene i that makes the repressor. Without lactose the repressor binds the operator and blocks RNA polymerase, so the genes are off. With lactose, lactose (inducer) binds the repressor, which changes shape and leaves the operator, so the polymerase transcribes z, y and a. β-galactosidase splits lactose, permease brings lactose in and transacetylase also acts. When lactose is gone the repressor returns and the operon is switched off.

</details>

## Case-based question

**Read this.** A student studies a short gene whose coding region on the mRNA reads 5′-AUG GCU ACU GAA CCU UAA-3′. She makes three mutant versions: (a) the second base of the fourth codon GAA changes to U; (b) one base is deleted from the first codon; (c) the first base of the last codon changes to G.

19. (i) Write the protein from the original mRNA (AUG Met, GCU Ala, ACU Thr, GAA Glu, CCU Pro, UAA stop). (ii) Which kind of mutation is (a), and what happens? (iii) What happens in (b)? (iv) What is the effect in (c), where UAA becomes GAA?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(i) Met-Ala-Thr-Glu-Pro, then stop. (ii) (a) GAA becomes GUA, so Glu becomes Val: a point (missense) mutation changing one amino acid, as in sickle-cell haemoglobin. (iii) (b) is a deletion that shifts the reading frame (a frameshift): every codon after it is read wrongly, so the protein is almost completely changed or ends early. (iv) (c) The stop codon is lost: GAA codes for Glu, so translation continues past the end of the gene and the protein is longer.

</details>

## Assertion-reason

Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

20. **A:** In the Hershey-Chase experiment, the supernatant after centrifuging held the radioactive sulphur. **R:** Protein coats of the virus stay outside the bacterial cell.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A: the coats were shaken off and stayed in the liquid, carrying the 35S.</details>

21. **A:** The lagging strand is made in short pieces. **R:** DNA polymerase can add nucleotides only in the 5′ to 3′ direction.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true, and R explains A: the lagging template runs the wrong way for continuous copying.</details>

22. **A:** The mRNA has the same base sequence as the template strand. **R:** The mRNA is made by copying the template strand.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(D) A is false: the mRNA is complementary to the template and matches the coding strand. R is true: the template is what RNA polymerase reads.</details>

- Pair A-T (2 bonds), G-C (3 bonds); strands antiparallel; 10 bp and 3.4 nm per turn; Chargaff A = T, G = C.
- DNA is the genetic material (Griffith, Avery, Hershey-Chase); replication is semi-conservative (Meselson-Stahl) with leading and lagging strands.
- mRNA is complementary to the template and matches the coding strand with U for T; eukaryotic hnRNA is spliced, capped and tailed.
- The code: 64 triplets, 61 sense, 3 stop, AUG start, degenerate, unambiguous. A frameshift changes all later amino acids.
- Lac operon: no lactose means the repressor blocks the operator; lactose is the inducer.
- DNA fingerprinting uses VNTR patterns (cut, gel, blot, probe).
