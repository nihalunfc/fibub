**Practice · Class 12 Biology · Chapter 12.** Read the chapter lesson, [Ecosystem](/learn/courses/ncert-class-12-biology/ecosystem/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Ecosystem answers lose marks for two mistakes: mixing up energy (one-way, lost as heat) with matter (cycles), and forgetting that only the pyramid of energy is always upright. State the rule first, then the numbers.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For energy problems, write the 10 per cent rule: each step keeps one tenth of the level below.
2. For a pyramid question, draw the shape, label the trophic levels and say whether it is upright or inverted and why.
3. For succession or decomposition, list the stages in order with one key word each.
4. For a cycle, say if it is gaseous or sedimentary and name the reservoir.

## 1-mark questions

**1.** Define primary productivity.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The rate of production of organic matter (biomass) per unit area per unit time by the producers through photosynthesis.</details>

**2.** What is humus?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A dark, amorphous substance formed during humification of detritus; it is resistant to microbial action and decays very slowly, serving as a reservoir of nutrients.</details>

**3.** Name the reservoir of the phosphorus cycle.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Rocks (the Earth's crust). It is a sedimentary cycle.</details>

**4.** What is a pioneer species?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The first species to colonise a bare area in succession, such as lichens on bare rock or phytoplankton in a new pond.</details>

**5.** Which food chain is the major route of energy flow in an aquatic ecosystem?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The grazing food chain.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

**6.** (2 marks) Why can the pyramid of energy never be inverted?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Only about 10 per cent of the energy of one trophic level is passed to the next; the rest is lost as heat in respiration and other work, or goes to decomposers. So each level has less energy than the one below it.</details>

**7.** (2 marks) Differentiate between GPP and NPP.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>GPP is the total rate of organic matter produced in photosynthesis. NPP is GPP minus the part used by the producers in their own respiration; it is the biomass available to consumers.</details>

**8.** (3 marks) Describe the pyramid of biomass in a sea and explain why it is inverted.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>In the sea the biomass of phytoplankton (producers) is small compared with that of zooplankton and small fish that feed on them, so the pyramid has a narrow base and a wider middle. The phytoplankton are small with a short life and reproduce very quickly, so a small standing crop supports a larger biomass of consumers.</details>

**9.** (3 marks) What factors affect the rate of decomposition?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Chemical nature of detritus: lignin and chitin slow decomposition; nitrogen and sugars speed it. Climate: warm, moist conditions with enough oxygen speed it, while low temperature or anaerobic conditions inhibit it.</details>

**10.** (3 marks) Write three ecosystem services of forests.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Any three: purify air and water, mitigate floods and droughts, cycle nutrients, generate fertile soil, provide habitat for wildlife, maintain biodiversity, pollinate crops, store carbon, provide aesthetic and cultural value.</details>

## 5-mark questions

**11.** (5 marks) Describe the process of decomposition with its five steps.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Decomposition breaks detritus (dead plant and animal remains) into inorganic matter.

1. **Fragmentation:** detritivores such as earthworms break detritus into small particles.
2. **Leaching:** water-soluble nutrients move down into the soil.
3. **Catabolism:** bacterial and fungal enzymes break the detritus into simple inorganic substances.
4. **Humification:** formation of humus, which decays very slowly and stores nutrients.
5. **Mineralisation:** humus is broken down and inorganic nutrients are released.

Decomposition needs oxygen. It is faster in warm, moist conditions and with nitrogen-rich detritus.

</details>

**12.** (5 marks) What is ecological succession? Describe hydrarch succession in a pond.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Succession is the gradual and fairly predictable change in the species composition of a community over time until a stable climax is reached. The sequence is called a sere.

Hydrarch succession (water to land):

1. phytoplankton (pioneers);
2. submerged plants;
3. free-floating plants;
4. reed-swamp;
5. marsh-meadow;
6. scrub (shrubs);
7. climax forest.

Silt collects and the pond becomes shallower with each stage until it is dry land with a forest. Species diversity and biomass rise as the stages go on.

</details>

## Case-based question

**Case.** In an experiment, a food chain in a lake has these energy values in J per square metre per year: phytoplankton 20 000; zooplankton 1800; small fish 160; large fish 15. A student says: "More energy is lost than passed on."

**13a.** Calculate the percentage of energy passed from phytoplankton to zooplankton.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1800 ÷ 20 000 × 100 = 9 %.

</details>

**13b.** Is the student right? Explain.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Yes. About 90 per cent is lost at each step as heat in respiration and in movement, or goes to decomposers. Only about 10 per cent is passed on.</details>

**13c.** Why do food chains in lakes rarely have more than five links?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The energy falls by roughly 90 per cent at each step, so by the fifth level too little is left to support a population.</details>

**13d.** Name the trophic level of the large fish.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The fourth trophic level (tertiary consumer).</details>

## Assertion and reason

Options: (a) both A and R are true and R explains A; (b) both are true but R does not explain A; (c) A is true, R is false; (d) A is false, R is true.

**14.** **Assertion (A):** Energy flows in one direction in an ecosystem while nutrients circulate. **Reason (R):** Energy is lost as heat in each step and cannot be re-used by producers.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Both true and R explains A.</details>

**15.** **Assertion (A):** Primary succession on bare rock is a slow process. **Reason (R):** Soil must form before higher plants can grow.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Both true and R explains A.</details>

**16.** **Assertion (A):** The pyramid of numbers in a tree ecosystem is always upright. **Reason (R):** One tree supports a large number of insects.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(d) A is false: the pyramid of numbers in a tree is inverted in the middle. R is true.</details>
