<div class="bigidea">Population questions reward two habits: write the equation before you put in numbers (N(t+1) = N + B + I − D − E, N = N₀e^(rt), dN/dt = rN(K − N)/K), and for any interaction question write the pair of signs (+, −, 0) first, then name the example.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For a numerical, write the formula, substitute with units, and then state the answer.
2. For a graph question, name the axes, draw the shape (J or S), and mark K or the turning point.
3. For interactions, give the **signs for both species**, a definition, and one named example.
4. For adaptations, say whether the change is morphological, physiological or behavioural.

## 1-mark questions

**1.** Name the equation for logistic growth and say what K stands for.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>dN/dt = rN(K − N)/K. K is the carrying capacity: the maximum number of individuals the habitat can support.</details>

**2.** What is the term for animals that maintain a constant body temperature in spite of changes in the environment?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Regulators (thermoregulators or homeotherms), such as birds and mammals.</details>

**3.** What is meant by a eurythermal species?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A species that can tolerate a wide range of temperatures.</details>

**4.** Name two ways in which an organism may escape in time from stressful conditions.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Hibernation (in winter) or aestivation (in summer); also spores, seeds or diapause.</details>

**5.** What is the sign pair for commensalism?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(+, 0): one species benefits and the other is neither harmed nor helped.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

**6.** (2 marks) Write the equation for the change in a population size and name all the terms.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>N<sub>t+1</sub> = N<sub>t</sub> + [B + I] − [D + E], where N is the population size, B the births (natality), I the immigrants, D the deaths (mortality) and E the emigrants in the given period.</details>

**7.** (2 marks) Why does the logistic curve level off at K?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>As the population grows, food, space and other resources become limiting. Competition raises the death rate and lowers the birth rate until births equal deaths and the population stays at the carrying capacity.</details>

**8.** (3 marks) Explain with an example how predators help to maintain species diversity in a community.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Predators reduce the number of the strongest competitor among the prey, so weaker prey species are not eliminated. An example is the starfish <i>Pisaster</i> on a rocky shore that eats mussels: when it was removed, mussels took over and many other species disappeared. In general, predators also keep prey populations in check, as the moth <i>Cactoblastis</i> did for prickly pear in Australia.</details>

**9.** (3 marks) Distinguish between ectoparasites and endoparasites, with one example each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Ectoparasites live on the outer surface of the host (lice on humans, ticks on dogs). Endoparasites live inside the host's body, in organs or tissues (tapeworm in the intestine, liver fluke, <i>Plasmodium</i> in blood). Endoparasites often have complex life cycles and very high reproductive potential.</details>

**10.** (3 marks) What is resource partitioning? How does it help two similar species coexist?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Resource partitioning means that species using the same resource divide it by using different parts, times or ways. MacArthur's five warblers feed on different parts of the same tree and so avoid competitive exclusion. It lets similar species share a habitat.</details>

## 5-mark questions

**11.** (5 marks) Explain exponential and logistic growth. Draw both curves, write the equations and state when each applies.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Exponential:** when resources are unlimited, dN/dt = rN, which integrates to N<sub>t</sub> = N<sub>0</sub>e<sup>rt</sup>. The curve is J-shaped: growth keeps accelerating. r is the intrinsic rate of natural increase. It applies to a population in a new empty habitat, or in the first phase of growth.

**Logistic (Verhulst-Pearl):** resources are limited, so dN/dt = rN(K − N)/K. The curve is S-shaped: a lag phase, acceleration, a maximum growth rate at N = K/2, deceleration, and a plateau at the carrying capacity K. It applies to most natural populations.

(Draw: axes labelled time and N; a J-curve rising ever more steeply, and an S-curve levelling at a dashed horizontal line marked K.)

</details>

**12.** (5 marks) Explain the four ways in which organisms respond to changes in their environment. Give one example for each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. **Regulate:** maintain a constant internal environment by physiological means (mammals and birds keep body temperature constant by sweating, shivering).
2. **Conform:** the body condition changes with the outside (fish, reptiles and most plants).
3. **Migrate:** move temporarily to a better habitat and return (Siberian cranes at Bharatpur in winter).
4. **Suspend:** hibernation in winter (bear), aestivation in summer (snails), spores in bacteria and fungi, seeds in plants, diapause in zooplankton.

Regulation costs energy but allows life in many habitats; conforming is cheap but limits the habitats.

</details>

## Case-based question

**Case.** A forest officer counts a herd of 80 chital in a 20 km² reserve. After one year, 24 fawns were born, 10 chital died, 6 moved in from nearby forest and 4 moved out to a neighbouring reserve.

**13a.** Find the population density now.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

N = 80 + (24 + 6) − (10 + 4) = 96. Density = 96 ÷ 20 = 4.8 chital per km².

</details>

**13b.** Name the process that adds individuals from outside.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Immigration.</details>

**13c.** If the reserve can support at most 200 chital, what is the carrying capacity K and at what size would the herd grow fastest?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>K = 200; the growth rate is largest at N = K/2 = 100 chital.</details>

**13d.** If tigers are introduced into the reserve, which interaction is this? Give the signs.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Predation (+ for the tiger, − for the chital).</details>

## Assertion and reason

Options: (a) both A and R are true and R explains A; (b) both are true but R does not explain A; (c) A is true, R is false; (d) A is false, R is true.

**14.** **Assertion (A):** The growth rate of a logistic population is greatest at K/2. **Reason (R):** At K/2 the product N × (K − N) is the largest.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Both true and R explains A: dN/dt = r × N(K − N)/K, so it peaks where N(K − N) peaks.</details>

**15.** **Assertion (A):** Two closely related species with identical needs rarely live together for long. **Reason (R):** Competitive exclusion removes the inferior competitor.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Both true and R explains A.</details>

**16.** **Assertion (A):** Lichens are an example of commensalism. **Reason (R):** In lichens the alga and the fungus both benefit.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(d) A is false (lichens are mutualism); R is true.</details>
