**Chemistry · Class 11 · about 27 minutes.** Read [Organic chemistry basics (GOC) and isomerism](/learn/courses/engineering-entrance/goc/) first. Here are the question types, worked examples and practice.

<div class="bigidea">GOC questions test whether you can follow electrons and count arrangements systematically. Write every structure out, list the effects acting on it, and count isomers by a fixed plan (longest chain first, then shorter chains), never by guessing.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. IUPAC names.</b> Principal group, longest chain with it, lowest locants, alphabetical prefixes. <i>Trap:</i> the longest chain must contain the principal group, even if another chain is longer.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Counting structural isomers.</b> Chain by chain, then position, then functional. <i>Trap:</i> counting the same molecule twice drawn the other way round.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Geometrical isomers and E-Z.</b> CIP priorities by atomic number at the first point of difference. <i>Trap:</i> cis is not always Z.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Counting stereoisomers.</b> 2ⁿ, minus meso forms; add a factor for each stereogenic C=C. <i>Trap:</i> symmetric molecules (tartaric acid: 3, not 4).</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Stability of intermediates.</b> Resonance first, then hyperconjugation and induction. <i>Trap:</i> carbanion order is the reverse of carbocation order.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Acidity order.</b> Stabilise the conjugate base: −I, −R, sp character. <i>Trap:</i> o- and p-nitrophenol are both stronger than phenol, but the para isomer is slightly stronger: in the ortho isomer an intramolecular H-bond holds on to the proton.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Basicity order.</b> Lone pair availability; solvation in water. <i>Trap:</i> in water (CH₃)₃N is weaker than (CH₃)₂NH.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Electrophiles, nucleophiles and reaction types.</b> <i>Trap:</i> BF₃ and AlCl₃ are electrophiles with no charge; H₂O and NH₃ are neutral nucleophiles.</div>
<div class="step"><b>9. Purification and chromatography.</b> Pick the method from the property. <i>Trap:</i> R<sub>f</sub> is always below 1.</div>
<div class="step"><b>10. Quantitative analysis.</b> Liebig, Dumas, Kjeldahl, Carius. <i>Trap:</i> Dumas volume must be converted to STP (and dried); Kjeldahl fails for nitro compounds.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 3, Kjeldahl).** 0.5 g of an organic compound is digested and the ammonia absorbed in 50 mL of 0.5 M H₂SO₄. The leftover acid needs 60 mL of 0.5 M NaOH. Find %N. *Solution.* Acid taken = 50 × 0.5 × 2 = 50 meq. NaOH used = 30 meq, so NH₃ = 20 meq = 20 mmol of N. %N = 20 × 14 × 10⁻³/0.5 × 100 = **56%** (urea-like).

**Example 2 (level 4, counting stereoisomers).** How many stereoisomers does CH₃CH=CH-CH(Cl)CH₃ have? *Solution.* One chiral carbon (C4: H, Cl, CH₃, CH=CHCH₃) gives ×2. The C=C has different groups on each end, so cis and trans give ×2. No symmetry: **4 stereoisomers**.

**Example 3 (level 4, acidity order).** Order: phenol, p-nitrophenol, p-cresol (p-methylphenol), ethanol. *Solution.* p-Nitrophenol (−R, −I of NO₂ spreads the phenoxide charge) &gt; phenol &gt; p-cresol (+I and hyperconjugation of CH₃ destabilise the anion) &gt; ethanol (no resonance in ethoxide). **p-nitrophenol &gt; phenol &gt; p-cresol &gt; ethanol**.

**Example 4 (level 5, combustion analysis).** 0.30 g of a compound containing C, H and O gives 0.44 g CO₂ and 0.18 g H₂O. Its molar mass is 60. Find the formula. *Solution.* C = 12/44 × 0.44 = 0.12 g; H = 2/18 × 0.18 = 0.02 g; O = 0.30 − 0.14 = 0.16 g. Moles: C 0.01, H 0.02, O 0.01, ratio 1 : 2 : 1, empirical CH₂O (30). n = 60/30 = 2: **C₂H₄O₂** (for example ethanoic acid).

<div class="myth">

**"A molecule with a chiral carbon is always optically active."** Not if it has more than one chiral centre arranged symmetrically: meso-tartaric acid has two chiral carbons but a mirror plane, so it is optically inactive. And a molecule can be chiral with no chiral carbon at all (some allenes and biphenyls).

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-chemistry" data-topics="goc" data-mode="practice"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Longest chain must contain the principal group; lowest locants to it first.
- Stereoisomers: 2ⁿ for n stereo units, minus meso duplicates.
- Carbocation: resonance > 3° > 2° > 1°; carbanion: the reverse for alkyl groups.
- Acidity: the more stable the conjugate base, the stronger the acid.
- Amines in water: 2° > 1° > 3° > NH₃ (methyl series); aniline far weaker.
- Kjeldahl: %N = 1.4NV/w (N and V of the acid used by the ammonia); not for nitro, azo or ring N.

</div>
