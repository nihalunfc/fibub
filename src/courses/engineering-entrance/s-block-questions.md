**Chemistry · Class 11 · about 24 minutes.** Read [s-block elements and hydrogen](/learn/courses/engineering-entrance/s-block/) first. Here are the question types, worked examples and practice.

<div class="bigidea">s-block questions are mostly orders and reactions. For orders, ask whether size helps or hurts the property. For reactions, remember the few products that break the pattern: Li₂O and Li₃N, Na₂O₂, KO₂, Mg₃N₂ and the compounds that will not decompose.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Periodic trends.</b> Radius, IE, hydration, E°, melting point. <i>Trap:</i> Li has the most negative E° in group 1 though it reacts most gently.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Reaction with air and water.</b> Oxide, peroxide, superoxide; nitride for Li and Mg. <i>Trap:</i> Be does not react with water; Mg needs hot water.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Liquid ammonia solutions.</b> Blue, conducting, paramagnetic; concentrated bronze and diamagnetic. <i>Trap:</i> the colour is from ammoniated electrons, not the metal ion.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Anomalous Li and Be, diagonal pairs.</b> Li-Mg, Be-Al. <i>Trap:</i> LiF and Li₂CO₃ are sparingly soluble, like MgF₂ and MgCO₃.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Thermal stability.</b> Carbonates, nitrates rise down. <i>Trap:</i> Na₂CO₃ does not decompose; NaHCO₃ does.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Solubility.</b> Group 2 sulfates fall, hydroxides rise. Group 1 fluorides rise down the group. <i>Trap:</i> do not mix up the two group 2 orders.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Flame colours and uses.</b> Li crimson, Na yellow, K lilac, Ca brick red, Sr crimson, Ba apple green. <i>Trap:</i> Be and Mg give no colour.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Industrial compounds.</b> Solvay, chlor-alkali, plaster of Paris, cement. <i>Trap:</i> Solvay fails for K₂CO₃.</div>
<div class="step"><b>9. Stoichiometry.</b> Gas volumes from carbonates, hydrides and metals with water. <i>Trap:</i> 2NaHCO₃ gives one CO₂; CaH₂ gives two H₂.</div>
<div class="step"><b>10. Hydrogen and H₂O₂.</b> Isotopes, hydride types, hardness, volume strength = 11.2 M. <i>Trap:</i> "20 volume" means about 1.79 M, not 20 M.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 3, hydride with water).** What volume of H₂ at STP forms when 4.2 g of CaH₂ reacts with water? *Solution.* CaH₂ + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + 2H₂. Moles CaH₂ = 4.2/42 = 0.1; H₂ = 0.2 mol = **4.48 L**.

**Example 2 (level 4, mixture of carbonates).** 1.84 g of a mixture of CaCO₃ and MgCO₃ is heated to constant mass, leaving 0.96 g of residue. Find the mass of CaCO₃. *Solution.* Let x g CaCO₃ and (1.84 − x) g MgCO₃. Residue: 56x/100 + 40(1.84 − x)/84 = 0.96. 0.56x + 0.8762 − 0.4762x = 0.96, so 0.0838x = 0.0838 and **x = 1.00 g**.

**Example 3 (level 4, Solvay balance).** How much NH₄Cl forms with every tonne of NaHCO₃ in the Solvay tower, and why is it not wasted? *Solution.* 1 : 1 by moles: 1 t NaHCO₃ (84) goes with 53.5/84 = **0.64 t** NH₄Cl. It is heated with Ca(OH)₂ to give back NH₃ (2NH₄Cl + Ca(OH)₂ → 2NH₃ + CaCl₂ + 2H₂O), so only CaCl₂ is left as waste.

**Example 4 (level 5, solubility reasoning).** Explain why the solubility of group 2 sulfates falls down the group but that of hydroxides rises. *Solution.* Solubility depends on hydration energy (helps) against lattice energy (hurts). For sulfates the large SO₄²⁻ makes the lattice energy almost the same for every cation, while hydration energy falls steeply as the cation grows: **solubility falls**. For hydroxides the small OH⁻ makes the lattice energy fall faster than the hydration energy as the cation grows: **solubility rises**.

<div class="myth">

**"All alkali metal carbonates give CO₂ on heating, like limestone."** Only Li₂CO₃ does among group 1 carbonates. Na⁺ and K⁺ are too large to pull the carbonate ion apart at ordinary temperatures. A heating question with Na₂CO₃ gives no gas at all.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-chemistry" data-topics="s-block" data-mode="practice"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Air products: Li₂O + Li₃N, Na₂O₂, KO₂; Mg gives MgO + Mg₃N₂.
- Stability of carbonates and nitrates rises down both groups; only Li₂CO₃ decomposes in group 1.
- Group 2 sulfates: solubility falls; hydroxides: solubility rises.
- Diagonal pairs Li-Mg, Be-Al; Be compounds amphoteric and covalent.
- CaH₂ + 2H₂O → 2H₂; 2NaHCO₃ → 1 CO₂.
- Volume strength of H₂O₂ = 11.2 × molarity.

</div>
