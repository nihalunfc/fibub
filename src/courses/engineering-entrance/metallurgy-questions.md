**Chemistry · Class 12 · about 22 minutes.** Read [Metals and metallurgy](/learn/courses/engineering-entrance/metallurgy/) first. Here are the question types, worked examples and practice.

<div class="bigidea">Metallurgy questions are mostly matching: which ore, which method, which reagent, which metal. Learn the reason behind each match (sulphides float, carbon gets better when hot, noble metals fall as mud) and the matches stop needing memory. The numerical ones are Faraday's law and percentage composition.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Ores and composition.</b> Match the ore to the metal and formula. <i>Trap:</i> iron pyrites (FeS₂) is not an iron ore; calamine is ZnCO₃; malachite is a copper carbonate.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Concentration.</b> Gravity, magnetic, froth flotation, leaching. <i>Trap:</i> collectors, frothers and depressants are three different roles.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Calcination vs roasting.</b> <i>Trap:</i> roasting needs air and gives SO₂; calcination gives CO₂ or water.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Ellingham reading.</b> Lower line reduces higher oxide; crossing points; slopes from ΔS. <i>Trap:</i> CO is the better reducer below about 980 K, C above it.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Blast furnace.</b> Zones, flux, slag, kinds of iron. <i>Trap:</i> limestone is the flux, CaO the actual slag-former; SiO₂ is the gangue.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Copper and zinc.</b> Matte, converter, self-reduction, blister copper; zinc distilled. <i>Trap:</i> FeS is removed as FeSiO₃ slag.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Aluminium.</b> Bayer (leaching), Hall-Héroult (electrolysis). <i>Trap:</i> the anode is consumed as CO₂.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Refining and calculations.</b> Match method to metal; Faraday's law; mass of metal from ore. <i>Trap:</i> current efficiency below 100% reduces the useful charge.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 3, Ellingham reading).** At 1000 K, ΔG° per mol O₂ is about −490 kJ for 2Zn + O₂ → 2ZnO and −400 kJ for 2C + O₂ → 2CO. Can carbon reduce ZnO at 1000 K? *Solution.* ZnO + C → Zn + CO has ΔG° = ½(−400 − (−490)) = **+45 kJ** per mol ZnO, positive: **no**. The carbon line must pass below the zinc line, which happens near 1250 K, which is why zinc is smelted above about 1400 K.

**Example 2 (level 3, aluminium).** How long must 50 000 A flow to make 1 tonne of aluminium at 90% efficiency? *Solution.* 10⁶ g / 27 = 37 037 mol Al, needing 111 111 mol e⁻ = 1.072 × 10¹⁰ C. Useful current 45 000 A: t = 2.38 × 10⁵ s = **66 hours**.

**Example 3 (level 4, copper matte).** A matte has 60% Cu₂S and 40% FeS by mass. How much SiO₂ is needed per 100 kg to slag all the iron (as FeSiO₃) after the FeS is roasted to FeO? *Solution.* FeS = 40 kg / 88 = 0.4545 kmol, giving 0.4545 kmol FeO, needing 0.4545 kmol SiO₂ = **27.3 kg**.

**Example 4 (level 4, refining).** Match: (a) Ni (b) Zr (c) Ge (d) Cu with Mond, van Arkel, zone refining, electrolytic. *Solution.* **a-Mond, b-van Arkel, c-zone refining, d-electrolytic.** Reasons: Ni forms a volatile carbonyl; Zr forms a volatile iodide; impurities in Ge prefer the melt; copper anodes dissolve and pure copper plates out.

<div class="myth">

**"Since carbon reduces most oxides at high temperature, it is used for every metal."** Carbon reduces Al₂O₃ and MgO only at temperatures that are not practical, and it forms carbides with many metals (Al₄C₃, TiC). Electrolysis or a reactive metal is used instead.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-chemistry" data-topics="metallurgy" data-mode="practice"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Learn ore formulas for Al, Fe, Cu, Zn, Pb; iron pyrites is not used for iron.
- Sulphides: froth flotation, then roasting; carbonates and hydrates: calcination.
- Ellingham: lower line wins; crossing gives the minimum temperature.
- Blast furnace: CO reduces at the top; CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃.
- Copper: matte → converter → self-reduction → blister → electrorefining.
- Al: Bayer leaching then electrolysis in cryolite; anodes burn.
- Refining: Mond (Ni), van Arkel (Zr, Ti), zone (Si, Ge, Ga), electrolytic (Cu) with anode mud.

</div>
