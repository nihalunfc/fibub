**Chemistry · Class 12 · about 24 minutes.** Read [d- and f-block elements](/learn/courses/engineering-entrance/d-f-block/) first. Here are the question types, worked examples and practice.

<div class="bigidea">Most d-block questions come down to one number: the d electron count of the ion. From it you get unpaired electrons, magnetic moment, colour and which oxidation state is stable. The rest is knowing how dichromate and permanganate behave and counting their electrons in titrations.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Configurations of ions.</b> Remove 4s first. <i>Trap:</i> Cr and Cu atoms are 4s¹, but Cr³⁺ is 3d³ and Cu²⁺ is 3d⁹ either way.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Magnetic moment.</b> μ = √(n(n + 2)); or work back from μ to n. <i>Trap:</i> in complexes, strong-field ligands can pair electrons (see coordination compounds).</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Colour.</b> d⁰ and d¹⁰ are colourless; charge transfer for MnO₄⁻, CrO₄²⁻. <i>Trap:</i> Zn²⁺, Sc³⁺, Ti⁴⁺, Cu⁺ are colourless.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Oxidation states and E°.</b> Most states at Mn; stability from d⁰, d⁵, d¹⁰. <i>Trap:</i> Cu has a positive E°(M²⁺/M) and does not free H₂ from acid.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Dichromate and permanganate chemistry.</b> Preparation steps, colour changes, products by medium. <i>Trap:</i> chromate to dichromate is not a redox change.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Redox titrations.</b> Electrons given = electrons taken. <i>Trap:</i> permanganate in acid takes 5, dichromate 6 per ion, oxalate gives 2.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Disproportionation.</b> MnO₄²⁻ in acid, Cu⁺ in water (2Cu⁺ → Cu + Cu²⁺). Check with E° values.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Lanthanoids and actinoids.</b> Contraction and its results, +2/+4 states, actinoids' wider range. <i>Trap:</i> Ce⁴⁺ is an oxidiser, Eu²⁺ a reducer.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 3, which is more paramagnetic?).** Rank Mn²⁺, Fe²⁺, Ni²⁺ and Cu⁺ by magnetic moment. *Solution.* d counts 5, 6, 8, 10; unpaired 5, 4, 2, 0. Order: **Mn²⁺ > Fe²⁺ > Ni²⁺ > Cu⁺** (Cu⁺ is diamagnetic).

**Example 2 (level 4, mixed titration).** 25 mL of a solution containing FeSO₄ and FeC₂O₄ (iron(II) oxalate) needs 30 mL of 0.02 M KMnO₄ in acid. If the iron content is 2 mmol, find the oxalate. *Solution.* Electrons taken by MnO₄⁻ = 30 × 0.02 × 5 = 3 mmol. Fe²⁺ gives 2 × 1 = 2 mmol, so oxalate gives 1 mmol of electrons = **0.5 mmol of C₂O₄²⁻**. Remember: in FeC₂O₄, both the iron and the oxalate are oxidised.

**Example 3 (level 4, disproportionation).** Will Cu⁺ disproportionate in water? E°(Cu⁺/Cu) = +0.52 V, E°(Cu²⁺/Cu⁺) = +0.16 V. *Solution.* 2Cu⁺ → Cu + Cu²⁺: E° = 0.52 − 0.16 = **+0.36 V**, positive, so yes. That is why Cu⁺ salts are stable only as insoluble solids (CuCl, Cu₂O) or complexes.

**Example 4 (level 5, lanthanoid trend).** Why does basicity fall from La(OH)₃ to Lu(OH)₃? *Solution.* As Ln³⁺ shrinks, its pull on the OH⁻ grows and the bond becomes more covalent, so OH⁻ is released less easily. **Smaller, more polarising cation, weaker base.**

<div class="myth">

**"A d⁵ ion always has 5 unpaired electrons."** As a free ion or in a weak-field complex, yes. In a strong-field complex like [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻, the electrons pair up in the lower d set and only 1 stays unpaired (1.73 BM). Read whether the question means a free ion or a complex.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-chemistry" data-topics="d-f-block" data-mode="practice"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Find the d count first; 4s leaves before 3d.
- μ = √(n(n + 2)); free ions are high spin.
- Colourless: d⁰ and d¹⁰ (Sc³⁺, Ti⁴⁺, Zn²⁺, Cu⁺).
- Cr²⁺ reducing, Mn³⁺ oxidising, Cu⁺ disproportionates.
- Titrations: total electrons given = total taken; FeC₂O₄ gives 3 per formula unit.
- Lanthanoid contraction: Zr ≈ Hf, falling basicity of Ln(OH)₃; actinoids show more states.

</div>
