**Chemistry · Class 12 · about 24 minutes.** Read [Amines and diazonium salts](/learn/courses/engineering-entrance/amines/) first. Here are the question types, worked examples and practice.

<div class="bigidea">Ask where the nitrogen lone pair is going. Free on an alkyl amine: strong base and good nucleophile. Shared with a ring: weak base, very reactive ring. Shared with C=O (amide, acetanilide): almost not basic, ring tamed. Turned into N₂⁺: a leaving group that can be swapped.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Basicity order.</b> Gas phase by +I; water by +I, solvation and crowding; aryl amines by resonance. <i>Trap:</i> benzylamine is an aliphatic amine (pKb 4.7), far stronger than aniline.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Preparation and carbon count.</b> Nitrile reduction adds a carbon; Hofmann removes one; Gabriel keeps it. <i>Trap:</i> Gabriel cannot make aniline.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Distinguishing tests.</b> Carbylamine, Hinsberg, nitrous acid, azo dye test for aryl 1°. <i>Trap:</i> 2° amines give yellow N-nitrosoamines, not N₂.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Substitution in aniline.</b> Tribromination, protection by acetylation, nitration giving meta. <i>Trap:</i> no Friedel-Crafts on aniline.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Diazonium replacement.</b> Match reagent to product. <i>Trap:</i> Sandmeyer uses Cu(I) salts, Gattermann copper metal; KI needs no copper.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Coupling and dyes.</b> Para attack; phenol in base, aniline in mild acid. <i>Trap:</i> the N=N stays in the product.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Synthesis puzzles.</b> Use NH₂ as a temporary director, then remove it with H₃PO₂ (e.g. 1,3,5-tribromobenzene from aniline).</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Numericals.</b> Kjeldahl and Dumas %N, pH of amine solutions, NaNO₂ needed, yields. <i>Trap:</i> H₂SO₄ takes 2 NH₃ per molecule.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 3, basicity).** Rank: aniline, p-nitroaniline, p-toluidine, benzylamine. *Solution.* Benzylamine (lone pair not on the ring) > p-toluidine (CH₃ pushes) > aniline > p-nitroaniline (NO₂ pulls the lone pair). **Benzylamine > p-toluidine > aniline > p-nitroaniline.**

**Example 2 (level 4, synthesis).** How do you make 1,3,5-tribromobenzene from aniline? *Solution.* Bromine water gives 2,4,6-tribromoaniline (NH₂ directs to ortho and para, which are meta to each other). Diazotise (NaNO₂/HCl, 273 K), then H₃PO₂ removes the N₂⁺: **1,3,5-tribromobenzene**, a pattern no direct bromination of benzene can give.

**Example 3 (level 4, Dumas method).** 0.30 g of a compound gives 50 mL of N₂ at 300 K and 715 mm Hg (aqueous tension 15 mm). Find %N. *Solution.* Dry pressure = 700 mm. Volume at STP = 50 × (700/760) × (273/300) = 41.9 mL. Mass of N₂ = 41.9/22 400 × 28 = 0.0524 g. **%N = 17.5%.**

**Example 4 (level 5, unknown amine).** A compound C₃H₉N reacts with benzenesulphonyl chloride to give a product insoluble in KOH, and gives no carbylamine smell. What is it? *Solution.* No carbylamine: not 1°. Hinsberg product insoluble in KOH: 2°. The only 2° amine C₃H₉N is **N-methylethanamine, CH₃NHC₂H₅**.

<div class="myth">

**"Aniline reacts with nitrous acid like ethylamine, giving N₂ and phenol."** In the cold (0 to 5 °C) aniline gives a stable benzenediazonium salt. Only on warming does it break up to phenol and N₂. Temperature is the key word in these questions.

</div>

<div class="meaning">
<dl>

<dt>%N (Dumas)</dt><dd>(28/22 400) × V(N₂ at STP, mL) / w × 100</dd>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-chemistry" data-topics="amines" data-mode="practice"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Water order: 2° > 1° > 3° (methyl) or 2° > 3° > 1° (ethyl), all above NH₃ and far above aniline.
- Carbon count: nitrile reduction +1, Hofmann −1, Gabriel and nitro reduction 0.
- Carbylamine = 1°; Hinsberg separates 1°, 2°, 3°; N₂ from nitrous acid = 1° aliphatic.
- Protect NH₂ as acetanilide for controlled substitution; aniline gives meta products in strong acid.
- Diazonium: replace with Cl, Br, CN, I, F, OH, H; couple at para for azo dyes.
- Kjeldahl: count H₂SO₄ as 2 equivalents per mole.

</div>
