**Chemistry · Class 11 · about 27 minutes.** Read [Hydrocarbons](/learn/courses/engineering-entrance/hydrocarbons/) first. Here are the question types, worked examples and practice.

<div class="bigidea">Hydrocarbon questions are reagent puzzles. Learn, for each reagent, what it attacks and through which intermediate. Then a reaction sequence is just the same small set of steps in a row, and an ozonolysis puzzle is the same step run backwards.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Preparations.</b> Wurtz, Kolbe, decarboxylation, dehydration, dehydrohalogenation. <i>Trap:</i> carbon count (Wurtz doubles, decarboxylation removes one).</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Counting substitution products.</b> Count the different kinds of H (symmetry). <i>Trap:</i> stereoisomers are counted only if the question asks.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Halogenation selectivity.</b> Number of H × reactivity per H. <i>Trap:</i> bromination and chlorination use very different ratios.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Conformations.</b> Anti, gauche, eclipsed energies. <i>Trap:</i> conformers are not isomers that can be separated at room temperature.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Addition to alkenes.</b> Markovnikov; peroxide effect for HBr only; rearrangement after a carbocation forms. <i>Trap:</i> oxymercuration avoids rearrangement; hydroboration is anti-Markovnikov.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Oxidation and ozonolysis.</b> Baeyer diol; hot KMnO₄ cleavage; ozonolysis carbonyls. <i>Trap:</i> terminal =CH₂ becomes HCHO (ozonolysis) but CO₂ (hot KMnO₄).</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Alkynes.</b> Acidity test; Lindlar cis, Na/NH₃ trans; hydration gives a carbonyl. <i>Trap:</i> only ethyne gives an aldehyde.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Aromaticity.</b> Cyclic, planar, conjugated, 4n + 2. <i>Trap:</i> count lone pairs in the ring (furan, pyrrole) and charges.</div>
<div class="step"><b>9. Directing effects.</b> o/p or m, activating or not. <i>Trap:</i> halogens deactivate but direct o/p; in sequences, the order of steps decides the isomer.</div>
<div class="step"><b>10. Gas volume and mole problems.</b> CₓHᵧ + (x + y/4)O₂; contraction V(1 + y/4); H₂ uptake counts π bonds. <i>Trap:</i> KOH absorbs CO₂; water condenses on cooling.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 3, rearrangement).** What is the main product of HCl with 3-methylbut-1-ene? *Solution.* H⁺ adds to C1, giving a 2° cation on C2. A hydride shifts from C3 to make a more stable 3° cation on C3. Cl⁻ adds there: **2-chloro-2-methylbutane** (not the expected 2-chloro-3-methylbutane).

**Example 2 (level 4, eudiometry).** 10 mL of a gaseous hydrocarbon is exploded with 80 mL of O₂. After cooling the volume is 65 mL; after KOH it is 45 mL. Find the formula. *Solution.* CO₂ = 65 − 45 = 20 mL, so x = 2. Contraction on cooling = 90 − 65 = 25 mL = 10(1 + y/4), so y = 6: **C₂H₆**. (Check: O₂ used = 10 × 3.5 = 35 mL; 45 mL O₂ left over, which matches.)

**Example 3 (level 4, ozonolysis backwards).** An alkene C₆H₁₂ gives ethanal and butan-2-one on ozonolysis. Find it. *Solution.* Remove the two C=O oxygens and join the carbonyl carbons: CH₃CH= and =C(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ give **CH₃CH=C(CH₃)CH₂CH₃**, 3-methylpent-2-ene.

**Example 4 (level 5, order of steps).** Make m-bromonitrobenzene and p-bromonitrobenzene from benzene. *Solution.* For **meta**: nitrate first (NO₂ is a meta director), then brominate with Br₂/FeBr₃. For **para**: brominate first (Br directs o/p), then nitrate and separate the para isomer from the ortho one. Same two steps; the order decides the product.

<div class="myth">

**"Peroxide reverses the addition of every HX."** Only HBr. With HCl the H-Cl bond is too strong for the radical chain, and with HI the I• radical is too unreactive to add to the double bond. Both still add by the Markovnikov route.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-chemistry" data-topics="hydrocarbons" data-mode="practice"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Count kinds of H by symmetry for substitution products.
- Carbocation rearrangements: H or CH₃ shifts to give a more stable cation.
- Peroxide effect: HBr only. Hydroboration: anti-Markovnikov alcohol, no rearrangement.
- Ozonolysis backwards: join the carbonyl carbons with C=C.
- Terminal alkyne test: ammoniacal AgNO₃ or Cu₂Cl₂.
- Eudiometry: CO₂ from KOH absorption gives x; contraction gives y.
- In aromatic synthesis, the order of the steps sets o, m or p.

</div>
