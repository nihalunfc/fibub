**Chemistry · Class 11 · about 25 minutes.** Read [Redox reactions](/learn/courses/engineering-entrance/redox/) first. Here are the question types, worked examples and practice.

<div class="bigidea">Redox questions come down to counting electrons. Count them with oxidation numbers, make the electrons lost equal the electrons gained, and every balancing, n-factor and titration question falls out of that one equality.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Oxidation numbers.</b> Rules in priority order; sum = charge. <i>Trap:</i> peroxides and superoxides; CrO₅, H₂SO₅ and K₂S₂O₈ hide O-O links.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Average against structure.</b> Fe₃O₄ +8/3; S₄O₆²⁻ +2.5 (really +5 and 0); C₃O₂ +4/3. <i>Trap:</i> a fraction is a sign of an average.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Oxidising and reducing agents.</b> Highest state only oxidises; lowest only reduces; middle can do both. <i>Trap:</i> the oxidising agent is the one reduced.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Types of reaction.</b> Disproportionation, comproportionation, displacement, intramolecular. <i>Trap:</i> 2KClO₃ → 2KCl + 3O₂ is intramolecular, not disproportionation (two elements change).</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Balancing in acid and base.</b> Ion-electron method. <i>Trap:</i> in base, add OH⁻ to both sides after balancing with H⁺.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. n-factor.</b> Total ON change per formula unit. <i>Trap:</i> count both changing elements (FeC₂O₄ = 3); medium for KMnO₄.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Equivalent weight and normality.</b> E = M/n; N = M × n. <i>Trap:</i> hydrated salts use the full molar mass.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Titrations and iodometry.</b> Equivalents equal. <i>Trap:</i> in iodometry the thiosulfate counts the I₂, and 1 mol I₂ = 2 equivalents.</div>
<div class="step"><b>9. Electrochemical series.</b> Higher E° reduced; E°<sub>cell</sub> = cathode − anode. <i>Trap:</i> do not multiply E° by coefficients.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 3, balancing in base).** Balance MnO₄⁻ + I⁻ → MnO₂ + I₂ in basic solution. *Solution.* Reduction: MnO₄⁻ + 4H⁺ + 3e⁻ → MnO₂ + 2H₂O. Oxidation: 2I⁻ → I₂ + 2e⁻. Electrons: ×2 and ×3: 2MnO₄⁻ + 6I⁻ + 8H⁺ → 2MnO₂ + 3I₂ + 4H₂O. Add 8OH⁻ to both sides and cancel water: **2MnO₄⁻ + 6I⁻ + 4H₂O → 2MnO₂ + 3I₂ + 8OH⁻**.

**Example 2 (level 4, iodometry).** 0.5 g of a bleaching sample is treated with excess KI in acid; the I₂ freed needs 20 mL of 0.1 M Na₂S₂O₃. Find the mass of available chlorine (each Cl₂ frees one I₂). *Solution.* Thiosulfate = 2 mmol, so I₂ = 1 mmol, so Cl₂ = 1 mmol = 71 mg. Available chlorine = 71/500 × 100 = **14.2%**.

**Example 3 (level 4, mixture titration).** 25 mL of a solution with both Fe²⁺ and C₂O₄²⁻ (as FeC₂O₄) needs 30 mL of 0.02 M KMnO₄ in acid. Find the molarity of FeC₂O₄. *Solution.* Equivalents of MnO₄⁻ = 30 × 0.02 × 5 = 3 meq. FeC₂O₄ has n = 3, so 25 × M × 3 = 3, **M = 0.04 M**.

**Example 4 (level 5, n-factor in disproportionation).** Find the equivalent weight of Cl₂ in Cl₂ → Cl⁻ + ClO₃⁻ (hot alkali). *Solution.* Balanced: 3Cl₂ → 5Cl⁻ + ClO₃⁻ (5 Cl gain 1 e⁻ each, 1 Cl loses 5). 3 mol Cl₂ transfer 5 mol electrons, so n = 5/3 per Cl₂. E = 71 ÷ (5/3) = **42.6**.

<div class="myth">

**"Oxygen is always −2 and hydrogen always +1."** These are the usual values, but the rules have a priority order. In NaH, hydrogen is −1 because Na must be +1. In OF₂, oxygen is +2 because fluorine must be −1. In Na₂O₂, oxygen is −1.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-chemistry" data-topics="redox" data-mode="practice"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- A fractional ON is an average; check the structure.
- Highest state: only oxidant; lowest: only reductant; intermediate: both.
- In base: balance with H⁺, then add equal OH⁻ to both sides.
- n-factor counts every element that changes (FeC₂O₄ 3).
- Iodometry: 1 I₂ = 2 S₂O₃²⁻ = 2 equivalents.
- E° does not scale with coefficients.

</div>
