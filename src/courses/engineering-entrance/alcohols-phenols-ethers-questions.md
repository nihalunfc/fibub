**Chemistry · Class 12 · about 24 minutes.** Read [Alcohols, phenols and ethers](/learn/courses/engineering-entrance/alcohols-phenols-ethers/) first. Here are the question types, worked examples and practice.

<div class="bigidea">Questions in this chapter test four habits: compare stability of the conjugate base for acidity, follow the carbocation for dehydration and SN1, choose the less hindered carbon for SN2 (Williamson and ether cleavage), and remember which test gives which colour or precipitate.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Acidity order.</b> Phenols with substituents; alcohols 1° > 2° > 3°. <i>Trap:</i> methoxy at para lowers phenol acidity (resonance donation), even though O is electronegative.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Boiling points and solubility.</b> H-bonding, branching, intra- vs intermolecular H-bonds. <i>Trap:</i> o-nitrophenol boils lower than p-nitrophenol.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Distinguishing tests.</b> Lucas (1°/2°/3°), FeCl₃ (phenol), bromine water (phenol), iodoform (CH₃CH(OH)- group), Na (any OH). <i>Trap:</i> ethanol gives iodoform; methanol does not.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Dehydration products.</b> Carbocation, shifts, Saytzeff. <i>Trap:</i> neopentyl-type alcohols rearrange completely.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Oxidation.</b> Reagent decides aldehyde vs acid; hot Cu test. <i>Trap:</i> PCC stops at the aldehyde, K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺ goes to the acid.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Phenol named reactions.</b> Kolbe, Reimer-Tiemann, bromination, nitration, cumene process. <i>Trap:</i> Kolbe gives an acid (COOH), Reimer-Tiemann an aldehyde (CHO), both mainly ortho.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Ethers: making and breaking.</b> Williamson (primary halide), HI cleavage. <i>Trap:</i> anisole + HI gives phenol and CH₃I, never iodobenzene.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Stoichiometry.</b> H₂ with Na, CH₄ with Grignard, yields of phenol reactions, molar mass from acetylation. <i>Trap:</i> each OH gives ½ H₂ but one CH₄.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 3, acidity).** Rank: phenol, 4-methoxyphenol, 4-nitrophenol, 3-nitrophenol. *Solution.* 4-nitro (strong resonance pull) > 3-nitro (induction only) > phenol > 4-methoxy (resonance push). **4-nitrophenol > 3-nitrophenol > phenol > 4-methoxyphenol.**

**Example 2 (level 4, dehydration with shift).** Predict the major alkene from 2,2-dimethylpropan-1-ol (neopentyl alcohol) with conc. H₂SO₄. *Solution.* The 1° cation would be very unstable, so a methyl shifts as water leaves, giving the 3° cation (CH₃)₂C⁺-CH₂CH₃. Losing H⁺ gives the more substituted alkene: **2-methylbut-2-ene**.

**Example 3 (level 4, number of OH groups).** 0.92 g of a polyhydric alcohol (M = 92) reacts with excess acetic anhydride, and the product's mass is 2.18 g. How many OH groups does it have? *Solution.* Each acetylation replaces H by COCH₃, adding 42 g/mol. Moles of alcohol = 0.01; mass gained = 1.26 g = 0.01 × 42 × n, so **n = 3** (glycerol).

**Example 4 (level 5, ether cleavage).** Predict the products of (a) CH₃OCH₂CH₃ and (b) C₆H₅OCH₂CH₃ with excess hot HI. *Solution.* (a) First CH₃I + CH₃CH₂OH; excess HI turns the ethanol into CH₃CH₂I too: **CH₃I + C₂H₅I**. (b) The aryl-oxygen bond does not break: **C₆H₅OH + C₂H₅I** (phenol does not react further with HI).

<div class="myth">

**"Phenol is an alcohol, so it gives the Lucas test and is oxidised to an aldehyde."** The OH carbon in phenol is part of the ring and has no hydrogen to lose; the C-O bond is strengthened by resonance. Phenol does neither. It shows its own reactions: FeCl₃ colour, bromine water precipitate, solubility in NaOH.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-chemistry" data-topics="alcohols-phenols-ethers" data-mode="practice"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Acidity: judge the conjugate base; o/p-NO₂ up, o/p-CH₃ and -OCH₃ down.
- Intramolecular H-bond (ortho-nitrophenol) means lower boiling point and steam volatility.
- Tests: Lucas (alcohol class), FeCl₃ and bromine water (phenol), iodoform (CH₃CH(OH)-).
- Dehydration and SN1 can rearrange; look for a more stable cation one carbon away.
- Kolbe → salicylic acid; Reimer-Tiemann → salicylaldehyde.
- Williamson: alkoxide + primary halide; HI never breaks the aryl-O bond.

</div>
