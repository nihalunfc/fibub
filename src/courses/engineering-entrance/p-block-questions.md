**Chemistry · Classes 11 and 12 · about 25 minutes.** Read [p-block elements (groups 13 to 18)](/learn/courses/engineering-entrance/p-block/) first. Here are the question types, worked examples and practice.

<div class="bigidea">p-block questions look like memory tests but most can be reasoned out: size and electronegativity for orders, electron counting for shapes and oxidation states, and the O-H rule for basicity. Learn a short list of facts that break the pattern and you can handle the rest.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Order of a property.</b> Bond angle, acidity, boiling point, thermal stability, bond enthalpy. <i>Trap:</i> the first member breaks the trend (NH₃ b.p., F₂ bond, F electron gain).</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Shapes and hybridisation.</b> Count bonded atoms and lone pairs. <i>Trap:</i> lone pairs in a trigonal bipyramid go to the equator (ClF₃ is T-shaped, XeF₂ linear).</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Oxoacids.</b> Oxidation state, basicity, P-H and P-O-P bonds, acid strength. <i>Trap:</i> H₃PO₃ is dibasic, H₃PO₂ monobasic.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Identify the substance.</b> A colourless gas that turns lime water milky (CO₂) or acidified dichromate green (SO₂); a greenish-yellow gas that bleaches (Cl₂). <i>Trap:</i> both CO₂ and SO₂ turn lime water milky.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Industrial processes.</b> Catalyst, temperature, pressure, and why it is a compromise. <i>Trap:</i> SO₃ is absorbed in H₂SO₄, not water (water gives a mist).</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Reactions with alkali and water.</b> Disproportionation of Cl₂ and P₄; hydrolysis of XeF₆, SiCl₄, PCl₅. <i>Trap:</i> CCl₄ does not hydrolyse.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Counting bonds.</b> σ and π bonds, lone pairs, 3c-2e bonds. <i>Trap:</i> in diborane only 4 B-H bonds are ordinary 2-centre bonds.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Stoichiometry.</b> Haber volumes, Ostwald and contact yields, ozone by iodometry, borax titration. <i>Trap:</i> borax reacts with 2 HCl per formula unit.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 3, acid strength).** Arrange HOCl, HOBr and HOI by acid strength. *Solution.* The O-H hydrogen leaves more easily when the halogen pulls electron density away from O. Chlorine is the most electronegative: **HOCl > HOBr > HOI**. (Compare HOCl < HClO₂ < HClO₃ < HClO₄, where it is the number of O atoms that matters.)

**Example 2 (level 4, ozone by iodometry).** 1 L of ozonised oxygen at STP is passed through excess KI solution. The iodine freed needs 30 mL of 0.1 M Na₂S₂O₃. Find the volume of O₃. *Solution.* O₃ + 2KI + H₂O → I₂ + 2KOH + O₂, and I₂ + 2S₂O₃²⁻ → 2I⁻ + S₄O₆²⁻. Thiosulphate = 3 mmol, so I₂ = 1.5 mmol = O₃. Volume = 1.5 × 22.4 = **33.6 mL** (3.4% by volume).

**Example 3 (level 4, shapes).** Which have a square planar shape: XeF₄, SF₄, [ICl₄]⁻, CCl₄? *Solution.* XeF₄: 4 bonds + 2 lone pairs (octahedral arrangement), square planar. [ICl₄]⁻: I has 7 + 1 = 8 electrons, 4 used in bonds, 2 lone pairs: square planar. SF₄: 4 bonds + 1 lone pair, see-saw. CCl₄: tetrahedral. Answer: **XeF₄ and [ICl₄]⁻**.

**Example 4 (level 5, Haber volumes).** 30 mL N₂ and 60 mL H₂ react until 20% of the H₂ is used. Find the final total volume (same T and P). *Solution.* H₂ used = 12 mL; N₂ used = 4 mL; NH₃ formed = 8 mL. Final: N₂ 26 + H₂ 48 + NH₃ 8 = **82 mL**. Each mole of N₂ that reacts lowers the total by 2 moles.

<div class="myth">

**"HF is a strong acid because fluorine is the most electronegative element."** In water HF is weak (pKa about 3.2). The H-F bond is very strong, and F⁻ binds tightly to water and to HF itself. Bond strength, not electronegativity, decides acid strength down a group.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-chemistry" data-topics="p-block" data-mode="practice"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Learn the exceptions: NH₃/H₂O/HF boiling points, F₂ bond enthalpy, Cl electron gain, HF weak acid.
- Shapes: count lone pairs, then put them where they repel least (equatorial in a bipyramid, opposite in an octahedron).
- Oxoacids: basicity from O-H, reducing power from P-H, chlorine acid strength rises with O count.
- Cl₂ with cold dilute NaOH gives OCl⁻; hot concentrated gives ClO₃⁻.
- Lime water turns milky with CO₂ and SO₂; only SO₂ turns dichromate green.
- In gas stoichiometry, track volume changes step by step.

</div>
