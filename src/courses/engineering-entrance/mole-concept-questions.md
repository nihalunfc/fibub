**Chemistry · Class 11 · about 25 minutes.** Read [Some basic concepts: moles and stoichiometry](/learn/courses/engineering-entrance/mole-concept/) first. Here are the question types, worked examples and practice.

<div class="bigidea">Every mole-concept question is a chain: whatever you are given, change it to moles, use a ratio, and change back. The traps are in the first and last links.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Counting particles.</b> Molecules vs atoms vs ions vs electrons. <i>Trap:</i> "number of atoms" in a molecule of several atoms; electrons per molecule (count the atomic numbers).</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Formulae from analysis.</b> Percentages → empirical formula → molecular formula. <i>Trap:</i> ratios like 1 : 1.33 need ×3.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Limiting reagent and yield.</b> <i>Method:</i> moles ÷ coefficient, smallest wins. <i>Trap:</i> comparing masses.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Gas volumes.</b> 22.4 L/mol at STP (or 22.7 at 1 bar); same T and P → volume ratio = mole ratio. Eudiometry: volume changes in gas reactions.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Concentration conversions.</b> Molarity ↔ molality ↔ mole fraction ↔ mass %, with density. <i>Trap:</i> solution vs solvent mass.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Dilution and mixing.</b> M₁V₁ = M₂V₂; mixing adds moles and volumes.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Mixtures.</b> A mixture of two substances reacts; set up two unknowns (x g and y g) and two equations.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Equivalents and n-factor (JEE Advanced).</b> Equivalents of A = equivalents of B at the end point. <i>Trap:</i> n-factor of H₃PO₃ (2), H₃PO₂ (1), KMnO₄ depends on the medium.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 2, counting).** How many electrons are in 1.8 g of water? *Solution.* 1.8/18 = 0.1 mol of molecules; each H₂O has 1 + 1 + 8 = 10 electrons, so 1 mol of electrons = **6.022 × 10²³**.

**Example 2 (level 3, empirical formula).** A hydrocarbon is 85.7% carbon. Its vapour density is 21. Find its molecular formula. *Solution.* C: 85.7/12 = 7.14, H: 14.3/1 = 14.3 → ratio 1 : 2 → CH₂ (14). Molar mass = 2 × vapour density = 42, so n = 3: **C₃H₆**.

**Example 3 (level 3, mixture).** 10 g of a mixture of CaCO₃ and MgCO₃ gives 2.464 L of CO₂ at STP on heating. Find the mass of CaCO₃. *Solution.* CO₂ = 2.464/22.4 = 0.11 mol. Let x g CaCO₃ (100) and (10 − x) g MgCO₃ (84): x/100 + (10 − x)/84 = 0.11 → 84x + 1000 − 100x = 924 → 16x = 76 → **x = 4.75 g**.

**Example 4 (level 4, concentration).** A solution of urea (M = 60) in water has mole fraction of urea 0.1. Find its molality. *Solution.* In 1 mol of solution: 0.1 mol urea, 0.9 mol water = 16.2 g. m = 0.1/0.0162 = **6.17 mol/kg**.

**Example 5 (level 5, equivalents).** 25 mL of an Fe²⁺ solution needs 20 mL of 0.02 M KMnO₄ in acid. Find the molarity of Fe²⁺. *Solution.* MnO₄⁻ gains 5 electrons, Fe²⁺ loses 1. Equivalents: 20 × 0.02 × 5 = 2 = 25 × M × 1, so **M = 0.08 M**.

<div class="myth">

**"Molarity and molality are almost the same, so I can swap them."** Only for very dilute water solutions, where 1 L of solution is about 1 kg of water. Example 4 shows a big difference for a strong solution. Use density whenever it is given: the question is telling you to.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-chemistry" data-topics="mole-concept" data-mode="practice"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Change every given quantity to moles first.
- Electrons per molecule = sum of atomic numbers; atoms per molecule from the formula.
- Molar mass = 2 × vapour density.
- Mixtures: two unknowns, two equations (mass and moles of product).
- Equivalents: n × M × V on both sides; know the n-factors of H₃PO₃, H₃PO₂ and KMnO₄.

</div>
