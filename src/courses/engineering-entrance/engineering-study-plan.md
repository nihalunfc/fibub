**Start here · about 12 minutes.** By the end you will have a month-by-month plan and a weekly routine you can actually keep.

<div class="bigidea">A rank is made in three phases: learn every chapter once properly, revise it again and again with mixed questions, then practise full papers under exam conditions. Plan the months backwards from the exam.</div>

## 1. Plan backwards from the exam

Slide the months you have left and the hours you can study each day outside school. The bar shows how to split your time; the line under it shows how long each chapter can get.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="study-plan" data-chapters="80"><figcaption>About 80 chapters across Physics, Chemistry and Maths. Mock tests take about 15% of the time and revision about 25%; the rest is first-time learning.</figcaption></figure>

Why these three phases?

- **Learn** (about 60%): each chapter once, properly: the lesson, your own short notes, and at least 60 questions from levels 1 to 3.
- **Revise** (about 25%): mixed sets across chapters, because exams mix them. This is where most students gain the most marks.
- **Mock tests** (about 15%): full papers in the exam's own time, followed by careful analysis of every mistake.

## 2. The order of chapters

Learn the three subjects side by side, not one after another, so none goes cold. Within each subject, follow the course order: it is the school order, and each chapter uses the ones before it.

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><b>Months 1 to 4.</b> Physics: units, motion in a line and a plane, laws of motion. Chemistry: moles, atomic structure, periodic table. Maths: sets and functions, trigonometry, quadratics.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Months 5 to 8.</b> Physics: energy, momentum, rotation, gravitation. Chemistry: bonding, thermodynamics, equilibrium. Maths: sequences, permutations, binomial, straight lines and circles.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Months 9 to 12.</b> Physics: properties of matter, heat, oscillations, waves. Chemistry: redox, organic basics and hydrocarbons. Maths: conics, limits, statistics, complex numbers.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Class 12.</b> The same pattern for the Class 12 chapters, while one day a week goes to revising Class 11 with mixed questions.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Last months.</b> Revision by weightage (chapters that carry more marks first), then mock tests two or three times a week, each followed by a mistakes review.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"I'll finish the syllabus first and practise questions at the end."** This is the most common way to lose a year. Understanding is only proven by questions, and memory fades within weeks without them. Do questions from the first day of every chapter.

</div>

## 3. A week that works around school

| Day | What to do (3 to 4 hours outside school) |
|---|---|
| Mon to Fri | 1 new topic (lesson + notes), 20 to 30 questions on it, and 15 minutes revising one older chapter |
| Saturday | A mixed timed set of 30 to 45 questions across the week's chapters, then a mistakes review |
| Sunday | Spaced revision: look again at chapters from 1 week and 1 month ago, with questions; rest in the afternoon |

**Spaced revision** is the single most powerful habit: come back to each chapter after about a day, a week and a month. Each return should be questions, not rereading. Research on learning consistently finds that practice testing and spacing beat rereading and highlighting by a wide margin.

## 4. If you have less than six months

Skip nothing completely, but change the order: start with the chapters that carry the most marks in your exam, do their level 1 to 3 questions, and begin weekly mock tests early. The question-types lesson in every chapter lists the patterns that come up most, so read that first when time is short.

<div class="keypoints">

- Split your months into about 60% learning, 25% revision with mixed questions and 15% mock tests.
- Learn the three subjects side by side, in the course's (school) order.
- Do questions from the first day of every chapter; never save them for the end.
- Revise each chapter after about a day, a week and a month, using questions.
- Short on time: start with the high-weightage chapters and begin mock tests early.

</div>
