**Start here · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will know how to use every question in this course to climb, chapter by chapter, to top-rank level.

<div class="bigidea">Top rankers do not do more questions at random. They do the right level at the right time, mix chapters, learn from every mistake, and guess only when the numbers say so.</div>

## 1. Climb the five levels

Every question in the bank has a level:

| Level | What it is | Who needs it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Warm-up: one idea, one step | Everyone, first time through a chapter |
| 2 | Standard: KEAM, CUSAT, COMEDK level | Everyone |
| 3 | Exam: JEE Main level, usually two ideas | Everyone aiming for a good rank |
| 4 | Hard: the toughest JEE Main questions | Top 1% in JEE Main, all IIT aspirants |
| 5 | Top-rank: JEE Advanced depth, several chapters | IIT aspirants aiming for a top rank |

Work up: **move to the next level when you get about 8 of 10 right** at the current one. If you score below 5 of 10, go back to the lesson before doing more questions; more questions will not fix a gap in understanding.

## 2. Mix chapters

Doing 30 questions of one chapter tells you which method to use before you read each question. The real exam does not. Once a chapter is learnt, practise it **mixed** with earlier chapters. Studies of maths learning find that mixed (interleaved) practice gives much better test results than blocked practice, even though it feels harder.

<div class="myth">

**"If it feels hard, it is not working."** The opposite is usually true. Mixed sets and recalling from memory feel harder than rereading, and that effort is what makes the learning stick.

</div>

## 3. Keep a mistakes log

After every set, write each mistake in one line:

| Question | My wrong step | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ball thrown from tower | Took ground as +H | Sign convention | Always write "up is +" and the origin before the equation |

Causes come in four kinds: **concept** (go back to the lesson), **reading** (missed "NOT", a unit, "at rest"), **calculation** (slow down the last step) and **time** (do more timed sets). Read your log once a week. Most students find the same five or six mistakes cause most of their lost marks.

## 5. Mock tests: the analysis is the point

Take a full mock test in the exam's own time, at the time of day of the real exam, with no breaks. Then spend at least as long analysing it as you spent writing it:

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><b>Sort every question</b> into: right; wrong (concept, reading, calculation); skipped but could have solved; skipped and could not solve.</div>
<div class="step"><b>The "could have solved" pile</b> is your fastest gain: it is usually a time or order problem. Next time, do a first pass answering only the questions you can solve quickly.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Put every wrong answer in your mistakes log</b> with its cause and fix.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Redo the wrong questions</b> two days later without looking at the solution.</div>
</div>

## 6. How to use this course's bank

- In each chapter's **question-types lesson**, work through the examples, then the practice set from level 1 up.
- In the **practice bank**, filter by chapter, level, format and exam. Number questions marked as templates give new numbers each time: use them to practise until a method is automatic.
- Every question has a **full worked solution** and, where there is one, the **trap** it sets. Read the solution even when you were right: you may learn a faster way.

<div class="keypoints">

- Climb levels 1 to 5; move up at about 8 of 10 right, go back to the lesson below 5 of 10.
- Practise chapters mixed once they are learnt; it feels harder and works better.
- Keep a one-line mistakes log with cause (concept, reading, calculation, time) and fix; read it weekly.
- Guess when the expected value is positive: with +4/−1, cross out at least one option first.
- Analyse every mock test for as long as it took to write; redo wrong questions two days later.

</div>
