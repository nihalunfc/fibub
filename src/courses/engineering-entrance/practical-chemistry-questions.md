**Chemistry · Class 11 and 12 laboratory · about 24 minutes.** Read [Practical chemistry: salt analysis, titrations and tests](/learn/courses/engineering-entrance/practical-chemistry/) first. Here are the question types, worked examples and practice.

<div class="bigidea">Lab questions are either "how much" or "what is it". For "how much", write the balanced equation or use equivalents and keep track of millimoles. For "what is it", read every observation as a clue and rule options out one at a time: a single colour or gas is rarely enough, so look for the confirming second clue.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Standard solutions.</b> Mass = M × V × molar mass of what you weigh. <i>Trap:</i> use 126 for oxalic acid dihydrate, not 90.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Indicator choice.</b> Match the pH jump. <i>Trap:</i> methyl orange for a weak acid titration gives a far-too-early end point.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Acid-base calculations.</b> mmol acid × basicity = mmol base × acidity. <i>Trap:</i> oxalic acid and H₂SO₄ are dibasic.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Redox titrations.</b> Electrons balance; KMnO₄ 5, K₂Cr₂O₇ 6, Fe²⁺ 1, oxalate 2, thiosulphate 1. <i>Trap:</i> iodometry: 1 I₂ uses 2 thiosulphate.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Anion identification.</b> Gas, smell, precipitate and its solubility. <i>Trap:</i> CO₂ and SO₂ both turn lime water milky; AgBr is only partly soluble in ammonia.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Cation groups.</b> Group reagent and why. <i>Trap:</i> without NH₄Cl, Mg(OH)₂ would come down in group III.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Organic tests.</b> Lassaigne, functional groups. <i>Trap:</i> the blood-red colour means N and S together, not iron.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Preparations and chromatography.</b> Reagents, conditions, Rf. <i>Trap:</i> acetylation protects the amine before nitration in p-nitroacetanilide.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 3, back-titration).** 1.0 g of impure CaCO₃ is dissolved in 50 mL of 0.5 M HCl. The excess acid needs 20 mL of 0.5 M NaOH. Find the % purity. *Solution.* HCl taken 25 mmol, left 10 mmol, used 15 mmol. CaCO₃ + 2HCl, so CaCO₃ = 7.5 mmol = 0.75 g. **Purity 75%.**

**Example 2 (level 4, iodometry).** 25 mL of a CuSO₄ solution is treated with excess KI; the freed iodine needs 20 mL of 0.1 M Na₂S₂O₃. Find the molarity of Cu²⁺. *Solution.* 2Cu²⁺ + 4I⁻ → 2CuI + I₂; I₂ + 2S₂O₃²⁻ → 2I⁻ + S₄O₆²⁻. So Cu²⁺ : S₂O₃²⁻ = 1 : 1. Cu²⁺ = 2 mmol in 25 mL: **0.08 M**.

**Example 3 (level 4, salt puzzle).** A green salt gives a reddish-brown precipitate with NH₄OH/NH₄Cl after its solution is boiled with a drop of conc. HNO₃, and its solution with BaCl₂ gives a white precipitate insoluble in HCl. Identify it. *Solution.* Green salt, oxidised to Fe³⁺ by HNO₃, Fe(OH)₃ in group III: the cation is Fe²⁺. BaSO₄: sulphate. The salt is **FeSO₄** (green vitriol, FeSO₄·7H₂O). HNO₃ is added in group III precisely to convert Fe²⁺ to Fe³⁺, whose hydroxide is far less soluble.

**Example 4 (level 5, Lassaigne logic).** A compound's sodium extract gives no Prussian blue, gives violet with nitroprusside, and after boiling with HNO₃ gives a pale yellow precipitate with AgNO₃ that is partly soluble in ammonia. Which elements are present? *Solution.* No N; S present (violet); pale yellow, partly soluble in NH₃: **Br**. So the compound contains **S and Br** (besides C and H).

<div class="myth">

**"Phenolphthalein works for every titration because it gives such a clear colour."** In a strong acid-weak base titration (HCl with ammonia) the equivalence pH is about 5, and phenolphthalein would only turn pink after a large excess of base. Clarity of colour does not matter if it changes at the wrong pH.

</div>

<dl>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-chemistry" data-topics="practical-chemistry" data-mode="practice"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Weigh the hydrate you use: oxalic acid dihydrate 126, Mohr's salt 392.
- Indicator: phenolphthalein for weak acids, methyl orange for weak bases.
- Balance electrons in redox titrations; KMnO₄ needs dilute H₂SO₄.
- Two clues for every ion: CO₂ vs SO₂, AgCl vs AgBr vs AgI with ammonia.
- Group order and reagents: HCl, H₂S/HCl, NH₄OH/NH₄Cl, H₂S/NH₄OH, (NH₄)₂CO₃, Na₂HPO₄.
- Lassaigne colours: Prussian blue (N), violet (S), blood red (N + S); boil with HNO₃ before AgNO₃.

</div>
