**Chemistry · Class 11 · about 26 minutes.** Read [Chemical bonding and molecular structure](/learn/courses/engineering-entrance/chemical-bonding/) first. Here are the question types, worked examples and practice.

<div class="bigidea">Two counts answer most bonding questions: the electron pairs round the central atom (shape, hybridisation, polarity) and the electrons in molecular orbitals (bond order, magnetism). Get the count right and the rest follows.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Shape and lone pairs.</b> <i>Method:</i> lone pairs on central atom = (valence electrons − bonds ± charge)/2, with each double bond counted as using 2 of the atom's electrons. <i>Trap:</i> the shape ignores lone pairs (NH₃ is pyramidal, not tetrahedral).</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Hybridisation.</b> Steric number = σ bonds + lone pairs. <i>Trap:</i> π bonds do not count; in SO₂ sulfur is sp², not sp³.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Bond angle order.</b> More lone pairs, smaller angle; with the same shape, a more electronegative central atom gives a larger angle (NH₃ 107° > PH₃ 93.5°). <i>Trap:</i> NF₃ (102°) is smaller than NH₃.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Counting σ and π.</b> Draw every bond, including C-H. <i>Trap:</i> benzene has 12 σ and 3 π; a triple bond has one σ only.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Formal charge and resonance.</b> FC = V − L − B/2; bond order in resonance = total bonds ÷ positions. <i>Trap:</i> PO₄³⁻ bond order is 5/4, SO₄²⁻ is 6/4.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Dipole moment.</b> Shape first, then vector sum; percent ionic = μ/(4.8 d) × 100 with d in Å. <i>Trap:</i> cis-1,2-dichloroethene is polar, trans is not.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Fajans and lattice energy.</b> Small cation, big anion: more covalent, lower melting point. <i>Trap:</i> Born-Haber signs: lattice formation releases energy.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. MO theory.</b> Count electrons, fill (switch the σ2p/π2p order above 14), bond order, unpaired electrons. <i>Trap:</i> B₂ and C₂ use the "π below σ" order; B₂ is paramagnetic, C₂ is not.</div>
<div class="step"><b>9. Hydrogen bonding.</b> Boiling points, solubility, ortho vs para isomers. <i>Trap:</i> HF has the strongest single H-bond but water has the highest boiling point (two H and two lone pairs per molecule).</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 3, lone pairs and shape).** Find the shape of ClF₃. *Solution.* Cl has 7 valence electrons; 3 go into bonds, leaving 4 = 2 lone pairs. Steric number 5 (sp³d). Lone pairs take two equatorial positions, so the three F atoms make a **T-shape**, with F-Cl-F angles a little under 90°.

**Example 2 (level 4, MO order and magnetism).** Arrange O₂²⁻, O₂⁻, O₂, O₂⁺ by bond length and say which are paramagnetic. *Solution.* Electrons 18, 17, 16, 15; bond orders 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5. Bond length is the reverse: **O₂²⁻ > O₂⁻ > O₂ > O₂⁺**. Unpaired electrons: 0, 1, 2, 1. So O₂⁻, O₂ and O₂⁺ are paramagnetic; O₂²⁻ is diamagnetic.

**Example 3 (level 4, dipole moment of a bent molecule).** Each O-H bond in water has a dipole of 1.5 D and the angle is 104.5°. Find the dipole of water. *Solution.* Two equal vectors at angle θ add to 2μ cos(θ/2) = 2 × 1.5 × cos 52.25° = 3 × 0.612 = **1.84 D**, close to the measured 1.85 D.

**Example 4 (level 5, Born-Haber for MgO).** Explain why MgO exists as Mg²⁺O²⁻ though making Mg²⁺ costs about 2189 kJ/mol and O²⁻ costs about +700 kJ/mol overall from O. *Solution.* The lattice enthalpy of MgO is about 3800 kJ/mol, around four times that of NaCl, because both ions carry charge 2 and are small (energy grows with q₁q₂/r). That huge release more than pays for the second ionisation and the second electron gain, so ΔH<sub>f</sub> is about −602 kJ/mol.

<div class="myth">

**"Hybridisation causes the shape."** It is the other way round. The shape comes from electron pair repulsion (or experiment); hybridisation is a model we choose to describe orbitals pointing in those directions.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-chemistry" data-topics="chemical-bonding" data-mode="practice"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Steric number = σ bonds + lone pairs; 2 to 6 gives sp to sp³d².
- Lone pairs go equatorial in a trigonal bipyramid, opposite each other in an octahedron.
- Resonance bond order = total bonds ÷ positions (CO₃²⁻ 4/3, PO₄³⁻ 5/4).
- Vector sum for dipoles: 2μ cos(θ/2) for two equal bonds.
- MO: switch order above 14 electrons; B₂, O₂ paramagnetic; bond order sets length.
- Lattice enthalpy rises sharply with ionic charge (MgO ≫ NaCl).

</div>
