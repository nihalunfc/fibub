**Chemistry · Class 12 · about 25 minutes.** Read [Aldehydes, ketones and carboxylic acids](/learn/courses/engineering-entrance/aldehydes-ketones-acids/) first. Here are the question types, worked examples and practice.

<div class="bigidea">Look for three features in any structure: the H on an aldehyde's C=O (oxidation tests, Cannizzaro), α-hydrogens (aldol, haloform, HVZ), and the CH₃CO- group (iodoform). For acids, look at what pulls or pushes electrons near COOH. Those features answer most questions in this chapter.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Order of reactivity to nucleophiles.</b> Charge on carbon and crowding. <i>Trap:</i> benzaldehyde is less reactive than ethanal (resonance).</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Distinguishing tests.</b> Tollens, Fehling, iodoform, 2,4-DNP, NaHCO₃. <i>Trap:</i> benzaldehyde fails Fehling; methanoic acid passes Tollens.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Aldol products.</b> Count α-H, write the enolate, then dehydrate on heating. <i>Trap:</i> crossed aldol of two enolisable aldehydes gives 4 products.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Cannizzaro.</b> No α-H. <i>Trap:</i> in crossed Cannizzaro, HCHO is always oxidised.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Reagent matching.</b> Rosenmund, Stephen, Etard, Gattermann-Koch, Clemmensen, Wolff-Kishner, HVZ, Kolbe. <i>Trap:</i> NaBH₄ does not reduce acids or esters.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Acid strength.</b> -I groups closer is stronger; F > Cl > Br; ortho effect. <i>Trap:</i> HCOOH is stronger than CH₃COOH.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Derivatives.</b> Order of reactivity; conversions between them. <i>Trap:</i> amides are the least reactive.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Numericals.</b> Silver from Tollens, iodoform yield, acid titration for molar mass, pH, esterification with K = 4. <i>Trap:</i> HCHO gives 4 Ag.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 3, crossed aldol).** How many aldol (β-hydroxy carbonyl) products, ignoring stereoisomers, form from ethanal and propanal in dilute NaOH? *Solution.* Each has α-H, so each can be the enolate or the acceptor: ethanal + ethanal, propanal + propanal, ethanal enolate + propanal, propanal enolate + ethanal. **4 products.**

**Example 2 (level 4, molar mass by titration).** 0.244 g of a monobasic aromatic acid needs 20 mL of 0.1 M NaOH. Find its molar mass and suggest the acid. *Solution.* NaOH = 2 mmol = acid. M = 0.244/0.002 = **122 g/mol**: benzoic acid, C₆H₅COOH.

**Example 3 (level 4, unknown ketone).** A ketone C₅H₁₀O gives a yellow precipitate with I₂/NaOH and does not reduce Tollens. Which ketones fit? *Solution.* It needs CH₃CO-: pentan-2-one or 3-methylbutan-2-one. Pentan-3-one is excluded. Ozonolysis or the acid formed in the iodoform reaction (butanoic vs 2-methylpropanoic acid) tells these two apart.

**Example 4 (level 5, acid strength).** Rank: benzoic acid, 4-nitrobenzoic acid, 4-methoxybenzoic acid, 2-methylbenzoic acid. *Solution.* 4-nitro (strong −R) is strongest. 2-methyl is stronger than benzoic by the **ortho effect** (the ring twists, so it no longer pushes electrons into COOH). 4-methoxy (+R) is weakest. **4-nitro > 2-methyl > benzoic > 4-methoxy.**

<div class="myth">

**"Iodoform works for any ketone."** It needs the CH₃CO- group (or CH₃CH(OH)-, which is oxidised to it). Diethyl ketone and benzophenone give no iodoform. Ethanal is the only aldehyde that gives it; ethanol is the only primary alcohol that does.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-chemistry" data-topics="aldehydes-ketones-acids" data-mode="practice"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Reactivity to nucleophiles: HCHO > RCHO > RCOR; aromatic slower.
- Fehling fails for benzaldehyde; Tollens works for all aldehydes and HCOOH.
- Iodoform: CH₃CO- or CH₃CH(OH)-; ethanal, ethanol, methyl ketones, propan-2-ol.
- Aldol: α-H, dilute base; Cannizzaro: no α-H, concentrated base; crossed: HCHO oxidised.
- Acid strength: closer and more electronegative substituents win; ortho effect in benzoic acids.
- Titration: moles of NaOH = moles of acid × basicity; molar mass follows.

</div>
