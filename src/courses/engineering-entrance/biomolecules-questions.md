**Chemistry · Class 12 · about 22 minutes.** Read [Biomolecules](/learn/courses/engineering-entrance/biomolecules/) first. Here are the question types, worked examples and practice.

<div class="bigidea">Biomolecule questions mostly test a short list of facts and one or two reasons behind each: why glucose is a ring, why sucrose does not reduce, why cellulose is not food for us, why amino acids melt so high. The numerical ones are short: peptide masses, base pairing, rotations and pI.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Glucose structure evidence.</b> Match each reaction to what it proves. <i>Trap:</i> nitric acid gives saccharic acid (both ends), bromine water gluconic acid (CHO only).</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Ring forms and mutarotation.</b> α and β anomers, pyranose and furanose. <i>Trap:</i> anomers are not enantiomers; they differ only at C-1.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Reducing or not.</b> Free anomeric carbon. <i>Trap:</i> fructose, a ketose, still reduces Tollens' and Fehling's reagents.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Disaccharides and polysaccharides.</b> Units and links. <i>Trap:</i> amylose is α(1→4), cellulose β(1→4); amylopectin and glycogen have α(1→6) branches.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Amino acids.</b> Zwitterion, pI, essential vs non-essential, charge at a given pH. <i>Trap:</i> at pH below pI the amino acid is positive and moves to the cathode.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Peptides and proteins.</b> Count peptides, molar mass, structure levels, denaturation. <i>Trap:</i> denaturation does not touch the primary structure.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Enzymes, vitamins, hormones.</b> Match vitamin to disease and solubility. <i>Trap:</i> B₁₂ is water-soluble but stored in the liver.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Nucleic acids.</b> Bases, sugars, nucleoside vs nucleotide, Chargaff's rule, H-bonds. <i>Trap:</i> uracil replaces thymine only in RNA.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 3, base composition).** A double-stranded DNA has 3000 base pairs and 600 adenines. Find the number of hydrogen bonds. *Solution.* A = T = 600 (A-T pairs: 600). G-C pairs = 3000 − 600 = 2400. H-bonds = 2 × 600 + 3 × 2400 = **8400**.

**Example 2 (level 4, invert sugar rotation).** Sucrose solution is completely hydrolysed into equal moles of glucose (+52.5°) and fructose (−92°). Taking the rotation of an equimolar mixture as the average, find its specific rotation. *Solution.* (52.5 − 92)/2 = **−19.75°**, about −20°. The rotation has turned from + to −, hence "invert".

**Example 3 (level 4, pI).** Glycine has pKa values 2.34 and 9.60. Find its pI and its main form at pH 1. *Solution.* pI = (2.34 + 9.60)/2 = **5.97**. At pH 1 (well below 2.34), both groups carry H⁺: **⁺H₃N-CH₂-COOH**, net charge +1.

**Example 4 (level 5, sequence from hydrolysis).** A tripeptide gives Gly and Ala on hydrolysis in a 2 : 1 ratio, and its N-terminal residue is Ala. Partial hydrolysis gives Ala-Gly but no Gly-Ala. Find the sequence. *Solution.* Starts with Ala; must contain Ala-Gly; no Gly-Ala means Ala is not after Gly. With two Gly: **Ala-Gly-Gly**.

<div class="myth">

**"α-glucose and β-glucose are mirror images."** They are anomers: only C-1 differs, while C-2 to C-5 are the same. Mirror images (enantiomers) would be D- and L-glucose, which differ at every chiral carbon.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-chemistry" data-topics="biomolecules" data-mode="practice"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Glucose evidence: HI (straight chain), Br₂ water (CHO), acetylation (5 OH), HNO₃ (both ends oxidised).
- Free anomeric carbon means reducing; sucrose is the classic non-reducing sugar.
- α links in starch and glycogen; β(1→4) in cellulose.
- Zwitterions explain high melting points; pI is where net charge is zero.
- n different amino acids give n! peptides using each once.
- A=T (2 H-bonds), G≡C (3); DNA has T and deoxyribose, RNA U and ribose.

</div>
