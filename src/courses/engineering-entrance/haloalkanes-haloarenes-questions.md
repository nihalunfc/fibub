**Chemistry · Class 12 · about 24 minutes.** Read [Haloalkanes and haloarenes](/learn/courses/engineering-entrance/haloalkanes-haloarenes/) first. Here are the question types, worked examples and practice.

<div class="bigidea">For any halide question, ask three things: what kind of carbon carries the halogen (1°, 2°, 3°, allylic, benzylic, aryl), what the reagent is (strong or weak nucleophile, bulky base, metal), and what the solvent is. Those three decide SN1, SN2, E2, or no reaction at all.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Order of SN1 or SN2 reactivity.</b> SN2 by crowding, SN1 by cation stability. <i>Trap:</i> allylic and benzylic halides are fast in both.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Rate laws and kinetics.</b> SN2 second order, SN1 first order. <i>Trap:</i> changing [Nu⁻] has no effect on an SN1 rate.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Stereochemistry.</b> SN2 inversion, SN1 racemisation; optical purity of products. <i>Trap:</i> inversion of configuration does not always flip the R/S label (priorities can change).</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Substitution or elimination.</b> Aqueous vs alcoholic KOH; Saytzeff product. <i>Trap:</i> a bulky base (tert-butoxide) gives the less substituted (Hofmann) alkene.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Reagents and named reactions.</b> SOCl₂, Finkelstein, Swarts, Sandmeyer, Wurtz, Fittig. <i>Trap:</i> Wurtz with two different halides gives three products.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Grignard reagents.</b> React with active hydrogen to give R-H. <i>Trap:</i> water, alcohols, amines and terminal alkynes all destroy them.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Aryl halides.</b> Resistance to SN, activation by o/p-NO₂, o/p directing in electrophilic substitution, dipole moments. <i>Trap:</i> meta-NO₂ barely helps.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Analysis and environment.</b> Carius % halogen, AgNO₃ test (aryl and vinyl halides give no precipitate), CFCs, chloroform storage.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 3, SN2 order).** Rank for SN2 with I⁻: 1-bromobutane, 2-bromobutane, 1-bromo-2,2-dimethylpropane (neopentyl bromide), bromomethane. *Solution.* **CH₃Br > 1-bromobutane > 2-bromobutane > neopentyl bromide.** Neopentyl is primary but the bulky tert-butyl group next door blocks the back of the carbon: it is very slow by SN2.

**Example 2 (level 4, Wurtz mixtures).** A mixture of CH₃Br and C₂H₅Br is treated with sodium in dry ether. Name the alkanes formed. *Solution.* CH₃-CH₃ (ethane), CH₃-C₂H₅ (propane) and C₂H₅-C₂H₅ (butane). **Three alkanes**, which is why Wurtz is used only for symmetric alkanes.

**Example 3 (level 4, optical purity).** (R)-2-bromooctane of specific rotation −34° undergoes SN1 hydrolysis. The 2-octanol formed shows +1.6° when pure (R)-2-octanol is −10°. Find the percentage of racemisation. *Solution.* Optical purity of product = 1.6/10 = 16%, in the inverted (S) form. So 16% net inversion; the rest, **84%**, is racemic. SN1 is not perfectly racemic because the leaving bromide briefly shields one face.

**Example 4 (level 5, aryl halide reactivity).** Arrange for hydrolysis with aqueous NaOH: chlorobenzene, 4-nitrochlorobenzene, 3-nitrochlorobenzene, 2,4-dinitrochlorobenzene. *Solution.* **2,4-dinitro > 4-nitro > 3-nitro > chlorobenzene.** Nitro groups at ortho and para hold the negative charge of the intermediate on their oxygens; at meta they help only by induction.

<div class="myth">

**"Every alkyl halide gives a white or yellow precipitate with aqueous AgNO₃."** Only those that release X⁻ easily. Aryl and vinyl halides give no precipitate. Tertiary and benzylic halides give one quickly (SN1), primary ones slowly. That difference is a classic test.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-chemistry" data-topics="haloalkanes-haloarenes" data-mode="practice"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- SN2: methyl best, neopentyl very slow; SN1: 3°, allylic, benzylic best.
- SN2 rate depends on both reactants; SN1 only on RX.
- Aqueous KOH substitutes; alcoholic KOH eliminates (Saytzeff); bulky bases give Hofmann alkenes.
- KCN → nitrile, AgCN → isocyanide; KNO₂ → nitrite, AgNO₂ → nitroalkane.
- Wurtz with mixed halides gives three alkanes.
- Aryl halides: no AgNO₃ precipitate; o/p-NO₂ makes SN easy; para-dichlorobenzene has zero dipole.

</div>
