**Chemistry · Class 12 · about 25 minutes.** Read [Coordination compounds](/learn/courses/engineering-entrance/coordination-compounds/) first. Here are the question types, worked examples and practice.

<div class="bigidea">Every complex question starts with three numbers: the metal's oxidation state, its d count and its coordination number. Add the ligand's field strength and you can answer shape, hybridisation, magnetism, colour and CFSE in one go.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Oxidation state, CN and charge.</b> <i>Trap:</i> en counts 2 donor atoms; NO can be NO⁺ in nitroprusside.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Names and formulae.</b> Alphabetical ligands, -ate for anions, ferrate/cuprate/argentate. <i>Trap:</i> ammine (NH₃) vs amine; "di" does not count in alphabetical order.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Werner tests.</b> AgCl precipitated, conductivity, freezing point (ions per formula). <i>Trap:</i> only Cl⁻ outside the bracket reacts.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Counting isomers.</b> Geometrical and optical. <i>Trap:</i> [M(AA)₂B₂]: cis is chiral, so 3 stereoisomers in all.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Hybridisation and shape.</b> d⁸ with strong field: dsp²; with weak field and CN 4: sp³. <i>Trap:</i> d¹⁰ Zn²⁺ is always sp³ for CN 4.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Magnetic moment.</b> Decide high or low spin, count unpaired. <i>Trap:</i> [CoF₆]³⁻ high spin (4 unpaired) but [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ low spin (0).</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. CFSE, colour and wavelength.</b> −0.4/+0.6 for octahedral, −0.6/+0.4 for tetrahedral. λ (nm) ≈ 119 600/Δ (kJ/mol). <i>Trap:</i> stronger ligand, shorter wavelength absorbed.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. EAN, carbonyls, stability constants.</b> EAN = Z − oxidation state + 2 × CN. β = K₁K₂…Kₙ. <i>Trap:</i> back bonding lowers the C-O bond order.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 3, isomer count).** How many stereoisomers does [Co(NH₃)₃Cl₃] have? *Solution.* MA₃B₃ octahedral: **2** (fac and mer). Neither is chiral, since each has a mirror plane.

**Example 2 (level 4, isomer count).** How many stereoisomers does [Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺ have? *Solution.* trans (achiral) and cis; the cis form is non-superimposable on its mirror image, giving d and l. **3 in all.**

**Example 3 (level 4, freezing point).** A 0.01 m solution of CoCl₃·5NH₃ (fully ionised) freezes at −0.0558 °C (K_f = 1.86). Write its formula. *Solution.* i = 0.0558/(1.86 × 0.01) = 3, so one formula unit gives 3 ions: one complex ion and two Cl⁻. Co(III) needs CN 6, so 5 NH₃ and 1 Cl sit inside: **[Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂**. *Check:* always confirm that your formula gives CN 6 for Co(III) and the right number of ions.

**Example 4 (level 5, magnetism and CFSE together).** For [Mn(CN)₆]³⁻, find unpaired electrons, μ and CFSE (Δo and P). *Solution.* Mn³⁺ is d⁴; CN⁻ is strong field: t₂g⁴ e_g⁰. Unpaired = 2, μ = √8 = **2.83 BM**. CFSE = 4 × (−0.4) = **−1.6Δo + P** (one extra pair compared with the high-spin ion).

<div class="myth">

**"Square planar complexes of d⁸ ions are always paramagnetic, like tetrahedral ones."** dsp² square planar d⁸ complexes have all 8 electrons paired in the four lower d orbitals: diamagnetic. [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ is diamagnetic; [NiCl₄]²⁻ (tetrahedral) has 2 unpaired electrons.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-chemistry" data-topics="coordination-compounds" data-mode="practice"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Ions in solution = complex ion + counter-ions; AgCl counts only free Cl⁻.
- MA₄B₂ and MA₃B₃ have 2 geometrical isomers each; [M(AA)₃] and cis-[M(AA)₂B₂] are chiral.
- d⁸: strong field square planar diamagnetic; weak field tetrahedral paramagnetic.
- Octahedral CFSE: −0.4 per t₂g, +0.6 per e_g; add P for each extra pair.
- EAN = Z − oxidation state + 2 × CN; Ni(CO)₄ and Fe(CO)₅ reach 36.
- Back bonding: stronger M-C, weaker C-O.

</div>
