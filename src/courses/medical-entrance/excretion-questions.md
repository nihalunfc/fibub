**Biology · Class 11 · about 24 minutes.** Read [Excretory products and their elimination](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/excretion/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">Excretion questions turn on one map: each segment of the nephron and what it lets through (water, salt, or both). Add the four hormones as switches on that map, and the numbers (125 mL, 180 L, 99%, 300 to 1200), and you have the chapter.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Waste and animal.</b> Ammonotelic, ureotelic, uricotelic with examples. <i>Trap:</i> marine fishes are ureotelic; bony freshwater fishes ammonotelic.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Excretory organs.</b> Flame cells, nephridia, Malpighian tubules, green glands. <i>Trap:</i> Branchiostoma has protonephridia; prawns have green glands.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Kidney structure.</b> Position, size, hilum, pelvis, calyces, pyramids, columns of Bertini; which nephron parts are in the cortex.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Segment functions.</b> <i>Method:</i> a two-column table (water, salt) for each segment. <i>Trap:</i> descending limb: water only; ascending limb: salt only.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Numbers.</b> Renal blood flow 1100 to 1200 mL/min; GFR 125 mL/min; 180 L/day; 99% reabsorbed; urine 1 to 1.5 L, 25 to 30 g urea; medulla 300 to 1200 mOsmol/L.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Countercurrent.</b> Which structures, which solutes (NaCl and urea), which way the flows go.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Hormones.</b> ADH (water), renin–angiotensin–aldosterone (pressure and salt), ANF (opposes). <i>Trap:</i> ADH comes from the neurohypophysis but is made in the hypothalamus; renin is an enzyme from JG cells.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Micturition, other organs, disorders.</b> Reflex, liver and skin products, uraemia and dialysis, calculi, glomerulonephritis, glycosuria.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, numbers).** GFR is 120 mL a minute and urine is 1.8 L a day. What percentage of the filtrate is reabsorbed? *Solution.* Filtrate = 120 × 1440 = 172.8 L. Reabsorbed = 171 L. 171 / 172.8 × 100 = **98.96%**.

**Example 2 (level 4, assertion-reason).** A: Urine can be about four times as concentrated as the glomerular filtrate. R: The medullary interstitium has an osmolarity gradient from about 300 to 1200 mOsmol/L. *Solution.* Both true; R explains A (1200/300 = 4). **Both true; R explains A.**

**Example 3 (level 5, sequence).** Put in order: (a) aldosterone acts on the distal tubule, (b) GFR falls, (c) angiotensin II forms, (d) JG cells release renin, (e) adrenal cortex stimulated. *Solution.* b → d → c → e → a.

**Example 4 (level 5, statements).** Statement I: The ascending limb of Henle's loop is permeable to water. Statement II: Vasa recta are well developed in cortical nephrons. *Solution.* The ascending limb is impermeable to water, so I is false. Vasa recta are absent or tiny in cortical nephrons, so II is false. **Both incorrect.**

<div class="myth">

**"Juxtamedullary nephrons are the majority."** Most nephrons are cortical, with short loops. The fewer juxtamedullary nephrons, with long loops and vasa recta, do most of the concentrating.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-biology" data-topics="excretion" data-mode="practice" data-exam="neet"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Marine fish ureotelic; birds, reptiles, insects uricotelic; urea made in the liver.
- Corpuscle, PCT, DCT in the cortex; loops in the medulla; most nephrons cortical.
- Descending limb water out; ascending limb salt out; PCT reabsorbs 70 to 80%.
- GFR 125 mL/min = 180 L/day; 99% reabsorbed; medulla 300 to 1200 mOsmol/L.
- ADH saves water; renin → angiotensin II → aldosterone raises BP; ANF lowers it.
