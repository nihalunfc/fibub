**Start here · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will know exactly how NEET is set, marked and turned into a seat.

<div class="bigidea">NEET is one 3-hour paper of 180 questions from Plus Two Physics, Chemistry and Biology. Biology is half the marks, every wrong answer costs a mark, and your all-India rank decides your seat.</div>

## 1. The paper

<figure class="fig" data-fig="exam-compare" data-exams="neet"><figcaption>NEET UG pattern for the 2025-26 cycle (checked 2026-11-22). The pattern can change: always read the current NTA bulletin.</figcaption></figure>

- **180 questions, all compulsory, in 180 minutes**: one minute per question on average.
- **Physics 45, Chemistry 45, Botany 45, Zoology 45.**
- **+4 for a right answer, −1 for a wrong one, 0 for a blank.** Full marks: 720.
- Pen and paper: you fill bubbles on an OMR sheet, so leave time to fill them carefully.

## 2. How the score works

```math
\text{score} = 4 \times \text{right} - 1 \times \text{wrong}
```

<div class="meaning">
<dl>
<dt>right</dt><dd>questions answered correctly</dd>
<dt>wrong</dt><dd>questions answered incorrectly (blanks are neither)</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Four careless mistakes cancel one correct answer. At the top, ranks are separated by a few marks, so accuracy matters as much as knowledge.</dd>
</dl>
</div>

## 3. Where the marks come from

Biology carries 360 marks, and most Biology questions test lines, tables and diagrams from the NCERT books. That makes **NCERT Biology, read line by line and practised with questions**, the single biggest lever on your rank. Physics usually separates students most, because many find it hardest; a good Physics score lifts a rank sharply. Chemistry sits between: organic and inorganic reward memory with understanding, physical chemistry rewards practice with numbers.

<div class="myth">

**"NEET is only Biology."** Biology is half the paper, but students with similar Biology scores are separated by Physics and Chemistry. A strong all-round score beats a perfect Biology score with weak Physics.

</div>

## 4. From score to seat

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><b>Qualify.</b> NTA publishes a qualifying percentile (in recent years the 50th percentile for the general category and lower for reserved categories). Qualifying is necessary, not enough for a seat.</div>
<div class="step"><b>All-India rank.</b> Everyone gets one all-India rank from the NEET score, with tie-break rules for equal scores.</div>
<div class="step"><b>All-India quota.</b> The Medical Counselling Committee fills 15% of seats in government colleges and all seats in central institutes such as AIIMS and JIPMER by all-India rank.</div>
<div class="step"><b>State quota.</b> Each state fills its own share (85% of state government seats, and private colleges) by NEET rank through its own counselling, for example the CEE in Kerala and the KEA in Karnataka.</div>
<div class="step"><b>The same rank opens other courses</b> too: BDS, AYUSH (BAMS, BHMS, BSMS, BUMS), veterinary science and some nursing and allied courses, each with its own counselling.</div>
</div>
