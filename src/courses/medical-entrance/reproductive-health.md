**Biology · Class 12 · about 26 minutes.** By the end you will be able to say how every contraceptive method works and which step it blocks, list the STIs and which ones are not curable, choose the right assisted reproductive technique for a given problem, and handle NEET's statement and matching questions on this chapter.

<div class="bigidea">Reproductive health means a healthy reproductive life in every sense: physical, emotional, behavioural and social. In practice it is about three choices: having children when you want (contraception, MTP), staying free of infections (STIs), and having children when it is hard (infertility treatment).</div>

## 1. Reproductive health and India's programmes

The World Health Organization describes reproductive health as total well-being in all aspects of reproduction, not just working organs. India was one of the first countries to start a national family planning programme, in **1951**. It has since grown into the **Reproductive and Child Health Care (RCH)** programmes: awareness and sex education in schools, birth control, care of mothers before and after birth, promoting breastfeeding, preventing STIs, and early detection of problems.

**Amniocentesis** takes a little amniotic fluid to check fetal cells for chromosomal disorders. Because it can also reveal the sex of the fetus, India has a **statutory ban on its use for sex determination**, to stop female foeticide.

## 2. Population and birth control

World population was about 2 billion in 1900 and about 6 billion by 2000. India's was about 350 million at independence and crossed 1 billion in 2000. Better health care lowered the death rate, the maternal mortality rate and the infant mortality rate, and more people reached reproductive age. The 2011 census showed a growth rate below 2% (20 per 1000 per year), still high. Measures include promoting small families, raising the legal marriage age (18 years for women, 21 for men) and making contraception widely available.

An ideal contraceptive is **user-friendly, easily available, effective, reversible, with few or no side effects, and does not interfere with sexual drive or the act**.

## 3. Contraceptive methods

Choose a method below and see which link in the chain to pregnancy it breaks.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="contraception-block"><figcaption>The five links from sperm and egg to pregnancy. Each method breaks one or more of them. For learning only, not medical advice.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>Natural methods</b> stop egg and sperm meeting, with no devices or drugs. <b>Periodic abstinence</b>: avoid intercourse from about day 10 to day 17, when ovulation is likely. <b>Withdrawal (coitus interruptus)</b>: withdraw before ejaculation. <b>Lactational amenorrhoea</b>: intense breastfeeding delays ovulation and periods, reliable only up to about six months after birth. Cheap, no side effects, but high failure rates.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Barriers</b> physically stop sperm meeting egg. <b>Condoms</b> (thin rubber or latex; male and female versions; brand "Nirodh" is the familiar free government one) also protect against STIs and AIDS. <b>Diaphragms, cervical caps and vaults</b> cover the cervix and are reusable; spermicidal creams, jellies and foams are often used with them.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Intrauterine devices (IUDs)</b>, put in the uterus by a doctor. Non-medicated (Lippes loop); copper-releasing (CuT, Cu7, Multiload 375): Cu ions lower sperm movement and fertilising ability; hormone-releasing (Progestasert, LNG-20): make the uterus unsuitable for implantation and the cervix hostile to sperm. All raise phagocytosis of sperm in the uterus. Ideal for women who want to delay pregnancy or space children; one of the most widely accepted methods in India.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Pills.</b> Progestogens alone or progestogen-estrogen combinations, taken daily for 21 days starting within the first five days of the cycle, then a 7-day break. They stop ovulation and implantation and change cervical mucus to block sperm. <b>Saheli</b>, developed by CDRI Lucknow, is a once-a-week, non-steroidal pill with very few side effects. Progestogens can also be given as injections or implants under the skin, lasting much longer.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Emergency contraception.</b> Progestogens or progestogen-estrogen pills, or an IUD, within 72 hours of unprotected intercourse or rape. Very effective for that one event, not for routine use.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Surgical methods (sterilisation)</b>, for people who want no more children. <b>Vasectomy</b>: a small part of each vas deferens is removed or tied through a small cut in the scrotum. <b>Tubectomy</b>: a small part of each fallopian tube is removed or tied. Very effective, but reversal is poor. The testes and ovaries keep making hormones, so sexual life is unchanged.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Vasectomy stops ejaculation or male hormones."** Semen is still made by the seminal vesicles and prostate, and the testes still make testosterone. Only the sperm are kept out.

</div>

Contraceptives are not a substitute for careful choices: they can have side effects such as nausea, abdominal pain, breakthrough bleeding, irregular bleeding or, rarely, breast cancer, so a doctor should guide their use.

## 4. Medical termination of pregnancy (MTP)

Intentional ending of a pregnancy before full term. India legalised MTP in **1971** with strict conditions to stop misuse, such as sex-selective abortion. Under the current law (amended in 2021), one doctor's opinion is enough up to 20 weeks, and two doctors' opinions are needed from 20 to 24 weeks for certain categories of women. MTPs are **relatively safe in the first trimester** (up to 12 weeks) and much riskier in the second trimester. Unsafe abortion by untrained people is dangerous and can kill.

## 5. Sexually transmitted infections (STIs)

Also called venereal diseases or reproductive tract infections. Examples: **gonorrhoea, syphilis, genital herpes, chlamydiasis, genital warts, trichomoniasis, hepatitis B and HIV**. Hepatitis B and HIV also spread by sharing needles and syringes, by transfusion of infected blood, and from an infected mother to her fetus.

All except **hepatitis B, genital herpes and HIV** are completely curable if detected early and treated properly. Early symptoms are mild (itching, fluid discharge, slight pain, swelling in the genital region), so many people ignore them, and the infections can lead to pelvic inflammatory disease, abortions, stillbirths, ectopic pregnancy, infertility and cancer of the reproductive tract. People aged 15 to 24 are most at risk. Prevention: avoid sex with unknown or multiple partners, always use condoms, and see a qualified doctor early.

## 6. Infertility and ART

A couple unable to conceive after two years of unprotected intercourse may be infertile; the cause can lie with either partner. **Assisted reproductive technologies (ART)**:

| Method | What happens |
|---|---|
| **IVF** (in vitro fertilisation) + **ET** (embryo transfer) | egg and sperm are joined in the lab ("test tube baby"); the zygote or embryo is transferred |
| **ZIFT** | zygote or embryo of up to 8 blastomeres placed in the fallopian tube |
| **IUT** | embryo of more than 8 blastomeres placed in the uterus |
| **GIFT** | an ovum from a donor is placed in the fallopian tube of a woman who cannot make eggs but can support fertilisation and pregnancy |
| **ICSI** | one sperm is injected directly into the ovum in the lab |
| **AI / IUI** | semen (from the husband or a donor) is placed in the vagina or the uterus; for low sperm count or poor sperm |

Fertilisation inside the body (in vivo) can also be followed by embryo transfer. These methods need skilled staff and are costly, so they are available in few places. Adoption is another way to have a family, and it gives a home to orphaned children.

<div class="keypoints">

- India's family planning began in 1951; now RCH programmes. Amniocentesis for sex determination is banned.
- Ideal contraceptive: user-friendly, available, effective, reversible, few side effects.
- Natural (periodic abstinence days 10 to 17, withdrawal, lactational amenorrhoea up to 6 months), barriers, IUDs, pills (Saheli weekly, non-steroidal, CDRI), emergency within 72 hours, surgical (vasectomy, tubectomy).
- Copper IUDs lower sperm motility; hormone IUDs block implantation and make the cervix hostile.
- MTP legal since 1971; safest in the first trimester.
- STIs: hepatitis B, genital herpes and HIV are not completely curable; risk highest at 15 to 24 years.
- ART: IVF-ET, ZIFT (tube, up to 8 cells), IUT (uterus, more than 8), GIFT (ovum), ICSI (sperm injected), AI/IUI.

</div>
