**Biology · Class 11 · about 34 minutes.** By the end you will be able to classify any animal from six features, give the hallmark and two examples for every phylum, and tell the seven vertebrate classes apart by skeleton, skin, heart and reproduction.

<div class="bigidea">Animals are sorted by body plan, not by habitat or size. Six questions (how organised, what symmetry, how many germ layers, what body cavity, segmented or not, notochord or not) place almost every animal NEET names.</div>

## 1. The six features

- **Level of organisation.** Cellular (cells loosely grouped: sponges), tissue (Cnidaria, Ctenophora), organ (Platyhelminthes) and organ system (the rest). The gut is **incomplete** (one opening) in flatworms and **complete** (mouth and anus) from roundworms onward. Circulation is **open** (blood flows through spaces) or **closed** (always in vessels).
- **Symmetry.** Asymmetrical (most sponges), **radial** (any plane through the central axis gives two equal halves: Cnidaria, Ctenophora, adult echinoderms) or **bilateral** (only one such plane: annelids, arthropods and most others).
- **Germ layers.** **Diploblastic**: ectoderm and endoderm with a jelly-like mesoglea between (Cnidaria, Ctenophora). **Triploblastic**: a true mesoderm as well (flatworms onward).
- **Body cavity.** **Coelom** lined by mesoderm (annelids, molluscs, arthropods, echinoderms, hemichordates, chordates). **Pseudocoelom**, with mesoderm in scattered pouches (Aschelminthes). **Acoelomate**, no cavity (Platyhelminthes).
- **Segmentation.** In **metameric segmentation** the body is divided outside and inside into repeated segments (earthworm).
- **Notochord.** A rod of mesoderm on the back of the embryo; animals with one are chordates.

<div class="myth">

**"Radial symmetry means a round animal."** It means any cut through the central axis gives mirror halves. Adult starfish are radial, but their larvae are bilateral.

</div>

## 2. Non-chordate phyla

Pick a phylum and read its six features and hallmark.

|---|---|---|
| Porifera | water canal system, choanocytes, spicules or spongin; hermaphrodite | *Sycon*, *Spongilla*, *Euspongia* |

| Aschelminthes | round; pseudocoelom; muscular pharynx; females longer | *Ascaris*, *Wuchereria*, *Ancylostoma* |
| Annelida | metameric segments; nephridia; closed circulation; parapodia in *Nereis* | *Nereis*, *Pheretima*, *Hirudinaria* |

**Useful arthropods:** *Apis* (honey bee), *Bombyx* (silkworm), *Laccifer* (lac insect). **Vectors:** *Anopheles*, *Culex*, *Aedes*. **Pest:** *Locusta*. **Living fossil:** *Limulus*. Arthropods are the largest phylum, about two-thirds of all named species.

**Common names that mislead:** a jellyfish (*Aurelia*) is a cnidarian, a starfish an echinoderm, a silverfish an insect, a cuttlefish (*Sepia*) a mollusc and a devil fish (*Octopus*) a mollusc. None of them is a fish.

## 3. Chordates

All chordates have, at some stage, a **notochord**, a **dorsal hollow nerve cord**, **pharyngeal gill slits** and a **post-anal tail**; the heart is ventral. Non-chordates have a ventral, solid, double nerve cord and no notochord.

- **Urochordata:** notochord only in the larval tail (*Ascidia*, *Salpa*, *Doliolum*).
- **Cephalochordata:** notochord from head to tail all through life (*Branchiostoma*, also called amphioxus).
- **Vertebrata:** the notochord is replaced by a vertebral column in the adult. So every vertebrate is a chordate, but not every chordate is a vertebrate.

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>Cyclostomata</b> (jawless). Circular sucking mouth, 6 to 15 pairs of gill slits, no scales, no paired fins, cartilaginous skeleton. Ectoparasites on fish; marine but swim to fresh water to spawn, then die. <i>Petromyzon</i> (lamprey), <i>Myxine</i> (hagfish).</div>
<div class="step"><b>Chondrichthyes</b> (cartilage fish). Ventral mouth, gill slits without an operculum, tiny placoid scales, no air bladder (so they keep swimming), two-chambered heart, males with claspers, internal fertilisation, many viviparous. Electric organ in <i>Torpedo</i>, poison sting in <i>Trygon</i>. <i>Scoliodon</i>, <i>Pristis</i>, <i>Carcharodon</i>.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Osteichthyes</b> (bony fish). Terminal mouth, four pairs of gills under an operculum, cycloid or ctenoid scales, air bladder for buoyancy, two-chambered heart, external fertilisation. <i>Exocoetus</i> (flying fish), <i>Hippocampus</i> (sea horse), <i>Labeo</i> (rohu), <i>Catla</i>, <i>Clarias</i> (magur), <i>Betta</i>, <i>Pterophyllum</i>.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Amphibia.</b> Moist skin without scales, eyelids, a tympanum for the ear, cloaca, three-chambered heart (two atria, one ventricle), breathing by gills, lungs and skin, external fertilisation, a tadpole stage. <i>Bufo</i>, <i>Rana</i>, <i>Hyla</i>, <i>Salamandra</i>, <i>Ichthyophis</i> (limbless).</div>
<div class="step"><b>Reptilia.</b> Dry skin with epidermal scales or scutes, no external ear opening, three-chambered heart (four in crocodiles), internal fertilisation, shelled eggs, direct development. Snakes and lizards shed their scales as a skin cast. <i>Chelone</i>, <i>Testudo</i>, <i>Calotes</i>, <i>Hemidactylus</i>, <i>Crocodilus</i>, <i>Naja</i>, <i>Bungarus</i>, <i>Vipera</i>.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Aves.</b> Feathers, beak, forelimbs as wings, scaly hind limbs, only an oil gland at the tail base, <b>pneumatic</b> (air-filled) bones, crop and gizzard, air sacs, four-chambered heart, warm-blooded, oviparous. <i>Corvus</i>, <i>Columba</i>, <i>Psittacula</i>, <i>Struthio</i>, <i>Pavo</i>, <i>Aptenodytes</i> (penguin), <i>Neophron</i>.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Mammalia.</b> Mammary glands, hair, external ear (pinna), different kinds of teeth, four-chambered heart, warm-blooded, mostly viviparous (the platypus <i>Ornithorhynchus</i> lays eggs). <i>Macropus</i>, <i>Pteropus</i> (flying fox), <i>Delphinus</i>, <i>Balaenoptera</i> (blue whale), <i>Camelus</i>, <i>Elephas</i>, <i>Panthera</i>.</div>
</div>

**Cold or warm blooded.** Fishes, amphibians and reptiles are **poikilotherms** (body temperature follows the surroundings); birds and mammals are **homoiotherms** (they keep a constant body temperature).

## 4. Worked example

**Place an animal from clues:** "triploblastic, pseudocoelomate, sexes separate, female longer than male". *Method:* pseudocoelom alone points to Aschelminthes; the size difference confirms it. **Answer:** *Ascaris* or another roundworm.

<div class="keypoints">

- Six features: organisation, symmetry, germ layers, coelom, segmentation, notochord.
- Diploblastic: Cnidaria and Ctenophora; acoelomate: flatworms; pseudocoelomate: roundworms.
- Hallmarks: canal system (sponges), cnidoblasts (Cnidaria), comb plates (Ctenophora), flame cells (flatworms), nephridia (annelids), jointed legs (arthropods), radula (molluscs), water vascular system (echinoderms), stomochord (hemichordates).
- Urochordates have a notochord in the larval tail; cephalochordates keep it for life; vertebrates replace it.
- Hearts: fish 2 chambers; amphibians and most reptiles 3; crocodiles, birds, mammals 4.
- Chondrichthyes: placoid scales, no air bladder, claspers; Osteichthyes: operculum, air bladder.
- Birds: pneumatic bones, air sacs; mammals: mammary glands, pinna; platypus lays eggs.

</div>
