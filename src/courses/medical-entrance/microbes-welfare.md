**Biology · Class 12 · about 26 minutes.** By the end you will be able to match every microbe in the syllabus to its product or job, explain sewage treatment and BOD, describe how biogas forms, and say why biocontrol and biofertilisers are better for the soil than chemicals.

<div class="bigidea">Microbes are tiny chemical factories. Grown in the right conditions, they turn milk into curd, sugar into alcohol, waste into clean water and gas, and air nitrogen into fertiliser. This chapter is a catalogue of which factory makes what.</div>

## 1. In the household

- **Lactic acid bacteria** (*Lactobacillus*) turn milk into **curd**: they make acid that curdles the milk proteins. A little curd added to fresh milk works as the **starter (inoculum)**. LAB also raise **vitamin B₁₂** and, in our stomach, check disease-causing microbes.
- **Dough** for idli and dosa is fermented by bacteria; the puffed look comes from **CO₂**. Bread dough is raised by **baker's yeast**, *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*.
- **Toddy**, a traditional drink in parts of southern India, is made by fermenting palm sap with microbes.
- **Cheese**: different microbes give different textures and flavours. *Propionibacterium sharmanii* makes the large holes in **Swiss cheese** (lots of CO₂); special fungi ripen **Roquefort** cheese.

## 2. In industry

Industrial production needs very large vessels called **fermentors**.

**Beverages**: *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* (**brewer's yeast**) ferments malted cereals and fruit juices to ethanol. **Wine and beer** are made without distillation; **whisky, brandy and rum** are distilled from the fermented broth.

**Antibiotics** (chemicals made by some microbes that kill or stop the growth of disease-causing microbes): **penicillin** was the first discovered, by chance, by **Alexander Fleming**, who saw that a mould, *Penicillium notatum*, stopped *Staphylococci* growing around it. **Ernst Chain and Howard Florey** showed its full value as a medicine, which was used widely in the Second World War. The three shared the 1945 Nobel Prize. Antibiotics changed the treatment of plague, whooping cough, diphtheria and leprosy.

## 3. Sewage treatment

Towns make large amounts of **sewage**: waste water with human excreta and organic matter, full of disease-causing microbes. It cannot go straight into rivers. **Sewage treatment plants (STPs)** clean it in two stages.

**Primary treatment** (physical): floating debris is removed by filtration and grit by sedimentation. Solids that settle form the **primary sludge**; the liquid above is the **effluent**.

**Secondary treatment** (biological): the effluent goes into large **aeration tanks** where it is stirred and air is pumped in. Useful aerobic microbes grow into **flocs** (masses of bacteria held together with fungal filaments in a mesh-like structure). They eat the organic matter, which lowers the **BOD** sharply.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="sewage-bod"><figcaption>BOD of sewage through primary and secondary treatment (typical values, schematic). Longer aeration lets the flocs remove more organic matter.</figcaption></figure>

**BOD (biochemical oxygen demand)** is the amount of oxygen that would be used if all the organic matter in one litre of water were oxidised by bacteria. It measures organic pollution: the higher the BOD, the more polluted the water.

When BOD is low enough, the effluent goes to a **settling tank**, where flocs settle as **activated sludge**. A small part is pumped back into the aeration tank as inoculum; most goes to **anaerobic sludge digesters**, where other bacteria digest the bacteria and fungi and release **biogas** (methane, hydrogen sulphide and CO₂). The treated effluent is released into rivers and streams.

India's Ministry of Environment and Forests started the **Ganga Action Plan** and **Yamuna Action Plan** to build many STPs so that only treated sewage reaches these rivers.

## 4. Biogas

**Biogas** is mostly **methane**, with CO₂ and H₂, made by **methanogens** such as *Methanobacterium* growing anaerobically on cellulose-rich material. They live in sewage digesters and in the **rumen of cattle**, where they help digest cellulose, so cattle **dung (gobar)** is rich in them. A biogas plant has a concrete tank (10 to 15 feet deep) where slurry of dung is fed, a floating cover that rises as gas forms, a gas outlet pipe to houses, and an outlet for the spent slurry, which is used as fertiliser. Biogas is used for cooking and lighting. The technology was developed in India mainly through the efforts of **IARI** (Indian Agricultural Research Institute) and **KVIC** (Khadi and Village Industries Commission).

## 5. Biocontrol agents

**Biocontrol** uses living things to control plant pests and diseases, instead of chemical pesticides that harm useful organisms and pollute food and soil. Organic farmers aim to keep pests at manageable levels rather than wipe them out, because many insects are useful, as pollinators or as food for other animals.

- **Ladybird beetles** eat aphids; **dragonflies** eat mosquitoes.
- ***Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt)**: spores are sprayed on plants; caterpillars eat them, and the toxin released in their gut kills them, sparing other insects. Bt toxin genes have also been put into plants (Bt cotton).
- ***Trichoderma***: free-living fungi in the root ecosystem that control many plant pathogens.
- **Baculoviruses**, mostly of the genus ***Nucleopolyhedrovirus***, attack insects and other arthropods. They are **species-specific with a narrow spectrum**, have no harmful effect on plants, mammals, birds, fish or non-target insects, and are ideal for **integrated pest management (IPM)**, especially in ecologically sensitive areas.

## 6. Biofertilisers

**Biofertilisers** are organisms that enrich the soil with nutrients, an answer to the pollution caused by heavy chemical fertiliser use.

- **Bacteria**: *Rhizobium* fixes nitrogen in the **root nodules of legumes**; *Azotobacter* and *Azospirillum* fix nitrogen **free-living** in soil.
- **Fungi**: **mycorrhiza**, a partnership between fungi (many belong to the genus ***Glomus***) and plant roots. The fungus takes up **phosphorus** from soil for the plant and also gives resistance to root pathogens and tolerance to salinity and drought, with better overall growth.
- **Cyanobacteria**: autotrophic, fix atmospheric nitrogen, for example *Anabaena*, *Nostoc*, *Oscillatoria*. In **paddy fields** they are important biofertilisers and also add organic matter to the soil.
