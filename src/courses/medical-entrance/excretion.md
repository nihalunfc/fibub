**Biology · Class 11 · about 36 minutes.** By the end you will be able to match animals to their nitrogenous waste, label a nephron and say what each part does, explain how the kidney makes concentrated urine, describe the hormones that control it, and do the GFR and reabsorption sums NEET sets.

<div class="bigidea">The kidney does not pick out wastes. It throws out almost all of the plasma (except proteins) and then takes back what the body needs, about 99% of it. The loop of Henle and its blood vessels build a salty medulla that lets the body take back water when hormones say so.</div>

## 1. Nitrogenous wastes and excretory organs

| Waste | Toxicity and water need | Animals |
|---|---|---|
| Ammonia | most toxic; needs a lot of water | **ammonotelic**: many bony fishes, aquatic amphibians, aquatic insects (diffuses out as NH₄⁺ across body or gill surfaces) |
| Urea | less toxic; made from ammonia in the **liver** | **ureotelic**: mammals, many land amphibians, marine fishes |
| Uric acid | least toxic; leaves as a paste or pellet with little water | **uricotelic**: reptiles, birds, land snails, insects |

**Excretory structures:** **protonephridia** or flame cells (flatworms such as *Planaria*, rotifers, some annelids, the cephalochordate *Branchiostoma*), mainly for osmoregulation; **nephridia** (earthworms and other annelids); **Malpighian tubules** (insects, including cockroaches); **antennal or green glands** (crustaceans such as prawns); **kidneys** (vertebrates).

## 2. The human excretory system

A pair of **kidneys**, two **ureters**, a **urinary bladder** and a **urethra**. The kidneys are reddish-brown, bean-shaped, about 10 to 12 cm long, 5 to 7 cm wide, 2 to 3 cm thick and 120 to 170 g each, lying between the last thoracic and third lumbar vertebrae against the back wall of the abdomen. The ureter, vessels and nerves enter at the **hilum**, on the inner concave side, which leads to the funnel-shaped **renal pelvis** and its projections, the **calyces**.

Inside the tough capsule: an outer **cortex** and an inner **medulla** divided into **medullary pyramids** that point into the calyces. The cortex dips between the pyramids as **renal columns (columns of Bertini)**. Each kidney has about **one million nephrons**.

**The nephron:**
- **Glomerulus:** a tuft of capillaries fed by the **afferent arteriole** (from the renal artery) and drained by the narrower **efferent arteriole**. With the cup-shaped **Bowman's capsule** around it, it forms the **Malpighian body (renal corpuscle)**.
- **Renal tubule:** Bowman's capsule → **PCT** (coiled) → **loop of Henle** (descending and ascending limbs) → **DCT** (coiled) → **collecting duct**, shared by many nephrons, which runs through the pyramid to the pelvis.
- The renal corpuscle, PCT and DCT lie in the **cortex**; the loop dips into the **medulla**. Most nephrons are **cortical** (short loops); **juxtamedullary** nephrons have long loops deep in the medulla.
- The efferent arteriole forms **peritubular capillaries** around the tubule; a branch running alongside the loop forms the U-shaped **vasa recta**, absent or tiny in cortical nephrons.

## 3. How urine forms

**Filtration.** The kidneys receive about 1100 to 1200 mL of blood a minute, about a fifth of the output of each ventricle. Blood pressure forces fluid through three layers: the glomerular **endothelium**, the **basement membrane** and the capsule's epithelium of **podocytes**, which leave tiny **filtration slits**. Everything in plasma except proteins passes: this is **ultrafiltration**. **GFR** is about **125 mL a minute, 180 L a day**.

The **juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA)**, where the DCT touches the afferent arteriole, guards the GFR: if it falls, JG cells release **renin**, which raises glomerular blood flow and brings GFR back.

Since only about 1.5 L of urine leaves a day, **about 99% of the filtrate is reabsorbed**. Follow the filtrate along the tubule.

## 4. The countercurrent mechanism

In the loop of Henle the fluid flows down one limb and up the other, in opposite directions; blood does the same in the vasa recta. Their closeness builds an osmotic gradient in the medulla, from about **300 mOsmol/L** in the cortex to about **1200 mOsmol/L** in the inner medulla, made by **NaCl and urea**.

- NaCl leaving the ascending limb is picked up by the descending limb of the vasa recta and returned to the medulla by the ascending vasa recta.
- Some urea enters the thin ascending limb and is passed back to the medulla by the collecting duct.

Water then leaves the collecting duct into this salty medulla, so the kidney can make urine **about four times as concentrated** as the filtrate.

<div class="myth">

**"The loop of Henle reabsorbs most of the water."** The PCT takes back most of it (70 to 80%). The loop's main job is to build the salty medulla that lets the collecting duct take back the rest when needed.

</div>

## 5. Regulation

- **ADH (vasopressin):** when the body loses fluid, **osmoreceptors** stimulate the hypothalamus, and the **neurohypophysis** releases ADH. It makes the late parts of the tubule take back water, preventing **diuresis** (much urine). Plenty of fluid switches it off. ADH also narrows blood vessels and raises blood pressure.
- **Renin–angiotensin:** a fall in glomerular blood flow, pressure or GFR makes JG cells release **renin**, which turns **angiotensinogen** into angiotensin I and then **angiotensin II**, a strong vasoconstrictor that raises glomerular pressure and GFR. Angiotensin II also makes the **adrenal cortex** release **aldosterone**, which causes Na⁺ and water reabsorption in the distal tubule, raising blood pressure and GFR.
- **ANF (atrial natriuretic factor):** when more blood stretches the atria, the heart releases ANF, which widens blood vessels and lowers blood pressure. It checks the renin–angiotensin system.

**Micturition.** As the bladder fills, stretch receptors signal the CNS, which makes the bladder's smooth muscle contract and the urethral sphincter relax: the **micturition reflex**. An adult passes 1 to 1.5 L of light yellow, slightly acidic (about pH 6) urine a day, carrying 25 to 30 g of urea. Glucose (**glycosuria**) or ketone bodies (**ketonuria**) in urine suggest diabetes mellitus.

## 6. Other organs and disorders

**Lungs** remove about 200 mL of CO₂ a minute and much water. The **liver** puts bilirubin, biliverdin, cholesterol, broken-down steroid hormones, vitamins and drugs into bile. **Skin:** sweat glands remove NaCl, a little urea and lactic acid; **sebaceous glands** remove sterols, hydrocarbons and waxes in sebum. Saliva carries small amounts of nitrogenous waste.

- **Uraemia:** urea builds up in blood when kidneys fail. **Haemodialysis:** blood from an artery is mixed with an anticoagulant (heparin) and passed through a cellophane tube bathed in dialysing fluid like plasma but without nitrogenous wastes; wastes diffuse out, then anti-heparin is added and blood returns through a vein.
- **Kidney transplant:** a working kidney, ideally from a close relative to lower the chance of rejection.
- **Renal calculi:** stones of crystallised salts such as oxalates. **Glomerulonephritis:** inflammation of the glomeruli.

<div class="keypoints">

- Ammonotelic (bony fish, aquatic amphibians), ureotelic (mammals, marine fish), uricotelic (birds, reptiles, insects); urea made in the liver.
- Flame cells (flatworms), nephridia (annelids), Malpighian tubules (insects), green glands (prawns).
- Kidney: hilum, pelvis, calyces, cortex, medullary pyramids, columns of Bertini; about a million nephrons each.
- Malpighian body = glomerulus + Bowman's capsule; podocytes and filtration slits; GFR 125 mL/min, 180 L/day; 99% reabsorbed.
- PCT reabsorbs 70 to 80%; descending limb lets water out; ascending limb lets salt out; collecting duct takes back water under ADH.
- Countercurrent: loop and vasa recta; medulla 300 to 1200 mOsmol/L by NaCl and urea; urine up to four times concentrated.
- ADH from neurohypophysis; renin → angiotensin II → aldosterone; ANF lowers BP.
- Urine 1 to 1.5 L/day, pH 6, 25 to 30 g urea; uraemia, dialysis, calculi, glomerulonephritis.
