**Start here · about 11 minutes.** By the end you will know how to use the bank to climb to top-rank level.

<div class="bigidea">At the top of NEET, ranks are decided by accuracy and speed. Climb the levels in order, mix chapters, learn from every mistake and guess only when the numbers say so.</div>

## 1. Climb the five levels

| Level | What it is |
|---|---|
| 1 | Warm-up: one NCERT fact or one-step numerical |
| 2 | Standard: a straightforward NEET question |
| 3 | NEET level: two ideas, or a reworked NCERT line |
| 4 | Hard: the toughest NEET questions, tricky statements |
| 5 | Top-rank: finest NCERT detail, several ideas together |

Move up at about 8 of 10 right; go back to the lesson below 5 of 10.

## 2. When to guess

<div class="meaning">
<dl>
<dt>Expected marks</dt><dd>(chance right) × 4 − (chance wrong) × 1</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Even a blind guess among four is worth +0.25 on average, and each option you cross out makes it better. Guess when you can cross out at least one option; it also adds risk, so do not guess wildly in a paper you are acing.</dd>
</dl>

## 3. Mistakes log and mock tests

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><b>After each set:</b> write each mistake in one line: question, wrong step, cause (concept, reading, recall, calculation, time) and fix.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Every week:</b> read the log. The same few mistakes usually cause most lost marks.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Mock tests:</b> 180 questions in 3 hours, on an OMR-style sheet, at the real exam's time of day.</div>
<div class="step"><b>After each mock:</b> sort questions into right, wrong, skipped-but-solvable and unsolvable. Fix the skipped-but-solvable pile first (time and order), then log every wrong one.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Finishing first is the goal."** In NEET, a calm first pass that answers every sure question, then a second pass for the rest, beats rushing. Leave 10 minutes to check the OMR bubbles.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Climb levels 1 to 5; move up at about 8 of 10, go back to the lesson below 5 of 10.
- Mix chapters once learnt; it trains you to recognise what each question needs.
- In NEET a guess pays on average when you can cross out at least one option.
- Keep a mistakes log with causes; read it weekly.
- Analyse every mock; recover the skipped-but-solvable questions first.

</div>
