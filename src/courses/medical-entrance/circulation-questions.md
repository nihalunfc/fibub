**Biology · Class 11 · about 24 minutes.** Read [Body fluids and circulation](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/circulation/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">This chapter is half numbers (counts, percentages, timings, cardiac output) and half sequences (clotting, conduction, the cycle). NEET mixes them in statements. Keep one numbers card and two sequence chains in your head.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Blood numbers.</b> Plasma 55%, RBC 5 to 5.5 million, WBC 6000 to 8000, platelets 1.5 to 3.5 lakh, Hb 12 to 16 g, RBC life 120 days. <i>Trap:</i> RBCs are destroyed in the spleen and made in red bone marrow.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. White cell jobs.</b> <i>Trap:</i> basophils release histamine, serotonin and heparin; eosinophils deal with allergy; both neutrophils and monocytes are phagocytic.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Transfusion.</b> <i>Method:</i> the donor's antigens must not meet the recipient's antibodies. <i>Trap:</i> O plasma has both antibodies; AB plasma has none.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Rh incompatibility.</b> Which mother, which baby, which pregnancy, the prevention.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Clotting order.</b> Thrombokinase → prothrombin to thrombin → fibrinogen to fibrin; Ca²⁺ needed. <i>Trap:</i> thrombin is the enzyme that acts on fibrinogen.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Heart chambers across animals.</b> 2, 3, 4; crocodiles 4; open circulation in arthropods and molluscs.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Conduction and the cycle.</b> SA node → AV node → bundle of His → Purkinje fibres; valves open and shut in each phase; lub and dub.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. ECG.</b> P, QRS, T meanings; heart rate from R–R intervals. <i>Trap:</i> T is ventricular repolarisation, not atrial.</div>
<div class="step"><b>9. Output numbers.</b> CO = SV × HR; SV = end-diastolic − end-systolic volume; cycle time = 60 / HR.</div>
<div class="step"><b>10. Regulation and disorders.</b> Sympathetic vs parasympathetic; hypertension 140/90; angina, CAD, heart failure vs cardiac arrest vs heart attack.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, numbers).** A heart beats 75 times a minute. How long is one cycle, and what is the cardiac output if stroke volume is 70 mL? *Solution.* Cycle = 60/75 = **0.8 s**. Output = 70 × 75 = 5250 mL = **5.25 L per minute**.

**Example 2 (level 4, transfusion).** A patient of group B needs blood. Which donors are safe? *Solution.* B plasma has anti-A, so donor cells must lack antigen A: **B or O**.

**Example 3 (level 5, assertion-reason).** A: The SA node is called the pacemaker. R: It can generate the maximum number of action potentials per minute. *Solution.* Both true, and R explains A: the fastest-firing tissue sets the rhythm for the rest. **Both true; R explains A.**

**Example 4 (level 5, statements).** Statement I: During joint diastole, the semilunar valves are open. Statement II: Most ventricular filling happens before atrial systole. *Solution.* In joint diastole the semilunar valves are closed (the AV valves are open), so I is false. II is true: atrial systole adds only about 30%. **I incorrect, II correct.**

<div class="myth">

**"A heart attack and cardiac arrest are the same."** A heart attack is damage to heart muscle from a blocked blood supply. Cardiac arrest means the heart stops beating. Heart failure means it pumps too weakly.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-biology" data-topics="circulation" data-mode="practice" data-exam="neet"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Neutrophils most numerous; basophils fewest; RBCs die in the spleen after 120 days.
- AB universal recipient, O universal donor; Rh problem in a later Rh⁺ pregnancy of an Rh⁻ mother.
- Clotting: thrombokinase, thrombin, fibrin; Ca²⁺.
- SA → AV → bundle of His → Purkinje; lub = AV valves close, dub = semilunar valves close.
- P atria, QRS ventricles depolarise, T ventricles repolarise; HR = 60 / R–R interval.
- CO = SV × HR ≈ 5 L/min; sympathetic up, parasympathetic down.
