**Biology · Class 11 · about 34 minutes.** By the end you will be able to place every endocrine gland, name each hormone it makes with its target and effect, explain how peptide and steroid hormones act differently, describe feedback control, and match each disorder to the hormone behind it.

<div class="bigidea">Hormones are chemical letters posted into the blood. Every cell receives the post, but only cells with the right receptor can read a given letter. The hypothalamus and pituitary send most of the letters that tell other glands what to do, and the results come back to them as feedback.</div>

## 1. Hormones and glands

**Endocrine glands** have no ducts; they release **hormones**, non-nutrient chemicals made in tiny amounts that act as messengers between cells. Human endocrine glands are the **pituitary, pineal, thyroid, parathyroid, thymus, adrenal, pancreas and gonads**; the **hypothalamus**, gut, liver, kidney and heart also make hormones. Pick a gland on the map.

## 2. Hypothalamus and pituitary

The **hypothalamus**, at the base of the forebrain, has groups of **neurosecretory cells** (nuclei). They make **releasing hormones** (for example **GnRH**, which makes the pituitary release gonadotrophins) and **inhibiting hormones** (for example **somatostatin**, which stops growth hormone release). These reach the anterior pituitary through a **portal circulation**. The posterior pituitary is controlled directly by nerves from the hypothalamus.

The **pituitary** sits in a bony cavity, the **sella turcica**, on a stalk from the hypothalamus. It has an **adenohypophysis** (pars distalis and pars intermedia) and a **neurohypophysis** (pars nervosa).

| Part | Hormone | Main action |
|---|---|---|
| Pars distalis (anterior) | GH | growth; excess gives gigantism (child) or acromegaly (adult); lack gives pituitary dwarfism |
| | Prolactin | growth of mammary glands, milk formation |
| | TSH | makes the thyroid release its hormones |
| | ACTH | makes the adrenal cortex release glucocorticoids |
| | LH, FSH | gonadotrophins: in males LH → androgens, FSH + androgens → spermatogenesis; in females LH → ovulation and corpus luteum, FSH → follicle growth |
| Pars intermedia | MSH | skin pigmentation (melanocytes); this part is almost merged with the pars distalis in humans |
| Pars nervosa (posterior) | Oxytocin | contracts uterus at birth; ejects milk |
| | Vasopressin (ADH) | water reabsorption in distal tubules; lack gives **diabetes insipidus** (much dilute urine, dehydration) |

<div class="myth">

**"The posterior pituitary makes oxytocin and ADH."** It only stores and releases them. They are made in the hypothalamus and carried down axons to the neurohypophysis.

</div>

## 3. Pineal, thyroid, parathyroid and thymus

- **Pineal** (back of the forebrain): **melatonin**, which sets the **diurnal (24-hour) rhythm** of sleep, waking and body temperature, and affects metabolism, pigmentation, the menstrual cycle and defence.
- **Thyroid:** two lobes on either side of the trachea joined by an **isthmus**; made of **follicles** whose cells make **thyroxine (T₄)** and **triiodothyronine (T₃)**. They regulate the **basal metabolic rate**, help red cell formation, control carbohydrate, protein and fat metabolism and keep water and electrolyte balance. **Iodine** is needed to make them. Iodine lack gives **hypothyroidism** and an enlarged thyroid (**goitre**). Hypothyroidism in pregnancy harms the baby: stunted growth (**cretinism**), intellectual disability, abnormal skin, deaf-mutism; in adult women it can make periods irregular. **Hyperthyroidism** (from nodules or cancer) speeds the body up; **exophthalmic goitre (Graves' disease)** adds an enlarged thyroid, bulging eyes, high BMR and weight loss. The thyroid also makes **thyrocalcitonin (TCT)**, which lowers blood calcium.
- **Parathyroid:** four small glands on the back of the thyroid. **PTH** raises blood Ca²⁺: it dissolves bone (resorption), increases Ca²⁺ reabsorption in kidney tubules and Ca²⁺ absorption from food. It is **hypercalcaemic**; TCT works against it.
- **Thymus:** behind the sternum, in front of the aorta. **Thymosins** help **T lymphocytes** mature (cell-mediated immunity) and help antibody production (humoral immunity). The thymus shrinks with age, so old people have weaker immunity.

## 4. Adrenal, pancreas and gonads

<div class="steps" data-ms="8500">
<div class="step"><b>Adrenal medulla.</b> <b>Adrenaline</b> (epinephrine) and <b>noradrenaline</b> (norepinephrine), the <b>catecholamines</b>: emergency or <b>fight-or-flight</b> hormones. They raise alertness, dilate pupils, raise hairs (piloerection), cause sweating, speed up heart rate, heart force and breathing, and break down glycogen, fats and proteins.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Adrenal cortex.</b> Three layers: zona glomerulosa (outer), zona fasciculata (middle), zona reticularis (inner). <b>Glucocorticoids</b> (mainly <b>cortisol</b>): gluconeogenesis, lipolysis, proteolysis; keep the heart, vessels and kidneys working; anti-inflammatory; suppress immunity; stimulate red cell production. <b>Mineralocorticoids</b> (mainly <b>aldosterone</b>): Na⁺ and water reabsorption and K⁺ and phosphate excretion in the kidney tubules, keeping fluid volume and blood pressure. Small amounts of <b>androgens</b> help body hair grow at puberty.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Pancreas.</b> Both exocrine and endocrine. The <b>islets of Langerhans</b> (1 to 2 million, 1 to 2% of the tissue): <b>α cells</b> make <b>glucagon</b> (raises blood glucose: glycogen breakdown and gluconeogenesis in liver cells; hyperglycaemic); <b>β cells</b> make <b>insulin</b> (lowers blood glucose: uptake into liver and fat cells, glycogen formation; hypoglycaemic). Long-lasting high blood glucose is <b>diabetes mellitus</b>: glucose and harmful <b>ketone bodies</b> appear in urine; insulin therapy treats it.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Testis.</b> <b>Leydig (interstitial) cells</b> between the seminiferous tubules make <b>androgens</b>, mainly <b>testosterone</b>: development of the accessory sex organs, muscle growth, facial and armpit hair, deeper voice, aggressiveness, spermatogenesis, sexual drive (libido), and anabolic effects on protein and carbohydrate metabolism.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Ovary.</b> Growing follicles make <b>oestrogen</b>: growth of female sex organs, follicle development, female secondary sex characters (such as a high-pitched voice), mammary growth, sexual behaviour. After ovulation the follicle becomes the <b>corpus luteum</b>, which makes mainly <b>progesterone</b>: supports pregnancy and forms milk-storing alveoli in the breasts.</div>
</div>

**Other organs.** The heart's atrial wall makes **ANF**, which widens blood vessels and lowers blood pressure. Kidney **JG cells** make **erythropoietin**, which drives red cell formation. The gut makes **gastrin** (HCl and pepsinogen from gastric glands), **secretin** (water and bicarbonate from the pancreas), **cholecystokinin** (pancreatic enzymes and bile) and **GIP** (slows stomach secretion and movement). Many tissues also make **growth factors** for growth and repair.

Switch the pancreas between healthy, type 1 and type 2, and change the meal size.

## 5. How hormones act

A hormone acts only on cells with its **receptor**; hormone + receptor form a **hormone–receptor complex** that changes the cell's chemistry.

| Kind | Examples | Receptor | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peptide, polypeptide, protein | insulin, glucagon, pituitary and hypothalamic hormones | on the cell membrane | stays outside; makes **second messengers** (cyclic AMP, IP₃, Ca²⁺) inside |
| Steroid | cortisol, testosterone, oestradiol, progesterone | inside the cell (mostly nuclear) | enters; complex acts on genes (gene expression) |
| Iodothyronine | thyroid hormones | inside the cell | acts on genes |
| Amino-acid derivative | adrenaline | on the membrane | second messengers |

**Feedback.** Most glands are kept steady by **negative feedback**: the hormone's effect switches off its own release. A worked example: when thyroxine is low, the hypothalamus releases more TRH, the pituitary more TSH, and the thyroid more thyroxine; when thyroxine rises, it damps the hypothalamus and pituitary. With iodine lacking, thyroxine stays low, TSH stays high, and the constant push enlarges the thyroid: that is why iodine lack causes **goitre**. Calcium (PTH vs TCT) and glucose (insulin vs glucagon) are balanced by pairs of opposing hormones in the same way.

<div class="keypoints">

- Endocrine glands are ductless; hormones are non-nutrient messengers made in trace amounts.
- Hypothalamus: releasing (GnRH) and inhibiting (somatostatin) hormones via a portal system; makes oxytocin and ADH.
- Anterior pituitary: GH, PRL, TSH, ACTH, LH, FSH; pars intermedia MSH; posterior stores oxytocin and ADH.
- GH: gigantism, acromegaly, dwarfism; ADH lack: diabetes insipidus.
- Melatonin: diurnal rhythm. T₃, T₄: BMR; iodine lack: goitre; cretinism; Graves' disease. TCT lowers, PTH raises blood Ca²⁺.
- Thymosins: T cell maturation; thymus shrinks with age.
- Adrenal medulla: adrenaline, noradrenaline (fight or flight); cortex: cortisol (glucose, anti-inflammatory), aldosterone (Na⁺ and water kept).
- α cells glucagon (raises glucose), β cells insulin (lowers); diabetes mellitus with ketone bodies.
- Leydig cells: testosterone; follicles: oestrogen; corpus luteum: progesterone.
- ANF, erythropoietin, gastrin, secretin, CCK, GIP. Peptides: membrane receptors and second messengers; steroids and thyroid hormones: intracellular receptors and genes.

</div>
