**Biology · Class 11 · about 32 minutes.** By the end you will be able to compare the classification systems, tell the three algal classes apart by pigment, food and wall, trace the life cycle of a moss, a fern and a pine, and name the pattern of any plant life cycle.

<div class="bigidea">The story of the plant kingdom is a move from water to land. Each new group solves one more land problem: bryophytes still need water to fertilise, pteridophytes add pipes (xylem and phloem), gymnosperms add seeds, and angiosperms wrap those seeds in fruit.</div>

## 1. Ways to classify

| System | Based on | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Artificial | a few easily seen characters, mostly vegetative or the androecium | Linnaeus |
| Natural | many characters together: external features plus anatomy, embryology and plant chemistry | Bentham and Hooker (for flowering plants) |
| Phylogenetic | evolutionary relationships; assumes members of a taxon share an ancestor | used today |

Newer helpers: **numerical taxonomy** (all characters given equal weight, coded as numbers and handled by computers), **cytotaxonomy** (chromosome number, structure and behaviour) and **chemotaxonomy** (chemical contents of the plant).

<div class="myth">

**"Artificial systems used too many characters."** The opposite: they used a few, gave vegetative and sexual characters equal weight, and so put unrelated plants together.

</div>

## 2. Algae

Simple, chlorophyll-bearing, mostly aquatic, with a thallus (no true root, stem or leaf). They reproduce vegetatively (fragmentation), asexually (spores, often flagellated **zoospores**) and sexually: **isogamous** (gametes alike: *Ulothrix*, flagellated; *Spirogyra*, not flagellated), **anisogamous** (gametes of different size: some *Chlamydomonas*) or **oogamous** (large non-motile egg and small motile sperm: *Volvox*, *Fucus*).

| | Chlorophyceae (green) | Phaeophyceae (brown) | Rhodophyceae (red) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pigments | chlorophyll a, b | chlorophyll a, c, fucoxanthin | chlorophyll a, d, r-phycoerythrin |
| Stored food | starch (in pyrenoids) | laminarin, mannitol | floridean starch |
| Wall | cellulose (inner), pectose (outer) | cellulose with algin | cellulose, pectin, polysulphate esters |
| Flagella | 2 to 8, equal, apical | 2, unequal, lateral | none |
| Examples | *Chlamydomonas*, *Volvox*, *Ulothrix*, *Spirogyra*, *Chara* | *Ectocarpus*, *Dictyota*, *Laminaria*, *Sargassum*, *Fucus* | *Polysiphonia*, *Porphyra*, *Gracilaria*, *Gelidium* |

Brown algae can be huge kelps with a **holdfast** (anchor), a **stipe** (stalk) and a leaf-like **frond**. Red algae live deep in the sea because phycoerythrin absorbs the blue-green light that reaches there.

**Uses.** Algae do about half of all carbon fixation on Earth. **Agar** comes from *Gelidium* and *Gracilaria* (used for growing microbes, ice cream and jellies). **Algin** comes from brown algae and **carrageen** from red algae. *Chlorella*, rich in protein, is a food supplement even for space travellers; *Porphyra*, *Laminaria* and *Sargassum* are eaten.

## 3. Life cycle patterns

Every plant alternates between a haploid (n) **gametophyte** that makes gametes and a diploid (2n) **sporophyte** that makes spores by meiosis. Groups differ in which phase is the main plant.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="plant-life-cycles"><figcaption>One coloured arc is the 2n phase and the other the n phase. Fertilisation starts the 2n phase and meiosis ends it. As plants move onto land, the 2n phase takes over.</figcaption></figure>

- **Haplontic:** the plant is n; only the zygote is 2n (most algae: *Volvox*, *Spirogyra*, some *Chlamydomonas*).
- **Diplontic:** the plant is 2n; the gametophyte is tiny (gymnosperms, angiosperms, and the alga *Fucus*).
- **Haplo-diplontic:** both phases are multicellular. Bryophytes have a dominant n gametophyte; pteridophytes a dominant 2n sporophyte. Among algae, *Ectocarpus*, *Polysiphonia* and kelps are haplo-diplontic.

## 4. Bryophytes: amphibians of the plant kingdom

They live on land but need water for the sperm to swim. The plant (gametophyte) is a thallus or has leaf-like parts, fixed by **rhizoids**, with no true roots or vascular tissue. The **antheridium** makes biflagellate antherozoids; the flask-shaped **archegonium** holds one egg. The zygote grows into a sporophyte of **foot, seta and capsule**, attached to the gametophyte. Meiosis in the capsule makes spores.

- **Liverworts** (*Marchantia*): flat thallus; asexual reproduction by **gemmae**, green buds in gemma cups that grow into new plants.
- **Mosses** (*Funaria*, *Polytrichum*, *Sphagnum*): the spore grows into a creeping green **protonema**, which gives rise to the upright **leafy stage** that bears the sex organs. Spore dispersal is elaborate.

Bryophytes with lichens are among the first plants on bare rock, they help make soil, and moss mats reduce soil erosion. *Sphagnum* forms **peat**, used as fuel and as packing because it holds water.

## 5. Pteridophytes: the first vascular plants

The main plant is the **sporophyte**, with true roots, stem and leaves and with **xylem and phloem**. Leaves are small **microphylls** (*Selaginella*) or large **macrophylls** (ferns). Spore-bearing leaves (**sporophylls**) may group into cones (**strobili**: *Selaginella*, *Equisetum*). A spore grows into a small, free-living, green gametophyte, the **prothallus**, which needs cool, damp places and water for fertilisation; this keeps pteridophytes in narrow habitats.

Most are **homosporous** (one kind of spore). *Selaginella* and *Salvinia* are **heterosporous**: megaspores grow into female and microspores into male gametophytes. Keeping the female gametophyte on the parent plant is the first step towards the **seed habit**.

Four classes: Psilopsida (*Psilotum*), Lycopsida (*Selaginella*, *Lycopodium*), Sphenopsida (*Equisetum*), Pteropsida (*Dryopteris*, *Pteris*, *Adiantum*).

## 6. Gymnosperms and angiosperms

**Gymnosperms** (*gymnos*, naked) have ovules not enclosed in an ovary, so the seeds are naked. Roots may be partners: *Pinus* has **mycorrhiza** (fungus), *Cycas* has **coralloid roots** with nitrogen-fixing cyanobacteria. *Cycas* has an unbranched stem and pinnate leaves; *Pinus* and *Cedrus* are branched. Needle leaves with a thick cuticle and **sunken stomata** cut water loss. All are **heterosporous**: microsporophylls form male cones and megasporophylls carry ovules in female cones. Pollen reaches the ovule by air, and a pollen tube carries the male gametes to an archegonium. The giant redwood *Sequoia* is one of the tallest trees.

**Angiosperms** enclose their seeds in a fruit. They range from tiny *Wolffia* to tall *Eucalyptus*. Inside the ovule, the embryo sac has 7 cells and 8 nuclei. **Double fertilisation**: one male gamete fuses with the egg (zygote, 2n) and the other with the two polar nuclei (primary endosperm nucleus, 3n).

<div class="keypoints">

- Artificial (Linnaeus, few characters), natural (Bentham and Hooker, many), phylogenetic (evolution); plus numerical, cyto- and chemotaxonomy.
- Algae: green (chl a, b; starch), brown (chl a, c, fucoxanthin; laminarin, mannitol; algin), red (chl a, d, phycoerythrin; floridean starch; no flagella).
- Agar from Gelidium and Gracilaria; Chlorella is a protein supplement.
- Haplontic (Volvox, Spirogyra), diplontic (seed plants, Fucus), haplo-diplontic (bryophytes, pteridophytes, Ectocarpus, Polysiphonia, kelps).
- Bryophytes: dominant gametophyte, rhizoids, water for fertilisation, sporophyte of foot, seta and capsule; gemmae in Marchantia; protonema in mosses; Sphagnum gives peat.
- Pteridophytes: first vascular plants, dominant sporophyte, prothallus; Selaginella and Salvinia heterosporous.
- Gymnosperms: naked seeds, coralloid roots in Cycas, mycorrhiza in Pinus, sunken stomata; angiosperms: seeds in fruit, double fertilisation.

</div>
