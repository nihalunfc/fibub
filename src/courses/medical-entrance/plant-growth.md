**Biology · Class 11 · about 34 minutes.** By the end you will be able to measure growth and tell absolute from relative growth rate, sketch arithmetic, geometric and sigmoid curves, and match each plant growth regulator to its discovery, its effects and its farm uses.

<div class="bigidea">Plants keep growing all their life because meristems never stop dividing. A handful of small molecules, the plant growth regulators, decide where that growth goes: up or sideways, roots or shoots, ripen now or wait.</div>

## 1. Growth and how to measure it

**Growth** is an irreversible, permanent increase in size of an organ, its parts or a cell, usually powered by metabolism. Plant growth is **open** (indeterminate) because meristems keep adding cells. Root and shoot apical meristems give **primary growth** (length); lateral meristems give **secondary growth** (girth).

Measure whatever changes most: cell number (a single maize root apex can make more than 17,500 new cells an hour), cell size (a watermelon cell can grow up to 350,000 times), length (a pollen tube), area (a leaf), or fresh or dry weight.

**Three phases** along a root tip:
1. **Meristematic** (at the tip): cells rich in protoplasm, large nuclei, thin primary cellulose walls, many plasmodesmata.
2. **Elongation** (just behind): cells enlarge, take in water into vacuoles and lay down new wall.
3. **Maturation** (further back): cells reach their full size, thicken their walls and change their protoplasm for a job.

## 2. Growth rates

**Absolute growth rate** = increase per unit time. **Relative growth rate** = increase per unit time per unit of starting size, a measure of efficiency.

- **Arithmetic growth:** after each division, only one daughter cell keeps dividing and the other matures. Length rises by a fixed amount each day: **L<sub>t</sub> = L<sub>0</sub> + rt**, a straight line (a root growing at a steady rate).
- **Geometric growth:** both daughter cells keep dividing. Size multiplies: **W<sub>1</sub> = W<sub>0</sub>e<sup>rt</sup>**, where W<sub>0</sub> is the starting size, W<sub>1</sub> the final size, r the relative growth rate (efficiency index), t the time and e the base of natural logarithms.
- **Sigmoid curve:** in nature, a slow **lag phase**, then a fast **log (exponential) phase**, then a **stationary phase** as nutrients run short. A whole plant, an organ or a cell population all show this S-shape.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="growth-curves"><figcaption>Arithmetic growth is a straight line; geometric growth curves up ever faster; with a food limit it becomes sigmoid. Raise r to shorten the doubling time.</figcaption></figure>

**Worked example.** Leaves P and Q both gain 5 cm² in a week; P started at 5 cm² and Q at 50 cm². Absolute rates are equal (5 cm² a week). Relative rates are 5/5 = 1 and 5/50 = 0.1 per week, so P is growing ten times more efficiently.

**Conditions for growth:** water (cells enlarge by taking it in, and it carries enzymes' reactions), oxygen (for the energy), nutrients, a suitable temperature, and environmental signals such as light and gravity.

## 3. Differentiation and development

Cells from meristems **differentiate** to do a job (a tracheid loses its protoplasm and builds a strong lignified wall). Some living, differentiated cells can **dedifferentiate** and divide again, as when parenchyma forms the interfascicular cambium or cork cambium; their daughters then **redifferentiate**. **Development** is the sum of growth and differentiation, from germination to senescence.

**Plasticity:** the same plant can form different structures in different conditions or stages. **Heterophylly** shows it: young and mature leaves differ in cotton, coriander and larkspur (with age), and in buttercup leaves differ in air and under water (with habitat).

## 4. Plant growth regulators

PGRs are small molecules of varied chemistry: indole compounds (IAA), adenine derivatives (kinetin), carotenoid derivatives (ABA), terpenes (gibberellic acid) and gases (ethylene). Broadly, auxins, gibberellins and cytokinins **promote** growth; ABA **inhibits** it; ethylene does a bit of both, mostly as an inhibitor.

<div class="steps" data-ms="9000">
<div class="step"><b>Auxins</b> (IAA, IBA natural; NAA, 2,4-D synthetic). <i>Discovery:</i> Charles and Francis Darwin saw canary grass coleoptiles bend towards light; F. W. Went isolated auxin from oat coleoptile tips. <i>Effects and uses:</i> rooting of stem cuttings; flowering in pineapple; stop early fruit and leaf drop but promote shedding of older leaves and fruits; <b>apical dominance</b> (the shoot tip stops side buds; cutting it off, as in tea plantations and hedges, makes plants bushy); parthenocarpic fruit (tomato); 2,4-D kills dicot weeds in cereal fields; xylem differentiation; cell division.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Gibberellins</b> (more than 100: GA₁, GA₂, GA₃ ...; GA₃ found first). <i>Discovery:</i> E. Kurosawa, bakanae disease of rice caused by <i>Gibberella fujikuroi</i>. <i>Effects and uses:</i> lengthen the axis (longer grape stalks); elongate and improve the shape of apples; delay senescence so fruit can be picked later; speed malting in brewing; longer sugarcane stems (yield up by as much as 20 tonnes per acre); early maturity and seed production in young conifers; <b>bolting</b> (sudden stem elongation before flowering) in beet, cabbage and other rosette plants.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Cytokinins</b> (kinetin, zeatin). <i>Discovery:</i> Skoog and Miller found <b>kinetin</b>, a modified adenine, in autoclaved herring sperm DNA; <b>zeatin</b> was later found in corn kernels and coconut milk. <i>Effects:</i> cell division (cytokinesis); new leaves and chloroplasts; lateral shoot growth and adventitious shoots; overcome apical dominance; move nutrients; <b>delay leaf senescence</b>. They are made where cell division is fast: root tips, shoot buds, young fruits.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Ethylene</b> (a gas). <i>Discovery:</i> Cousins found a gas from ripe oranges that hastened ripening of stored bananas. <i>Effects:</i> horizontal growth, swelling of the axis and apical hook in dicot seedlings; senescence and shedding of leaves and flowers; <b>fruit ripening</b> (raises respiration: the respiratory climacteric); breaks seed and bud dormancy (peanut seeds, potato tubers); fast internode elongation in deep-water rice; root hairs; flowering and synchronised fruit set in pineapple, flowering in mango. <i>Ethephon</i>, which releases ethylene, ripens tomatoes and apples, thins cotton, cherry and walnut, and promotes female flowers in cucumber.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Abscisic acid</b> (ABA). <i>Discovery:</i> three inhibitors (inhibitor-B, abscission II, dormin) turned out to be the same chemical. <i>Effects:</i> general growth inhibitor; inhibits seed germination; <b>closes stomata</b>; raises tolerance to stress (the <b>stress hormone</b>); seed development, maturation and <b>dormancy</b>, which helps seeds survive drought. ABA acts against gibberellins.</div>
</div>

**Tissue culture tip:** Skoog and Miller also showed that the ratio of cytokinin to auxin decides what a callus forms: more cytokinin gives shoots, more auxin gives roots.

<div class="myth">

**"Each process is controlled by one hormone."** Most are joint: apical dominance is auxin against cytokinin, dormancy is ABA against gibberellin, and ripening and shedding involve ethylene with others.

<div class="keypoints">

- Growth is irreversible; plant growth is open because of meristems; measure cells, size, length, area or weight.
- Phases: meristematic, elongation, maturation.
- Arithmetic L = L₀ + rt (one daughter divides); geometric W₁ = W₀e^(rt) (both divide); sigmoid has lag, log and stationary phases.
- Absolute rate = gain per time; relative rate = gain per time per starting size.
- Dedifferentiation gives interfascicular and cork cambium; heterophylly in cotton, coriander, larkspur, buttercup.
- Auxins: Darwins and Went; rooting, apical dominance, parthenocarpy, 2,4-D weedkiller.
- Gibberellins: Kurosawa, bakanae; stem length, malting, sugarcane, bolting.
- Cytokinins: kinetin from herring sperm DNA, zeatin from corn and coconut milk; cell division, delay senescence.
- Ethylene: ripening, abscission, female flowers in cucumber (ethephon); ABA: stomatal closure, dormancy, stress.

</div>
