**Biology · Class 12 · about 22 minutes.** Read [Organisms and populations](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/organisms-populations/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">Half the questions here are short calculations (per capita rates, dN/dt), and half are sorting (which interaction, which response, which pyramid). Write the sign pair (+, −, 0) for every interaction and the formula for every rate, and both halves become quick.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Per capita rates.</b> <i>Method:</i> events ÷ individuals. <i>Trap:</i> giving the raw count (8 births) instead of the rate (0.4 per individual).</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Net change.</b> N<sub>t+1</sub> = N<sub>t</sub> + (B + I) − (D + E). <i>Trap:</i> forgetting emigration or immigration.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Growth equations.</b> Exponential dN/dt = rN, N<sub>t</sub> = N<sub>0</sub>e<sup>rt</sup>; logistic dN/dt = rN(K − N)/K; maximum logistic rate at N = K/2. <i>Trap:</i> at N = K the growth rate is zero, not maximum.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Curves.</b> J-shaped = exponential (no limits); S-shaped = logistic with an asymptote at K.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Age pyramids.</b> Triangular (expanding), bell (stable), urn (declining).</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Responses to stress.</b> Regulate, conform, migrate, suspend; hibernation (winter), aestivation (summer), diapause (zooplankton). <i>Trap:</i> most animals are conformers, not regulators.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Adaptations.</b> Kangaroo rat, CAM, Allen's rule, blubber, high-altitude changes (more RBCs, lower Hb affinity, faster breathing).</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Interactions with examples.</b> <i>Method:</i> write the sign pair, then match. Lichens and mycorrhizae (+ +), cuckoo and liver fluke (+ −), orchid on mango and egret with cattle (+ 0). <i>Trap:</i> the orchid on a mango tree is an epiphyte, not a parasite.</div>
<div class="step"><b>9. Classic cases.</b> Prickly pear and moth, <i>Pisaster</i>, Monarch butterfly, Gause, Connell's barnacles, MacArthur's warblers, Abingdon tortoise, <i>Ophrys</i>.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, net change).** A deer population of 500 has 80 births, 30 deaths, 15 immigrants and 25 emigrants in a year. What is N next year? *Solution.* 500 + (80 + 15) − (30 + 25) = 500 + 95 − 55 = **540**.

**Example 2 (level 5, logistic).** For r = 0.4 per year and K = 1000, compare dN/dt at N = 500 and N = 900. *Solution.* At 500: 0.4 × 500 × 500/1000 = 100 per year. At 900: 0.4 × 900 × 100/1000 = 36 per year. Growth slows as N nears K.

**Example 3 (level 4, assertion-reason).** A: Very small mammals are rarely found in polar regions. R: Small animals have a large surface area relative to volume and lose body heat quickly. *Solution.* Both true; R explains A. **Option 1.**

**Example 4 (level 5, trap).** Which pair is wrongly matched? (a) Cuscuta – ectoparasite; (b) Cuckoo – brood parasitism; (c) Ophrys – sexual deceit; (d) Balanus and Chthamalus – mutualism. *Solution.* **(d).** Connell's barnacles are a classic case of competition.

<div class="myth">

**"Predators harm the prey population as a whole."** They keep prey numbers in check and reduce competition among prey species, which helps keep the community diverse.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="keypoints">

- Per capita rate = events ÷ individuals.
- N<sub>t+1</sub> = N<sub>t</sub> + (B + I) − (D + E).
- Logistic growth is fastest at K/2 and zero at K.
- Triangular, bell and urn pyramids: expanding, stable, declining.
- Sign pairs: + + mutualism, − − competition, + − predation and parasitism, + 0 commensalism, − 0 amensalism.
