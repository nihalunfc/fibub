**Biology · Class 12 · about 34 minutes.** By the end you will be able to match each disease to its pathogen, spread and symptoms, trace the malaria life cycle, explain every kind of immunity and the antibody, describe how HIV and cancer damage the body, and name the effects of the commonly abused drugs.

<div class="bigidea">Health is a balance between the body and everything that attacks it: germs, faulty cells and harmful habits. The immune system is the body's defence, with a fast general layer (innate) and a slower, specific layer that remembers (acquired). Vaccines, hygiene and good choices help it win.</div>

## 1. Common diseases

| Disease | Pathogen | Spread | Key signs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typhoid | *Salmonella typhi* (bacterium) | contaminated food and water | sustained high fever (39 to 40 °C), weakness, stomach pain, loss of appetite; intestinal perforation in severe cases. Confirmed by the **Widal test**. "Typhoid Mary" was a cook who carried it for years. |
| Pneumonia | *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae* | droplets, sharing utensils | alveoli fill with fluid; fever, chills, cough; lips and nails may turn grey to bluish |
| Common cold | rhinoviruses | droplets, contaminated objects | infects nose and airways, not lungs; lasts 3 to 7 days |
| Malaria | *Plasmodium vivax, P. malariae, P. falciparum* (protozoa); *falciparum* malaria is the most serious and can kill | bite of female *Anopheles* | chills and high fever recurring every 3 to 4 days |
| Amoebiasis (amoebic dysentery) | *Entamoeba histolytica* (protozoan, large intestine) | houseflies carry cysts to food and water | constipation, cramps, stools with mucus and blood clots |
| Ascariasis | *Ascaris* (roundworm, intestine) | eggs in soil, water, raw vegetables | internal bleeding, muscle pain, fever, anaemia, blocked intestine |
| Filariasis (elephantiasis) | *Wuchereria bancrofti, W. malayi* | bite of female mosquito | lymph vessels of the lower limbs inflamed for years, gross swelling; genitals may also be deformed |
| Ringworm | *Microsporum, Trichophyton, Epidermophyton* (fungi) | soil, towels, combs; heat and moisture help | dry scaly patches on skin, nails, scalp; intense itching; skin folds such as the groin |

**Malaria life cycle.** Sporozoites (infective stage) enter with the mosquito's bite → multiply asexually in **liver cells** → enter red blood cells and multiply there; the RBCs burst and release a toxin, **haemozoin**, which causes the chills and fever every 3 to 4 days → some parasites form **gametocytes** in the blood → a female *Anopheles* takes them up; **fertilisation and development happen in the mosquito's gut**, and sporozoites move to its salivary glands. So the mosquito is the **definitive host** (sexual stage) and the human the **intermediate host**.

**Prevention**: clean water, safe food, waste disposal, disinfecting reservoirs, no stagnant water (mosquito breeding), mosquito nets, larva-eating fish such as *Gambusia* in ponds, insecticide spraying, and vaccines. Ronald Ross showed that mosquitoes carry malaria; William Harvey described blood circulation and showed that normal body temperature is constant; Edward Jenner made the first vaccine (smallpox), and smallpox has since been eradicated.

## 2. Immunity

**Innate immunity** (present from birth, non-specific) has four barriers:

1. **Physical**: skin, mucus lining the airways, gut and urogenital tract.
2. **Physiological**: stomach acid, saliva, tears.
3. **Cellular**: leucocytes (neutrophils, monocytes, natural killer lymphocytes) and macrophages that engulf germs.
4. **Cytokine**: virus-infected cells release **interferons**, which protect nearby cells.

**Acquired immunity** is specific and has **memory**. The first encounter gives a weak **primary response**; a later one gives a strong **secondary (anamnestic) response**.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="immune-response"><figcaption>Antibody level after a first and second exposure to the same antigen (schematic). Memory cells make the second response faster and larger, which is why vaccines work.</figcaption></figure>

- **B lymphocytes** make **antibodies** in the blood: **humoral immunity**. An antibody has **four chains, two heavy and two light (H₂L₂)**. Types: IgA, IgM, IgE, IgG.
- **T lymphocytes** do not make antibodies but help B cells and kill infected or foreign cells: **cell-mediated immunity (CMI)**. CMI causes **graft rejection**, which is why tissue matching and immunosuppressants are needed for transplants.

| Active immunity | Passive immunity |
|---|---|
| the body makes its own antibodies after infection or vaccination | ready-made antibodies are given |
| slow to start, long-lasting | quick, short-lived |
| e.g. vaccines | colostrum (IgA), IgG crossing the placenta to the fetus, antitoxins (e.g. for tetanus or snake bite) |

**Vaccination** gives inactivated or weakened germs or their proteins, so the body forms memory cells. Recombinant DNA lets us make antigens in bacteria or yeast, for example the **hepatitis B vaccine made in yeast**.

**Allergy** is an exaggerated response to harmless things (pollen, mites, dust). It involves **IgE** antibodies, and **mast cells** release **histamine and serotonin**; symptoms include sneezing, watery eyes and difficulty breathing. Treated with antihistamines, adrenaline and steroids. **Autoimmunity**: the immune system attacks the body's own cells, as in **rheumatoid arthritis**.

**Lymphoid organs.** Primary (where lymphocytes form and mature): **bone marrow** (all blood cells form here) and **thymus** (T cells mature; large at birth, shrinks with age). Secondary (where lymphocytes meet antigens): **spleen** (a large bean-shaped organ that filters blood and stores RBCs), **lymph nodes**, tonsils, **Peyer's patches** of the small intestine, appendix. **MALT** (mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue) lines the airways, gut and urogenital tract and makes up about 50% of the body's lymphoid tissue.

**"Vaccines give passive immunity because the person does nothing."** The body makes its own antibodies and memory cells after a vaccine, so it is active immunity. Passive means ready-made antibodies.

## 3. AIDS

AIDS (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome) was first reported in 1981. It is caused by **HIV**, a **retrovirus** with RNA. It spreads by sexual contact with an infected person, transfusion of contaminated blood, sharing needles, and from mother to child through the placenta. It does **not** spread by touch. High-risk groups include people with multiple partners, people who inject drugs, and people who need repeated transfusions.

Inside the body: HIV enters **macrophages**, where **reverse transcriptase** copies its RNA into DNA, which joins the host DNA; the macrophage keeps making virus (an "HIV factory"). The virus then enters **helper T cells**, multiplies and kills them, so their number falls. Years may pass between infection and AIDS. The person gets infections such as *Mycobacterium*, *Toxoplasma* and fungi. Diagnosis: **ELISA**. Antiretroviral drugs prolong life but do not cure. India's **NACO** (National AIDS Control Organisation) and NGOs run awareness programmes.

## 4. Cancer

Normal cells stop dividing when they touch each other (**contact inhibition**). Cancer cells have lost it and form tumours. **Benign** tumours stay put and do little harm; **malignant** tumours invade nearby tissue and spread through the blood to form new tumours elsewhere (**metastasis**), the most feared property.

Causes (**carcinogens**): **ionising radiation** (X-rays, gamma rays), **non-ionising** UV, chemicals such as those in **tobacco smoke** (a major cause of lung cancer), and **oncogenic viruses**. Normal cells carry **proto-oncogenes (c-onc)** that can turn into cancer genes when activated.

Detection: **biopsy and histopathology**, blood and bone marrow tests (leukaemia), radiography, **CT** and **MRI** (strong magnetic fields and non-ionising radiation, for soft tissue), antibodies against cancer antigens, and molecular tests for inherited risk genes. Treatment: surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy (with side effects such as hair loss and anaemia), and immunotherapy with **α-interferon**, which activates the immune system.

## 5. Drugs and alcohol abuse

| Group | Source | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Opioids** | heroin ("smack", diacetylmorphine) from morphine, from the latex of the poppy *Papaver somniferum* | bind opioid receptors in the brain and gut; heroin is a depressant that slows body functions |
| **Cannabinoids** | *Cannabis sativa*: marijuana, hashish, charas, ganja | act on cannabinoid receptors; affect the heart and blood vessels |
| **Coca alkaloid** | cocaine from *Erythroxylum coca* | blocks dopamine transport; stimulates, then hallucinations |
| **Hallucinogens** | *Atropa belladonna*, *Datura* | hallucinations |

Barbiturates, amphetamines, benzodiazepines and LSD are useful medicines for depression or insomnia but are often abused. **Tobacco**: **nicotine** makes the adrenal glands release adrenaline and noradrenaline, raising blood pressure and heart rate; smoking causes lung, throat and bladder cancer, bronchitis and emphysema, and raises **carbon monoxide** in blood, lowering oxygen supply. Chewing tobacco causes oral cancer.

**Adolescence** (12 to 18 years) brings curiosity, peer pressure and stress that can lead to drug or alcohol use. Effects include reckless behaviour, falling school performance and, with injected drugs, HIV and hepatitis B. Chronic alcohol use damages the liver (cirrhosis) and nerves; in pregnancy, drugs and alcohol harm the fetus. Prevention: avoid undue peer pressure, education and counselling, seek help from parents and peers, watch for danger signs, and get professional help.

<div class="keypoints">

- Typhoid (*Salmonella typhi*, Widal), pneumonia (*S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae*), cold (rhinoviruses), malaria (*Plasmodium*, female *Anopheles*), amoebiasis (*Entamoeba*, houseflies), ascariasis, filariasis (*Wuchereria*), ringworm (three fungi).
- Malaria: sporozoites → liver → RBCs (haemozoin) → gametocytes → mosquito gut; mosquito is the definitive host.
- Innate barriers: physical, physiological, cellular, cytokine (interferons). Acquired: B cells (antibodies, H₂L₂), T cells (CMI, graft rejection).
- Active (vaccines) vs passive (colostrum IgA, placental IgG); allergy uses IgE and histamine; rheumatoid arthritis is autoimmune.
- Primary lymphoid organs: bone marrow, thymus; MALT is about 50% of lymphoid tissue.
- HIV: retrovirus, macrophages then helper T cells; ELISA.
- Cancer: lost contact inhibition, metastasis; α-interferon in immunotherapy.
- Opioids (poppy), cannabinoids (*Cannabis*), cocaine (*Erythroxylum*, dopamine); nicotine raises adrenaline.
