**Biology · Class 12 · about 20 minutes.** Read [Biodiversity and conservation](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/biodiversity/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">This chapter is mostly numbers and examples. NEET checks whether you can attach the right number to the right fact (2.4% vs 8.1%, 0.1 to 0.2 vs 0.6 to 1.2) and the right example to the right cause. One species-area sum may also appear.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Levels with examples.</b> Genetic (<i>Rauwolfia vomitoria</i>, rice strains, mango varieties), species (Western Ghats amphibians), ecological (India vs Norway). <i>Trap:</i> reserpine variation is genetic, not species, diversity.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Global and Indian numbers.</b> 1.5 million described, 7 million estimated; animals over 70%, insects 70% of animals, plants at most 22%; India 2.4% land, 8.1% species, 12 mega diversity countries.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Latitudinal gradient.</b> Colombia 1400, New York 105, Greenland 56, India 1200+ bird species; three reasons for tropical richness.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Species-area relationship.</b> <i>Method:</i> log S = log C + Z log A; S₂/S₁ = (A₂/A₁)<sup>Z</sup>. <i>Trap:</i> Z for continents (0.6 to 1.2) vs regions (0.1 to 0.2).</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Importance.</b> Tilman (less variation, more productivity), Ehrlich (rivet popper; wing rivets = key species).</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Extinctions.</b> 784 in 500 years (338 vertebrates, 359 invertebrates, 87 plants); dodo, quagga, thylacine, Steller's sea cow, three tiger subspecies; 32% amphibians threatened.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Evil quartet with examples.</b> Habitat loss (rainforests 14% → 6%, Amazon), over-exploitation (Steller's sea cow, passenger pigeon), aliens (Nile perch, <i>Parthenium</i>, <i>Lantana</i>, <i>Eichhornia</i>, African catfish), co-extinction (host and parasite).</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. In situ or ex situ.</b> In situ: hotspots, biosphere reserves, national parks, sanctuaries, sacred groves. Ex situ: zoos, botanical gardens, safari parks, cryopreservation, seed banks, tissue culture.</div>
<div class="step"><b>9. Agreements.</b> Rio Earth Summit 1992; Johannesburg 2002 (190 countries, reduce loss by 2010).</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, species-area).** Within a region Z = 0.2. If one forest has 100 species, about how many would a forest 32 times larger have? *Solution.* 32<sup>0.2</sup> = 2 (since 2⁵ = 32). S = 100 × 2 = **200 species**.

**Example 2 (level 4, match).** (a) Nile perch, (b) Steller's sea cow, (c) host fish and its parasite, (d) Amazon clearing. Causes: (p) co-extinction, (q) alien invasion, (r) habitat loss, (s) over-exploitation. *Solution.* a-q, b-s, c-p, d-r.

**Example 3 (level 3, assertion-reason).** A: Tropical regions have greater species diversity than temperate ones. R: Tropical regions have remained relatively undisturbed for millions of years, giving more time for speciation. *Solution.* Both true; R is one correct explanation. **Option 1.**

**Example 4 (level 5, how many correct).** How many are ex situ: national park, seed bank, cryopreservation of gametes, sacred grove, botanical garden, biosphere reserve? *Solution.* Seed bank, cryopreservation, botanical garden. **Three.**

<div class="myth">

**"A larger area always adds species at the same rate."** On log-log axes the line is straight, but on ordinary axes it flattens: each extra square kilometre adds fewer new species, because the bigger area mostly holds species already counted.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-biology" data-topics="biodiversity" data-mode="practice" data-exam="neet"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Genetic, species and ecological diversity each have a textbook example.
- India: 2.4% land, 8.1% species; world: about 1.5 million described, 7 million estimated.
- log S = log C + Z log A; Z 0.1 to 0.2 regions, 0.6 to 1.2 continents.
- Evil quartet: habitat loss is the biggest; know one example of each.
- In situ protects habitats; ex situ keeps species outside them.
