**Biology · Class 12 · about 24 minutes.** Read [Molecular basis of inheritance](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/molecular-inheritance/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">This chapter mixes quick sums (Chargaff, lengths, codons) with experiments and enzymes. The sums all follow from pairing and triplets; the facts follow from asking "which molecule, which direction, which enzyme".</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Chargaff sums.</b> <i>Method:</i> A = T, G = C, A + G = 50%. <i>Trap:</i> single-stranded DNA (like φX174) does not follow the rule.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Hydrogen bonds and length.</b> H-bonds = 2 × (A-T pairs) + 3 × (G-C pairs). Length = bp × 0.34 nm. Turns = bp ÷ 10. <i>Trap:</i> counting nucleotides instead of base pairs (a 1000 bp DNA has 2000 nucleotides).</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Nucleosomes.</b> Number ≈ bp ÷ 200. Histones are positive (lysine, arginine). <i>Trap:</i> "histones are acidic".</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Experiments.</b> Griffith (transformation, mice), Avery-MacLeod-McCarty (DNase destroys it), Hershey-Chase (³²P inside, ³⁵S outside), Meselson-Stahl (¹⁵N to ¹⁴N, CsCl). <i>Trap:</i> which isotope labels what.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Meselson-Stahl fractions.</b> After n generations in ¹⁴N: hybrid = 2 molecules out of 2ⁿ, light = rest. Generation 1: all hybrid; 2: 1/2 hybrid; 3: 1/4 hybrid.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Strands and directions.</b> Template 3′ → 5′; mRNA and coding strand 5′ → 3′, same sequence; new DNA always grows 5′ → 3′.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. RNA polymerases and processing.</b> I: 28S, 18S, 5.8S rRNA; II: hnRNA; III: tRNA, 5S rRNA, snRNA. Capping 5′, tailing 3′, splicing removes introns. <i>Trap:</i> 5S rRNA is made by pol III, not I.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Codon arithmetic.</b> Nucleotides = 3 × amino acids (+3 for stop). Frameshifts: ±1 or ±2 bases scramble downstream; ±3 adds or removes one amino acid.</div>
<div class="step"><b>9. lac operon, HGP and fingerprinting.</b> i, p, o, z, y, a; inducer; negative control. HGP numbers; VNTR, satellite DNA, Southern blot.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, H-bonds).** A 500 bp DNA has 30% thymine. How many hydrogen bonds hold its strands? *Solution.* 500 bp means 1000 bases. T = 30%, so A = 30% too, and A + T = 60% of 1000 = 600 bases, which is 300 A-T pairs (not 150: each pair holds one A and one T). G-C pairs = 500 − 300 = 200. H-bonds = 2 × 300 + 3 × 200 = **1200**.

**Example 2 (level 4, Meselson-Stahl).** *E. coli* grown in ¹⁵N is shifted to ¹⁴N for three generations. What fraction of DNA molecules is light? *Solution.* 2³ = 8 molecules; 2 hybrid; 6 light. **6/8 = 3/4.**

**Example 3 (level 5, frameshift).** In an mRNA reading AUG UUU CCC GGG AAA ..., the first U after AUG is deleted. What are the next amino acid codons? *Solution.* The frame shifts left by one: AUG UUC CCG GGA AA... so codons after AUG are **UUC, CCG, GGA**, a completely new sequence. Deleting UUU (three bases) would instead remove one amino acid and keep the rest.

**Example 4 (level 5, statements).** Statement I: In bacteria the ρ factor is needed to start transcription. Statement II: In eukaryotes hnRNA is capped at the 3′ end. *Solution.* σ starts and ρ stops, so I is false; capping is at the 5′ end and tailing at the 3′ end, so II is false. **Both incorrect.**

<div class="myth">

**"Every base in DNA codes for protein."** In humans less than 2% does. Introns, repeats and regulatory regions make up the rest.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Chargaff: A = T, G = C; not for single-stranded DNA.
- H-bonds 2 per A-T, 3 per G-C; base pairs, not nucleotides, for length.
- ³²P labels DNA, ³⁵S labels protein; Meselson-Stahl gives 2 hybrid molecules each generation after the first.
- σ starts, ρ stops; pol III makes tRNA and 5S rRNA; cap 5′, tail 3′.
- 3 bases per amino acid; frameshifts scramble, triplet changes do not.
- lac: z β-galactosidase, y permease, a transacetylase; lactose induces.
