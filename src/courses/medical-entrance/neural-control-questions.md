**Biology · Class 11 · about 22 minutes.** Read [Neural control and coordination](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/neural-control/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">Neural questions test two maps and one movie: a map of the neural system's divisions, a map of the brain's parts and jobs, and the movie of an impulse (which ion moves, which way, in what order). Most traps swap one ion or one brain part.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Divisions.</b> CNS, PNS, somatic, autonomic, sympathetic, parasympathetic, visceral; afferent vs efferent. <i>Trap:</i> afferent fibres carry impulses towards the CNS.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Neuron structure.</b> Nissl's granules in cell body and dendrites (not the axon); synaptic knobs; direction in dendrites (towards the cell body) and axons (away).</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Neuron types and myelin.</b> Multipolar, bipolar, unipolar with locations; Schwann cells, nodes of Ranvier. <i>Trap:</i> non-myelinated fibres still have Schwann cells.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Resting potential.</b> Ion concentrations on each side, permeability, the pump ratio. <i>Trap:</i> 3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in, not the reverse.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Action potential.</b> Order of events, polarity at the stimulated point, direction of local current. <i>Method:</i> current flows inside from the active point to the resting point, outside the other way.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Experiments.</b> Low Na⁺ outside, blocked Na⁺ channels, blocked K⁺ channels, a stopped pump. <i>Method:</i> ask which ion movement is lost and which phase needs it.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Synapses.</b> Electrical vs chemical; steps of release; excitatory or inhibitory.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Brain parts.</b> Part to job, part to division (forebrain, midbrain, hindbrain), brain stem members, meninges order.</div>
<div class="step"><b>9. Reflex arc.</b> Minimum neurons, dorsal root, effector; knee jerk.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, experiment).** A drug blocks the K⁺ channels that open after depolarisation. What happens? *Solution.* Depolarisation still happens (Na⁺ enters), but K⁺ cannot leave quickly, so the membrane is **slow to repolarise**: the fibre stays depolarised longer and cannot fire again soon.

**Example 2 (level 4, assertion-reason).** A: Transmission across an electrical synapse is faster than across a chemical synapse. R: At an electrical synapse current flows directly from one neuron to the next. *Solution.* Both true; R explains A (no chemical step). **Both true; R explains A.**

**Example 3 (level 5, match the lists).** List I: corpora quadrigemina, corpus callosum, hypothalamus, medulla. List II: thirst and temperature, breathing centre, midbrain lobes, joins the hemispheres. *Solution.* Corpora quadrigemina: midbrain lobes. Corpus callosum: joins hemispheres. Hypothalamus: thirst and temperature. Medulla: breathing centre.

**Example 4 (level 5, statements).** Statement I: At rest, the axon membrane is more permeable to Na⁺ than to K⁺. Statement II: The inside of a resting axon is negative with respect to the outside. *Solution.* At rest it is more permeable to K⁺, so I is false. II is true. **I incorrect, II correct.**

<div class="myth">

**"The cerebellum is part of the brain stem."** The brain stem is the midbrain, pons and medulla. The cerebellum sits behind it in the hindbrain.

</div>

<dl>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-biology" data-topics="neural-control" data-mode="practice" data-exam="neet"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Afferent to the CNS, efferent away; autonomic = sympathetic + parasympathetic.
- Nissl's granules in cell body and dendrites; bipolar neurons in the retina.
- Rest: K⁺ high inside, Na⁺ high outside; pump 3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in; inside negative.
- Action potential: Na⁺ in, polarity reversed; repolarisation: K⁺ out; all or none.
- Brain stem = midbrain + pons + medulla; medulla has breathing and heart centres; hypothalamus temperature, hunger, thirst.
