**Biology · Class 12 · about 20 minutes.** Read [Biotechnology and its applications](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/biotech-applications/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">Each application is a short story with a fixed cast: an organism, a gene, a vector or method, and a product. NEET asks you to name a missing character or spot the one that does not belong to the story.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. GM crop benefits.</b> Stress tolerance, less pesticide, fewer post-harvest losses, better mineral use, nutrition (Golden rice, vitamin A). <i>Trap:</i> "GM crops always need more fertiliser".</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Bt toxin action.</b> Protoxin → alkaline gut → active toxin → binds midgut epithelium → pores → swelling and lysis. <i>Trap:</i> "activated by acidic pH".</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. cry genes.</b> cryIAc and cryIIAb: cotton bollworms; cryIAb: corn borer. Insect groups: lepidopterans, coleopterans, dipterans.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. RNAi.</b> <i>Meloidogyne incognita</i>, tobacco, <i>Agrobacterium</i> vector, sense + antisense → dsRNA → silencing of nematode mRNA. <i>Trap:</i> RNAi blocks translation; it does not change the nematode's DNA.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Insulin.</b> A and B chains, disulphide bonds, proinsulin with C peptide, 1983, <i>E. coli</i>. <i>Trap:</i> the C peptide is not part of mature insulin.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Gene therapy.</b> 1990, 4-year-old girl, ADA deficiency, lymphocytes, retroviral vector, cDNA; periodic infusions; permanent cure only with early embryonic cells.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Diagnosis.</b> PCR (very low amounts of DNA: HIV, cancer mutations), probe + autoradiography (mutated clone does not show), ELISA (antigen-antibody).</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Transgenic animals.</b> Over 95% mice; five uses; α-1-antitrypsin (emphysema), Rosie (α-lactalbumin, 2.4 g/L, 1997), polio vaccine safety.</div>
<div class="step"><b>9. Ethics.</b> GEAC, Basmati (27 varieties, 1997 patent), turmeric and neem, biopiracy, Indian Patents Bill second amendment.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, statements).** Statement I: Bt toxin kills insects by making pores in the cells of their midgut. Statement II: Bt toxin is active inside the bacterium. *Solution.* I true; II false (inactive protoxin until the alkaline gut). **I correct, II incorrect.**

**Example 2 (level 4, how many correct).** How many are true about ADA gene therapy? (i) the first patient was a 4-year-old girl; (ii) a retroviral vector was used; (iii) one treatment gives a lifelong cure; (iv) the cells used were lymphocytes; (v) the enzyme helps the immune system. *Solution.* (iii) is false because lymphocytes are not immortal. **Four.**

**Example 3 (level 3, assertion-reason).** A: PCR can detect HIV before symptoms appear. R: PCR amplifies very small amounts of nucleic acid. *Solution.* Both true and R explains A. **Option 1.**

**Example 4 (level 5, trap).** Which is correctly matched? (a) Rosie – α-1-antitrypsin; (b) RNAi – *Bacillus thuringiensis*; (c) GEAC – decides on the safety of GM organisms in India; (d) Humulin – made from pig pancreas. *Solution.* **(c).** Rosie's milk had α-lactalbumin; RNAi was used against a nematode; Humulin is recombinant.

<div class="myth">

**"Transgenic animals are mostly cows and sheep."** Over 95% are mice, because they are small, breed fast and their genetics is well known.

</div>
