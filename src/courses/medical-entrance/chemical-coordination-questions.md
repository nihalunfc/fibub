**Biology · Class 11 · about 24 minutes.** Read [Chemical coordination: hormones](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/chemical-coordination/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">Hormone questions are a three-column table: gland, hormone, effect, with a fourth column for disorders. NEET swaps one cell of the table, so learn each row as one sentence: "β cells, insulin, lowers blood glucose, lack gives diabetes mellitus".</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Gland to hormone.</b> <i>Trap:</i> oxytocin and ADH are made in the hypothalamus but released by the posterior pituitary; MSH is from the pars intermedia.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Hormone to effect.</b> <i>Trap:</i> LH triggers ovulation and keeps the corpus luteum; FSH grows follicles; prolactin makes milk, oxytocin ejects it.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Disorders.</b> Gigantism, acromegaly, dwarfism (GH); diabetes insipidus (ADH); goitre, cretinism, Graves' disease (thyroid); diabetes mellitus (insulin); tetany (low Ca²⁺). <i>Trap:</i> diabetes insipidus is ADH, not insulin.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Opposing pairs.</b> Insulin vs glucagon; PTH vs TCT; ANF vs renin-angiotensin-aldosterone; GnRH vs somatostatin are both hypothalamic but act on different pituitary hormones.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Location facts.</b> Sella turcica, isthmus, four parathyroids on the thyroid, thymus behind the sternum, adrenals on the kidneys, Leydig cells between tubules.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Adrenal layers and products.</b> Medulla: catecholamines; cortex: glomerulosa, fasciculata, reticularis; cortisol vs aldosterone.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Heart, kidney and gut hormones.</b> ANF, erythropoietin, gastrin, secretin, CCK, GIP.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Mechanism.</b> Membrane receptor plus second messenger vs intracellular receptor plus genes; chemical class of each hormone.</div>
<div class="step"><b>9. Feedback.</b> Predict what happens to the other hormones when one is too high or too low (goitre from iodine lack).</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, feedback).** A person's diet lacks iodine for years. What happens to TSH and to the size of the thyroid? *Solution.* Thyroxine stays low, so negative feedback does not damp the pituitary; **TSH stays high** and keeps stimulating the gland, which **enlarges (goitre)**.

**Example 2 (level 4, assertion-reason).** A: Adrenaline is called an emergency hormone. R: It is secreted rapidly in response to stress and raises heart rate, alertness and blood glucose. *Solution.* Both true; R explains A. **Both true; R explains A.**

**Example 3 (level 5, match the lists).** List I: thymosin, melatonin, aldosterone, PTH. List II: Na⁺ reabsorption, T cell maturation, raises blood calcium, diurnal rhythm. *Solution.* Thymosin: T cells. Melatonin: diurnal rhythm. Aldosterone: Na⁺ reabsorption. PTH: raises blood calcium.

**Example 4 (level 5, statements).** Statement I: Glucagon is a hypoglycaemic hormone. Statement II: Insulin increases the uptake of glucose by liver and fat cells. *Solution.* Glucagon raises blood glucose (hyperglycaemic), so I is false. II is true. **I incorrect, II correct.**

<div class="myth">

**"Prolactin ejects milk."** Prolactin makes the mammary glands grow and form milk; oxytocin makes the muscle around the alveoli contract to eject it.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-biology" data-topics="chemical-coordination" data-mode="practice" data-exam="neet"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Posterior pituitary stores oxytocin and ADH made in the hypothalamus.
- Diabetes insipidus = ADH lack; diabetes mellitus = insulin action lack.
- Insulin lowers, glucagon raises glucose; PTH raises, TCT lowers calcium.
- Secretin: pancreatic water and bicarbonate; CCK: enzymes and bile; erythropoietin from JG cells; ANF from atria.
- Peptides use membrane receptors and second messengers; steroids and thyroid hormones act on genes.
- Iodine lack: low thyroxine, high TSH, goitre.
