**Biology · Class 11 · about 24 minutes.** Read [Respiration in plants](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/plant-respiration/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">Respiration questions ask where, how many and in what order. Learn one table (stage, place, direct ATP, NADH, FADH₂, CO₂) and two sequences (glycolysis and the Krebs cycle), and every count can be worked out instead of remembered.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Place of each stage.</b> Cytoplasm, matrix, inner membrane. <i>Trap:</i> fermentation is in the cytoplasm; the ETS is on the inner membrane, not in the matrix.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Glycolysis steps.</b> Where ATP is used, where ATP and NADH are made, where water is removed. <i>Trap:</i> PGAL → BPGA makes NADH, not ATP.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Fermentation.</b> Enzymes and products in yeast and muscle; net ATP; why NAD⁺ is needed. <i>Trap:</i> lactic acid fermentation makes no CO₂.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Link reaction and Krebs cycle.</b> First product (citric acid), decarboxylation steps, the GTP step, the FADH₂ step. <i>Method:</i> per turn 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 GTP, 2 CO₂; double per glucose.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. ETS.</b> Complex names, entry of NADH vs FADH₂, cytochrome c, final acceptor, F₀ and F₁. <i>Trap:</i> F₀ is the channel; F₁ makes ATP.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. ATP accounting.</b> Per stage or per glucose; mixed aerobic and anaerobic. <i>Method:</i> NADH × 3 + FADH₂ × 2 + direct ATP.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Amphibolic pathway.</b> Entry points of glycerol, fatty acids and amino acids. <i>Trap:</i> fatty acids enter as acetyl CoA; glycerol as PGAL.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. RQ.</b> Values by substrate, or worked from volumes or a formula. <i>Trap:</i> fats less than 1, organic acids more than 1.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, counts).** How many NADH are made in all when 4 glucose molecules are completely oxidised? *Solution.* Per glucose: 2 (glycolysis) + 2 (link) + 6 (Krebs) = 10. For 4: **40**. The trap answer 32 forgets the link reaction.

**Example 2 (level 4, RQ).** A seed uses 50 mL of O₂ and releases 35 mL of CO₂ per hour. What is it respiring? *Solution.* RQ = 35/50 = 0.7: **fat** (as in oily seeds such as castor or groundnut).

**Example 3 (level 5, assertion-reason).** A: Respiration is called an amphibolic pathway. R: Acetyl CoA from respiration can be used to make fatty acids. *Solution.* Both true, and R is an example of the anabolic side that justifies the name. **Both true; R explains A.**

**Example 4 (level 5, statements).** Statement I: Oxygen is needed at every step of aerobic respiration. Statement II: Oxygen acts as the final hydrogen acceptor in the ETS. *Solution.* Oxygen acts only at the end, so I is false; II is true. Without that final acceptor, though, the earlier steps stop too. **I incorrect, II correct.**

<div class="myth">

**"Glycolysis makes 4 ATP per glucose."** It makes 4 but uses 2, so the net gain is 2. Read whether the question asks for gross or net.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Glycolysis cytoplasm; link and Krebs matrix; ETS inner membrane.
- Net glycolysis 2 ATP; ATP used at hexokinase and the bisphosphate step.
- Krebs per turn: 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 GTP, 2 CO₂; two turns per glucose.
- Per glucose: 10 NADH, 2 FADH₂, 6 CO₂, 38 ATP (NCERT).
- F₀ channel, F₁ makes ATP; cytochrome c is mobile.
- RQ: carbohydrate 1, fat 0.7, protein 0.9, organic acid above 1.
