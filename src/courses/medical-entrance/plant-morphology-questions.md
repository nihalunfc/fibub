**Biology · Class 11 · about 22 minutes.** Read [Morphology of flowering plants](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/plant-morphology/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">This chapter is a set of example lists: one term, three or four examples. NEET asks you to match them, or hides one wrong example in a list. Learn each term with its examples as one unit, and learn origins for modifications.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Modification by origin.</b> "Which pair are both stem modifications?" <i>Method:</i> look for nodes and axillary buds. <i>Trap:</i> pea tendrils (leaf) vs cucumber tendrils (stem); cactus spines (leaf) vs Citrus thorns (stem); sweet potato (root) vs potato (stem).</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Roots.</b> Prop (banyan), stilt (maize, sugarcane), pneumatophores (Rhizophora), regions of the root tip. <i>Trap:</i> root hairs grow in the region of maturation, not elongation.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Leaf.</b> Simple vs compound (a bud in the axil means a leaf), pinnate (neem) vs palmate (silk cotton), phyllotaxy, phyllode (Australian Acacia).</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Flower terms.</b> Symmetry, ovary position, aestivation, androecium joining, placentation, each with examples. <i>Trap:</i> ray florets of sunflower are epigynous; sunflower placentation is basal.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Fruit and seed.</b> Drupe (mango, coconut), parthenocarpy, parts of maize and pea seeds. <i>Trap:</i> castor is an endospermic dicot seed.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Floral formula.</b> Read or write one, or name the family. <i>Method:</i> A(9)+1 means Fabaceae; P(3+3) means Liliaceae; epipetalous A5 with G(2) means Solanaceae.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Family features and uses.</b> Pulses and root nodules (Fabaceae), tomato, potato, tobacco, belladonna (Solanaceae), Aloe, tulip, Colchicum (Liliaceae).</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Statements and odd one out.</b> Four examples, one from another group. Name the group of each before you choose.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, how many are correct).** How many have axile placentation: china rose, tomato, pea, lemon, mustard? *Solution.* China rose ✓, tomato ✓, pea ✗ (marginal), lemon ✓, mustard ✗ (parietal). **Three.**

**Example 2 (level 4, assertion-reason).** A: Ginger is a stem, though it grows underground. R: It has nodes, internodes and scale leaves with buds. *Solution.* Both true, and R is exactly why it is a stem. **Both true; R explains A.**

**Example 3 (level 5, floral formula).** Which family fits: bracteate, actinomorphic, bisexual, six tepals in two joined whorls, six stamens, a superior ovary of three joined carpels? *Solution.* Br ⊕ ⚥ P(3+3) A3+3 G(3): **Liliaceae**. A trimerous flower with a perianth points to a monocot family at once.

**Example 4 (level 5, statements).** Statement I: In racemose inflorescences the flowers open from the base upward. Statement II: In cymose inflorescences the main axis keeps growing. *Solution.* I is true (acropetal). In a cyme the main axis ends in a flower, so II is false. **I correct, II incorrect.**

<div class="myth">

**"A flower with free carpels is syncarpous."** Free carpels make an apocarpous gynoecium (lotus, rose). Syncarpous means joined (mustard, tomato).

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-biology" data-topics="plant-morphology" data-mode="practice" data-exam="neet"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Origin decides the name: axillary bud means stem, leaf position means leaf.
- Ovary: hypogynous (mustard, brinjal), perigynous (rose, plum, peach), epigynous (guava, cucumber).
- Aestivation and placentation: learn three examples each.
- Maize seed: scutellum, coleoptile, coleorhiza, aleurone layer; castor endospermic.
- Formulae: A(9)+1 Fabaceae, A5 epipetalous with G(2) Solanaceae, P(3+3) Liliaceae.

</div>
