**Biology · Class 12 · about 32 minutes.** By the end you will be able to follow pollen and egg from their mother cells to the seed, give the ploidy of every cell in a flower and seed, explain every pollination trick and outbreeding device, and solve the counting questions NEET sets on meiosis and seeds.

<div class="bigidea">A flower is a factory for two tiny plants: the pollen grain (male gametophyte, 3 cells at most) and the embryo sac (female gametophyte, 7 cells). One pollen tube brings two male gametes, and both are used: one makes the embryo, the other makes its food store. That double use is unique to flowering plants.</div>

## 1. The stamen and the pollen grain

A stamen has a stalk (**filament**) and a head (**anther**). A typical anther is **bilobed** and each lobe has two chambers (thecae), so it is **dithecous** with **four microsporangia**.

The wall of a microsporangium has four layers, from outside in: **epidermis, endothecium, middle layers, tapetum**. The tapetum feeds the developing pollen; its cells often have more than one nucleus. In the centre, **sporogenous tissue** forms **microspore mother cells** (2n), and each divides by meiosis into a **tetrad** of four microspores (n). This is **microsporogenesis**.

Each microspore becomes a **pollen grain**:

- **Exine**: tough outer wall of **sporopollenin**, which resists heat, strong acids and alkalis, and no known enzyme breaks it. That is why pollen survives as fossils. Gaps in the exine are **germ pores**, where sporopollenin is absent and the tube comes out.
- **Intine**: thin inner wall of cellulose and pectin.
- Inside: a large **vegetative cell** (food, large irregular nucleus) and a small **generative cell** floating in it. In about 60% of angiosperms pollen is shed at this **2-celled** stage; in the rest the generative cell divides first and pollen is shed **3-celled** (vegetative cell + two male gametes).

Pollen viability varies: about 30 minutes in rice and wheat, months in some members of Rosaceae, Leguminosae and Solanaceae. Pollen can be stored for years in liquid nitrogen (−196 °C) in pollen banks. Pollen of *Parthenium* (carrot grass) causes allergy; pollen tablets are sold as food supplements.

## 2. The pistil, ovule and embryo sac

A pistil has a **stigma**, **style** and **ovary**. Inside the ovary, ovules sit on the **placenta**. One ovule has: a stalk (**funicle**) joined at the **hilum**; one or two protective **integuments** leaving an opening, the **micropyle**; the opposite end, the **chalaza**; and inside, the **nucellus** (food-rich parent tissue) holding the **embryo sac**.

**Megasporogenesis**: a **megaspore mother cell** (2n) near the micropyle divides by meiosis into four megaspores. Usually three die and only one is functional. Development of the embryo sac from a single megaspore is called **monosporic**. That megaspore then does three free-nuclear mitoses (no walls) to make eight nuclei, which are then walled into cells. The mature embryo sac: at the micropylar end the **egg apparatus** (one egg + two **synergids**; synergids have a thickened **filiform apparatus** that guides the pollen tube), at the chalazal end three **antipodals**, and in the middle the large **central cell** with two **polar nuclei**.

## 3. Pollination

Pollination is the transfer of pollen from anther to stigma. It is not fertilisation.

| Kind | Pollen comes from | Genetically |
|---|---|---|
| **Autogamy** | the same flower | self |
| **Geitonogamy** | another flower of the same plant | self (same genes), though it needs an agent like cross-pollination |
| **Xenogamy** | a flower of another plant | true cross; new gene combinations |

Some plants (*Viola*, *Oxalis*, *Commelina*) make two kinds of flowers: **chasmogamous** (open) and **cleistogamous** (never open, always autogamous, seed assured).

**Agents.** Wind: light, non-sticky pollen, well exposed anthers, large feathery stigma, often one ovule per ovary, flowers packed in an inflorescence (grasses, maize "tassels"). Water: *Vallisneria* (female flowers reach the surface on long stalks, pollen floats to them), *Hydrilla*, *Zostera* (long ribbon-like pollen carried underwater). Most water plants like water hyacinth and water lily are actually pollinated by insects or wind. Animals: bees most of all, plus birds (sunbirds, hummingbirds) and bats; flowers are large, colourful, scented, with nectar; the *Amorphophallus* flower is about 6 feet tall. *Yucca* and its moth cannot complete their life cycles without each other.

<div class="myth">

**"Geitonogamy is cross-pollination."** It needs a pollinator like cross-pollination does, but the pollen carries the same genes as the stigma, so genetically it is self-pollination.

</div>

## 4. Outbreeding devices and pollen-pistil interaction

Continued self-pollination causes **inbreeding depression**. Flowers avoid it by: pollen release and stigma receptivity at different times; anther and stigma at different positions; **self-incompatibility** (a genetic block on pollen of the same plant); and unisexual flowers. **Dioecy** (male and female flowers on different plants, as in papaya) prevents both autogamy and geitonogamy; **monoecy** (castor, maize) prevents only autogamy.

The pistil "recognises" the pollen through chemical signals. Compatible pollen germinates, the tube grows through the style, enters the ovule (usually through the micropyle) and one synergid through the filiform apparatus. **Artificial hybridisation** uses this: **emasculation** (remove anthers from the bud before they burst), **bagging** (cover with a butter-paper bag), then dust chosen pollen when the stigma is ready and re-bag.

## 5. Double fertilisation

The tube releases two male gametes into a synergid.

- **Syngamy**: male gamete + egg → **zygote** (2n).
- **Triple fusion**: male gamete + two polar nuclei → **primary endosperm nucleus**, PEN (3n). The central cell becomes the primary endosperm cell.

Both fusions together are **double fertilisation**. The synergids and antipodals degenerate afterwards.

**Endosperm** develops before the embryo, to feed it. In the common **free-nuclear** type the PEN divides many times without walls: the liquid in a tender coconut is free-nuclear endosperm with thousands of nuclei, and the white kernel is cellular endosperm. Seeds where the embryo eats it all up are **non-albuminous** (pea, groundnut, bean); seeds that keep some are **albuminous** (wheat, maize, barley, castor). A leftover nucellus in the seed is the **perisperm** (black pepper, beet).

**Embryo**: the zygote divides at the micropylar end. A dicot embryo has an **embryonal axis** with two **cotyledons**: above them the **epicotyl** ending in the **plumule**, below the **hypocotyl** ending in the **radicle** with its root cap. A monocot (grass) embryo has one cotyledon, the **scutellum**; the radicle is covered by the **coleorhiza** and the plumule by the **coleoptile**.

## 6. Seed and fruit

The **ovule becomes the seed**: integuments harden into seed coats, and the micropyle stays as a small pore that lets in oxygen and water during germination. The **ovary becomes the fruit**: its wall becomes the **pericarp**. Most seeds dry to 10 to 15% moisture and go **dormant**. Fruits from the thalamus as well (apple, strawberry, cashew) are **false fruits**; fruits formed without fertilisation are **parthenocarpic** (banana), and growth hormones can induce them. Seeds can last very long: a date palm seed about 2000 years old from King Herod's palace (Masada) and a lupine seed about 10,000 years old have germinated.

## 7. Apomixis and polyembryony

**Apomixis** is seed formation without fertilisation: asexual reproduction that looks like sexual. It is seen in some grasses and Asteraceae. A diploid egg may form without reduction division, or nucellus cells may push into the embryo sac and grow into embryos. When extra embryos form, the seed has **polyembryony** (citrus, mango). Apomixis matters to farmers: if hybrid seed were apomictic, the hybrid traits would not segregate, and farmers could save seed every year instead of buying new hybrid seed.

## 8. Worked counting example

*How many meiotic divisions make 50 pollen grains and 50 embryo sacs?* Pollen: 50 ÷ 4 = 12.5, so at least 13 meioses (a meiosis cannot be half done). Embryo sacs: one meiosis each, since three megaspores die, so 50. Total 63. Questions that ask for seeds use whole multiples of 4 to avoid the fraction.

<div class="keypoints">

- Anther: bilobed, dithecous, four microsporangia; wall layers epidermis, endothecium, middle layers, tapetum (nourishes pollen).
- Pollen: exine of sporopollenin with germ pores, intine of cellulose and pectin; shed 2-celled (about 60%) or 3-celled.
- Ovule: funicle, hilum, integuments, micropyle, chalaza, nucellus; embryo sac monosporic, 7-celled and 8-nucleate.
- Autogamy, geitonogamy (genetically self), xenogamy; cleistogamous flowers are always selfed.
- Outbreeding: timing, position, self-incompatibility, unisexual flowers; dioecy stops both autogamy and geitonogamy.
- Double fertilisation: syngamy (zygote 2n) + triple fusion (PEN 3n).
- Ovule to seed, ovary to fruit; perisperm is leftover nucellus; apomixis gives seeds without fertilisation.
- Counts: 1 meiosis gives 4 pollen grains but only 1 embryo sac.

</div>
