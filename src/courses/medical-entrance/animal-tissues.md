**Biology · Class 11 · about 28 minutes.** By the end you will be able to name each animal tissue from its cells and matrix, say where it is found and what it does, and spot the swapped-location traps NEET builds from this chapter.

<div class="bigidea">Bodies use just four tissues. Epithelium covers and lines, connective tissue holds things together and supports, muscle moves, and neural tissue sends messages. Each tissue's shape fits its job.</div>

A **tissue** is a group of similar cells, with the material between them, that does one job together. Tissues make organs (heart, lung) and organs make organ systems.

## 1. Epithelial tissue

Epithelium has a **free surface** facing a body fluid or the outside, cells packed close with very little matrix between them, and sits on a non-cellular **basement membrane**. Pick a type to see its cells.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="tissue-gallery"><figcaption>Simple epithelia are one layer thick; compound epithelium has many layers. Shape follows job: thin for diffusion, tall for secretion and absorption, many layers for protection.</figcaption></figure>

| Type | Cells | Where | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Squamous | one layer of flat cells with uneven edges | walls of blood vessels, air sacs of lungs | diffusion boundary |
| Cuboidal | one layer of cube-like cells | ducts of glands, tubules of nephrons (microvilli in the PCT) | secretion, absorption |
| Columnar | one layer of tall cells, nuclei at the base, often microvilli | lining of stomach and intestine | secretion, absorption |
| Ciliated | cuboidal or columnar cells with cilia | bronchioles, fallopian tubes | moves mucus or eggs in one direction |
| Compound | many layers | dry skin surface, moist lining of mouth and pharynx, inner lining of salivary and pancreatic ducts | protection from chemical and mechanical stress |

**Glandular epithelium.** Some columnar or cuboidal cells become secretory. **Unicellular** glands are single cells, such as **goblet cells** of the gut lining; **multicellular** glands are clusters, such as salivary glands. **Exocrine** glands pour their products through ducts (mucus, saliva, earwax, oil, milk, digestive enzymes). **Endocrine** glands have no ducts and release **hormones** straight into the fluid around them.

**Cell junctions** link cells in all tissues, especially epithelia:
- **Tight junctions** seal neighbouring cells so substances cannot leak across the tissue.
- **Adhering junctions** cement neighbouring cells together.
- **Gap junctions** connect the cytoplasm of neighbouring cells so ions, small molecules and sometimes big molecules pass quickly.

## 2. Connective tissue

The most abundant and widespread tissue. Except in blood, its cells secrete fibres of **collagen** or **elastin** that give strength, elasticity and flexibility, and a **matrix** (ground substance) of modified polysaccharides.

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>Loose connective tissue.</b> Cells and fibres loosely arranged in a semi-fluid ground substance. <b>Areolar tissue</b> lies under the skin and supports epithelium; it has fibroblasts (make fibres), macrophages and mast cells. <b>Adipose tissue</b>, also under the skin, stores fat; extra nutrients not used at once become fat and are stored here.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Dense connective tissue.</b> Fibres and fibroblasts packed tightly. <b>Dense regular:</b> collagen fibres in parallel bundles, as in <b>tendons</b> (muscle to bone) and <b>ligaments</b> (bone to bone). <b>Dense irregular:</b> fibres running in many directions, as in the skin.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Cartilage.</b> A solid, pliable matrix that resists compression. Its cells (chondrocytes) sit in small cavities (lacunae) in the matrix they make. Found in the tip of the nose, the outer ear, between vertebrae and at the ends of limb bones in adults; most cartilage in the embryo is replaced by bone.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Bone.</b> A hard, non-pliable matrix rich in calcium salts and collagen, which gives strength. Bone cells (osteocytes) lie in lacunae. Bones support the body, protect organs and work with muscles for movement; the <b>marrow</b> of some bones makes blood cells.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Blood.</b> A fluid connective tissue: plasma with red cells, white cells and platelets. It carries substances around the body. (Details in Body fluids and circulation.)</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Tendons join bone to bone."** Tendons join muscle to bone; ligaments join bone to bone. Both are dense regular connective tissue.

</div>

## 3. Muscle tissue

Muscle fibres are long and cylindrical, packed with **myofibrils**, and contract in response to a stimulus.

| Muscle | Fibres | Control | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Skeletal | long, cylindrical, striated, many nuclei, in parallel bundles | voluntary | attached to bones (biceps) |
| Smooth | spindle-shaped (taper at both ends), no striations, one nucleus | involuntary | walls of blood vessels, stomach, intestine |
| Cardiac | branched, striated, joined by **intercalated discs** | involuntary | heart only |

At intercalated discs the cell membranes fuse and gap junctions let the cells contract as one unit: when one cell gets the signal, its neighbours follow.

## 4. Neural tissue

Neural tissue controls how the body responds to change. The **neuron** is the excitable unit: a strong enough stimulus starts an electrical signal that travels along its membrane and passes to other neurons, muscles or glands. **Neuroglia** cells protect and support neurons and make up more than half the volume of neural tissue.

## 5. Worked example

**Identify the tissue:** "fibroblasts in rows between parallel bundles of collagen". *Method:* parallel bundles mean regular; packed fibres mean dense. **Dense regular connective tissue**, as in a tendon or ligament.

<div class="keypoints">

- Four tissues: epithelial, connective, muscular, neural.
- Squamous (vessels, alveoli), cuboidal (gland ducts, nephron tubules), columnar (stomach, intestine), ciliated (bronchioles, fallopian tubes), compound (skin, mouth, pharynx, salivary and pancreatic ducts).
- Goblet cells are unicellular glands; exocrine glands have ducts, endocrine glands do not.
- Tight junctions seal, adhering junctions cement, gap junctions connect cytoplasm.
- Areolar and adipose under the skin; tendons and ligaments dense regular; skin dense irregular.
- Cartilage pliable (nose, ear, between vertebrae); bone hard with calcium salts and marrow; blood is fluid connective tissue.
- Skeletal striated and voluntary; smooth spindle and involuntary; cardiac branched with intercalated discs.
- Neuroglia make up more than half of neural tissue.

</div>
