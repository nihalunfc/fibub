**Biology · Class 11 · about 22 minutes.** Read [Cell cycle and cell division](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/cell-cycle/) first. Here are the question types, worked examples and practice.

<div class="bigidea">Cell-cycle questions test sequence (what happens when) and counting (how many chromosomes, chromatids and how much DNA). A stage table and the C-value rule answer almost all of them.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Identify the stage</b> from a description or diagram. <i>Trap:</i> metaphase I (bivalents on the plate) vs metaphase II and mitotic metaphase (single chromosomes).</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Count chromosomes, chromatids and DNA.</b> <i>Method:</i> chromosomes = centromeres; DNA in C (2C → 4C in S). <i>Trap:</i> anaphase doubles chromosomes, not DNA.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Prophase I substages in order or by event.</b> Leptotene, zygotene, pachytene, diplotene, diakinesis: "Lazy Zebras Pace Dizzy Distances" helps.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Mitosis vs meiosis.</b> Pairing, crossing over and reduction happen only in meiosis I.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Cytokinesis.</b> Furrow (animals) vs cell plate from the centre outwards (plants); syncytium if cytokinesis is skipped.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Phases and their events.</b> S: DNA replication and centriole duplication; G0: quiescent, metabolically active.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Significance.</b> Mitosis: growth, repair, meristems; meiosis: constant number across generations, variation.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 3, counting).** An organism has 2n = 12 and 10 pg of DNA in G1. Give the chromosome number and DNA content at metaphase I and after meiosis II. *Solution.* Metaphase I: 12 chromosomes (6 bivalents), 20 pg (4C). After meiosis II: 6 chromosomes, 5 pg (1C).

**Example 2 (level 3, match).** Match: A. zygotene, B. pachytene, C. diplotene, D. diakinesis with p. terminalisation, q. synapsis, r. crossing over, s. chiasmata seen. *Solution.* **A-q, B-r, C-s, D-p.**

**Example 3 (level 4, assertion-reason).** A: The DNA content of a cell doubles in S phase, but its chromosome number does not change. R: Replicated chromosomes are made of two sister chromatids held at one centromere. *Solution.* Both true and R explains A. **Option 1.**

**Example 4 (level 5, statements).** How many are correct? (i) Interphase lasts more than 95% of the cycle in a typical human cell. (ii) Centrioles duplicate in G2. (iii) Cell plate formation starts at the centre of the cell. (iv) Anaphase I separates sister chromatids. *Solution.* (i) ✓; (ii) ✗ (in S, in the cytoplasm); (iii) ✓; (iv) ✗ (homologues). **Two.**

<div class="myth">

**"Meiosis II is a reduction division."** Meiosis I halves the chromosome number (reductional); meiosis II is equational, like mitosis, separating sister chromatids.

</div>

<dl>
<dt>Chromosome count</dt><dd>count centromeres</dd>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-biology" data-topics="cell-cycle" data-mode="practice" data-exam="neet"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Chromosomes = centromeres; DNA in C units; S doubles DNA, anaphase doubles chromosome count.
- Prophase I order: leptotene, zygotene (synapsis), pachytene (crossing over), diplotene (chiasmata), diakinesis (terminalisation).
- Meiosis I is reductional; meiosis II is equational.
- Centrioles duplicate in S; G0 is quiescent but active.
