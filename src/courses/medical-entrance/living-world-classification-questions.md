**Biology · Class 11 · about 22 minutes.** Read [The living world and biological classification](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/living-world-classification/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">Almost every question here is a sorting question: which box, which rank, which feature chose the box. Learn one or two examples per group and the feature that defines the group, and the swapped-example traps fall apart.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Characteristics of life.</b> "Which is a defining property?" <i>Method:</i> defining means no exceptions both ways. <i>Trap:</i> growth and reproduction look right but have exceptions (crystals grow, mules are sterile).</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Nomenclature rules.</b> Capital, small, italics, underline separately, Latin, author name after. <i>Trap:</i> a capital on the epithet, or one underline under both words.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Hierarchy.</b> Order the ranks or place a taxon (Poaceae is a family, Primata an order, Diptera an order, Insecta a class). <i>Trap:</i> "division" is the plant word for phylum; "family" ends in -aceae in plants and -idae in animals.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Taxonomical aids.</b> Match aid to description. <i>Trap:</i> a key uses couplets of contrasting characters; a monograph covers one taxon; a flora covers an area.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Five kingdom criteria and problems.</b> Five criteria (cell type, body, nutrition, reproduction, phylogeny). <i>Trap:</i> Protista mixed unicellular algae with protozoa; Monera mixed archaebacteria with true bacteria; viruses and lichens are not placed.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Monera.</b> Shapes, archaebacteria habitats, cyanobacteria, heterocysts, mycoplasma. <i>Trap:</i> "blue-green algae" are bacteria, not algae.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Protista and protozoa.</b> Silica wall (diatoms), cellulose plates and red tides (dinoflagellates), pellicle (euglenoids), plasmodium (slime moulds); four protozoan groups with one disease each.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Fungi.</b> Class from spore type and septa; examples (Albugo, Penicillium, Claviceps, Puccinia, Ustilago, Alternaria, Trichoderma). <i>Trap:</i> yeast is an ascomycete and unicellular.</div>
<div class="step"><b>9. Viruses, viroids, prions, lichens.</b> Who discovered what (Ivanowsky, Beijerinck, Stanley, Diener), which nucleic acid, which disease. <i>Trap:</i> viroid = RNA without coat and smaller than a virus; prion = protein with no nucleic acid.</div>
<div class="step"><b>10. Statements and assertion-reason.</b> NCERT lines with one changed word. Mark each line true or false before reading the options.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, how many are correct).** How many are archaebacteria: halophiles, methanogens, *Nostoc*, thermoacidophiles, *Rhizobium*? *Solution.* Halophiles ✓, methanogens ✓, *Nostoc* ✗ (cyanobacterium, a eubacterium), thermoacidophiles ✓, *Rhizobium* ✗ (eubacterium). **Three.**

**Example 2 (level 4, assertion-reason).** A: Diatoms leave behind large deposits called diatomaceous earth. R: Diatom cell walls contain silica, which does not decay easily. *Solution.* Both true, and the indestructible silica walls are why deposits build up over millions of years. **Both true; R explains A.**

**Example 3 (level 5, match the lists).** List I: *Albugo*, *Claviceps*, *Puccinia*, *Trichoderma*. List II: deuteromycete, ascomycete, phycomycete, basidiomycete. *Solution.* Start with the surest pair: *Albugo* (white rust of mustard) is a phycomycete. *Claviceps* (ergot) is an ascomycete. *Puccinia* (rust of wheat) is a basidiomycete. *Trichoderma* has no known sexual stage: deuteromycete.

**Example 4 (level 5, statements).** Statement I: Viruses contain both DNA and RNA. Statement II: Viroids are smaller than viruses and lack a protein coat. *Solution.* A virus has either RNA or DNA, never both, so I is false. II is true (the potato spindle tuber viroid is free RNA). **I incorrect, II correct.**

<div class="myth">

**"Lichens are a single organism in kingdom Plantae."** A lichen is a partnership of an alga and a fungus, and the five kingdom system leaves it out. Lichens disappear from polluted air, so they are used as pollution indicators.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-biology" data-topics="living-world-classification" data-mode="practice" data-exam="neet"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Defining features have no exceptions: metabolism, cellular organisation, consciousness.
- Name rules: genus capital, epithet small, italics or separate underlines.
- Know a rank for common taxa (Poaceae family, Diptera order, Insecta class, Mammalia class).
- Fungi by spores: aseptate phycomycetes, ascospores, basidiospores, no sexual stage in deuteromycetes.
- Viruses have RNA or DNA; viroids are naked RNA; prions are protein; lichens are alga plus fungus.
- In statements, look for "both", "all", "only" and swapped examples.

</div>
