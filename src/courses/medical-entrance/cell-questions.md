**Biology · Class 11 · about 22 minutes.** Read [Cell: the unit of life](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/cell/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">Cell questions are mostly about three facts per organelle: how many membranes, whether it has its own DNA, and its job. Questions mix these facts across organelles and change one word to make a line false.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Organelle features.</b> "Which pair are both double-membraned?" <i>Method:</i> keep the three-column table (membranes, DNA, job) in your head. <i>Trap:</i> lysosomes and vacuoles (single), ribosomes and centrioles (none).</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Prokaryote vs eukaryote.</b> <i>Trap:</i> prokaryotes do have ribosomes (70S), a cell membrane and often a wall; they lack membrane-bound organelles.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Match organelle to job</b> (List I and List II). <i>Method:</i> eliminate with your surest pair. <i>Trap:</i> Golgi (packaging, glycosylation) vs smooth ER (lipids, steroids).</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Who discovered what.</b> Schleiden, Schwann, Virchow, Robert Brown (nucleus), Camillo Golgi, George Palade (ribosomes), Singer and Nicolson (fluid mosaic).</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Numbers.</b> Sizes (Mycoplasma 0.3 µm), membrane composition (RBC: about 52% protein, 40% lipid), Svedberg units (50S + 30S = 70S), 9 + 2 and 9 triplets, vacuole up to 90% of the volume.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Statements and assertion-reason.</b> NCERT lines with one changed word: "all", "only", "never". Mark each ✓ or ✗ first.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Structure from a diagram or a passage.</b> Read the labels' positions: cis and trans faces, cristae, thylakoids and grana, kinetochores.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 3, statements).** Statement I: The plasma membrane is a static structure. Statement II: Membrane proteins can move sideways within the bilayer. *Solution.* The fluid mosaic model says the membrane is fluid, so I is false; II is true. **I incorrect, II correct.**

**Example 2 (level 3, assertion-reason).** A: Mitochondria can make some of their own proteins. R: Mitochondria have their own DNA and ribosomes. *Solution.* Both true, and R explains A. **Option 1.**

**Example 3 (level 4, how many are correct).** How many of these lack a membrane: ribosome, lysosome, centriole, nucleolus, peroxisome? *Solution.* Ribosome ✓, lysosome ✗ (single), centriole ✓, nucleolus ✓ (not membrane-bound), peroxisome ✗ (single). **Three.**

**Example 4 (level 5, trap).** Which is correct about the Svedberg unit? (a) 60S + 40S ribosomal subunits always give a 100S ribosome; (b) it measures how fast a particle sediments, which depends on size and shape, so subunit values do not add; (c) it is a unit of mass; (d) 70S ribosomes occur only in prokaryotes. *Solution.* **(b).** (a) gives 80S, not 100S; (c) it is a sedimentation coefficient; (d) mitochondria and chloroplasts have 70S ribosomes too.

<div class="myth">

**"Animal cells have no vacuoles."** They have small ones; it is the large central vacuole that is a plant feature. Read questions for the word "large".

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-biology" data-topics="cell" data-mode="practice" data-exam="neet"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Know membranes, DNA and job for every organelle; most questions mix these.
- Prokaryotes have ribosomes and membranes but no membrane-bound organelles.
- Endomembrane system: ER, Golgi, lysosomes, vacuoles only.
- Learn the discoverers and the key numbers (0.3 µm, 52% / 40%, 9 + 2, 70S and 80S).
- In statements, look for "all", "only", "never" and swapped examples.

</div>
