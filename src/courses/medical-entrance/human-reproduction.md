**Biology · Class 12 · about 32 minutes.** By the end you will be able to name every part of both reproductive systems, follow sperm and egg from stem cell to gamete with their chromosome and DNA counts, read the menstrual cycle from its hormones, and trace a pregnancy from fertilisation to birth and milk.

<div class="bigidea">Humans make gametes by meiosis, bring them together inside the body, and grow the embryo inside the mother. The male makes millions of sperm every day for life; the female starts with a fixed stock of eggs and releases about one a month. Hormones from the brain, ovary and placenta keep the timing.</div>

## 1. The male reproductive system

- **Testes** sit outside the abdomen in the **scrotum**, which keeps them 2 to 2.5 °C cooler, as sperm formation needs. Each testis has about 250 **testicular lobules**, each with one to three coiled **seminiferous tubules**.
- Inside the tubules: **spermatogonia** (male germ cells) and **Sertoli cells** (nourish the germ cells). Between the tubules: **Leydig (interstitial) cells** that secrete androgens, mainly testosterone.
- Ducts in order: seminiferous tubules → **rete testis** → **vasa efferentia** → **epididymis** (on the back of the testis) → **vas deferens** → joins the duct of the seminal vesicle to form the **ejaculatory duct** → **urethra** → out through the **penis** (tip: glans penis, covered by the foreskin).
- Glands: paired **seminal vesicles**, one **prostate** and paired **bulbourethral glands**. Their secretions make the **seminal plasma**, rich in fructose, calcium and some enzymes; bulbourethral fluid also lubricates the penis.

## 2. The female reproductive system

- **Ovaries** (2 to 4 cm long) make eggs and hormones. Each has an outer cortex and inner medulla, with follicles in the **ovarian stroma**.
- **Oviduct (fallopian tube)**, 10 to 12 cm, from the ovary inward: **infundibulum** with finger-like **fimbriae** that catch the egg, then the wider **ampulla** (where fertilisation usually happens), then the narrow **isthmus**.
- **Uterus** (womb): wall of three layers: **perimetrium** (outer), **myometrium** (thick smooth muscle that contracts strongly at birth) and **endometrium** (glandular lining that changes every cycle). It opens into the vagina through the **cervix**.
- External genitalia: mons pubis, labia majora, labia minora, hymen, clitoris. The hymen can tear in many ways and its presence or absence says nothing reliable about sexual history.
- **Mammary glands**: glandular tissue in 15 to 20 lobes with alveoli that make milk → mammary tubules → mammary duct → mammary ampulla → lactiferous duct → nipple.

## 3. Gametogenesis

| Step | Spermatogenesis (in the testis, from puberty for life) | Oogenesis (in the ovary, starts before birth) |
|---|---|---|
| Stem cells (2n) | spermatogonia multiply by mitosis | oogonia form in the fetus; no new ones after birth |
| Meiosis I begins | primary spermatocyte (2n) | primary oocyte (2n), arrested in prophase I until puberty |
| After meiosis I | 2 secondary spermatocytes (n) | 1 secondary oocyte (n) + first polar body |
| After meiosis II | 4 spermatids (n) | 1 ovum (n) + second polar body; meiosis II finishes only when a sperm enters |
| Final change | spermiogenesis (spermatid → sperm); release from Sertoli cells is spermiation | none |

At birth each ovary holds about 60,000 to 80,000 primary follicles, and the number falls with age. A primary oocyte with a layer of granulosa cells is a **primary follicle**; more layers and a theca make a **secondary follicle**; a fluid-filled space (antrum) makes a **tertiary follicle**, which matures into the **Graafian follicle**. The secondary oocyte is wrapped in the **zona pellucida**.

**Hormones:** GnRH from the hypothalamus makes the pituitary release LH (acts on Leydig cells: androgens) and FSH (acts on Sertoli cells: factors for spermiogenesis).

**The sperm**: head (haploid nucleus capped by the **acrosome**, full of enzymes for entering the egg), neck, middle piece (many **mitochondria** for the energy to swim), tail. A normal ejaculate has 200 to 300 million sperm, of which at least 60% should be normal in shape and at least 40% should swim vigorously for normal fertility.

## 4. The menstrual cycle

The first cycle (**menarche**) starts at puberty; cycles stop at **menopause** (around 50 years). One cycle averages 28 to 29 days. Move through the cycle and watch the hormones.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="menstrual-cycle"><figcaption>Relative levels of LH, FSH, estrogen and progesterone through one cycle (schematic shapes). Change the cycle length: ovulation always comes about 14 days before the next period.</figcaption></figure>

1. **Menstrual phase** (days 1 to 5): the endometrium and its blood vessels break down and leave as blood. It happens only if the released egg was not fertilised. Missed periods can mean pregnancy, but also stress or poor health.
2. **Follicular (proliferative) phase**: rising LH and FSH grow the follicle, which secretes **estrogen**; the endometrium regenerates.
3. **Ovulation** (about day 14 in a 28-day cycle): an **LH surge** at mid-cycle ruptures the Graafian follicle and releases the secondary oocyte.
4. **Luteal (secretory) phase**: the leftover follicle becomes the **corpus luteum**, which secretes **progesterone**, keeping the endometrium ready. No fertilisation: it degenerates, progesterone falls and the next menstruation begins.

## 5. Fertilisation and implantation

Sperm reach the **ampulla**, where the oocyte waits. One sperm contacts the zona pellucida and changes it so that **other sperm are blocked** (no polyspermy). Acrosome enzymes help the sperm enter. Now the secondary oocyte finishes meiosis II, giving the haploid ovum and the second polar body; the two nuclei fuse into a **diploid zygote**.

**Sex is decided at fertilisation by the sperm.** Every ovum carries an X. Half the sperm carry X (XX zygote: female), half carry Y (XY: male). So the chance of a girl or boy is 50% each time, and the father's sperm decides, not the mother.

The zygote divides by mitosis (**cleavage**) as it moves down the oviduct: 2, 4, 8, 16 cells (**blastomeres**). The 8 to 16 cell ball is the **morula**, which becomes a **blastocyst**: an outer layer, the **trophoblast**, and an **inner cell mass**. The trophoblast attaches to the endometrium and the blastocyst sinks into it: **implantation**. The inner cell mass becomes the embryo.

## 6. Pregnancy and the placenta

Finger-like **chorionic villi** of the trophoblast interlock with uterine tissue to form the **placenta**, joined to the embryo by the **umbilical cord**. It carries oxygen and food in and carbon dioxide and wastes out, and it is an endocrine organ: it secretes **hCG**, **hPL** (human placental lactogen), estrogens, progestogens and, late in pregnancy, **relaxin** (the ovary also makes relaxin). hCG keeps the corpus luteum alive in early pregnancy and is what home pregnancy tests detect.

The inner cell mass makes **ectoderm, mesoderm and endoderm**, the three layers that give every tissue. It contains **stem cells** able to become any tissue.

| Time | Landmark |
|---|---|
| 1 month | heart forms; first sign of growth is the heartbeat |
| End of 2nd month | limbs and digits |
| End of 12 weeks (1st trimester) | most organs formed; limbs and external genitalia well developed |
| 5th month | first movements felt; hair on the head |
| End of 24 weeks (2nd trimester) | fine hair, eyelids separate, eyelashes |
| About 9 months | ready for birth |

## 7. Birth and lactation

**Parturition** (birth) is started by a **neuroendocrine** signal: the fully grown fetus and placenta trigger mild uterine contractions (the **fetal ejection reflex**), which make the mother's pituitary release **oxytocin**. Oxytocin makes the myometrium contract harder, which releases more oxytocin: a positive feedback loop that ends with the baby and then the placenta being pushed out.

Milk production (**lactation**) begins towards the end of pregnancy. The first milk, **colostrum**, carries antibodies (IgA) that protect the newborn, which is why breastfeeding in the first days matters so much.

<div class="keypoints">

- Testis: seminiferous tubules (spermatogonia, Sertoli cells), Leydig cells between them; ducts rete testis → vasa efferentia → epididymis → vas deferens → ejaculatory duct → urethra.
- Oviduct: infundibulum (fimbriae), ampulla (fertilisation), isthmus; uterus wall: perimetrium, myometrium, endometrium.
- 1 primary spermatocyte → 4 sperm; 1 primary oocyte → 1 ovum + polar bodies; oocyte released at metaphase II.
- Cycle: menstrual, follicular (estrogen), LH surge and ovulation, luteal (progesterone from corpus luteum); ovulation about 14 days before the next period.
- Fertilisation in the ampulla; the sperm decides sex; morula → blastocyst → implantation.
- Placenta hormones: hCG, hPL, estrogens, progestogens, relaxin; heartbeat at 1 month, organs by 12 weeks.
- Birth by the fetal ejection reflex and oxytocin (positive feedback); colostrum gives IgA.
