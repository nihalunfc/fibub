**Biology · Class 11 · about 22 minutes.** Read [Animal kingdom](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/animal-kingdom/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">Animal kingdom questions give you a clue and ask for a group, or give a group and ask for an example. The fastest route is one hallmark and two examples per phylum and class, plus the six features as a grid.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Feature to phylum.</b> "Pseudocoelomate", "diploblastic", "metamerically segmented". <i>Method:</i> use the six-feature grid. <i>Trap:</i> Cnidaria and Ctenophora are both diploblastic and radial.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Hallmark structure.</b> Choanocytes, cnidoblasts, comb plates, flame cells, nephridia, Malpighian tubules, radula, water vascular system, stomochord. <i>Trap:</i> insects excrete by Malpighian tubules, not nephridia.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Example to group</b> (often a list). <i>Trap:</i> Pennatula, Gorgonia and Meandrina are cnidarians; Antedon, Cucumaria and Ophiura are echinoderms; Dentalium and Chaetopleura are molluscs.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Common names.</b> Jellyfish, starfish, cuttlefish, devil fish, silverfish, sea horse, flying fox. <i>Trap:</i> only the sea horse and the flying fish are fishes; the flying fox is a bat.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Chordate subphyla.</b> Where the notochord is, and for how long. <i>Trap:</i> Balanoglossus is a hemichordate (non-chordate) with a stomochord.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Vertebrate class features.</b> Scales, gills, air bladder, heart chambers, fertilisation, temperature. <i>Trap:</i> crocodiles have four chambers; sharks have no air bladder; platypus is an egg-laying mammal.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Trick pairs.</b> Hydra vs Obelia (metagenesis in Obelia), Nereis vs earthworm (parapodia and separate sexes in Nereis), Ascaris vs Fasciola (round vs flat).</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Statements and assertion-reason.</b> One changed word per line. Mark each line before you read the options.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, how many are correct).** How many have a closed circulatory system: earthworm, cockroach, *Nereis*, frog, prawn? *Solution.* Annelids and chordates have closed systems; arthropods have open ones. Earthworm ✓, cockroach ✗, *Nereis* ✓ (an annelid), frog ✓, prawn ✗ (an arthropod). **Three.**

**Example 2 (level 4, assertion-reason).** A: Cartilaginous fishes have to swim constantly to avoid sinking. R: They lack an air bladder. *Solution.* Both true, and R explains A. **Both true; R explains A.**

**Example 3 (level 5, match the lists).** List I: *Pteropus*, *Hippocampus*, *Ichthyophis*, *Neophron*. List II: limbless amphibian, bird, mammal, bony fish. *Solution.* *Neophron* (vulture) is a bird; *Pteropus* (flying fox) is a mammal; *Hippocampus* (sea horse) is a bony fish; *Ichthyophis* is the limbless amphibian.

**Example 4 (level 5, statements).** Statement I: All chordates are vertebrates. Statement II: In vertebrates, the notochord is replaced by a vertebral column in the adult. *Solution.* Urochordates and cephalochordates are chordates without vertebrae, so I is false. II is true. **I incorrect, II correct.**

<div class="myth">

**"All animals with a body cavity are coelomates."** A cavity not fully lined by mesoderm is a pseudocoelom (roundworms). Coelomates start with annelids.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-biology" data-topics="animal-kingdom" data-mode="practice" data-exam="neet"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Six-feature grid places any phylum; Cnidaria vs Ctenophora by cnidoblasts vs comb plates.
- Excretory organs: flame cells, nephridia, Malpighian tubules, proboscis gland; none in echinoderms.
- Names that mislead: jellyfish, starfish, cuttlefish, devil fish, silverfish, flying fox.
- Notochord: larval tail (urochordates), lifelong (cephalochordates), replaced (vertebrates).
- Hearts 2, 3, 4; crocodiles have 4; sharks lack an air bladder; platypus lays eggs.

</div>
