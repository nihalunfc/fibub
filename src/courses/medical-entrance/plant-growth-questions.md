**Biology · Class 11 · about 24 minutes.** Read [Plant growth and development](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/plant-growth/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">Two kinds of question dominate: growth rate numbers and hormone matching. For numbers, decide first whether the growth is arithmetic or geometric. For hormones, learn a table of discovery, effect and use, and watch for the pairs that act against each other.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Phases of growth.</b> Features of meristematic, elongation and maturation zones. <i>Trap:</i> vacuoles enlarge in the elongation zone; thin walls and many plasmodesmata are meristematic features.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Growth rate numbers.</b> Absolute vs relative, arithmetic L = L₀ + rt, geometric W₁ = W₀e^(rt), doubling time ln 2 / r. <i>Trap:</i> equal absolute gain does not mean equal relative rate.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Curves.</b> Straight line, J, S; lag, log, stationary phases. <i>Trap:</i> the exponential part is only the middle of the S.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Differentiation terms.</b> Dedifferentiation (parenchyma to cambium), redifferentiation, plasticity, heterophylly examples (cotton, coriander, larkspur by age; buttercup by habitat).</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Hormone discovery.</b> Darwins and Went (auxin), Kurosawa (GA), Skoog and Miller (kinetin), Cousins (ethylene), three inhibitors (ABA).</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Hormone effects and uses.</b> <i>Method:</i> group by job: length (GA), roots and apical dominance (auxin), division and youth (cytokinin), ripening and shedding (ethylene), stress and dormancy (ABA).</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Antagonists.</b> Auxin vs cytokinin (apical dominance), GA vs ABA (dormancy, germination). Tissue culture ratio: cytokinin-rich shoots, auxin-rich roots.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Statements and match the lists.</b> Use the surest pair first: bolting = GA, 2,4-D = auxin, ethephon = ethylene.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, numbers).** A culture doubles every 4 hours. What is r? *Solution.* rt = ln 2 at t = 4 h, so r = 0.693/4 = **0.173 per hour**. After 12 hours the culture is 2³ = 8 times bigger.

**Example 2 (level 4, assertion-reason).** A: Removing the shoot tip makes a plant bushy. R: Auxin from the shoot tip suppresses the growth of lateral buds. *Solution.* Both true, and R explains A (apical dominance is lifted). **Both true; R explains A.**

**Example 3 (level 5, match the lists).** List I: GA₃, ethephon, 2,4-D, zeatin. List II: malting in brewing, dicot weed killer, coconut milk, female flowers in cucumber. *Solution.* GA₃: malting. Ethephon: cucumber female flowers. 2,4-D: weed killer. Zeatin: coconut milk.

**Example 4 (level 5, statements).** Statement I: Ethylene promotes rapid internode elongation in deep-water rice. Statement II: ABA promotes seed germination. *Solution.* I is true (so leaves stay above the water). ABA inhibits germination, so II is false. **I correct, II incorrect.**

<div class="myth">

**"Gibberellins and auxins both cause bolting."** Bolting in rosette plants such as cabbage and beet is a gibberellin effect. Auxin's best-known jobs are rooting and apical dominance.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-biology" data-topics="plant-growth" data-mode="practice" data-exam="neet"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Arithmetic: straight line; geometric: J then S in nature; doubling time = 0.693 / r.
- Relative rate = gain ÷ (starting size × time).
- Auxin: rooting, apical dominance, parthenocarpy, 2,4-D; GA: length, malting, bolting, sugarcane.
- Cytokinin: division, delay senescence, overcome apical dominance; ethylene: ripening, abscission, cucumber female flowers.
- ABA: stomatal closure, dormancy, stress; antagonist of GA.

</div>
