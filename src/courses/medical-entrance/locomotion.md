**Biology · Class 11 · about 34 minutes.** By the end you will be able to describe a muscle fibre down to its proteins, explain contraction as sliding filaments step by step, work out band lengths in a sarcomere, count the bones of any part of the skeleton, and name each joint and disorder.

<div class="bigidea">A muscle shortens because millions of tiny myosin heads pull thin actin filaments inward, a few nanometres per stroke, powered by ATP and switched on by calcium. Bones and joints turn that pull into movement.</div>

## 1. Kinds of movement

- **Amoeboid:** by pseudopodia formed by streaming protoplasm, using microfilaments (*Amoeba*; in humans, macrophages and white blood cells).
- **Ciliary:** cilia beat in a coordinated way (they clear dust and mucus from the trachea; they move the egg along the female reproductive tract).
- **Muscular:** limbs, jaws and tongue in humans and most animals.

*Paramecium* uses cilia both to feed and to swim; *Hydra* uses its tentacles to catch prey and to move. **Locomotion** means moving from place to place; every locomotion is a movement, but not every movement is locomotion.

## 2. Muscle

Muscle is mesodermal and makes up 40 to 50% of an adult's body weight. Its properties are excitability, contractility, extensibility and elasticity. **Skeletal** muscle is striated and voluntary; **visceral (smooth)** muscle, in the walls of hollow organs such as the gut and reproductive tract, is unstriated and involuntary; **cardiac** muscle is striated, branched and involuntary.

**Structure of a skeletal muscle:** the muscle is made of bundles (**fascicles**) held together by a collagen layer, the **fascia**. Each **muscle fibre** is a single long cell with many nuclei (a **syncytium**). Its membrane is the **sarcolemma**, its cytoplasm the **sarcoplasm**, and its endoplasmic reticulum the **sarcoplasmic reticulum**, a store of **Ca²⁺**. The fibre is packed with **myofibrils**, which show alternate light and dark bands:

- **I band** (light, isotropic): only thin **actin** filaments; bisected by the **Z line**, to which the actin is fixed.
- **A band** (dark, anisotropic): the thick **myosin** filaments, held together in the middle by the **M line**.
- **H zone:** the central part of the A band that thin filaments do not reach at rest.
- **Sarcomere:** the stretch between two Z lines, the unit of contraction.

**Contractile proteins.** Each thin filament is two **F-actin** strands wound round each other, each a polymer of **G-actin** units, with two strands of **tropomyosin** along it and **troponin** at intervals. At rest, troponin covers the sites on actin where myosin would bind. Each thick filament is a polymer of **meromyosins**: each has a globular head with a short arm (**heavy meromyosin**) and a tail (**light meromyosin**). The heads stick out as **cross arms**; each head is an **ATPase** with binding sites for ATP and for actin.

## 3. The sliding filament theory

Drag the slider to contract the sarcomere and watch which bands change.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="sarcomere-slide"><figcaption>Thin actin filaments (from the Z lines) slide over thick myosin filaments. The A band stays 1.6 µm; the I band and H zone shrink, and the H zone can vanish.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Signal.</b> A motor neuron carries a signal from the CNS. A <b>motor unit</b> is one motor neuron and all the fibres it supplies. At the <b>neuromuscular junction</b> (motor end plate), it releases <b>acetylcholine</b>, which starts an action potential in the sarcolemma.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Calcium release.</b> The action potential spreads through the fibre and makes the sarcoplasmic reticulum release <b>Ca²⁺</b> into the sarcoplasm.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Sites uncovered.</b> Ca²⁺ binds a subunit of <b>troponin</b>; the masking of the myosin-binding sites on actin is removed.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Cross bridge and power stroke.</b> The myosin head, energised by splitting ATP (ADP and Pi stay bound), binds actin to form a <b>cross bridge</b> and pulls the actin towards the centre of the A band. The Z lines move in; the sarcomere shortens.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Release and repeat.</b> The head lets go of ADP and Pi; a new ATP binds and the cross bridge breaks; the ATP is split and the head re-cocks. The cycle repeats while Ca²⁺ is present.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Relaxation.</b> Ca²⁺ is pumped back into the sarcoplasmic reticulum, the actin sites are covered again, and the Z lines return to their resting place.</div>
</div>

**Fatigue:** repeated contraction without enough oxygen breaks glycogen down anaerobically, and **lactic acid** builds up. **Red fibres** have much **myoglobin** (a red, oxygen-storing pigment) and many mitochondria: they work aerobically. **White fibres** have little myoglobin, few mitochondria and a lot of sarcoplasmic reticulum: they work anaerobically.

<div class="myth">

**"Actin and myosin filaments shorten during contraction."** Neither filament changes length. They slide past each other, which is why the A band (the myosin length) stays the same.

<div class="step"><b>Ca²⁺ arrives.</b> Calcium binds troponin and the sites are uncovered.</div>

## 4. The skeleton: 206 bones

**Axial skeleton (80 bones).**
- **Skull (22):** 8 cranial and 14 facial bones, plus the U-shaped **hyoid** at the base of the mouth and three **ear ossicles** in each middle ear (malleus, incus, stapes). The skull meets the first vertebra through two **occipital condyles** (it is dicondylic).
- **Vertebral column (26):** 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 1 sacral (fused) and 1 coccygeal (fused). The first vertebra is the **atlas**. Almost all mammals, including humans, have 7 cervical vertebrae.
- **Sternum (1)** and **ribs (12 pairs):** pairs 1 to 7 are **true ribs** (joined to the sternum by hyaline cartilage); pairs 8 to 10 are **vertebrochondral (false) ribs**, joined to the 7th rib's cartilage; pairs 11 and 12 are **floating ribs**. Thoracic vertebrae, ribs and sternum make the rib cage.

**Appendicular skeleton (126 bones).**
- **Each fore limb (30):** humerus, radius, ulna, 8 carpals, 5 metacarpals, 14 phalanges.
- **Each hind limb (30):** femur (the longest bone), tibia, fibula, 7 tarsals, 5 metatarsals, 14 phalanges and the **patella** (knee cap).
- **Pectoral girdle:** on each side a **clavicle** (collar bone) and a **scapula** (a flat triangular bone over the 2nd to 7th ribs, with the acromion and the **glenoid cavity** for the head of the humerus).
- **Pelvic girdle:** two **coxal** bones, each formed by the fused ilium, ischium and pubis; the **acetabulum** holds the head of the femur; the two pubic bones meet at the **pubic symphysis**.

## 5. Joints and disorders

| Joint | Movement | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Fibrous | none | sutures between flat skull bones |
| Cartilaginous | limited | between adjacent vertebrae |
| Synovial: ball and socket | in all directions | humerus with the pectoral girdle |
| Synovial: hinge | one plane | knee |
| Synovial: pivot | rotation | atlas and axis |
| Synovial: gliding | sliding | between carpals |
| Synovial: saddle | two planes | carpal and metacarpal of the thumb |

Synovial joints have a fluid-filled synovial cavity between the bones.

**Disorders.** **Myasthenia gravis:** an autoimmune disorder of the neuromuscular junction, causing fatigue, weakness and paralysis of skeletal muscle. **Muscular dystrophy:** progressive breakdown of skeletal muscle, mostly genetic. **Tetany:** rapid spasms from low Ca²⁺ in body fluids. **Arthritis:** inflamed joints. **Osteoporosis:** loss of bone mass with age, so bones break easily; low oestrogen is a common cause. **Gout:** inflamed joints from uric acid crystals.

<div class="keypoints">

- Amoeboid (macrophages, leucocytes), ciliary (trachea, oviduct), muscular movement.
- Fascicles in fascia; fibre is a syncytium; sarcoplasmic reticulum stores Ca²⁺.
- I band actin, A band myosin, Z line bisects I, M line bisects A; sarcomere Z to Z.
- Actin: F-actin of G-actin units, tropomyosin, troponin; myosin: meromyosins with ATPase heads (HMM) and tails (LMM).
- Acetylcholine → action potential → Ca²⁺ → troponin → cross bridge with ATP → actin pulled in; A band constant, I and H shrink.
- Red fibres (myoglobin, aerobic) vs white fibres (anaerobic); lactic acid causes fatigue.
- 206 bones: axial 80 (skull 22 + hyoid + 6 ossicles, 26 vertebrae, sternum, 24 ribs), appendicular 126.
- Ribs: 7 true, 3 vertebrochondral, 2 floating pairs; femur longest; coxal = ilium + ischium + pubis.
- Joints: fibrous, cartilaginous, synovial (ball and socket, hinge, pivot, gliding, saddle); myasthenia gravis, tetany, gout, osteoporosis.

</div>
