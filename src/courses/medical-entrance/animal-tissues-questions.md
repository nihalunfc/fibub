**Biology · Class 11 · about 20 minutes.** Read [Structural organisation in animals](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/animal-tissues/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">Tissue questions pair a tissue with a place or a job. NEET's favourite trick is a real tissue in the wrong place. Learn each tissue with its exact locations as one unit.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Epithelium and location.</b> <i>Method:</i> shape follows job (thin for diffusion, tall for absorption, many layers for protection). <i>Trap:</i> the PCT is cuboidal with microvilli, not columnar; bronchioles are ciliated.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Glands.</b> Unicellular (goblet) vs multicellular (salivary); exocrine (ducts) vs endocrine (hormones, no ducts). <i>Trap:</i> earwax, oil, milk and digestive enzymes are exocrine products.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Junctions.</b> Tight (seal), adhering (cement), gap (cytoplasm link). <i>Trap:</i> only gap junctions let molecules pass between cells.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Connective tissue from a description.</b> Fibres parallel or random, matrix soft or hard. <i>Trap:</i> skin is dense irregular; tendons and ligaments are dense regular.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Cartilage vs bone.</b> Pliable vs hard; chondrocytes vs osteocytes, both in lacunae; marrow makes blood cells.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Muscle types.</b> Striations, branching, nuclei, control, location. <i>Trap:</i> cardiac is striated but involuntary.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Neural tissue.</b> Neurons excitable; neuroglia over half the volume.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Statements and matching.</b> Four tissues, four places; start with the surest pair.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, how many are correct).** How many pairs are right: squamous, alveoli; cuboidal, PCT; columnar, bronchioles; compound, pharynx; ciliated, fallopian tube? *Solution.* Alveoli ✓, PCT ✓, bronchioles ✗ (ciliated), pharynx ✓, fallopian tube ✓. **Four.**

**Example 2 (level 4, assertion-reason).** A: Cardiac muscle cells contract as a unit. R: Intercalated discs contain gap junctions that pass the signal quickly from cell to cell. *Solution.* Both true, and R explains A. **Both true; R explains A.**

**Example 3 (level 5, match the lists).** List I: tendon, adipose, cartilage, areolar. List II: stores fat, supports epithelium under skin, resists compression between vertebrae, parallel collagen bundles. *Solution.* Tendon: parallel collagen. Adipose: fat. Cartilage: between vertebrae. Areolar: supports epithelium.

**Example 4 (level 5, statements).** Statement I: All connective tissues secrete collagen or elastin fibres. Statement II: Blood is a fluid connective tissue. *Solution.* Blood cells do not secrete fibres, so I ("all") is false. II is true. **I incorrect, II correct.**

<div class="myth">

**"All gland ducts are lined by cuboidal cells."** Many are, but the inner lining of salivary and pancreatic ducts is compound epithelium. Watch for the words "inner lining".

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-biology" data-topics="animal-tissues" data-mode="practice" data-exam="neet"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Squamous: vessels, alveoli; cuboidal: ducts, PCT; columnar: stomach, intestine; ciliated: bronchioles, fallopian tubes; compound: skin, mouth, pharynx, inner salivary and pancreatic ducts.
- Goblet cell = unicellular gland; endocrine glands have no ducts.
- Gap junctions alone let molecules cross between cells.
- Dense regular: tendons, ligaments; dense irregular: skin.
- Cardiac: striated, branched, involuntary, intercalated discs.
