**Start here · about 11 minutes.** By the end you will have a month-by-month NEET plan and a weekly routine.

<div class="bigidea">Plan backwards from the exam: learn each chapter once properly, revise NCERT and mixed questions again and again, then practise full papers. Give Physics daily number practice and Biology daily recall.</div>

## 1. Plan backwards from the exam

<figure class="fig" data-fig="study-plan" data-chapters="81"><figcaption>About 81 chapters across Physics, Chemistry and Biology for NEET. Mock tests take about 15% of the time and revision about 25%.</figcaption></figure>

## 2. Balance the three subjects every day

| Subject | Daily habit |
|---|---|
| Biology | Read one NCERT section, then recall it with 30 to 40 questions; revise an older chapter for 15 minutes |
| Physics | 15 to 25 numerical questions on the current chapter |
| Chemistry | Alternate: physical (numbers), organic (reactions and mechanisms), inorganic (trends and facts) |

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><b>Class 11 months.</b> Biology: diversity and structure (living world, plant and animal kingdoms, morphology, anatomy, cell). Physics: units to laws of motion. Chemistry: moles, atomic structure, periodic table, bonding.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Middle of Class 11.</b> Biology: cell cycle, biomolecules, plant physiology. Physics: energy, rotation, gravitation, properties of matter. Chemistry: thermodynamics, equilibrium, organic basics.</div>
<div class="step"><b>End of Class 11.</b> Biology: human physiology (breathing, circulation, excretion, movement, nerves, hormones). Physics: heat, oscillations, waves. Chemistry: hydrocarbons, redox.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Class 12.</b> The same pattern for the Class 12 chapters (genetics and biotechnology need special care), with one day a week revising Class 11.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Last months.</b> NCERT Biology line-by-line revision, weightage-first Physics and Chemistry revision, and two or three full mocks a week with analysis.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"I'll read NCERT many times and do questions at the end."** Rereading feels productive but fades fast. Questions after every section, and coming back after a day, a week and a month, is what makes NCERT lines stick.

</div>

## 3. A week that works around school

| Day | What to do (3 to 4 hours outside school) |
|---|---|
| Mon to Fri | One Biology section with questions, one Physics or Chemistry topic with questions, 15 minutes of spaced revision |
| Saturday | A timed mixed set of 45 to 90 questions, then a mistakes review |
| Sunday | Revise chapters from 1 week and 1 month ago with questions; rest |

<div class="meaning">
<dl>
<dt>Learn</dt><dd>lesson, NCERT, notes and level 1 to 3 questions</dd>
<dt>Revise</dt><dd>mixed questions at growing gaps</dd>
<dt>Mock</dt><dd>a full 180-question paper in 3 hours on an OMR-style sheet, then analysis</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>You remember what you practise recalling. A good plan is a schedule of recalls.</dd>
</dl>
</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Split the months: about 60% learning, 25% revision, 15% mock tests.
- Every day: Biology recall with questions, Physics numericals, one area of Chemistry.
- Recall NCERT through questions and revisit each chapter after a day, a week and a month.
- Practise full papers on an OMR-style sheet in the last months and analyse each one.

</div>
