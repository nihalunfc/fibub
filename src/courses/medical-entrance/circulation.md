**Biology · Class 11 · about 36 minutes.** By the end you will be able to list the parts of blood with their numbers and jobs, solve any transfusion and Rh question, follow a beat through the nodal tissue, match each part of the cardiac cycle to its ECG wave and heart sound, and calculate cardiac output.

<div class="bigidea">Blood is a moving tissue that carries gases, food, wastes and defenders. The heart is a double pump that keeps oxygen-rich and oxygen-poor blood apart, and it beats on its own: a small patch of nodal tissue sets the rhythm, and nerves only speed it up or slow it down.</div>

## 1. Blood

**Plasma** (about 55% of blood) is 90 to 92% water and 6 to 8% proteins: **fibrinogen** (clotting), **globulins** (defence) and **albumins** (osmotic balance). It also carries minerals, glucose, amino acids and lipids. Plasma without clotting factors is **serum**.

**Formed elements** (about 45%):

| Cell | Count | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Red blood cells (erythrocytes) | 5 to 5.5 million per mm³ | made in red bone marrow; biconcave; no nucleus in most mammals; haemoglobin 12 to 16 g per 100 mL; live about 120 days; destroyed in the spleen |
| White blood cells (leucocytes) | 6000 to 8000 per mm³ | nucleated; short-lived |
| Platelets (thrombocytes) | 1,50,000 to 3,50,000 per mm³ | cell fragments from megakaryocytes; clotting |

**White cells:** **granulocytes** are neutrophils (60 to 65%, the most numerous, phagocytic), eosinophils (2 to 3%, infections and allergy) and basophils (fewest; release histamine, serotonin and heparin in inflammation). **Agranulocytes** are lymphocytes (20 to 25%, B and T cells, immunity) and monocytes (6 to 8%, phagocytic).

## 2. Blood groups

| Group | Antigen on red cells | Antibody in plasma | Can receive from |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | A | anti-B | A, O |
| B | B | anti-A | B, O |
| AB | A and B | none | A, B, AB, O (universal recipient) |
| O | none | anti-A and anti-B | O only (O is the universal donor) |

**Rh factor:** an antigen first found in Rhesus monkeys, present in about 80% of people (Rh⁺). An Rh⁻ person exposed to Rh⁺ blood makes anti-Rh antibodies. **Erythroblastosis foetalis:** an Rh⁻ mother carrying an Rh⁺ baby is exposed to the baby's blood mainly at the first delivery. In a later Rh⁺ pregnancy her antibodies cross the placenta and destroy the baby's red cells, causing anaemia and jaundice. Giving the mother anti-Rh antibodies right after the first delivery prevents it.

<div class="myth">

**"Group O blood has no antibodies."** O cells have no antigens, but O plasma has both anti-A and anti-B. That is why O people can give red cells to anyone but receive only O.

</div>

## 3. Clotting and lymph

**Coagulation:** at an injury, platelets and damaged tissue release factors that start a cascade forming the **thrombokinase** complex. It converts inactive **prothrombin** into **thrombin**, and thrombin converts soluble **fibrinogen** into insoluble **fibrin** threads that trap cells into a clot. **Calcium ions** are needed throughout.

**Lymph:** as blood passes through capillaries, water and small solutes leak out as **tissue (interstitial) fluid**, with the same minerals as plasma but without large proteins and most cells. **Lymphatic vessels** collect it and return it to major veins. Lymph is colourless, carries lymphocytes for immunity, and absorbs fats through the **lacteals** of the intestinal villi.

## 4. Circulatory systems and the human heart

**Open** circulation (arthropods, molluscs): blood flows into open spaces. **Closed** (annelids, chordates): blood stays in vessels. Fishes have a **2-chambered** heart and single circulation; amphibians and most reptiles a **3-chambered** heart (two atria, one ventricle, so blood mixes: incomplete double circulation); crocodiles, birds and mammals a **4-chambered** heart and complete **double circulation**.

**The human heart** develops from mesoderm, lies between the lungs tilted slightly left, is about the size of a clenched fist and sits in a double **pericardium** with pericardial fluid. Two thin-walled **atria** receive blood; two thick-walled **ventricles** pump it. The **tricuspid** valve guards the right atrioventricular opening, the **bicuspid (mitral)** valve the left one, and **semilunar** valves the openings of the pulmonary artery and aorta. Valves let blood go one way only.

**Nodal tissue** makes the heart **auto-excitable**. The **SA node** (upper right corner of the right atrium) fires 70 to 75 times a minute and is the **pacemaker**. The signal spreads over the atria to the **AV node** (lower left corner of the right atrium, near the AV septum), then through the **AV bundle (bundle of His)** at the top of the interventricular septum, its right and left branches, and the **Purkinje fibres** in the ventricle walls.

## 5. Cardiac cycle and ECG

Move through one beat. Read where each wave falls and when each valve shuts.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="cardiac-cycle"><figcaption>One beat lasts 0.8 s at 72 beats a minute: atrial systole 0.1 s, ventricular systole 0.3 s, joint diastole 0.4 s. "Lub" is the AV valves closing; "dub" is the semilunar valves closing.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>Joint diastole.</b> All four chambers relaxed. AV valves open, semilunar valves closed. Blood flows from the veins through the atria into the ventricles; most filling happens now.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Atrial systole.</b> The SA node fires (P wave). The atria contract and add about 30% more blood to the ventricles.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Ventricular systole.</b> The signal passes through the AV node and bundle of His (QRS complex). The ventricles contract; the AV valves close (<b>first sound, lub</b>); pressure rises until the semilunar valves open and blood leaves through the pulmonary artery and aorta.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Back to diastole.</b> The ventricles relax (end of the T wave). Pressure falls, the semilunar valves close (<b>second sound, dub</b>), the AV valves open and filling starts again.</div>
</div>

Each ventricle pumps about **70 mL** per beat (**stroke volume**). **Cardiac output** = stroke volume × heart rate ≈ 70 × 72 ≈ **5 L per minute**; athletes have a much larger output.

**ECG** (electrocardiograph): the **P wave** is atrial depolarisation (atrial contraction follows); the **QRS complex** is ventricular depolarisation (ventricular contraction follows); the **T wave** is ventricular repolarisation (its end marks the end of systole). Counting QRS complexes in a time gives the heart rate.

## 6. Double circulation, regulation and disorders

**Pulmonary circulation:** right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary veins → left atrium. **Systemic circulation:** left ventricle → aorta → body → venae cavae → right atrium. The **hepatic portal vein** carries blood from the intestine to the liver before it returns to the systemic veins. The **coronary** vessels supply the heart muscle itself.

**Regulation.** The heart's rhythm is intrinsic (myogenic). A centre in the **medulla oblongata** adjusts it through autonomic nerves: **sympathetic** nerves raise the heart rate and the strength of contraction (more output); **parasympathetic** nerves lower the rate and slow conduction. Hormones of the adrenal medulla also raise output.

**Disorders.**
- **Hypertension:** blood pressure persistently at or above 140/90 mm Hg (normal about 120/80); it harms the heart, brain and kidneys.
- **Coronary artery disease (atherosclerosis):** deposits of calcium, fat, cholesterol and fibrous tissue narrow the coronary arteries.
- **Angina pectoris:** sudden chest pain when heart muscle gets too little oxygen.
- **Heart failure:** the heart cannot pump enough blood; lung congestion is a main sign (congestive heart failure). It is not the same as **cardiac arrest** (the heart stops) or a **heart attack** (heart muscle suddenly damaged by a blocked blood supply).
