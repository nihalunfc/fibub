**Biology · Class 11 · about 24 minutes.** Read [Locomotion and movement](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/locomotion/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">This chapter has two halves: the sarcomere (a picture and a sequence) and the skeleton (a set of counts). Draw the sarcomere and write the 206 breakdown once, and the questions turn into reading and adding.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Types of movement.</b> Amoeboid, ciliary, muscular with examples. <i>Trap:</i> macrophages and leucocytes show amoeboid movement.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Fibre structure.</b> Fascicle, fascia, sarcolemma, sarcoplasm, sarcoplasmic reticulum, syncytium. <i>Trap:</i> calcium is stored in the sarcoplasmic reticulum, not the mitochondria.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Bands.</b> Which band has which filament, which line bisects which band, which change in contraction. <i>Trap:</i> I = isotropic, light, actin only; A = anisotropic, dark, myosin.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Sarcomere numbers.</b> <i>Method:</i> A band = myosin length (constant); I band = sarcomere − A; H zone = sarcomere − 2 × actin reach.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Contractile proteins.</b> F-actin, G-actin, tropomyosin, troponin; heavy and light meromyosin; which part is the ATPase.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Sequence of contraction.</b> Acetylcholine → action potential → Ca²⁺ → troponin → cross bridge → sliding → Ca²⁺ pumped back. <i>Trap:</i> ATP is needed both for the stroke and for breaking the cross bridge.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Red vs white fibres.</b> Myoglobin, mitochondria, sarcoplasmic reticulum, aerobic or anaerobic.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Bone counts.</b> Skull 22, vertebrae 26, ribs 24, limbs 30 each, girdles 4 + 2; 206 in all. <i>Trap:</i> the hyoid and ossicles are counted separately from the 22 skull bones.</div>
<div class="step"><b>9. Joints and disorders.</b> Joint type to example; disease to cause.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, sarcomere).** A sarcomere shortens from 2.6 µm to 2.2 µm. The A band is 1.6 µm and actin reaches 1.0 µm from each Z line. Find the new I band and H zone. *Solution.* I band = 2.2 − 1.6 = **0.6 µm** (was 1.0). H zone = 2.2 − 2 × 1.0 = **0.2 µm** (was 0.6). A band: still 1.6 µm.

**Example 2 (level 4, counts).** How many bones are in both hands (wrist, palm and fingers only)? *Solution.* One hand = 8 carpals + 5 metacarpals + 14 phalanges = 27; two hands = **54**.

**Example 3 (level 5, assertion-reason).** A: Red muscle fibres can work for a long time without tiring. R: They have plenty of myoglobin and mitochondria and make ATP aerobically. *Solution.* Both true; R explains A (aerobic respiration avoids lactic acid build-up). **Both true; R explains A.**

**Example 4 (level 5, statements).** Statement I: In a relaxed muscle, troponin masks the myosin-binding sites on actin. Statement II: During contraction the length of the A band decreases. *Solution.* I is true. The A band stays the same, so II is false. **I correct, II incorrect.**

<div class="myth">

**"Floating ribs are attached to nothing."** They are attached to the vertebral column at the back; they just do not reach the sternum at the front.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- A band constant; I band and H zone shrink; Z line bisects I, M line bisects A.
- Ca²⁺ from the sarcoplasmic reticulum binds troponin; myosin heads are ATPases.
- Red fibres: myoglobin, aerobic; white fibres: anaerobic.
- 206 bones: axial 80, appendicular 126; ribs 7 true, 3 false, 2 floating pairs.
- Pivot (atlas-axis), hinge (knee), saddle (thumb), gliding (carpals), ball and socket (shoulder).
- Myasthenia gravis autoimmune; tetany low Ca²⁺; gout uric acid; osteoporosis low oestrogen.
