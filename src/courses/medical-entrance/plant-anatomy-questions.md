**Biology · Class 11 · about 22 minutes.** Read [Anatomy of flowering plants](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/plant-anatomy/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">Anatomy questions are about three pairs of words (endarch or exarch, radial or conjoint, open or closed), three tissue systems and one growth story. If you can place a feature on one of six cross-sections, you can answer almost anything here.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Tissue features.</b> Walls, living or dead, job. <i>Trap:</i> collenchyma is living with uneven cellulose-pectin thickening; sclerenchyma is dead and lignified.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Xylem and phloem parts.</b> Which are living, which mark angiosperms. <i>Trap:</i> xylem parenchyma (living), phloem fibres (bast); gymnosperms lack vessels and companion cells.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Bundle types.</b> Radial, conjoint, open, closed, endarch, exarch. <i>Trap:</i> roots are exarch and radial; stems endarch and conjoint.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Identify the section.</b> A list of features names one of six sections. <i>Method:</i> scattered bundles → monocot stem; polyarch with big pith → monocot root; ring of open bundles → dicot stem; 2 to 4 xylem patches → dicot root.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Leaves.</b> Dorsiventral vs isobilateral, where stomata are, bulliform cells, palisade position.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Secondary growth sequence.</b> Order the events, or name where a cambium comes from. <i>Trap:</i> interfascicular cambium from medullary rays; root cambium from tissue under the phloem and pericycle.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Wood and bark.</b> Spring vs autumn wood, heartwood vs sapwood, periderm vs bark, lenticels. <i>Trap:</i> bark includes secondary phloem; periderm does not.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Meristems.</b> Apical, intercalary, lateral with examples. <i>Trap:</i> intercalary meristems are primary meristems, though they sit between permanent tissues.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, identify the section).** A section shows a sclerenchyma hypodermis, many vascular bundles each with a sclerenchyma sheath, smaller bundles near the outside and water-filled cavities. *Solution.* Scattered sheathed bundles mark a **monocot stem**. The bundles are conjoint and closed.

**Example 2 (level 4, assertion-reason).** A: Monocot stems usually do not show secondary growth. R: Their vascular bundles are closed. *Solution.* Both true; with no cambium in the bundles there is nothing to add secondary tissue. **Both true; R explains A.**

**Example 3 (level 5, how many are correct).** How many of these lie outside the vascular cambium in an old dicot stem: secondary phloem, phellogen, heartwood, phelloderm, cork? *Solution.* Secondary phloem ✓, phellogen ✓, heartwood ✗ (inside, in the wood), phelloderm ✓, cork ✓. **Four**, and all four are part of the bark.

**Example 4 (level 5, statements).** Statement I: Spring wood has fewer and narrower vessels than autumn wood. Statement II: An annual ring is one layer of spring wood plus one of autumn wood. *Solution.* Spring wood has more and wider vessels, so I is false. II is true. **I incorrect, II correct.**

<div class="myth">

**"Bark and periderm are the same."** Periderm is cork, cork cambium and secondary cortex. Bark is every tissue outside the vascular cambium, so it also includes the secondary phloem.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-biology" data-topics="plant-anatomy" data-mode="practice" data-exam="neet"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Living: parenchyma, collenchyma, xylem parenchyma, sieve tubes (no nucleus), companion cells; dead: sclerenchyma, tracheids, vessels, fibres.
- Roots radial and exarch; stems conjoint and endarch; dicot stem open, monocot closed.
- Monocot stem: scattered sheathed bundles; monocot root: polyarch with large pith.
- Isobilateral monocot leaf with bulliform cells; dorsiventral dicot leaf with palisade above.
- Interfascicular cambium from medullary rays; bark includes secondary phloem; heartwood does not conduct.
