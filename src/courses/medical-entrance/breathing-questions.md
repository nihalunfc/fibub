**Biology · Class 11 · about 24 minutes.** Read [Breathing and exchange of gases](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/breathing/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">Breathing questions rest on three tables: the four volumes, the partial pressure table and the transport percentages. Know them cold and the numeric questions become additions, while the statement questions become look-ups.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Respiratory organs across animals.</b> Body surface, skin, tracheae, gills, lungs. <i>Trap:</i> earthworms breathe through moist skin; insects through tracheae, not lungs.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. The airway.</b> Order of parts, epiglottis, incomplete rings, 5th thoracic vertebra, pleura, conducting vs exchange part.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Mechanism.</b> Which muscles contract, which way pressure goes. <i>Trap:</i> in inspiration the diaphragm contracts and flattens; in quiet expiration nothing contracts.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Volumes and capacities.</b> <i>Method:</i> draw the four stacked boxes (RV, ERV, TV, IRV) and read capacities as runs of boxes. <i>Trap:</i> EC (TV + ERV) vs FRC (ERV + RV).</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Partial pressures.</b> The 5 × 2 table. <i>Trap:</i> oxygenated blood is 95, not 104; tissue pCO₂ is 45.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Transport percentages.</b> O₂: 97 and 3; CO₂: 70, 20 to 25 and 7. 5 mL O₂ and 4 mL CO₂ per 100 mL blood.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Dissociation curve.</b> Shape (sigmoid), which factors shift it which way. <i>Method:</i> "tissue conditions" (high CO₂, H⁺, heat) move it right.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Regulation and disorders.</b> Medulla, pons, chemosensitive area, aortic and carotid receptors; asthma, emphysema, fibrosis. <i>Trap:</i> O₂ plays only a small part in regulation.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, capacities).** A person has VC = 4200 mL, TV = 500 mL and ERV = 1100 mL. Find IRV and IC. *Solution.* IRV = VC − TV − ERV = 4200 − 500 − 1100 = **2600 mL**. IC = TV + IRV = **3100 mL**.

**Example 2 (level 4, assertion-reason).** A: CO₂ diffuses across the alveolar membrane as readily as O₂ although its pressure gradient is much smaller. R: CO₂ is 20 to 25 times more soluble than O₂. *Solution.* Both true, and R explains A. **Both true; R explains A.**

**Example 3 (level 5, how many are correct).** How many favour the release of O₂ from oxyhaemoglobin: high pCO₂, low pO₂, high pH, higher temperature, high H⁺? *Solution.* High pCO₂ ✓, low pO₂ ✓, high pH ✗ (means fewer H⁺), higher temperature ✓, high H⁺ ✓. **Four.**

**Example 4 (level 5, statements).** Statement I: About 70% of CO₂ is carried as carbamino-haemoglobin. Statement II: Carbonic anhydrase is present in high concentration in red blood cells. *Solution.* 70% is bicarbonate; carbamino-haemoglobin is 20 to 25%, so I is false. II is true. **I incorrect, II correct.**

<div class="myth">

**"Emphysema is an infection."** It is long-term damage to alveolar walls, which shrinks the exchange surface; smoking is the main cause. Asthma is inflammation of the bronchi and bronchioles.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-biology" data-topics="breathing" data-mode="practice" data-exam="neet"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Inspiration: diaphragm and external intercostals contract; quiet expiration is passive.
- IC = TV + IRV; EC = TV + ERV; FRC = ERV + RV; VC = ERV + TV + IRV; TLC = VC + RV.
- pO₂ 159 / 104 / 40 / 95 / 40; pCO₂ 0.3 / 40 / 45 / 40 / 45.
- O₂ 97% on haemoglobin; CO₂ 70% bicarbonate, 20 to 25% carbamino, 7% dissolved.
- Right shift: high pCO₂, high H⁺, higher temperature.
- Rhythm centre in medulla, pneumotaxic centre in pons; CO₂ and H⁺ drive breathing.

</div>
