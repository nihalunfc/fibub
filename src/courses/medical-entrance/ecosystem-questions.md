**Biology · Class 12 · about 20 minutes.** Read [Ecosystem](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/ecosystem/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">Ecosystem questions are of three kinds: energy sums (10% law, NPP = GPP − R), process order (decomposition steps), and shapes (which pyramid can be inverted). Get the direction right: energy always falls going up, and only numbers and biomass can flip.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. 10 per cent law.</b> <i>Method:</i> count steps between levels; multiply by 0.1 per step going up, by 10 going down. <i>Trap:</i> counting levels instead of steps (producers to secondary consumers is two steps).</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Productivity.</b> NPP = GPP − R; GPP = NPP + R. <i>Trap:</i> consumers get NPP, not GPP.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Global numbers.</b> 170 billion tons per year (biosphere), 55 billion tons (oceans), 70% (ocean area), PAR less than 50%, 2 to 10% captured.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Decomposition order.</b> Fragmentation, leaching, catabolism, humification, mineralisation. <i>Trap:</i> fragmentation is done by detritivores; catabolism by bacterial and fungal enzymes.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Rate factors.</b> Faster: nitrogen, sugars, warm, moist, aerobic. Slower: lignin, chitin, cold, anaerobic.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Food chains.</b> GFC starts with living producers, DFC with detritus. GFC dominant in water, DFC on land. Saprotrophs digest outside their bodies.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Pyramid shapes.</b> Number: inverted for a tree. Biomass: inverted in the sea. Energy: always upright.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Pyramid limits and terms.</b> No place for saprophytes, no food webs, no multi-level species. Standing crop = biomass at a level at one time.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, 10% law).** Tertiary consumers in a lake receive 15 J. How much energy did the phytoplankton fix? *Solution.* Three steps down: 15 × 10 × 10 × 10 = **15,000 J**.

**Example 2 (level 4, NPP).** GPP of a crop field is 12,000 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹, and 40% is used in respiration. What energy can herbivores use, at most? *Solution.* R = 4800; NPP = 12,000 − 4800 = **7200 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹**.

**Example 3 (level 3, assertion-reason).** A: An energy pyramid is never inverted. R: Energy is lost as heat at each trophic transfer. *Solution.* Both true; R explains A. **Option 1.**

**Example 4 (level 5, statements).** Statement I: In terrestrial ecosystems more energy flows through the grazing food chain than through the detritus food chain. Statement II: Humus is highly resistant to microbial action. *Solution.* I is false (DFC carries more energy on land); II is true. **I incorrect, II correct.**

<div class="myth">

**"Energy is recycled by decomposers like nutrients are."** Decomposers recycle nutrients. The energy they use ends up as heat; energy flows one way only.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-biology" data-topics="ecosystem" data-mode="practice" data-exam="neet"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Count steps, not levels, for the 10% law.
- NPP = GPP − R; consumers get NPP.
- Decomposition: fragmentation, leaching, catabolism, humification, mineralisation.
- Faster with nitrogen, sugars, warmth and moisture; slower with lignin, chitin, cold, no oxygen.
- Only number and biomass pyramids can be inverted; energy pyramids never.
