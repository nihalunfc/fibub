**Start here · about 12 minutes.** By the end you will have a method for every NEET format and know the trap in each.

<div class="bigidea">NEET asks the same Biology, Chemistry and Physics in a handful of formats. A fixed method for each format, and an eye for the one changed word, turns traps into marks.</div>

## 1. The formats, one by one

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>Single answer.</b> <i>Method:</i> read the stem twice; underline NOT, INCORRECT, EXCEPT. Answer before reading the options when you can. <i>Trap:</i> options that are true facts but answer a different question.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Assertion (A) and Reason (R).</b> <i>Method:</i> judge A alone, judge R alone, and only if both are true ask whether R explains A. <i>Trap:</i> two true statements that are unrelated.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Statement I and Statement II.</b> Options: both correct; I correct, II incorrect; I incorrect, II correct; both incorrect. <i>Method:</i> mark ✓ or ✗ against each before reading the options. <i>Trap:</i> one word changed from the NCERT line.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Match List I with List II.</b> <i>Method:</i> use the pair you are surest of to cross out options, then the next surest. <i>Trap:</i> two items that sound alike (for example, two hormones from the same gland).</div>
<div class="step"><b>How many are correct.</b> Five or so statements; the options are counts. <i>Method:</i> ✓ or ✗ each one and count. <i>Trap:</i> a statement that is "mostly" right is wrong.</div>
<div class="step"><b>NCERT-line and diagram questions.</b> A label, a table row or a sentence from NCERT, reworked. <i>Method:</i> picture the page and the diagram; recall the exact word. <i>Trap:</i> examples from the book swapped between groups (for example, which plant belongs to which family).</div>
<div class="step"><b>Numerical (Physics and physical chemistry).</b> <i>Method:</i> keep symbols, put numbers in at the end, check units and the size of the answer. <i>Trap:</i> options that match the common slips.</div>
</div>

## 2. The standard assertion-reason options

1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
3. A is true, but R is false.
4. A is false, but R is true.

<div class="myth">

**"If I recognise every word, the statement must be true."** NEET often keeps every familiar word and changes one: "all", "only", "never", or swaps two examples. Read each statement as if you had to defend it.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Underline NOT, INCORRECT and EXCEPT before answering.
- Assertion-reason: judge A, judge R, then ask whether R explains A.
- Statements and "how many are correct": mark each ✓ or ✗ first, then use the options.
- Match the lists: eliminate options with your surest pair.
- NCERT-line questions hinge on one changed word or swapped example.
