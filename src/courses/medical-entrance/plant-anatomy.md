**Biology · Class 11 · about 34 minutes.** By the end you will be able to tell every plant tissue by its walls and job, draw and label a cross-section of a dicot and monocot root, stem and leaf from memory, and explain how a tree trunk thickens year by year.

<div class="bigidea">A plant is built from a few tissues arranged in three systems: a skin (epidermal), a filling (ground) and pipes (vascular). Roots, stems and leaves, dicot or monocot, differ mostly in where the pipes sit and whether a cambium keeps adding to them.</div>

## 1. Meristems

Meristems are zones of cells that keep dividing. **Apical meristems** at the root and shoot tips make the plant longer (primary growth); cells left behind by the shoot apex form **axillary buds**. **Intercalary meristems** at the base of grass leaves and internodes let grasses regrow after grazing. **Lateral meristems** (vascular cambium and cork cambium) make the plant thicker (secondary growth). Cells made by meristems that stop dividing become **permanent tissues**.

## 2. Simple and complex tissues

| Tissue | Cells and wall | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Parenchyma | living, thin cellulose walls | storage, photosynthesis, secretion |
| Collenchyma | living, walls thickened at the corners with cellulose, hemicellulose and pectin | flexible support of young stems and petioles (dicots; usually absent in monocots) |
| Sclerenchyma | dead, thick lignified walls with pits; fibres (long) and sclereids (short) | support; sclereids make nut shells hard and guava, pear and sapota pulp gritty |

**Xylem** carries water and minerals: **tracheids** and **vessels** (dead, lignified; vessels are the mark of angiosperms, absent in most gymnosperms), **xylem fibres** (dead) and **xylem parenchyma** (the only living part). The first-formed **protoxylem** lies towards the centre in stems (**endarch**) and towards the outside in roots (**exarch**); **metaxylem** forms later.

**Phloem** carries food: **sieve tube elements** joined end to end by sieve plates (no nucleus when mature, run by the **companion cells** beside them), phloem parenchyma (absent in most monocots) and **phloem fibres** (bast; jute, flax and hemp are commercial fibres). Gymnosperms have sieve cells and albuminous cells instead of sieve tubes and companion cells.

## 3. Three tissue systems

- **Epidermal:** a single layer of epidermis, usually with a waxy **cuticle** (absent in roots). **Stomata** have two guard cells, kidney-shaped in dicots and dumb-bell-shaped in grasses, plus **subsidiary cells**; all together form the stomatal apparatus. Root hairs are single cells; stem hairs (trichomes) are usually many-celled.
- **Ground:** everything between the epidermis and the vascular bundles: cortex, pericycle, pith and medullary rays; in leaves, the mesophyll.
- **Vascular:** xylem and phloem in bundles. **Radial** bundles (xylem and phloem on different radii) are found in roots. **Conjoint** bundles (on the same radius, phloem usually outside) are found in stems and leaves. **Open** bundles have cambium between xylem and phloem (dicot stems); **closed** bundles have none (monocots).

## 4. Inside roots, stems and leaves

<div class="steps" data-ms="8500">
<div class="step"><b>Dicot root</b> (sunflower). Epiblema with root hairs, a wide parenchyma cortex, an <b>endodermis</b> with <b>Casparian strips</b> (bands of waterproof suberin), a pericycle (lateral roots and later cambium start here), 2 to 4 radial xylem patches (diarch to tetrarch), conjunctive tissue, a small or no pith.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Monocot root</b> (maize). Same plan, but more than 6 xylem bundles (polyarch) and a large pith. No secondary growth.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Dicot stem</b> (sunflower). Epidermis with cuticle and hairs, a collenchyma <b>hypodermis</b>, cortex, an endodermis full of starch (starch sheath), a pericycle with patches of sclerenchyma, a <b>ring</b> of conjoint, open, endarch bundles separated by medullary rays, and a large pith.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Monocot stem</b> (maize). A sclerenchyma hypodermis, many <b>scattered</b> bundles, each conjoint and closed and wrapped in a sclerenchyma bundle sheath; peripheral bundles smaller; water-filled cavities in the bundles; no phloem parenchyma; no clear cortex, endodermis or pith.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Dicot (dorsiventral) leaf.</b> More stomata on the lower (abaxial) epidermis. Mesophyll split into tall <b>palisade</b> cells on the upper side and loose <b>spongy</b> cells below. Veins with bundle sheaths; reticulate venation shows bundles of many sizes.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Monocot (isobilateral) leaf.</b> Stomata on both surfaces; mesophyll not split into palisade and spongy. Grasses have <b>bulliform cells</b> in the upper epidermis: large, empty cells that lose water and make the leaf roll up in dry weather. Parallel veins give bundles of similar size.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"The endodermis is part of the epidermis."** It is the innermost layer of the cortex (ground tissue). Its Casparian strips force water to pass through living cells before it reaches the xylem.

</div>

## 5. Secondary growth

In dicot stems, the cambium inside each bundle (**intrafascicular**) joins cambium that forms from medullary ray cells between bundles (**interfascicular**), making a complete ring. This **vascular cambium** cuts off secondary xylem inward and secondary phloem outward, more xylem than phloem. Primary and old secondary phloem get crushed, so wood piles up but the phloem stays thin. Move the slider to age the stem.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="stem-secondary-growth"><figcaption>Each year adds one annual ring of secondary xylem. The cambium is a thin ring between the wood and the phloem; the cork cambium works further out.</figcaption></figure>

- **Spring (early) wood:** made when the cambium is very active; many wide vessels, light colour, low density. **Autumn (late) wood:** fewer, narrower vessels, darker and denser. One spring plus one autumn layer is an **annual ring**, so counting rings gives the age of a tree.
- **Heartwood (duramen):** the central, dark, dead wood, filled with tannins, resins, oils and gums; hard and resistant to microbes, but it no longer conducts water. **Sapwood (alburnum):** the outer, lighter wood that still carries water.
- **Cork cambium (phellogen)** forms in the cortex. It makes **cork (phellem)** outward, with suberin that keeps water out, and **secondary cortex (phelloderm)** inward. Phellem + phellogen + phelloderm = **periderm**. **Bark** is everything outside the vascular cambium, including secondary phloem. **Lenticels** are lens-shaped openings in the bark that let gases in and out.
- **In dicot roots**, the vascular cambium is wholly secondary: it starts in tissue below the phloem and in the pericycle opposite the protoxylem. Gymnosperms thicken too; monocots usually do not.

**Worked example.** A trunk shows 18 dark-light pairs. *Answer:* about 18 years old; the light bands formed in spring and the dark ones in autumn.

<div class="keypoints">

- Apical meristems lengthen, lateral meristems thicken, intercalary meristems help grasses regrow.
- Collenchyma: corner thickenings, living; sclerenchyma: lignified, dead (fibres and sclereids).
- Xylem parenchyma is the only living xylem; vessels mark angiosperms; companion cells run sieve tubes.
- Stems endarch, roots exarch; roots radial bundles; dicot stem ring of open bundles; monocot stem scattered closed bundles.
- Dicot root 2 to 4 xylem bundles, monocot root more than 6 with large pith; Casparian strips in endodermis.
- Dorsiventral dicot leaf (palisade above, spongy below); isobilateral monocot leaf; bulliform cells in grasses.
- Vascular cambium: intra- plus interfascicular; spring and autumn wood make annual rings; heartwood dark and non-conducting.
- Periderm = phellem + phellogen + phelloderm; bark = all outside the vascular cambium; lenticels for gas exchange.

</div>
