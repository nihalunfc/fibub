**Biology · Class 12 · about 22 minutes.** Read [Evolution](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/evolution/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">Evolution questions are either a Hardy-Weinberg sum or a sorting job (homologous or analogous, which theory, which factor, which era). The sums always start from q² for the recessive phenotype; the sorting always asks "same origin or same job?" and "who said it?".</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Hardy-Weinberg from the recessive phenotype.</b> <i>Method:</i> affected fraction = q², so q = √(that); p = 1 − q; carriers = 2pq; dominant phenotype = p² + 2pq = 1 − q². <i>Trap:</i> using q = affected fraction without the square root.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Allele frequency from counts.</b> p = (2 × AA + Aa) ÷ (2 × total). <i>Trap:</i> dividing by people instead of alleles.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Homology or analogy.</b> <i>Method:</i> same origin (homology, divergent) or same function only (analogy, convergent)? <i>Trap:</i> sweet potato (root) and potato (stem) are analogous.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Selection examples.</b> Industrial melanism, resistance to DDT, herbicides and antibiotics. <i>Trap:</i> "the environment caused the variation".</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Theories and people.</b> Oparin-Haldane, Miller, Pasteur, Darwin, Wallace, Lamarck, de Vries (<i>Oenothera</i>, saltation), Hardy and Weinberg.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Factors changing allele frequency.</b> Gene flow, drift (founder effect), mutation, recombination, selection. <i>Trap:</i> random mating and large size keep equilibrium; they do not disturb it.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Types of selection from a graph.</b> Narrower peak at the mean: stabilising; shifted peak: directional; two peaks: disruptive.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Time line and human evolution.</b> Order the ancestors and match brain sizes (650 to 800, 900, 1400 cc) and dates.</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, Hardy-Weinberg).** In a town of 10,000 at equilibrium, 900 people have a recessive trait. How many carriers are there? *Solution.* q² = 900/10,000 = 0.09, q = 0.3, p = 0.7. 2pq = 0.42, so **4200 carriers**.

**Example 2 (level 5, two-step).** A population at equilibrium has 36% AA. What is the percentage of Aa? *Solution.* p² = 0.36, p = 0.6, q = 0.4, 2pq = 0.48. **48%.**

**Example 3 (level 4, assertion-reason).** A: Adaptive radiation of Australian marsupials and placental mammals produced similar-looking pairs. R: This similarity is an example of convergent evolution. *Solution.* Both true, and R explains A: similar habitats shaped two unrelated radiations alike. **Both true, R explains A.**

**Example 4 (level 5, trap).** Which is NOT a condition for Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium? (a) Large population; (b) Random mating; (c) No migration; (d) Natural selection favouring heterozygotes. *Solution.* **(d).** Any selection changes frequencies, so it breaks the equilibrium.

<div class="myth">

**"Evolution means progress towards a better or more complex form."** Selection favours what leaves more offspring in a particular environment. If the environment changes, the favoured form changes too; simple forms like bacteria are among the most successful living things.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-biology" data-topics="evolution" data-mode="practice" data-exam="neet"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Start Hardy-Weinberg from q², take the square root, then fill in p, p² and 2pq.
- Count alleles (2 per person) for allele frequencies.
- Homology: same origin (divergent); analogy: same job (convergent).
- Five forces: gene flow, drift, mutation, recombination, selection.
- Know who proposed what, and the human ancestors in order with brain sizes.

</div>
