**Biology · Class 12 · about 22 minutes.** Read [Human health and disease](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/health-disease/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">This chapter is matching: disease to pathogen, barrier to example, drug to plant, organ to job. NEET hides one wrong pair among right ones, or one wrong word in a true line. A clean table in your head beats long reading.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Pathogen to disease.</b> <i>Method:</i> sort by group: bacteria (typhoid, pneumonia), virus (common cold, AIDS), protozoa (malaria, amoebiasis), worms (ascariasis, filariasis), fungi (ringworm). <i>Trap:</i> <i>Haemophilus influenzae</i> causes pneumonia, not influenza.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Symptoms and tests.</b> Widal (typhoid), ELISA (HIV), grey-blue lips (pneumonia), mucus and blood clots in stools (amoebiasis), fever every 3 to 4 days (malaria).</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Malaria life cycle.</b> Sporozoites (enter) → liver → RBCs (haemozoin) → gametocytes → mosquito gut → salivary glands. <i>Trap:</i> sexual stage in the mosquito, so it is the definitive host.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Innate barriers.</b> Physical (skin, mucus), physiological (acid, saliva, tears), cellular (neutrophils, monocytes, NK cells, macrophages), cytokine (interferons).</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Active or passive.</b> Made by the body = active (infection, vaccine); given ready-made = passive (colostrum, placenta, antitoxin). <i>Trap:</i> colostrum gives IgA, the placenta IgG.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Antibodies and cells.</b> H₂L₂; IgE in allergy (mast cells, histamine); B cells humoral, T cells CMI and graft rejection.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Lymphoid organs.</b> Primary: bone marrow, thymus. Secondary: spleen, lymph nodes, tonsils, Peyer's patches, appendix, MALT (about 50%).</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. HIV and cancer.</b> Retrovirus; macrophage factory; helper T cells fall. Benign vs malignant; carcinogen types; α-interferon.</div>
<div class="step"><b>9. Drugs.</b> Source plant and action: poppy (opioids, receptors in CNS and gut), <i>Cannabis</i> (cardiovascular), <i>Erythroxylum</i> (dopamine), <i>Datura</i> and <i>Atropa</i> (hallucinogens), nicotine (adrenaline).</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, how many correct).** How many are protozoan diseases: malaria, typhoid, amoebiasis, ringworm, filariasis, common cold? *Solution.* Malaria ✓, amoebiasis ✓; typhoid (bacterium), ringworm (fungus), filariasis (worm), cold (virus). **Two.**

**Example 2 (level 4, statements).** Statement I: The thymus is large at birth and shrinks with age. Statement II: The spleen is a primary lymphoid organ. *Solution.* I true. The spleen is secondary (lymphocytes meet antigens there), so II false. **I correct, II incorrect.**

**Example 3 (level 3, assertion-reason).** A: An antitoxin given after a snake bite gives quick protection. R: It contains ready-made antibodies. *Solution.* Both true and R explains A (passive immunity). **Option 1.**

**Example 4 (level 5, trap).** Which is correct about HIV? (a) It is a DNA virus that infects B cells; (b) reverse transcriptase makes RNA from DNA; (c) infected macrophages keep producing virus; (d) it spreads by shaking hands. *Solution.* **(c).** HIV has RNA and targets helper T cells; reverse transcriptase makes DNA from RNA; it does not spread by touch.

<div class="myth">

**"Antibiotics treat the common cold."** The cold is caused by rhinoviruses, and antibiotics act only on bacteria. Misusing them helps resistant bacteria spread.

</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-biology" data-topics="health-disease" data-mode="practice" data-exam="neet"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Sort pathogens by group; <i>Haemophilus</i> causes pneumonia.
- Malaria: sexual stage in the mosquito gut; haemozoin causes the fever.
- Innate barriers: four kinds; interferons are the cytokine barrier.
- Passive: colostrum (IgA), placenta (IgG), antitoxins; active: infection and vaccines.
- Primary lymphoid organs are bone marrow and thymus only.
- HIV attacks helper T cells after building up in macrophages.

</div>
