**Biology · Class 11 · about 24 minutes.** Read [Biomolecules and enzymes](/learn/courses/medical-entrance/cell-biomolecules/) first. Here you meet every question type NEET builds from this chapter, then practise.

<div class="bigidea">Biomolecule questions mix three kinds of fact: building block and bond, examples (most abundant, secondary metabolites, purines), and enzyme behaviour on a graph. The numbers are small and always the same few: n − 1 bonds, 18 Da of water per bond, 2 and 3 hydrogen bonds, half of V<sub>max</sub> at K<sub>m</sub>.</div>

## The question types

<div class="steps" data-ms="8000">
<div class="step"><b>1. Acid-soluble vs insoluble.</b> <i>Trap:</i> lipids are small but come down in the insoluble fraction.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Primary vs secondary metabolites.</b> Learn the examples by group: alkaloids (morphine, codeine), toxins (abrin, ricin), lectins (concanavalin A), drugs (vinblastine, curcumin), pigments, essential oils, rubber and gums.</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Bonds and building blocks.</b> Peptide, glycosidic, phosphodiester, ester. <i>Trap:</i> the phosphodiester bond links sugars, not bases; bases pair by hydrogen bonds.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Amino acid and base types.</b> R groups (glycine H, alanine CH₃, serine CH₂OH), acidic, basic, aromatic; purines vs pyrimidines; nucleoside vs nucleotide.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Polysaccharides.</b> Iodine test, reducing end, inulin (fructose), chitin (amino sugars). <i>Trap:</i> cellulose gives no colour with iodine.</div>
<div class="step"><b>6. Protein structure and jobs.</b> Four levels, N- to C-terminal, haemoglobin chains, collagen vs RuBisCO, GLUT-4.</div>
<div class="step"><b>7. Numbers.</b> Peptide bonds and mass, DNA hydrogen bonds, turn length (10 bp, 3.4 nm). <i>Method:</i> bonds = residues − chains (linear); water lost = bonds.</div>
<div class="step"><b>8. Enzyme action and factors.</b> Activation energy, ES complex, temperature and pH optima, denaturing. <i>Trap:</i> low temperature inactivates reversibly; high temperature denatures.</div>
<div class="step"><b>9. Inhibition and kinetics.</b> <i>Method:</i> competitive changes K<sub>m</sub>, non-competitive changes V<sub>max</sub>. <i>Trap:</i> malonate is a competitive inhibitor of succinate dehydrogenase.</div>
<div class="step"><b>10. Classes and cofactors.</b> Six classes in order; prosthetic group (haem), coenzyme (NAD, niacin), metal ion (zinc in carboxypeptidase).</div>
</div>

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (level 4, numbers).** A protein has two linear chains of 51 and 99 amino acids. How many water molecules were released when it formed? *Solution.* Bonds = (51 − 1) + (99 − 1) = 148, one water per bond. **148.** The trap answer 149 forgets that each chain has its own two ends.

**Example 2 (level 4, kinetics).** An enzyme has V<sub>max</sub> = 120 and K<sub>m</sub> = 5 mM. At what [S] does it run at 75% of V<sub>max</sub>? *Solution.* v/V<sub>max</sub> = [S]/(K<sub>m</sub> + [S]) = 0.75, so [S] = 3K<sub>m</sub> = **15 mM**. (At 50% the answer is K<sub>m</sub>; at 90% it is 9K<sub>m</sub>.)

**Example 3 (level 5, assertion-reason).** A: A competitive inhibitor's effect can be overcome by adding more substrate. R: A competitive inhibitor binds at a site other than the active site. *Solution.* A is true. R is false: it binds the active site, which is exactly why more substrate wins. **A true, R false.**

**Example 4 (level 5, statements).** Statement I: In a polypeptide, the left end carries a free carboxyl group. Statement II: Adult haemoglobin has quaternary structure. *Solution.* By convention the left end is the N-terminal (free amino group), so I is false; II is true (four chains). **I incorrect, II correct.**

<div class="myth">

**"A low K<sub>m</sub> means a slow enzyme."** K<sub>m</sub> is about affinity, not speed. A low K<sub>m</sub> enzyme reaches half its top speed at low substrate, because it binds substrate tightly.

</div>

<div class="meaning">
<dl>
<dt>Graph rule</dt><dd>competitive: curve shifts right, same ceiling; non-competitive: lower ceiling, same K<sub>m</sub></dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>A competitive inhibitor fights the substrate for one seat, so a crowd of substrate wins. A non-competitive one removes seats, so no crowd helps.</dd>
</dl>
</div>

## Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="entrance-biology" data-topics="cell-biomolecules" data-mode="practice" data-exam="neet"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Lipids sit in the acid-insoluble pellet though they are small.
- Secondary metabolites: alkaloids, toxins, lectins, drugs, pigments, oils, rubber, gums.
- Linear peptide: bonds = n − 1; mass = sum of residues − 18 × bonds.
- DNA: A–T 2 and G–C 3 hydrogen bonds; 10 bp per 3.4 nm turn.
- Competitive raises Km, non-competitive lowers Vmax; v = Vmax[S]/(Km + [S]).
- Lyase removes groups (double bond), ligase joins; haem is a prosthetic group, NAD a coenzyme.

</div>
