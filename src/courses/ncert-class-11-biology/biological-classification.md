**Biology · Class 11 · Chapter 2 · about 60 minutes.** By the end you will be able to give the basis of the five-kingdom system, describe the main features of Monera, Protista and Fungi with examples, outline the plant and animal kingdoms, and explain viruses, viroids, prions and lichens.

<div class="bigidea">Biologists sort living things by asking the same few questions: is there a nucleus, is the body one cell or many, is there a cell wall, how does it get food? The answers divide life into five kingdoms (Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia). Viruses, viroids and prions do not fit, because they are not cells, and lichens do not fit either, because each is two organisms in one.</div>

## 1. From two kingdoms to five

Linnaeus put every organism in one of two kingdoms: **Plantae** (green, fixed in place) or **Animalia** (they move and eat). It broke down fast. Where do bacteria go? They have a cell wall, so "plants"? But they have no nucleus. Fungi have a wall but no chlorophyll. *Euglena* photosynthesises like a plant and moves like an animal.

In **1969 R. H. Whittaker** proposed **five kingdoms**, using five criteria: **cell structure** (prokaryotic or eukaryotic), **body organisation** (one cell or many), **mode of nutrition** (autotroph or heterotroph), **reproduction** and **phylogenetic relationships** (how closely related).

| Feature | Monera | Protista | Fungi | Plantae | Animalia |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cell type | prokaryote | eukaryote | eukaryote | eukaryote | eukaryote |
| Cell wall | peptidoglycan (not in mycoplasma) | present in some | chitin | cellulose | none |
| Body | single cell | single cell | multicellular (hyphae); yeast single | multicellular | multicellular |
| Nutrition | autotroph or heterotroph | autotroph or heterotroph | heterotroph (absorptive) | autotroph | heterotroph (ingestive) |
| Example | *E. coli* | *Amoeba* | mushroom | mango | frog |

## 2. Kingdom Monera

Monera has all the **bacteria**, the most abundant organisms: a spoonful of garden soil in Kerala has billions. They are **prokaryotes**: no nuclear membrane, no mitochondria or other membrane-bound organelles; the DNA lies free in the cytoplasm, with ribosomes. A rigid wall of **peptidoglycan** outside the membrane gives shape (except in mycoplasma).

**Shape.** Four shapes: **coccus** (sphere), **bacillus** (rod), **vibrio** (comma) and **spirillum** (spiral).

**Gram stain.** Hans Christian Gram's stain sorts bacteria in two: **Gram-positive** (thick peptidoglycan wall holds the violet dye) and **Gram-negative** (thin wall and an outer membrane; the violet washes out, and the cells take the pink counterstain).

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c11b-bclass-bacteria"><figcaption>Choose a shape and a Gram result. The zoomed cell envelope shows why the stain behaves differently.</figcaption></figure>

**Nutrition.** Some are **autotrophs**: photosynthetic (use light) or **chemosynthetic** (use energy from inorganic chemicals such as ammonia, nitrite and sulphur compounds; they also recycle nitrogen and phosphorus). Most are **heterotrophs**: decomposers (they recycle dead matter), pathogens, and symbionts.

**Reproduction.** Mainly **binary fission**: one cell becomes two. In good conditions some divide every 20 minutes. Some form spores in bad conditions. Some exchange DNA by **conjugation**, **transformation** or **transduction**, which creates variation (see the Class 12 notes).

<div class="problem" data-problem="c11b-bclass-magnify"></div>

**Groups of Monera.**

- **Archaebacteria.** They live in extreme places: **halophiles** (very salty places such as the Rann of Kutch salt flats), **thermoacidophiles** (hot springs, acidic) and **methanogens** (marshes, the gut of cattle; they make methane, used in **gobar gas** plants). Their cell wall has **no peptidoglycan**. Their membranes and some genes differ, which helps them survive harsh conditions, and is why they are placed apart from other bacteria.
- **Eubacteria ("true bacteria").** Most common bacteria. **Cyanobacteria** (blue-green algae, such as *Nostoc* and *Anabaena*) are photosynthetic autotrophs, single, filamentous or colonial, often with a jelly sheath; some have **heterocysts** that fix atmospheric nitrogen. They form blooms in polluted water. Other eubacteria include **nitrogen fixers** (*Rhizobium* in pulses), decomposers (making **curd** from milk) and pathogens that cause cholera, typhoid, tetanus and citrus canker.
- **Mycoplasma.** The smallest living cells, with **no cell wall** and able to live without oxygen. Some cause disease in plants and in animals (some pneumonia).

Bacteria are useful (curd, antibiotics, nitrogen fixation, sewage treatment) and harmful (disease, food spoilage). See Chapter "Microbes in Human Welfare" in Class 12.

## 3. Kingdom Protista

Protista are **single-celled eukaryotes**, mostly aquatic, a "bridge" between Monera and the three higher kingdoms. They have a nucleus and organelles, and move with **cilia, flagella** or false feet. Reproduction is by **cell fusion and zygote formation** (sexual) or by division.

- **Chrysophytes.** Diatoms and golden algae (desmids), found in fresh and salt water. **Diatoms** have a two-piece **silica shell** that does not decay; the dead shells make **diatomaceous earth** used in polishing and filtration. They are the chief **producers** of the oceans.
- **Dinoflagellates.** Mostly marine and photosynthetic. Their cells have stiff cellulose plates and two flagella. Some species (e.g. *Gonyaulax*) multiply so fast that the sea turns red, a **"red tide"** that can poison fish.
- **Euglenoids.** Mostly fresh water. No cell wall; a flexible **pellicle**. *Euglena* has chloroplasts and photosynthesises in sunlight; with no light it feeds on small organisms, so it is both autotrophic and heterotrophic, a case that shows why a two-kingdom system fails.
- **Slime moulds.** Saprophytic protists that move on rotting wood and leaves. Under good conditions they form a **plasmodium**, a mass with many nuclei that can spread over metres. In bad conditions the plasmodium forms fruiting bodies bearing **spores** with tough walls, that the wind spreads.
- **Protozoans.** Heterotrophs, predators or parasites, thought to be the first animals. Four groups: **amoeboid** (*Amoeba*, false feet), **flagellated** (*Trypanosoma*, sleeping sickness), **ciliated** (*Paramecium*, cilia sweep food) and **sporozoans** (*Plasmodium*, the malaria parasite).

## 4. Kingdom Fungi

Fungi are **heterotrophic eukaryotes** that **absorb** food: they release enzymes onto the food and absorb the small molecules. They are **saprophytes** (rotting matter: bread mould, mushrooms), **parasites** (rusts and smuts on crops, ringworm) or **symbionts** (lichens, mycorrhiza).

**Structure.** Except for **yeast** (single-celled) all fungi are made of thin threads called **hyphae**, a network of which is a **mycelium**. In some, the hyphae have cross walls (**septate**); in others they are continuous tubes with many nuclei (**coenocytic**). The cell wall is **chitin** (a polysaccharide with nitrogen) and **no** cellulose. Food is stored as **glycogen** and oil, not starch.

**Reproduction.** Three ways, in all fungi.

1. **Vegetative:** fragmentation, fission and budding (yeast).
2. **Asexual:** spores such as **conidia**, **sporangiospores** and **zoospores**.
3. **Sexual:** **oospores**, **ascospores**, **basidiospores**, in three steps: **plasmogamy**, **karyogamy** and **meiosis**.

<div class="steps" data-fig="c11b-bclass-fungi">
<div class="step">Haploid (n) spores land on damp food.</div>
<div class="step">They germinate into hyphae and form the mycelium (n).</div>
<div class="step">Plasmogamy: two hyphae fuse; the cell has two nuclei (n + n).</div>
<div class="step">Karyogamy: the nuclei fuse to give a diploid (2n) zygote nucleus.</div>
<div class="step">Meiosis: haploid spores form and are released.</div>
</div>

**Classes of fungi.**

| Class | Mycelium | Sexual spores | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phycomycetes | coenocytic (no cross walls) | zygospores | *Rhizopus* (bread mould), *Mucor*, *Albugo* (white rust) |
| Ascomycetes (sac fungi) | septate, branched | ascospores in an **ascus** | *Penicillium*, *Aspergillus*, *Neurospora*, yeast, morels, truffles |
| Basidiomycetes (club fungi) | septate | basidiospores in a **basidium** | mushrooms (*Agaricus*), bracket fungi, *Puccinia* (rust), *Ustilago* (smut) |
| Deuteromycetes (imperfect fungi) | septate | none known (only asexual conidia) | *Alternaria*, *Colletotrichum*, *Trichoderma* |

Once the sexual stage of a Deuteromycete is found, the fungus moves to the Ascomycetes or Basidiomycetes. Fungi are useful: bread and beer (yeast), antibiotics (*Penicillium* gives penicillin), cheese ripening, edible mushrooms. They also cause crop diseases and food spoilage.

## 5. Kingdoms Plantae and Animalia

**Plantae** are multicellular, photosynthetic eukaryotes with a **cellulose** wall and chloroplasts. Their life cycle has **alternation of generations** between a haploid gametophyte and a diploid sporophyte, covered in Chapter 3 (Plant Kingdom). Some plants are partly heterotrophic: insectivorous ones (pitcher plant, sundew) and parasites (*Cuscuta*).

**Animalia** are multicellular heterotrophic eukaryotes with **no cell wall**, which eat (ingest) food and digest it inside the body. Most have nerves and muscles and show **direct or indirect** development (see Chapter 4).

## 6. Viruses, viroids, prions and lichens

These do not fit in the five kingdoms.

**Viruses.** In **1892 D. J. Ivanowsky** found that the sap of diseased tobacco leaves still infected plants after it passed through a filter that stops bacteria. In **1898 M. W. Beijerinck** showed that the sap infects healthy plants and called it *contagium vivum fluidum* (infectious living fluid). **W. M. Stanley** (1935) crystallised the virus and found it to be **protein**: it can be crystallised like a chemical, so is it alive?

A virus has a **nucleic acid** (DNA or RNA, never both, single- or double-stranded) in a **protein coat** (the **capsid**, built of units called **capsomeres**). Some animal viruses also have an **envelope**. They are **obligate parasites**: outside a host cell, they are inert; inside, they take over the host's machinery to make more virus. Plant viruses mostly have single-stranded **RNA** (TMV; mosaic, leaf rolling, yellowing, dwarfing); **bacteriophages** ("bacteria eaters") usually have double-stranded **DNA**; animal viruses have RNA or DNA (mumps, smallpox, herpes, influenza, **AIDS**, COVID-19).

**Viroids.** Discovered by **T. O. Diener** (1971). Smaller than any virus: **free RNA** (low molecular weight) with **no protein coat**. They cause the potato spindle tuber disease.

**Prions.** Abnormally folded **proteins** (not nucleic acids) that make normal proteins of the same kind fold wrongly. They are infectious and cause **BSE** (mad cow disease) in cattle and **CJD** (Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease) in humans, diseases of the brain.

**Lichens.** A lichen is a **symbiotic association** (mutualism) between an **alga** (or cyanobacterium; the **phycobiont**) and a **fungus** (the **mycobiont**). The alga makes food by photosynthesis; the fungus gives water, minerals, a firm body and shelter. Lichens are **pollution indicators** (they do not grow in air rich in sulphur dioxide) and the cold-region reindeer moss is food.

**Beyond the syllabus.** In 1990 Carl Woese and colleagues suggested three **domains**: Bacteria, Archaea and Eukarya, since archaebacteria share some molecular features with eukaryotes. The five-kingdom scheme is the one used for the exam.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Whittaker (1969): five kingdoms, using cell structure, body, nutrition, reproduction and relationships.
- **Monera**: prokaryotes with peptidoglycan walls (not mycoplasma); shapes coccus, bacillus, vibrio, spirillum; archaebacteria, eubacteria (incl. cyanobacteria), mycoplasma.
- **Protista**: single-celled eukaryotes: chrysophytes (diatoms), dinoflagellates, euglenoids, slime moulds, protozoans.
- **Fungi**: chitin wall, hyphae and mycelium, absorptive; plasmogamy, karyogamy, meiosis. Four classes: Phycomycetes, Ascomycetes, Basidiomycetes, Deuteromycetes.
- **Viruses**: nucleic acid (one kind) in a protein capsid; obligate parasites; **viroids**: free RNA; **prions**: infectious proteins.
- **Lichens**: alga + fungus mutualism; indicators of air pollution.

</div>
