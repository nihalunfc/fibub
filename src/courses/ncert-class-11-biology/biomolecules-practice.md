**Practice · Class 11 Biology · Chapter 9.** Read the chapter lesson, [Biomolecules](/learn/courses/ncert-class-11-biology/biomolecules/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Biomolecule questions follow a pattern: name the monomer, name the bond, name the polymer. Enzyme questions ask what changed (Km or Vmax) and why. Graph questions: read the shape, then say what is limiting at each part of the curve.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For structures, name the monomer, the bond and the polymer in one line.
2. For differences, use a table with at least three rows.
3. For enzyme graphs, label the axes, mark Vmax and Km and give the reason for each part of the curve.
4. For calculations, write the formula first.

## 1-mark questions

**Q1.** Name the bond between two amino acids.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The peptide bond.</details>

**Q2.** What is the most abundant protein in the whole biosphere?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>RuBisCO (ribulose bisphosphate carboxylase-oxygenase).</details>

**Q3.** Which sugar is present in RNA?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Ribose.</details>

**Q4.** Name the type of lipid that is the main part of cell membranes.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Phospholipids.</details>

**Q5.** What is the protein part of an enzyme without its cofactor called?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Apoenzyme.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

**Q6.** Differentiate between primary and secondary metabolites with an example each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Primary metabolites (amino acids, sugars, nucleotides) are needed for growth and normal life. Secondary metabolites (alkaloids such as morphine, rubber, antibiotics, pigments) have no known role in growth but help the organism in other ways, such as defence.</details>

**Q7.** Why do saturated fats stay solid and unsaturated oils stay liquid at room temperature?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Saturated fatty acids have straight chains that pack tightly, so they need much heat to melt. Cis double bonds in unsaturated fatty acids bend the chain, so the molecules cannot pack well and the substance melts at a lower temperature.</details>

**Q8.** What is a nucleotide? How are nucleotides linked?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A nucleotide is a nitrogen base, a pentose sugar and a phosphate. They are linked by phosphodiester bonds between the 3' carbon of one sugar and the 5' phosphate of the next, forming a sugar-phosphate backbone.</details>

**Q9.** Write three differences between starch and cellulose.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Starch: α-glucose units; coiled; storage in plants; digestible by humans. Cellulose: β-glucose; straight chains forming fibres; structural in the wall; not digestible by humans.</details>

**Q10.** Why does heating an enzyme destroy its activity, but cooling it does not?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Heat breaks the weak bonds holding the shape, so the active site is lost (denaturation, irreversible). Cold only slows the molecules; the shape is not harmed, so activity returns on warming.</details>

**Q11.** An enzyme has Vmax of 80 units and Km 4 mM. Find the rate at [S] = 4 mM and at [S] = 36 mM.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

At 4 mM the rate is half of Vmax = 40 units. At 36 mM: v = 80 × 36 ÷ (4 + 36) = 72 units.

</details>

**Q12.** State Chargaff's rule and use it: a DNA has 22 % guanine. Find the percentage of thymine.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

A = T and G = C. G = 22 % so C = 22 %, so A + T = 100 − 44 = 56 %, so T = 28 %.

</details>

## 5-mark questions

**Q13.** Describe the four levels of protein structure with examples.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Primary:** the order of amino acids joined by peptide bonds (insulin has two chains of known sequence). **Secondary:** the polypeptide chain coils into an α-helix or folds as a β-pleated sheet, held by hydrogen bonds (keratin is α-helix rich). **Tertiary:** the helices and sheets fold into a compact 3D shape held by hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, disulphide bridges and hydrophobic interactions (myoglobin, enzymes: this shape makes the active site). **Quaternary:** several polypeptide chains join in a defined way (haemoglobin: 2α and 2β chains). Disturbing the shape denatures the protein and its function.

</details>

**Q14.** Explain how an enzyme speeds up a reaction and how substrate concentration affects the rate. Draw the graph.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The substrate binds to the active site (induced fit) to make the enzyme-substrate complex, which reaches the transition state with a lower activation energy than without the enzyme. Products form and leave, and the enzyme is unchanged. With more substrate the rate rises nearly in proportion at first; then, when all the active sites are busy, it levels off at Vmax (the curve is a rectangular hyperbola, v = Vmax[S]/(Km + [S])). Km, the [S] at Vmax/2, shows how strongly the enzyme binds its substrate. (Draw: axes labelled [S] and v, a curve rising and flattening, dashed lines for Vmax and Vmax/2, Km marked.)

</details>

## Case-based question

**Read this.** A student measures the rate of an enzyme at different substrate concentrations, once alone and once with an inhibitor X. Without X, Vmax = 100 units and Km = 2 mM. With X, Vmax is still 100 units but Km has risen to 6 mM. In a second test with inhibitor Y, Vmax falls to 50 units and Km stays 2 mM.

**Q15.** (i) What kind of inhibitor is X? (ii) What kind is Y? (iii) What is the rate with X when [S] = 6 mM? (iv) Which of them can be overcome by adding a lot of substrate?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(i) Competitive (Km rises, Vmax unchanged). (ii) Non-competitive (Vmax falls, Km unchanged). (iii) v = 100 × 6 ÷ (6 + 6) = 50 units. (iv) X, the competitive inhibitor, since the substrate competes with it for the active site.

</details>

## Assertion-reason

Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

**Q16.** **A:** Enzymes do not change the equilibrium of a reaction. **R:** They lower the activation energy for both the forward and the reverse reactions.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A.</details>

**Q17.** **A:** Cellulose cannot be digested by humans. **R:** It is made of α-glucose units.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(C) A is true; R is false: cellulose is made of β-glucose units, joined in a way that human enzymes cannot split.</details>

**Q18.** **A:** G-C rich DNA has a higher melting temperature. **R:** A G-C pair has three hydrogen bonds and an A-T pair has two.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A.</details>
