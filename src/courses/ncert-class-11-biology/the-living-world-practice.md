**Practice · Class 11 Biology · Chapter 1.** Read the chapter lesson, [The Living World](/learn/courses/ncert-class-11-biology/the-living-world/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Two habits help. For "give reasons", say the property and what happens in the example (for instance: a snowball grows, but only from outside, so it is not alive). For naming, write genus with a capital, species with a small letter, and underline both words separately by hand.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Define the term in one line, then give an example.
2. For differences, draw a two-column table.
3. For the ranks, list them in order from Kingdom to Species with an example organism.
4. Underline scientific names when you write by hand.

## 1-mark questions

**Q1.** Who gave the system of binomial nomenclature?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Carolus Linnaeus.</details>

**Q2.** Name the smallest taxonomic category.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Species.</details>

**Q3.** What is a couplet in a key?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A pair of contrasting statements, of which exactly one fits the specimen.</details>

**Q4.** Name a place where living plants are grown, labelled and conserved for study.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A botanical garden, for example Lalbagh in Bengaluru.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

**Q5.** Why is reproduction not a defining property of life? Give two examples.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A mule and a worker honey bee are alive, but cannot reproduce. Metabolism and cellular organisation are found in all living things.</details>

**Q6.** How does the growth of a living thing differ from the growth of a non-living thing?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

| Living | Non-living |
|---|---|
| Grows from inside | Grows by adding matter on the outside |
| Cell division, uses food | No cells, no metabolism |
| Example: seedling | Example: snowball, crystal |

</details>

**Q7.** Write the correct form of the scientific name of the mango, in print and by hand. Why is the genus given a capital letter and species a small letter?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Printed: *Mangifera indica*. Handwritten: <u>Mangifera</u> <u>indica</u>. The capital starts the genus and the small letter the species epithet, which is the international rule, so that readers can tell which word is the genus.</details>

**Q8.** Write the seven main ranks from the largest to the smallest, using the human as an example.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Kingdom Animalia, Phylum Chordata, Class Mammalia, Order Primata, Family Hominidae, Genus *Homo*, Species *sapiens*.</details>

**Q9.** Differentiate a herbarium from a museum.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A herbarium holds only dried, pressed and labelled plants on sheets. A museum holds preserved plants and animals (in preservative, pinned insects, stuffed animals) for study and teaching.</details>

**Q10.** A key has 12 organisms. How many couplets are needed at least? Explain.

<div class="problem" data-problem="c11b-living-couplets"></div>

<details><summary>Answer</summary>11. Each couplet splits one group into two, adding one group; from one group to 12 groups needs 11 splits.</details>

## 5-mark questions

**Q11.** Explain the properties that tell us something is alive. Which of them are exceptions in some organisms?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Cellular organisation (made of cells), metabolism, growth from inside, reproduction, and response to the environment (consciousness). Metabolism and cellular organisation are found in all living organisms and in no non-living thing. Exceptions: a mule cannot reproduce; many adult organisms do not grow; a dormant seed has very slow metabolism; non-living things like a crystal "grow".</details>

**Q12.** Describe the taxonomical aids that help in identification of organisms, with one function of each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Herbarium: dried labelled plants kept for comparison. Botanical garden: living labelled plants for study and conservation. Museum: preserved plants and animals. Zoological park: live wild animals kept in natural-like conditions for study. Key: contrasting couplets for identification. Flora, manuals, monographs and catalogues are books with information about the organisms of an area or group.</details>

**Q13.** State the rules of binomial nomenclature with two examples.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Two words in Latin form, genus then species epithet; genus capital, species small letter; italic in print, underlined separately in handwriting; the author name follows in plain type, for example *Mangifera indica* Linn. and *Homo sapiens* Linn.</details>

## Case-based question

A student collects three leaves from a garden: a mango leaf, a jamun leaf and a brinjal leaf. She presses and dries them and makes a herbarium sheet for each. Her teacher says the mango and jamun are in different families, while the brinjal and potato are in the same genus, *Solanum*.

**(a)** Name two things that must go on the label of each sheet.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Name of the plant (scientific name) and place or date or collector or family: any two.</details>

**(b)** The brinjal is *Solanum melongena* and the potato *Solanum tuberosum*. What do these names tell us?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>They share the genus <i>Solanum</i>, so they are closely related, but they are different species (different epithets).</details>

**(c)** Write the name of the brinjal in the way you would write it by hand.

<details><summary>Answer</summary><u>Solanum</u> <u>melongena</u>, each word underlined separately.</details>

## Assertion and reason

For each, choose: (a) both true and R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

**Q14.** **A:** Scientific names are used in all countries. **R:** A common name can differ with the language and the place.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). Because common names vary, one international name is needed.</details>

**Q15.** **A:** Species is the largest category in classification. **R:** Members of a species can breed together.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(d). Species is the smallest category; R is true.</details>
