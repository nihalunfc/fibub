## How to set out answers

1. Define the term in one line, then give an example.
2. For differences, use a table with the same heading rows.
3. For diagrams, use a sharp pencil and label every part that the question names.
4. Give reasons using the structure and the function together (thick wall, so strength).

## 1-mark questions

**Q1.** Which meristem makes a stem longer?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The apical meristem at the shoot tip.</details>

**Q2.** Name the dead tissue with very thick lignified walls and a tiny lumen.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Sclerenchyma (fibres and stone cells).</details>

**Q3.** What do bulliform cells do?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>They lose water in dry air and shrink, which rolls the grass leaf and cuts water loss.</details>

**Q4.** Name the part of a dicot leaf with tall, closely packed chloroplast-rich cells.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Palisade parenchyma, just below the upper epidermis.</details>

**Q5.** Which kind of vascular bundle is found in roots?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Radial: xylem and phloem lie on different radii.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

**Q6.** Write two differences between xylem and phloem.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Xylem carries water and minerals upwards; phloem carries food in both directions. Mature xylem conducting cells (vessels, tracheids) are dead; sieve tubes are living but without a nucleus. Xylem walls are lignified; phloem walls are cellulose.</details>

**Q7.** Why do monocot stems not show secondary growth?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Their vascular bundles are closed: there is no cambium between the xylem and phloem to make new xylem and phloem, and no cambial ring can form.</details>

**Q8.** What is the job of the endodermis and its Casparian strip?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The endodermis is the innermost layer of the cortex. Its Casparian strip of suberin blocks the gaps between its cells, so water and minerals must pass through the cell membranes. The plant can thus select what goes into the xylem.</details>

**Q9.** Distinguish spring wood from autumn wood.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Spring wood forms when water is plentiful: lighter, wide cells with wide vessels. Autumn wood forms as growth slows: darker, narrow cells, denser. One of each makes one annual ring.</details>

**Q10.** How does a stoma open and close?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Guard cells take up water (K⁺ ions enter first) and become turgid; their thick inner wall makes them curve apart and the pore opens. They lose water when the plant is short of water or in the dark, and become flaccid, so the pore closes.</details>

**Q11.** What is a lenticel and why is it needed?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A lenticel is a small loose-celled gap in the cork of woody stems. It allows exchange of gases (O₂ in, CO₂ out) for the living cells beneath the dead cork.</details>

**Q12.** Give three differences between dicot and monocot leaves.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Dicot: mesophyll in palisade and spongy parts; stomata mostly on the lower face; reticulate venation. Monocot: mesophyll the same on both sides; stomata on both faces; parallel venation and bulliform cells.</details>

## 5-mark questions

**Q13.** Describe the structure of a dicot stem as seen in a cross-section, from outside in.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Epidermis**: one layer with a cuticle and a few trichomes. **Hypodermis**: collenchyma, a few layers, for support. **Cortex**: parenchyma with some chloroplasts. **Endodermis**: the starch sheath. **Pericycle**: patches of sclerenchyma above the bundles. **Vascular bundles**: arranged in a ring, conjoint, open; phloem outside, cambium in the middle, xylem inside with the protoxylem pointing to the centre. **Medullary rays**: parenchyma between bundles. **Pith**: large, central, of parenchyma. A neat labelled figure carries marks.

</details>

**Q14.** Explain secondary growth in a dicot stem. How does it lead to bark and heartwood?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The fascicular cambium in the bundles and the interfascicular cambium from the medullary rays form a complete ring. It divides, cutting off secondary xylem (many cells) inward and secondary phloem (fewer) outward. The wood accumulates, and each year a ring is formed (spring and autumn wood). The outer tissues stretch and break; a cork cambium (phellogen) forms in the cortex, making cork outward and secondary cortex (phelloderm) inward; the periderm and everything outside the vascular cambium are bark. In the centre, the old xylem fills with tannins and resins, becomes dark, hard and stops conducting: the heartwood; the young outer layers are the sapwood.

</details>

## Case-based question

**Read this.** A teacher gives two slides of cross-sections of stems to a student. Slide A shows vascular bundles scattered over the whole section, each with a thick sclerenchyma sheath and without a visible cambium. Slide B shows 8 bundles in a ring with a thin layer of dividing cells between xylem and phloem and a large pith in the middle.

**Q15.** (i) Which slide is a monocot? (ii) Which type of bundle is in slide B? (iii) Which of the two can become a thick tree trunk? (iv) Name one plant for each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(i) Slide A. (ii) Conjoint, open (it has a cambium). (iii) Slide B, because its cambium makes secondary xylem and phloem. (iv) A: maize or sugarcane; B: sunflower or mango.

</details>

## Assertion-reason

Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

**Q16.** **A:** Sieve tubes can live without a nucleus. **R:** They have companion cells that control their activities.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A.</details>

**Q17.** **A:** Monocot roots have a small pith. **R:** They have more than six xylem bundles.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(D) A is false: monocot roots have a large pith. R is true.</details>

**Q18.** **A:** Heartwood does not carry water. **R:** Its cells are filled with resins and tannins and the vessels are blocked.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Meristem: dividing; permanent tissues: parenchyma (living, thin), collenchyma (living, corners thick), sclerenchyma (dead, thick).
- Xylem carries water up; phloem carries food. Only xylem parenchyma and phloem fibres break the usual rule of living and dead.
- Monocot: scattered closed bundles, no cambium, bulliform cells. Dicot: ring of open bundles, palisade and spongy mesophyll.
- Secondary growth: vascular cambium and cork cambium; annual rings, heartwood, sapwood, bark and lenticels.
