**Biology · Class 11 · Chapter 3 · about 65 minutes.** By the end you will be able to name the five groups of the plant kingdom with their features and examples, and describe the life cycles of a moss, a fern and a pine, using the idea of alternation of generations.

<div class="bigidea">Plants moved from water to land step by step. Mosses stay small and still need water to reproduce. Ferns get vascular tissue and grow tall. Gymnosperms add seeds, so water is no longer needed for fertilisation. Angiosperms add flowers and fruits. Through the whole climb, one thing runs underneath: a haploid phase (n) and a diploid phase (2n) that take turns, and the diploid phase slowly takes over.</div>

## 1. How plants are classified

Early systems used a few easy features (for example habit: tree, shrub, herb), which put unlike plants together. Later **natural systems** (Bentham and Hooker) used many natural features, and **phylogenetic systems** use evolutionary relationships. Today, chemistry (**chemotaxonomy**), cell structure (**cytotaxonomy**) and computer methods (**numerical taxonomy**) add more evidence.

The five-kingdom system puts **algae, bryophytes, pteridophytes, gymnosperms and angiosperms** in Plantae. Pick any feature and see the groups from simple to complex.

## 2. Algae

Algae are simple, **chlorophyll-bearing, mostly aquatic** plants without roots, stems or leaves. The body is a **thallus** (one cell, a colony, a filament or a leaf-like sheet). They live in fresh water, the sea, damp soil, on rocks, and even in association with fungi (lichens) and in animals (sloth fur). They are the main **producers** in water; they make about half of the oxygen made by photosynthesis, and some are food (*Porphyra*, *Laminaria*) or give **agar** (*Gelidium*, used in labs and in sweets) and **algin** (brown algae).

**Reproduction.** Vegetative (fragmentation), asexual (spores, e.g. **zoospores** with flagella) and sexual: from **isogamous** (two gametes alike, e.g. *Spirogyra*), through **anisogamous** (unlike sizes, e.g. *Eudorina*) to **oogamous** (small motile male gamete, large non-motile egg, e.g. *Volvox*, *Fucus*).

**Three main classes.**

| | Chlorophyceae (green) | Phaeophyceae (brown) | Rhodophyceae (red) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pigments | chlorophyll a, b | chlorophyll a, c, **fucoxanthin** | chlorophyll a, d, **phycoerythrin** |
| Food stored | starch | **laminarin**, mannitol | floridean starch |
| Cell wall | cellulose | cellulose + **algin** | cellulose + pectin and others |
| Habitat | mostly fresh water | marine, middle shores | marine, can live in deep water |
| Examples | *Chlamydomonas*, *Volvox*, *Spirogyra*, *Ulothrix* | *Ectocarpus*, *Dictyota*, *Laminaria*, *Fucus* | *Polysiphonia*, *Porphyra*, *Gracilaria*, *Gelidium* |

Why do they live at different depths? Sea water takes red light out of sunlight within a few metres; blue and green go deeper. Red algae have **phycoerythrin**, which uses the light that reaches deep water. Slide the depth.

## 3. Bryophytes

Bryophytes are the "amphibians of the plant kingdom": they live on land, but need water for fertilisation. They grow in damp, shady places: walls in the Kerala monsoon, rocks, tree bark. They include **liverworts** and **mosses**.

- **Body:** thallus-like or leafy, **no true roots, stem or leaves**, and **no vascular tissue**. Root-like **rhizoids** fix the plant to the ground.
- **Dominant phase:** the haploid **gametophyte**, green and free-living. The diploid **sporophyte** grows on it and depends on it for food.
- **Sex organs:** male **antheridium** makes biflagellate **antherozoids**; female **archegonium** (flask-shaped) holds one egg. Fertilisation needs water.
- **Zygote** grows into the sporophyte: **foot, seta, capsule**; meiosis in the capsule makes haploid spores.

**Liverworts** (*Marchantia*) have a flat, dorsiventral, thalloid body, and make **gemmae** (green buds in cups) for asexual reproduction. **Mosses** (*Funaria*, *Polytrichum*, *Sphagnum*) have a first stage, the thread-like **protonema** from a spore, and a second leafy stage (the gametophore).

**Economic value.** *Sphagnum* holds water and gives **peat**, used as fuel and as packing for plants and flowers. Mosses form dense mats that stop soil erosion, and help the first plants to grow on bare rock (**pioneers** of ecological succession).

## 4. Pteridophytes

Ferns, horsetails and club mosses are the first plants with **xylem and phloem** (vascular tissue), so they can grow tall and have **true roots, stems and leaves**. Examples: *Selaginella*, *Equisetum*, *Pteris*, *Adiantum*. They live in cool, damp, shady places (and the Western Ghats are full of them).

- The main plant is the **sporophyte** (2n). Leaves are small (**microphylls**, as in *Selaginella*) or large (**macrophylls**, as in ferns).
- Sporangia sit on **sporophylls**, which may form compact **strobili** (*Selaginella*, *Equisetum*) or **sori** under fern leaves.
- Spore mother cells divide by meiosis to give **spores**. These grow into a small, multicellular, free-living, mostly photosynthetic **prothallus**: the gametophyte, which bears antheridia and archegonia. Water is needed, so fertilisation is in damp places. The zygote becomes the new sporophyte.
- **Homosporous** pteridophytes make one kind of spore. **Heterosporous** ones (*Selaginella*, *Salvinia*) make large **megaspores** and small **microspores**; the female gametophyte stays on the parent sporophyte. This is a step towards the **seed habit**.

## 5. Gymnosperms

"Gymnosperm" means **naked seed**: the ovules are not enclosed in an ovary, so after fertilisation the seeds are not in a fruit. They include **pine, deodar, cycas, ginkgo and Ephedra**, and **Sequoia** (the tallest trees; the redwood).

- The plant body is a **sporophyte**: tall trees or shrubs, with a **tap root** (in *Pinus* often with fungal partners called **mycorrhiza**, and in *Cycas* with **coralloid roots** that hold cyanobacteria for nitrogen fixation).
- Leaves are **needle-like** (pine) or compound (*Cycas*), with thick cuticle and sunken stomata that reduce water loss.
- They are **heterosporous**: **microspores** (pollen grains) in male cones, **megaspores** in female cones. Cones are borne on the same tree (pine) or different trees (*Cycas*).
- The male gametophyte is only a few cells (the pollen grain), and the female gametophyte stays within the ovule: both are very small and depend on the sporophyte.
- **Pollination** by wind; the pollen tube carries the male gametes to the egg, so **no water is needed**. The ovule becomes a **seed**.

## 6. Angiosperms

Angiosperms are **flowering plants**: the ovules are enclosed in an **ovary** that becomes the **fruit**, with the seeds inside. They range from the tiny *Wolffia* to *Eucalyptus* trees over 100 m tall. They are the largest and most varied group, and give food, fibre, medicine and timber.

Two classes: **dicotyledons** (two seed leaves, reticulate venation, tap root, floral parts in fours or fives) and **monocotyledons** (one seed leaf, parallel venation, fibrous roots, floral parts in threes).

The male gametophyte is the **pollen grain** inside the anther; the female gametophyte is the **embryo sac** inside the ovule. Reproduction ends with **double fertilisation**: one male gamete fuses with the egg (2n zygote) and another with two polar nuclei (3n endosperm). ## 7. Life cycles and alternation of generations

All plants alternate between a **haploid gametophyte** (n, makes gametes by mitosis) and a **diploid sporophyte** (2n, makes spores by meiosis). This is **alternation of generations**. Three patterns:

- **Haplontic:** the body is haploid; the zygote is the only diploid cell and divides by **meiosis** at once (*Spirogyra*, *Volvox*, most green algae).
- **Diplontic:** the body is diploid; only the gametes are haploid (gymnosperms, angiosperms, and some algae such as *Fucus*).
- **Haplo-diplontic:** both phases are many-celled bodies (bryophytes, pteridophytes, and some algae such as kelps, *Ectocarpus*, *Polysiphonia*). Bryophytes have a dominant **gametophyte**; pteridophytes a dominant **sporophyte**.

<div class="meaning">

**What each term means.** *Gametophyte:* the haploid body that makes gametes. *Sporophyte:* the diploid body that makes spores. *Meiosis* turns 2n into n (making spores); *fertilisation* turns n + n into 2n. A table of chromosome numbers follows the cycle: if a fern sporophyte has 2n = 50, its spores and prothallus cells have n = 25.

</div>

<div class="history">

**Beyond the syllabus.** The oldest land-plant fossils (*Cooksonia*, about 430 million years old) were small, leafless, branched stems with spore capsules at the tips, a little like a moss sporophyte but with vascular tissue.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- **Algae:** chlorophyll-bearing thallus; green (starch), brown (fucoxanthin, laminarin, algin), red (phycoerythrin); oogamous, isogamous, anisogamous.
- **Bryophytes:** gametophyte dominant, no vascular tissue, water for fertilisation; liverworts and mosses; protonema; sporophyte = foot, seta, capsule.
- **Pteridophytes:** vascular tissue, sporophyte dominant, free-living prothallus; homospory and heterospory.
- **Gymnosperms:** naked seeds, wind pollination, no water, heterosporous, needle leaves.
- **Angiosperms:** flowers and fruits, double fertilisation, dicots and monocots.
- Life cycles: haplontic, diplontic, haplo-diplontic; alternation of generations.

</div>
