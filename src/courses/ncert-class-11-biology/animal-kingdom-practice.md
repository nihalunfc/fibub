**Practice · Class 11 Biology · Chapter 4.** Read the chapter lesson, [Animal Kingdom](/learn/courses/ncert-class-11-biology/animal-kingdom/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Match each question to a body-plan clue. Pores mean sponge; stinging cells mean Cnidaria; flat means flatworm; round with a pseudocoelom means roundworm; rings mean Annelida; jointed legs mean Arthropoda; shell and foot mean Mollusca; tube feet mean Echinodermata; notochord means Chordata.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For "differentiate", use a table with at least three points.
2. For a phylum, write the main features in the order: symmetry, germ layers, coelom, special feature, example.
3. Draw a simple labelled sketch where a question says "with a diagram".
4. Give at least two examples for each group.

## 1-mark questions

**Q1.** Name the phylum in which the water vascular system is found.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Echinodermata.</details>

**Q2.** What is the body cavity of an arthropod called?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Haemocoel (filled with blood).</details>

**Q3.** Name one egg-laying mammal.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The platypus (<i>Ornithorhynchus</i>).</details>

**Q4.** Which excretory structures are found in flatworms?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Flame cells.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

**Q5.** Distinguish diploblastic and triploblastic animals.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

| Diploblastic | Triploblastic |
|---|---|
| Two germ layers: ectoderm and endoderm | Three: ectoderm, mesoderm and endoderm |
| Jelly between the layers | Mesoderm between them |
| Cnidaria, Ctenophora | Platyhelminthes to Chordata |

</details>

**Q6.** What is a pseudocoelom? Name one animal that has it.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A body cavity between the gut and the body wall, not fully lined by mesoderm (mesoderm only in scattered pouches). Roundworms such as <i>Ascaris</i>.</details>

**Q7.** Why are Platyhelminthes called acoelomates and flatworms?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>They have no body cavity (the space between gut and body wall is filled with tissue) and the body is flattened from top to bottom.</details>

**Q8.** Name three features of Chordata.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A notochord, a dorsal hollow nerve cord, paired pharyngeal gill slits (and a post-anal tail, closed circulation with a ventral heart).</details>

**Q9.** A starfish has 5 arms, each with 2 rows of 100 tube feet. How many tube feet in all?

<div class="problem" data-problem="c11b-animalk-tubefeet"></div>

<details><summary>Answer</summary>5 × 2 × 100 = 1000.</details>

**Q10.** Compare the circulatory system of arthropods and annelids.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Annelids have a closed circulatory system (blood in vessels); arthropods have an open one (blood in the haemocoel).</details>

## 5-mark questions

**Q11.** Describe the bases on which animals are classified.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Levels of organisation (cellular, tissue, organ, organ system); symmetry (asymmetrical, radial, bilateral); germ layers (diploblastic, triploblastic); coelom (acoelomate, pseudocoelomate, coelomate); segmentation (metamerism); notochord (chordates and non-chordates). Give one example for each.</details>

**Q12.** Describe the characters of phylum Arthropoda with four examples.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The largest phylum. Bilateral, triploblastic, coelomate (haemocoel), metamerically segmented; chitin exoskeleton; jointed appendages; body of head, thorax and abdomen; open circulation; respiration by gills, tracheae or book lungs; Malpighian tubules; mostly dioecious. Examples: <i>Apis</i>, <i>Bombyx</i>, <i>Anopheles</i>, <i>Palaemon</i>, <i>Limulus</i>.</details>

**Q13.** Compare the classes Amphibia, Reptilia, Aves and Mammalia in a table.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

| | Amphibia | Reptilia | Aves | Mammalia |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Skin | moist, no scales | dry, with scales | feathers | hair, glands |
| Heart | 3 | 3 (4 in crocodile) | 4 | 4 |
| Temperature | cold-blooded | cold-blooded | warm-blooded | warm-blooded |
| Development | external fertilisation, eggs in water | internal, eggs on land | eggs | mostly viviparous |
| Example | frog | lizard | crow | human |

</details>

## Case-based question

A student is shown three animals: a jellyfish washed up on a beach in Goa, an earthworm in garden soil, and a crab from a market. She notices that the jellyfish has no head and its body can be cut in several ways to give equal halves, the earthworm's body has rings, and the crab has a hard shell and jointed legs.

**(a)** Name the symmetry of the jellyfish and its phylum.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Radial symmetry; Cnidaria.</details>

**(b)** Why is the earthworm called metamerically segmented?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Its body is divided into many repeated segments, shown as rings on the surface, with several organs repeated in each segment.</details>

**(c)** Which feature of the crab shows it is an arthropod?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The chitinous exoskeleton and jointed appendages (legs).</details>

## Assertion and reason

For each, choose: (a) both true and R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

**Q14.** **A:** Birds and mammals are warm-blooded. **R:** They have a four-chambered heart with no mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). The separation of the two blood types gives a high supply of oxygen for making heat.</details>

**Q15.** **A:** All echinoderms are marine. **R:** Their larvae and adults are both radially symmetrical.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(c). A is true; R is false because the larvae are bilateral.</details>
