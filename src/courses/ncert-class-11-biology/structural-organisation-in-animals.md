**Biology · Class 11 · Chapter 7 · about 70 minutes.** By the end you will be able to name the four animal tissues and their kinds, link each to its job, explain how cells, tissues, organs and organ systems build an animal, and describe the earthworm, cockroach and frog as examples.

<div class="bigidea">An animal's body is built in levels: cells, then tissues, then organs, then organ systems. Just four basic tissues (epithelial, connective, muscle and nervous) make up every organ. The shape of a cell and the kind of matrix around it tell you the job: look at the structure and you can say the function.</div>

## 1. Levels of organisation

A single-celled amoeba does everything in one cell. A many-celled animal shares the work: **cells** of one type form a **tissue**, a few tissues form an **organ**, and organs working on one big job form an **organ system**. Step through the idea with the stomach as the example.

<div class="step"><b>Cell.</b> One column-shaped lining cell of the stomach that makes mucus and acid.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Tissue.</b> A sheet of many such cells: epithelial tissue.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Organ.</b> The stomach: epithelium, smooth muscle, connective and nervous tissue together.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Organ system.</b> The digestive system: mouth to intestines, with the liver and pancreas.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Organism.</b> All systems working together as one animal.</div>

## 2. Animal tissues

A **tissue** is a group of similar cells (with their matrix) that do a particular job. There are four kinds.

### Epithelial tissue

Epithelium covers the outside of the body and lines every cavity and tube. Its cells fit closely with very little matrix, rest on a **basement membrane**, and have no blood vessels (food diffuses in from beneath).

- **Simple epithelium** has one layer: **squamous** (flat; lining of alveoli and blood vessels, for fast exchange), **cuboidal** (cubes; kidney tubules and glands, for secretion and absorption) and **columnar** (tall; stomach and intestine, for absorption; some with **cilia** in the windpipe and fallopian tube, some with microvilli).
- **Compound (stratified) epithelium** has many layers for protection against wear: skin surface, mouth, pharynx.
- **Glandular epithelium** is made of cells that secrete. **Unicellular** glands are single cells (goblet cells make mucus); **multicellular** glands are groups of cells (salivary gland). **Exocrine** glands send a secretion out through a duct; **endocrine** glands have no duct and release hormones into the blood.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c11b-sorg-epithelium"><figcaption>Pick an epithelium. Match the cell shape to the job: flat for fast exchange, tall for absorbing, layered for protecting, with cilia for moving mucus.</figcaption></figure>

Cells in a tissue are held together by **cell junctions**. **Tight junctions** seal the gap between cells so nothing leaks past (the skin, the gut wall). **Adhering junctions** glue cells together like spot welds. **Gap junctions** are tiny channels that let ions and small molecules pass straight from one cell to the next (in the heart, so beats spread together).

### Connective tissue

Connective tissue joins, supports and packs. Its cells are scattered in a **matrix** (fluid, jelly or solid) that they make. Fibres (collagen: strong; elastic: stretchy) lie in the matrix.

- **Loose connective tissue:** areolar tissue under the skin and between organs (fibroblasts, macrophages and mast cells); **adipose tissue** (fat cells store energy and insulate).
- **Dense connective tissue:** packed with fibres. **Dense regular** (tendons join muscle to bone, ligaments join bone to bone). **Dense irregular** (fibres in all directions: skin dermis).
- **Specialised connective tissue:** **cartilage** (firm, flexible matrix: nose, ear, windpipe, disc between vertebrae); **bone** (hard matrix with calcium salts and collagen, and canals; it supports and protects); **blood** (fluid matrix, the plasma, with red cells, white cells and platelets).

<div class="myth">

**“Blood is not a tissue; it is just a liquid.”** Blood is a connective tissue. Its cells (red, white, platelets) are in a matrix (plasma) that is made by the body, and it joins and serves all other tissues, like other connective tissues.

</div>

### Muscle tissue

Muscle cells (fibres) are long and can **contract**: they are packed with protein filaments (actin and myosin, see the chapter on movement). There are three kinds.

- **Skeletal (striated, voluntary):** long cylinder fibres with stripes and many nuclei; they pull on bones, in the biceps, legs and face.
- **Smooth (unstriated, involuntary):** spindle cells with one nucleus in the walls of the gut, blood vessels and bladder; slow and steady.
- **Cardiac (striated, involuntary):** branching cells with one nucleus, joined by **intercalated discs** (with gap junctions) that make the whole heart beat in time. Cardiac muscle does not tire.

### Neural tissue

Neural (nervous) tissue controls and coordinates the body. Its working cells are **neurons**: a cell body with branches (**dendrites** to receive, a long **axon** to send) that carry electrical signals. Between and around neurons are **neuroglial cells**, which feed, support and protect them (they make up more than half of the volume of the brain). Neurons are covered in a later chapter on neural control.

## 3. Organ and organ system

Organs are made of several tissues, but one tissue usually does the main job (muscle in the heart, nervous tissue in the brain). Organs that work on one job form a **system**: digestive, respiratory, circulatory, excretory, nervous, endocrine, reproductive and the muscle and skeleton system. As animals get more complex, the organs become more specialised and the systems more sharply divided. The next three sections show how this works in three animals, from the simplest to the most complex of them. (Check your school's syllabus; some boards ask all three, some only parts.)

## 4. Earthworm

The earthworm (*Pheretima*) lives in moist soil in burrows, feeds on organic matter and helps to plough and fertilise the soil. Its body is long, cylindrical and **segmented** (100 to 120 segments), with a thick band, the **clitellum**, on segments 14 to 16. It has no legs; short **setae** (bristles) in each segment grip the soil as it crawls. Moist skin takes in oxygen directly, so it is breathing through the skin.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c11b-sorg-earthworm"><figcaption>Switch between the digestive, circulatory and reproductive systems. The numbers on the bar are segments; the first 26 are drawn.</figcaption></figure>

- **Digestive system:** a straight tube from mouth to anus. Mouth, buccal cavity, **pharynx** (with saliva), **oesophagus**, **gizzard** (grinds), **stomach**, **intestine** with a folded **typhlosole** that increases the surface.
- **Circulatory system:** closed, with blood in vessels (dorsal and ventral vessels joined by hearts). Red haemoglobin is dissolved in the plasma.
- **Excretory system:** coiled tubes called **nephridia** (septal, pharyngeal and integumentary) take away waste and keep the water balance.
- **Nervous system:** a nerve ring round the pharynx and a ventral nerve cord with ganglia in each segment.
- **Reproductive system:** **hermaphrodite** (both sexes in one body), but two worms swap sperm. Fertilisation is external, in a **cocoon** made by the clitellum; the young that hatch are small worms (direct development).

## 5. Cockroach

The cockroach (*Periplaneta americana*) is an insect with a brown, flat body of **head, thorax and abdomen**, covered by a hard chitin skeleton in plates (**sclerites**). It has **6 jointed legs** and **two pairs of wings** (the front pair, the tegmina, are hard and protect the delicate hind wings). The head has two **compound eyes** (mosaic vision), a pair of antennae and **mouthparts** to bite and chew.

- **Digestive system:** mouth, pharynx, oesophagus, **crop** (stores), **gizzard** (grinds with chitin teeth), then **hepatic caecae** (make digestive juice) and the midgut and hindgut. At the start of the hindgut are **Malpighian tubules** which remove waste (uric acid).
- **Respiratory system:** a network of air tubes, **tracheae**, ending in tiny tracheoles, open through pores called **spiracles** along the sides. Oxygen goes straight to the cells, not through the blood.
- **Circulatory system:** **open**. A heart of 13 chambers pushes haemolymph (colourless; no haemoglobin) into the body cavity, which bathes the organs.
- **Reproductive system:** separate sexes. The female lays eggs in a case, the **ootheca**; the young are **nymphs**, which moult about 13 times before they become adults.

## 6. Frog

The common frog (*Rana tigrina*) lives on land and in water, which is why it is an **amphibian**. Its skin is smooth, moist and without scales; the colour changes (camouflage) and in winter and summer it hides in burrows (hibernates and aestivates). The head and trunk bear two pairs of limbs: the hind limbs are long with webbed feet for swimming and jumping.

- **Digestive system:** a short gut, because it eats meat (insects). Food goes from the mouth through the oesophagus into the stomach and the intestine, helped by the **liver** (bile) and **pancreas**. A long tongue catches insects.
- **Respiratory system:** the frog breathes through the **skin** (in water), the lining of the mouth (**buccal cavity**) and the **lungs** (on land).
- **Circulatory system:** closed, with a **three-chambered heart** (two atria, one ventricle) in a single circuit.
- **Excretory system:** two kidneys; the frog makes urea (ureotelic).
- **Reproduction:** separate sexes with external fertilisation in water; the eggs hatch into **tadpoles** which become frogs by **metamorphosis**.

<div class="keypoints">

- Levels: cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism.
- Epithelium: squamous, cuboidal, columnar (simple); stratified (compound); glandular. Junctions: tight, adhering, gap.
- Connective tissue: loose (areolar, adipose), dense (regular, irregular), cartilage, bone, blood. Matrix decides the job.
- Muscle: skeletal (striated, voluntary), smooth (involuntary), cardiac (striated, involuntary, intercalated discs).
- Neural tissue: neurons with dendrites and an axon, plus neuroglia.
- Earthworm: segmented, clitellum at 14 to 16, closed circulation, nephridia, hermaphrodite.
- Cockroach: head, thorax, abdomen, tracheae, open circulation, Malpighian tubules, nymphs.
- Frog: amphibian, three-chambered heart, skin breathing, tadpole and metamorphosis.

</div>
