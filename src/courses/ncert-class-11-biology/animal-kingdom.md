**Biology · Class 11 · Chapter 4 · about 70 minutes.** By the end you will be able to say how animals are classified, describe each of the eleven main phyla with examples, and compare the classes of vertebrates.

<div class="bigidea">More than a million animal species are named. We sort them by asking a few body-plan questions: how are the cells organised, how is the body arranged (symmetry), how many germ layers, is there a body cavity (coelom), is it segmented, is there a notochord? Each answer splits the kingdom, and the answers together give the eleven phyla.</div>

## 1. Basis of classification

1. **Levels of organisation.** *Cellular* (cells are loosely arranged, e.g. sponges); *tissue* (cells of one kind form tissues, e.g. Cnidaria); *organ* (tissues make organs, e.g. flatworms); *organ system* (organs work in systems, e.g. annelids to chordates).
2. **Symmetry.** *Asymmetrical* (no plane divides it into equal halves; sponges); *radial* (any plane through the central axis gives equal halves; Hydra, starfish); *bilateral* (only one plane; a right and a left half; most animals).
3. **Germ layers (diploblastic and triploblastic).** Embryos have **ectoderm** (outside) and **endoderm** (inside). **Diploblastic** animals (Cnidaria) have only these two, with a jelly between. **Triploblastic** animals have a third layer, the **mesoderm**, between them. Follow it from the zygote.

<div class="steps" data-fig="c11b-animalk-germ">
<div class="step">The zygote is one diploid cell.</div>
<div class="step">Cleavage: repeated mitosis makes a ball of cells.</div>
<div class="step">A hollow ball, the blastula, forms.</div>
<div class="step">Gastrulation: one side folds inwards to give ectoderm and endoderm.</div>
<div class="step">Diploblastic animals stop at two layers.</div>
<div class="step">Triploblastic animals add mesoderm; in coelomates a coelom opens inside it.</div>
</div>

4. **Coelom.** The body cavity lined by mesoderm. **Coelomates** have a true coelom (annelids, molluscs, arthropods, echinoderms, chordates). **Pseudocoelomates** have a cavity between gut and body wall where mesoderm is only in scattered patches (roundworms). **Acoelomates** have no cavity (flatworms).
5. **Segmentation (metamerism).** The body is divided into repeated units with some organs repeated (earthworm, with over a hundred segments).
6. **Notochord.** A rod of cells along the back. Animals with it are **chordates**; the rest are **non-chordates**.

<div class="myth">

**Myth: "A coelom is any body cavity."** A pseudocoelom is also a cavity, but only in a true coelom does mesoderm line the cavity on both the body-wall side and the gut side. The lining is what counts.

</div>

## 2. The eleven phyla (non-chordates and chordates)

Slide through the phyla below, from the simplest (Porifera) to the chordates, to see a sketch and the key facts of each.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c11b-animalk-phyla"><figcaption>Eleven phyla in order. The three rows show the level of organisation, symmetry and body cavity.</figcaption></figure>

### Phylum Porifera (sponges)

Mostly marine, **asymmetrical**, **cellular** level. The body has **pores** (ostia, one large osculum) and a canal system. **Choanocytes** (collar cells) with flagella create a water current that brings food and oxygen. A skeleton of **spicules** or **spongin** fibres. Reproduction: fragmentation, and sexual (**hermaphrodite**; eggs and sperm from the same individual; larva). Examples: *Sycon*, *Spongilla* (fresh water), *Euspongia* (bath sponge).

### Phylum Cnidaria (Coelenterata)

Aquatic, mostly marine; **radial**, **tissue** level, **diploblastic**. **Cnidoblasts** (stinging cells, with nematocysts) on the tentacles catch prey and defend. A central gut cavity (**coelenteron**) with one opening. Two body forms: the fixed **polyp** (*Hydra*) and the free-swimming **medusa** (*Aurelia*, jellyfish); some have both and alternate (*Obelia*). **Corals** build reefs of calcium carbonate. Examples: *Physalia*, *Adamsia* (sea anemone), *Pennatula*, *Gorgonia*, *Meandrina* (brain coral).

### Phylum Ctenophora (sea walnuts, comb jellies)

Marine, **radial**, **diploblastic**, tissue level. **Eight external rows of ciliated comb plates** for locomotion; **bioluminescence**; no stinging cells. Examples: *Pleurobrachia*, *Ctenoplana*.

### Phylum Platyhelminthes (flatworms)

**Dorsoventrally flattened**, **bilateral**, **triploblastic**, **acoelomate**, **organ level**. Many are parasites (**hooks and suckers** for holding to the host). **Flame cells** remove waste and regulate water. Mostly **hermaphrodite**; **regeneration** in *Planaria*. Examples: *Taenia* (tapeworm), *Fasciola* (liver fluke).

### Phylum Aschelminthes (roundworms)

**Circular in cross-section**, **bilateral**, **triploblastic**, **pseudocoelomate**, **organ-system** level. **Complete digestive tract** with mouth and anus. The sexes are **separate** (dioecious), females longer than males, with internal fertilisation. Many are parasites: *Ascaris* (roundworm), *Wuchereria* (filarial worm, which causes filariasis or elephantiasis), *Ancylostoma* (hookworm).

### Phylum Annelida (segmented worms)

Aquatic or terrestrial, **bilateral**, **triploblastic**, **coelomate**, **metamerically segmented** (the body surface shows rings). Longitudinal and circular **muscles** for movement. **Closed circulatory system.** **Nephridia** remove waste. Neural system: a paired **ventral nerve cord**. *Nereis* (sandworm) has parapodia for swimming. Earthworms (*Pheretima*) and leeches (*Hirudinaria*) are **hermaphrodites**.

### Phylum Arthropoda (jointed legs)

The **largest phylum**: more than two-thirds of all named species. Bilateral, triploblastic, coelomate, **segmented**, with an **exoskeleton of chitin** and **jointed appendages**. The body is usually divided into **head, thorax and abdomen**, with the coelom filled with blood (**haemocoel**) and an **open circulatory system**. Respiration by gills, book gills, book lungs or **tracheae**. Sense organs: antennae, compound eyes. Excretion by **Malpighian tubules**. Mostly **dioecious**, internal fertilisation. Examples: *Apis* (honey bee), *Bombyx* (silkworm), *Anopheles* (malaria mosquito), *Locusta*, *Laccifer* (lac insect), *Palaemon* (prawn), *Limulus* (king crab, a "living fossil").

### Phylum Mollusca

The **second largest phylum**. Terrestrial or aquatic (mostly marine). Bilateral, triploblastic, coelomate, **organ-system** level. A **calcareous shell** (usually); a soft **spongy mantle** over the visceral hump; a muscular **foot**; a feeding organ with rows of teeth, the **radula**. Respiration by **gills (ctenidia)**. Mostly dioecious. Examples: *Pila* (apple snail), *Pinctada* (pearl oyster), *Sepia* (cuttlefish), *Loligo* (squid), *Octopus* ("devil fish"), *Aplysia*, *Dentalium*, *Chaetopleura*.

### Phylum Echinodermata

Spiny skin (endoskeleton of **calcareous ossicles**), **all marine**. Adults **radially symmetrical**, larvae bilateral. **Water vascular system** with **tube feet** for locomotion, food capture and respiration. Digestive tract complete; **no excretory organs**. Sexes separate. Examples: *Asterias* (starfish), *Echinus* (sea urchin), *Antedon* (sea lily), *Cucumaria* (sea cucumber), *Ophiura* (brittle star).

<div class="problem" data-problem="c11b-animalk-tubefeet"></div>

### Phylum Hemichordata

A small group of worm-like marine animals with an **organ-system** level, **bilateral**, triploblastic, coelomate. The body has **proboscis, collar and trunk**. Open circulatory system, respiration through **gills**. Sexes separate, external fertilisation. *Balanoglossus* and *Saccoglossus*. (In the earlier books they were a subphylum of Chordata; they are now treated as a separate phylum.)

### Phylum Chordata

Animals with **a notochord** (a rod from the mesoderm, present at some stage), a **dorsal hollow nerve cord**, **paired pharyngeal gill slits** and a **post-anal tail**. **Closed circulatory system** with a ventral heart; bilateral, triploblastic, coelomate, organ-system level. A non-chordate has a **ventral solid** nerve cord, no notochord, usually no gill slits, and a dorsal heart if present. Switch between the two.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c11b-animalk-chordate"><figcaption>The four chordate characters in an embryo, a fish, an adult human and a non-chordate, so you can see what is lost or replaced in the adult.</figcaption></figure>

Chordata has three subphyla: **Urochordata** (tunicates, e.g. *Ascidia*), **Cephalochordata** (lancelet, *Branchiostoma*) with a notochord extending the whole body, and **Vertebrata**, in which the notochord is replaced by a **vertebral column** in the adult. Vertebrates have a ventral heart with 2 to 4 chambers, kidneys for excretion, and paired fins or limbs.

## 3. The vertebrates

**Superclass Agnatha** has no jaws (**Class Cyclostomata**: *Petromyzon*, lamprey; *Myxine*, hagfish). **Superclass Gnathostomata** has jaws: fishes (**Chondrichthyes**, **Osteichthyes**) and the tetrapods (**Amphibia, Reptilia, Aves, Mammalia**).

| Class | Skin, scales | Skeleton | Heart | Temperature | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cyclostomata | slimy, no scales | cartilage, no jaws | 2 chambers | cold-blooded | lamprey, hagfish |
| Chondrichthyes | placoid scales | cartilage | 2 | cold-blooded | shark, ray |
| Osteichthyes | cycloid or ctenoid scales | bone; air bladder | 2 | cold-blooded | rohu, catla, seahorse |
| Amphibia | moist skin, no scales | bone | 3 (2 atria, 1 ventricle) | cold-blooded | frog, toad |
| Reptilia | dry skin, scales | bone | 3 (4 in crocodile) | cold-blooded | lizard, snake, turtle |
| Aves | feathers; scales on legs | light bones with air cavities | 4 | warm-blooded | crow, pigeon |
| Mammalia | hair, glands | bone | 4 | warm-blooded | human, whale, bat |

**Extra facts for the exam.** Cartilaginous fish have **gill slits with no operculum**, are **viviparous**, and sink if they stop swimming; bony fish have an **operculum** that covers the gills and an **air bladder** that gives buoyancy. Amphibians breathe through gills, lungs and moist skin and fertilise externally. Reptiles lay **shelled eggs** on land with internal fertilisation. Birds have **air sacs** connected to the lungs and **hollow bones**. Mammals have **mammary glands**, **external ears (pinnae)**, a diaphragm, and most are viviparous (the **egg-laying** mammal *Ornithorhynchus*, the platypus, is an exception), while the **kangaroo** carries its young in a pouch.

<div class="history">

**Beyond the syllabus.** DNA studies have rearranged some animal groups. For example, roundworms and arthropods are close relatives (the "moulting animals", Ecdysozoa), while flatworms and molluscs are placed together with annelids. The textbook phyla are still the basis of the exam, though.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Classification uses level of organisation, symmetry, germ layers (diploblastic or triploblastic), coelom, segmentation and notochord.
- **Porifera** (cellular, pores, spicules); **Cnidaria** (cnidoblasts, polyp and medusa); **Ctenophora** (8 comb rows); **Platyhelminthes** (acoelomate, flame cells); **Aschelminthes** (pseudocoelomate); **Annelida** (segmented, closed circulation); **Arthropoda** (chitin, jointed legs, largest); **Mollusca** (shell, foot, radula); **Echinodermata** (water vascular system, radial adult); **Hemichordata** (proboscis, collar, trunk); **Chordata** (notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, gill slits).
- Vertebrate classes: Cyclostomata, Chondrichthyes, Osteichthyes, Amphibia, Reptilia, Aves, Mammalia; heart chambers 2, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4.

</div>
