**Practice · Class 11 Biology · Chapter 12.** Read the chapter lesson, [Respiration in Plants](/learn/courses/ncert-class-11-biology/respiration-in-plants/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Keep a table in your head: stage, place, inputs, outputs. Glycolysis: cytoplasm, glucose → 2 pyruvate, 2 ATP, 2 NADH. Link: matrix, pyruvate → acetyl CoA, CO₂, NADH. Krebs: matrix, acetyl CoA → 2 CO₂, 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 ATP per turn. ETC: inner membrane, NADH and FADH₂ + O₂ → water and about 34 ATP.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For ATP counts, give a table with NADH, FADH₂ and direct ATP and say which values you use (3 per NADH, 2 per FADH₂).
2. For "differentiate" questions use a two-column table.
3. For an RQ calculation write the equation, then the ratio CO₂ ÷ O₂.

## 1-mark questions

1. Where does glycolysis take place?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>In the cytoplasm.</details>

2. Name the first stable product of the link reaction.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Acetyl CoA.</details>

3. What is the RQ of anaerobic respiration?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Infinity (CO₂ is released and no O₂ is used).</details>

4. Which enzyme makes ATP in the inner mitochondrial membrane?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>ATP synthase (complex V).</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

5. Why do yeast cells grow faster in the presence of oxygen than in its absence?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>With O₂, one glucose gives about 38 ATP; in fermentation only 2. More ATP means more energy for growth and division.</details>

6. Differentiate between aerobic respiration and fermentation (three points).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

| Aerobic respiration | Fermentation |
|---|---|
| needs O₂ | no O₂ |
| complete oxidation to CO₂ and H₂O | incomplete: ethanol or lactic acid |
| about 38 ATP | 2 ATP |
| in cytoplasm and mitochondria | in the cytoplasm only |

</details>

7. Why is respiration called an amphibolic pathway?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It breaks down carbohydrates, fats and proteins (catabolism) and its intermediates (acetyl CoA, Krebs cycle acids) are also used to build fatty acids and amino acids (anabolism).</details>

8. A germinating castor seed has RQ 0.7 and a germinating pea seed has RQ 1. What can you say about the stored food?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Castor seed stores mainly fat (RQ about 0.7, a lot of O₂ is needed). Pea stores mainly starch (carbohydrate, RQ 1).</details>

9. How many ATP are made when 5 glucose molecules are completely oxidised (textbook count)?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

38 per glucose, so 5 × 38 = **190 ATP**.

</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

10. Describe the Krebs cycle: the starting molecules, the CO₂ released and the energy carriers formed.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Acetyl CoA (2 C) joins oxaloacetic acid (4 C) to form citric acid (6 C). Citric acid loses CO₂ to give α-ketoglutaric acid (5 C) and another CO₂ to give succinyl CoA (4 C). Succinyl CoA is converted to succinic acid with one ATP (GTP). Succinic acid gives FADH₂ and goes to malic acid, which gives NADH and returns as OAA. Per turn: 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 ATP, 2 CO₂. For one glucose the cycle runs twice.

</details>

11. Explain the electron transport system and ATP synthesis.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

NADH gives electrons to complex I and FADH₂ to complex II. Electrons pass through ubiquinone, complex III, cytochrome c and complex IV. As they flow downhill, H⁺ are pumped from the matrix into the space between the membranes, making a proton gradient. At complex IV the electrons and H⁺ join O₂ to make water. H⁺ flow back through ATP synthase, which makes ATP (oxidative phosphorylation).

</details>

12. Draw up the ATP balance sheet for one glucose in aerobic respiration.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Glycolysis: 2 ATP + 2 NADH (6) = 8. Link reaction: 2 NADH = 6. Krebs cycle: 2 ATP + 6 NADH (18) + 2 FADH₂ (4) = 24. Total = **38 ATP** (textbook, theoretical).

</details>

## Case-based question

A student seals germinating wheat seeds in a flask with a small tube of KOH solution (which absorbs CO₂). The liquid in a bent tube moves toward the flask by 12 mm. In a second flask with only water in place of KOH the liquid does not move.

13. (a) Why does the liquid move in the first flask? (b) Why not in the second? (c) What would you measure to get the RQ? (d) What RQ is expected for wheat (carbohydrate)?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) CO₂ is absorbed by KOH, so only the O₂ used reduces the volume; the pressure falls. (b) CO₂ released equals O₂ used (RQ about 1), so the volume stays the same. (c) The volume of O₂ used (flask 1) and the volume of CO₂ released = (O₂ used) − (change when no KOH). (d) About 1.</details>

## Assertion and reason

For each, choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

14. **A:** Cyanide poisoning stops ATP formation. **R:** Cyanide blocks cytochrome c oxidase, so electrons cannot reach oxygen.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) The chain jams at complex IV, the proton gradient is not built and ATP synthase stops.</details>

15. **A:** Fermentation produces less ATP than aerobic respiration. **R:** The products of fermentation still contain much energy.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) The glucose is only partly oxidised, and ethanol or lactic acid still carry most of the energy.</details>

16. **A:** The RQ of fats is less than 1. **R:** Fats contain more oxygen than carbohydrates.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(C) A is true; R is false. Fats contain less oxygen than carbohydrates, so more O₂ must be taken in to burn them.</details>

- Glycolysis 2 ATP net; Krebs per turn 3 NADH + 1 FADH₂ + 1 ATP; total 38 ATP (textbook).
- O₂ is the last electron acceptor; ATP synthase uses the H⁺ gradient.
- RQ: carbohydrate 1, fat 0.7, protein 0.9, organic acids above 1, anaerobic infinite.
- Respiration is amphibolic.
