**Practice · Class 11 Biology · Chapter 3.** Read the chapter lesson, [Plant Kingdom](/learn/courses/ncert-class-11-biology/plant-kingdom/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Most questions here ask "which group, and why?". Use a ladder: thallus and no vascular tissue means algae or bryophytes; vascular tissue and spores means pteridophytes; seeds with no fruit means gymnosperms; flowers and fruits mean angiosperms. For life cycles always say what is n and what is 2n.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For "differentiate", use a table with at least three points.
2. For life cycles, draw a labelled circle with n and 2n marked, and name where meiosis and fertilisation occur.
3. Give an example for each group.
4. Mark chromosome numbers when asked.

## 1-mark questions

**Q1.** Name the pigment which gives brown algae their colour.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Fucoxanthin.</details>

**Q2.** What is a protonema?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The first, thread-like stage that grows from a moss spore.</details>

**Q3.** Name the first plant group to have vascular tissue.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Pteridophytes.</details>

**Q4.** What are naked seeds?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Seeds that are not covered by a fruit, because the ovule is not inside an ovary (gymnosperms).</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

**Q5.** Why are bryophytes called the amphibians of the plant kingdom?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>They live on land, but the antherozoids swim in water to reach the egg, so fertilisation needs water.</details>

**Q6.** Differentiate green, brown and red algae on three points.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

| | Green | Brown | Red |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pigments | chlorophyll a, b | chlorophyll a, c, fucoxanthin | chlorophyll a, d, phycoerythrin |
| Food stored | starch | laminarin, mannitol | floridean starch |
| Example | *Spirogyra* | *Fucus* | *Gracilaria* |

</details>

**Q7.** What is heterospory? Why is it important?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Making two kinds of spores: small microspores (male) and large megaspores (female), as in <i>Selaginella</i>. The female gametophyte develops inside the megaspore on the sporophyte, an important step to the seed habit.</details>

**Q8.** Explain why gymnosperms do not need water for fertilisation.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Pollen grains are carried by wind to the ovule, and a pollen tube grows to the egg and delivers the male gametes. The gametes are not required to swim.</details>

**Q9.** A moss capsule has 120 spore mother cells. How many spores are formed?

<div class="problem" data-problem="c11b-plantk-spores"></div>

<details><summary>Answer</summary>120 × 4 = 480 spores.</details>

**Q10.** Distinguish haplontic and diplontic life cycles with an example of each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Haplontic: the body is haploid and the zygote is the only diploid cell (<i>Spirogyra</i>). Diplontic: the body is diploid and only the gametes are haploid (pine, mango).</details>

## 5-mark questions

**Q11.** Describe the life cycle of a moss. Mark the haploid and diploid stages.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A haploid spore (n) grows into the protonema (n), which grows buds that become the leafy gametophyte (n). Antheridia make antherozoids and archegonia make eggs (by mitosis). Antherozoids swim in water and fertilise the egg to give the zygote (2n), which grows into the sporophyte (2n) of foot, seta and capsule. Meiosis in the capsule gives haploid spores, which are scattered by wind.</details>

**Q12.** Describe the life cycle of a fern.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The fern plant is the diploid sporophyte with sori on leaves. Meiosis in sporangia gives haploid spores. A spore grows into a small free-living heart-shaped prothallus (n), which bears antheridia and archegonia. Antherozoids swim in a film of water to the egg; the zygote (2n) grows into a young sporophyte that is first nourished by the prothallus.</details>

**Q13.** Compare bryophytes, pteridophytes and gymnosperms in a table.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

| | Bryophytes | Pteridophytes | Gymnosperms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vascular tissue | absent | present | present |
| Dominant phase | gametophyte | sporophyte | sporophyte |
| Water for fertilisation | needed | needed | not needed |
| Seeds | none | none | naked seeds |
| Example | *Funaria* | *Pteris* | *Pinus* |

</details>

## Case-based question

On a trek to the Western Ghats a student finds three plants: a green, flat, ribbon-like plant on a wet rock with tiny cups of buds on top; a fern with brown dots under its leaves; and a pine tree with two kinds of cones.

**(a)** Identify the group of each plant.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Liverwort (bryophyte, <i>Marchantia</i>); a pteridophyte; a gymnosperm.</details>

**(b)** What are the brown dots, and what do they contain?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Sori, groups of sporangia, in which meiosis makes haploid spores.</details>

**(c)** Which of the three does not need water for fertilisation, and why?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The pine: pollen grains are carried by wind and the pollen tube delivers the male gamete.</details>

## Assertion and reason

For each, choose: (a) both true and R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

**Q14.** **A:** Red algae can live in deep water. **R:** They have phycoerythrin, which absorbs blue-green light.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). Red light does not reach depth, but blue-green does.</details>

**Q15.** **A:** The sporophyte of a fern is diploid. **R:** It is formed from a zygote.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). The zygote is formed by fusion of two haploid gametes, so it is 2n.</details>
