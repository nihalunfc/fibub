## 1-mark questions

1. Name the pacemaker of the heart.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The sinoatrial (SA) node.</details>

2. Which blood group is the universal donor?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Group O (Rh negative for the safest case).</details>

3. What is the normal blood pressure of a healthy adult?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>About 120/80 mm Hg.</details>

4. Which blood vessel carries deoxygenated blood from the heart to the lungs?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The pulmonary artery.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

5. Differentiate between arteries and veins (any three points).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

| Arteries | Veins |
|---|---|
| carry blood away from the heart | carry blood to the heart |
| thick, elastic, muscular wall | thin wall |
| no valves, narrow lumen | valves, wide lumen |
| high pressure, pulse felt | low pressure, no pulse |

</details>

6. What is the role of the AV node?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It receives the impulse from the atria and delays it for about 0.1 s so that the ventricles fill before they contract, and it passes it to the bundle of His.</details>

7. What is lymph and how does it differ from plasma?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Lymph is the fluid in the lymph vessels, formed from tissue fluid. It is colourless, has much less protein than plasma and has no red cells, but contains many lymphocytes; it carries absorbed fats.</details>

8. Why does a person with blood group O⁻ give blood to anyone, but can receive only O⁻?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The red cells of O⁻ have no A, B or Rh antigens so they are not attacked by any recipient's antibodies. But a person of group O has anti-A and anti-B antibodies and Rh⁻ people may make anti-Rh, so they can receive only O⁻.</details>

9. A person's ECG shows 6 QRS complexes in 4 seconds. What is the heart rate?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

6 beats in 4 s = 1.5 beats per second = 1.5 × 60 = **90 beats per minute**.

</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

10. Describe the cardiac cycle with the timing of each phase.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

One cycle lasts about 0.8 s. **Atrial systole (0.1 s):** the atria contract and push the last blood into the ventricles through the open AV valves. **Ventricular systole (0.3 s):** the ventricles contract, the AV valves shut ("lub"), the pressure opens the semilunar valves and blood is ejected into the aorta and pulmonary artery. **Joint diastole (0.4 s):** the heart relaxes, the semilunar valves shut ("dub"), the AV valves open and the chambers fill. Stroke volume about 70 mL, cardiac output about 5 L/min.

</details>

11. Describe the double circulation of blood in humans.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Right atrium → (tricuspid valve) → right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs (blood gains O₂, loses CO₂) → pulmonary veins → left atrium → (bicuspid valve) → left ventricle → aorta → body tissues → vena cavae → right atrium. The right side pumps to the lungs (pulmonary circuit) and the left side to the body (systemic circuit); the blood passes twice through the heart per lap, and oxygenated and deoxygenated blood do not mix.

</details>

12. Explain the clotting of blood.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

On injury, platelets and damaged tissue release thromboplastin. With Ca²⁺ it converts prothrombin to thrombin. Thrombin converts the soluble fibrinogen to insoluble fibrin threads. The threads form a mesh that traps blood cells and forms the clot.

</details>

## Case-based question

A doctor listens to a child's heart with a stethoscope and hears the usual "lub-dub", but also a soft whoosh between "lub" and "dub". An echocardiogram shows that the bicuspid valve does not close fully when the left ventricle contracts.

13. (a) What does "lub" represent? (b) During which phase of the cycle is the whoosh heard? (c) Which way does blood leak? (d) What may happen to the left atrium over time?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) The AV valves closing at the start of ventricular systole. (b) During ventricular systole (between lub and dub). (c) Backwards from the left ventricle into the left atrium. (d) It may enlarge and the pressure in the lungs may rise, because it receives extra blood.</details>

## Assertion and reason

For each, choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

14. **A:** The heart is described as myogenic. **R:** The SA node starts the heartbeat without nerve impulses.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both are true and R explains A.</details>

15. **A:** Heart failure is the same as cardiac arrest. **R:** In heart failure the heart still beats, but too weakly to meet the body's needs.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(D) A is false: cardiac arrest is a sudden stop of the heart. R is true and describes heart failure.</details>

16. **A:** The left ventricle has a thicker wall than the right. **R:** The left ventricle pumps blood to the whole body at high pressure.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A.</details>
