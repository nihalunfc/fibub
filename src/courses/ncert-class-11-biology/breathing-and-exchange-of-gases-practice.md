<div class="bigidea">Volume questions are addition: start with TV, IRV, ERV, RV and combine. Pressure questions are direction: gases always go from high to low partial pressure. Write the numbers 159, 104, 40 for O₂ and 0.3, 40, 45 for CO₂ on a little card and trace the path.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For capacities, write the formula in words, then the numbers.
2. For transport, give the three forms of CO₂ with percentages.
3. For mechanism, say which muscles contract, what happens to chest volume and what happens to pressure.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the enzyme that speeds up the formation of carbonic acid in red blood cells.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Carbonic anhydrase.</details>

2. Which part of the brain has the respiratory rhythm centre?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The medulla oblongata.</details>

3. What is the thin membrane that covers each lung called?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The pleura (a double layer with fluid between).</details>

4. Which disease is caused by long exposure to stone-grinding dust?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Silicosis.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

5. Differentiate between inspiration and expiration.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

| Inspiration | Expiration |
|---|---|
| diaphragm contracts and flattens | diaphragm relaxes and rises |
| external intercostals lift the ribs | external intercostals relax; ribs drop |
| thoracic volume rises, pressure falls | thoracic volume falls, pressure rises |
| air flows in | air flows out |

</details>

6. Why does CO₂ cross the alveolar membrane easily even though its pressure difference (5 mm Hg) is much smaller than that of O₂ (about 64 mm Hg)?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>CO₂ is about 20 to 25 times more soluble in water (and in the membrane's fluid) than O₂, so a small gradient is enough.</details>

7. What is the Bohr effect? Why is it useful?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>An increase in pCO₂, a fall in pH or a rise in temperature shifts the oxygen dissociation curve to the right, so haemoglobin releases more O₂. It is useful because active tissues, which make CO₂ and acid and are warm, get more O₂.</details>

8. A person's TV is 450 mL, IRV 2800 mL, ERV 1000 mL and RV 1100 mL. Find VC and TLC.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

VC = 450 + 2800 + 1000 = **4250 mL**. TLC = 4250 + 1100 = **5350 mL**.

</details>

9. How does carbon monoxide poisoning cause a person to be short of oxygen?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>CO binds haemoglobin about 200 times more strongly than O₂, so haemoglobin cannot carry O₂ to the tissues.</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

10. Explain the mechanism of inspiration and expiration in humans.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Inspiration: the diaphragm contracts and flattens, and the external intercostal muscles contract and lift the ribs and sternum. The thoracic volume increases front to back and top to bottom, the lung volume rises and the intrapulmonary pressure falls below atmospheric, so air flows in. Expiration: the muscles relax, the diaphragm returns to its dome shape and the ribs fall, the thoracic and lung volume falls, the pressure rises above atmospheric and air is pushed out. Forced breathing uses extra abdominal and internal intercostal muscles.

</details>

11. Describe how CO₂ is transported in the blood.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

About 70 % as bicarbonate: in the red cell, carbonic anhydrase speeds CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻; HCO₃⁻ moves to the plasma and Cl⁻ moves in (chloride shift). About 20 to 25 % as carbamino-haemoglobin (CO₂ on the haemoglobin, favoured by high pCO₂ and low pO₂). About 7 % dissolved in the plasma. In the lungs, the low pCO₂ reverses the reactions and CO₂ is released.

</details>

12. Describe the control of breathing.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The rhythm centre in the medulla sets the rate. The pneumotaxic centre in the pons can shorten inspiration and so raise the rate. The chemosensitive area near the rhythm centre is sensitive to CO₂ and H⁺, and the receptors in the aortic arch and carotid artery send signals too. When CO₂ rises, the rhythm centre makes breathing faster and deeper until CO₂ is removed.

</details>

## Case-based question

A boy in Leh (altitude 3500 m) feels breathless on a walk, though a friend from the same town is fine. The air pressure there is about 500 mm Hg. A doctor takes a spirogram showing TV 500 mL, ERV 1000 mL and IRV 2000 mL, and notes that the boy's chest X-ray shows over-inflated lungs with damaged alveolar walls and that he smokes.

13. (a) Calculate the partial pressure of O₂ in Leh. (b) Calculate his vital capacity. (c) Name the disease suggested by the X-ray. (d) Why does this cause breathlessness?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) 21 % of 500 = about 105 mm Hg (against 159 at sea level); this is the air breathed, so alveolar pO₂ is lower still. (b) 1000 + 500 + 2000 = 3500 mL. (c) Emphysema, caused by smoking. (d) The alveolar walls are destroyed, so there is less surface area for gas exchange, and with the thin air of altitude too little O₂ gets into the blood.</details>

## Assertion and reason

For each, choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

14. **A:** Alveolar air has a lower pO₂ than the atmosphere. **R:** Alveolar air is mixed with the air left in the lungs and gains CO₂ and water vapour.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A (159 against 104 mm Hg).</details>

15. **A:** The foetus gets oxygen from its mother's blood. **R:** Foetal haemoglobin has a lower affinity for O₂ than adult haemoglobin.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(C) A is true, R is false: foetal haemoglobin has a higher affinity, so the curve is to the left and it can take O₂ from maternal blood.</details>

16. **A:** Breathing becomes faster during exercise. **R:** The rise in CO₂ and H⁺ stimulates the chemosensitive area.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- VC = TV + IRV + ERV; TLC = VC + RV; FRC = ERV + RV.
- Gases diffuse down partial pressure gradients; CO₂ is more soluble.
- CO₂: 70 % bicarbonate, 20-25 % carbamino-Hb, 7 % dissolved. O₂: 97 % on haemoglobin.
- Bohr effect: more CO₂, H⁺ and heat shift the curve right.

</div>
