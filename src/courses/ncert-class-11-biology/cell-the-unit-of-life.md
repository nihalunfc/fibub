**Biology · Class 11 · Chapter 8 · about 80 minutes.** By the end you will be able to state the cell theory, compare prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells, describe the fluid mosaic membrane, name each organelle with its job, and tell a plant cell from an animal cell.

<div class="bigidea">Every living thing is made of cells, and every cell comes from another cell. A cell is a tiny factory in a closed room: the membrane is the wall and gate, the nucleus is the office with the blueprints, and the organelles are the workshops. Small cells with big surfaces work best, so living things are made of many small cells, not a few giant ones.</div>

## 1. What is a cell?

A **cell** is the smallest unit of life that can live and reproduce on its own. Anton van Leeuwenhoek saw living cells first. Robert Hooke (1665) saw the empty boxes of cork and named them "cells". In 1838 Matthias Schleiden (a botanist) and in 1839 Theodor Schwann (a zoologist) said that plants and animals are made of cells, and in 1855 **Rudolf Virchow** added that new cells come only from old cells (*omnis cellula e cellula*). These ideas together are the **cell theory**:

1. All living things are made of one or more cells and their products.
2. The cell is the basic unit of structure and function.
3. All cells arise from pre-existing cells.

Viruses do not fit well: they are not cells, and they multiply only inside a host cell.

Cells vary hugely in size. How large are they, and what can we see?

### Why are cells small?

A cell takes food and oxygen in through its surface and uses them in its volume. If the side of a cube-shaped cell is `$L$`, then the surface is `$6L^2$` and the volume is `$L^3$`, so

```math
\frac{S}{V} = \frac{6L^2}{L^3} = \frac{6}{L}
```

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>S</dt><dd>surface area (membrane for supply)</dd>
<dt>V</dt><dd>volume (cytoplasm that needs supply)</dd>
<dt>L</dt><dd>side of the cube-shaped cell</dd>
<dt>Meaning</dt><dd>It is like a tiffin box: the bigger the box, the more food inside for each inch of lid you can open. A huge cell would starve in the middle, so cells stay small, or become long, flat or folded (nerve cells, red cells, the lining of the intestine).</dd>
</dl>

## 2. Prokaryotic cells

**Prokaryotes** (bacteria, cyanobacteria, archaea, mycoplasma) are the simplest cells. Typically they are 1 to 5 µm, with no nucleus and no membrane-bound organelles.

- **Cell envelope:** a rigid **cell wall** (peptidoglycan; this is what penicillin attacks), the **plasma membrane**, and, in some, a slimy **capsule** (or thin slime layer). Mycoplasma, the smallest cells (about 0.3 µm), have no cell wall.
- **Genetic material:** one circular, naked DNA in an area called the **nucleoid**, and often small circles called **plasmids** that carry extras such as antibiotic resistance. No histones.
- **Ribosomes:** the **70S** type, floating free (the S stands for Svedberg units; the numbers do not add up, 50S + 30S = 70S).
- **Mesosomes:** infoldings of the membrane that help respiration and cell division.
- **Motion and sticking:** a **flagellum** (rotates to swim), **pili** and **fimbriae** (stick to surfaces; sex pili join two bacteria).
- **Inclusion bodies:** stored food as granules (phosphate, glycogen), or gas vacuoles in some cyanobacteria. Cyanobacteria and purple bacteria have photosynthetic membranes called **chromatophores**.

## 3. Eukaryotic cells

**Eukaryotes** (protists, fungi, plants, animals) have a true **nucleus** and membrane-bound organelles. They are 10 to 100 µm. **Animal cells** have centrioles and lysosomes; **plant cells** have a cell wall, plastids and a big vacuole. Tap each part in the drawing; choose the kind of cell.

## 4. Cell membrane and cell wall

### The plasma membrane

The **plasma membrane** surrounds every cell. In 1972 Singer and Nicolson proposed the **fluid mosaic model**: the membrane is a double layer (**bilayer**) of **phospholipids**, whose water-loving heads face out and water-hating tails face in, with proteins floating in it like a mosaic and kept in place by cholesterol and the cell's skeleton. The proteins can be **integral** (cross the whole membrane: channels and carriers) or **peripheral** (on the surface). Sugar chains on the outside form the **glycocalyx**, a cell identity tag. The membrane is **fluid** because lipids and proteins move sideways.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c11b-cell-membrane"><figcaption>Slide the temperature and switch cholesterol on and off. Heat makes the membrane runnier; cold makes it stiffer; cholesterol keeps it in a safe middle.</figcaption></figure>

The membrane is **selectively permeable**. Small non-polar molecules (O₂, CO₂) slip through the lipid layer by **simple diffusion**. Ions and polar molecules need proteins: **facilitated diffusion** through a channel or carrier (no energy, down the gradient) and **active transport** by a pump (uses ATP, goes up the gradient, like the sodium-potassium pump). **Osmosis** is the diffusion of water through the membrane towards the more concentrated solution.

### The cell wall

Plants, fungi and bacteria have a wall outside the membrane. A plant cell wall is of **cellulose** (hemicellulose, pectin, in fungi it is chitin). The **middle lamella** (calcium pectate) glues neighbours. Young cells have a thin **primary wall**; many add a **secondary wall** inside. **Plasmodesmata** are channels through the wall that join the cytoplasm of cells.

<div class="myth">

**“The cell wall controls what goes in and out.”** The wall is fully permeable; it only gives shape and strength. The plasma membrane is the selective gate.

</div>

## 5. Endomembrane system

The organelles that work together and exchange material by vesicles form the **endomembrane system**: the endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes and vacuoles. (Mitochondria, chloroplasts and peroxisomes are not part of it.)

- **Endoplasmic reticulum (ER):** a network of tubes and sacs joined to the nuclear envelope. **Rough ER** has ribosomes on it and makes proteins for export and membranes. **Smooth ER** has none: it makes lipids and steroids, stores calcium (in muscle) and detoxifies drugs (in the liver).
- **Golgi apparatus** (Camillo Golgi, 1898): flat sacs (cisternae) stacked with a **cis** (receiving) face near the ER and a **trans** (shipping) face. It modifies, sorts and packs proteins and lipids; it also makes glycoproteins and lysosomes.
- **Lysosomes:** vesicles with about 40 kinds of acid hydrolases (digesting enzymes) that work in acid; they digest food, germs and old organelles (autophagy). Leaky ones can digest the cell: "suicide bags".
- **Vacuoles:** a membrane-bound space. In plants the big central vacuole is bounded by the **tonoplast**, holds cell sap and keeps the cell turgid. Protists have a **contractile vacuole** to pump out water, and **food vacuoles**.

## 6. Mitochondria, plastids and ribosomes

**Mitochondria** are the powerhouses: double membrane, inner membrane folded into **cristae**, with a **matrix** that holds enzymes, circular DNA and 70S ribosomes. They make most of the ATP of the cell by aerobic respiration and divide by fission. **Plastids** are found in plants and algae: **chloroplasts** (green, with chlorophyll in the thylakoids stacked into **grana**, stroma around them), **chromoplasts** (yellow, orange, red pigments: carotene in carrots, tomato), and **leucoplasts** (colourless stores: amyloplasts store starch, elaioplasts fats, aleuroplasts proteins). Both mitochondria and chloroplasts have their own DNA and 70S ribosomes, which supports the idea that they began as free bacteria living inside a larger cell (the **endosymbiotic theory**, Lynn Margulis).

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c11b-cell-powerhouses"><figcaption>Switch between the two. Both have a double membrane, DNA and 70S ribosomes. Find the cristae and the grana, the matrix and the stroma.</figcaption></figure>

**Ribosomes** (George Palade) are granules of RNA and protein, made in the nucleolus. They are **80S** (60S + 40S) in the eukaryotic cytoplasm and **70S** (50S + 30S) in prokaryotes, mitochondria and chloroplasts. They are the site of protein synthesis, free in the cytoplasm or on the rough ER.

## 7. Cytoskeleton, cilia, flagella and centrosome

- The **cytoskeleton** is a network of protein filaments (microfilaments of actin, intermediate filaments and microtubules of tubulin). It gives shape, lets organelles move and the cell divide.
- **Cilia** and **flagella** are outgrowths of the membrane with a "9 + 2" core of microtubules: nine pairs in a ring and two in the centre. Cilia are short and many (windpipe); flagella are long and few (sperm tail). They are made from basal bodies.
- The **centrosome** holds two **centrioles** at right angles; each has nine triplets of microtubules in a ring ("9 + 0") like a cartwheel. The centrioles organise the spindle in animal cells.

## 8. Nucleus

The **nucleus** (Robert Brown, 1831) holds the DNA. It has:

- the **nuclear envelope**: two membranes with **nuclear pores** through which RNA and proteins pass;
- **nucleoplasm** and **nucleolus**, a dense spot where rRNA is made and ribosomes begin (not membrane-bound);
- **chromatin** (DNA with histone proteins), which condenses into **chromosomes** at cell division. A chromosome has a **centromere** with a **kinetochore**, and its position gives four types: **metacentric** (middle), **sub-metacentric**, **acrocentric** (near one end) and **telocentric** (at the end).

**Microbodies** are small membrane-bound vesicles with enzymes (such as peroxisomes, which break down hydrogen peroxide).

<div class="keypoints">

- Cell theory: all living things are made of cells, the cell is the unit, all cells come from cells (Virchow).
- S/V = 6/L for a cube: bigger cells have relatively less surface, so cells are small.
- Prokaryotes: no nucleus, nucleoid, 70S ribosomes, wall of peptidoglycan; eukaryotes: nucleus, organelles, 80S ribosomes.
- Fluid mosaic membrane: phospholipid bilayer with proteins and cholesterol; selectively permeable; passive and active transport.
- Endomembrane system: ER, Golgi, lysosomes, vacuoles. Rough ER makes protein; Golgi sorts; lysosomes digest.
- Mitochondria (cristae, ATP) and chloroplasts (grana, sugar) have their own DNA and 70S ribosomes.
- Nucleus: envelope with pores, nucleolus, chromatin; chromosome types by centromere position.

</div>
