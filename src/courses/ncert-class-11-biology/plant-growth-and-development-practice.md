<div class="bigidea">For hormone questions, remember the pairs: auxin and cytokinin (opposite on side buds), gibberellin and ABA (opposite on germination), ethylene (the gas: ripening). For growth questions, write the formula first: arithmetic L0 + rt, geometric W0 e^(rt), RGR = increase ÷ start.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Name the hormone, say where it is made and give two roles.
2. For growth rate calculations, write formula, numbers, unit.
3. For photoperiodism, say which factor counts (the unbroken night).

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the natural auxin.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Indole-3-acetic acid (IAA).</details>

2. Which gas hormone ripens fruit?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Ethylene.</details>

3. What is the term for fruit formed without fertilisation?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Parthenocarpy.</details>

4. Name the phase of sigmoid growth in which the growth rate is fastest.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The log (exponential) phase.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

5. What is apical dominance and how can it be broken?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Apical dominance is the inhibition of side buds by auxin made in the shoot tip. It can be broken by cutting the tip (pruning) or by applying cytokinin.</details>

6. Differentiate between arithmetic and geometric growth.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

| Arithmetic | Geometric |
|---|---|
| one daughter cell divides, the other differentiates | both daughter cells divide |
| size rises by a fixed amount (straight line) | size multiplies (J-shaped curve) |
| L = L₀ + rt | W = W₀ e^(rt) |
| example: root tip elongating at a steady rate | early growth of a fruit or a population |

</details>

7. What is vernalisation? Give one use.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A low-temperature treatment (0 to 5 °C) that allows a plant to flower later. Chilled seeds of winter wheat can be sown in spring and still flower and give grain.</details>

8. A seed does not germinate in spite of water and warmth. Give two reasons and a way to correct each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A hard seed coat: scarify it. Inhibitors such as ABA: wash them out or treat with gibberellin. (An immature embryo: wait or chill the seeds.)</details>

9. A short-day plant is exposed to 16 hours of light and 8 hours of uninterrupted dark each day. Will it flower? What changes if the dark period is lengthened to 14 hours?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It will not flower with only 8 hours of dark (critical dark period about 12 h). With 14 hours of unbroken dark it will flower.</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

10. Describe the roles of the five main plant growth regulators.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Auxin** (IAA, shoot tip): cell elongation, apical dominance, rooting, parthenocarpy, 2,4-D as weed killer. **Gibberellin**: stem elongation, bolting, breaking dormancy, malting. **Cytokinin** (root tip): cell division, side buds, delays senescence. **Ethylene** (gas): fruit ripening, abscission, flowering in pineapple. **ABA**: closes stomata in drought, keeps dormancy, stress hormone.

</details>

11. Explain the three phases of growth in a root and the three patterns of growth in a population.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Root: meristematic (cells divide near the tip), elongation (cells take in water and enlarge), maturation (cells differentiate). Patterns: arithmetic (L₀ + rt), geometric (W₀ e^(rt)), sigmoid (lag, log, stationary phases).

</details>

12. Explain differentiation, dedifferentiation and redifferentiation with examples.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Differentiation: cells from meristems become specialised, e.g. xylem vessels. Dedifferentiation: living specialised cells regain the power to divide, e.g. interfascicular cambium, cork cambium, callus. Redifferentiation: the cells formed by these meristems again specialise, e.g. secondary xylem and phloem or cork.

</details>

## Case-based question

A nursery worker cuts the tips of young tea plants. A few weeks later side shoots have grown and the bush is thicker. Another worker dips cuttings of rose in a powder before planting; roots appear sooner. A grower stores raw mangoes in a closed box with a few ripe ones.

13. (a) Which hormone change explains the side shoots? (b) What is in the powder? (c) Why do the mangoes ripen sooner in the box? (d) Name a hormone that would do the opposite of the first.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Removing the tip stops the auxin that holds the side buds back (apical dominance); cytokinin then wakes the buds. (b) Synthetic auxin (such as IBA or NAA) that makes roots. (c) Ripe fruit gives off ethylene, a gas that ripens the others. (d) Auxin (it prevents side buds).</details>

## Assertion and reason

For each, choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

14. **A:** A short-day plant flowers if the dark period is long and unbroken. **R:** A flash of light in the middle of the night breaks the dark period.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(B) Both are true, but R is a separate fact, an experiment that shows the first statement.</details>

15. **A:** Cytokinins delay the senescence of leaves. **R:** They keep chlorophyll and proteins from breaking down.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A.</details>

16. **A:** Geometric growth continues for ever. **R:** Cells in geometric growth all keep dividing.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(D) A is false: nutrients or space run out and growth levels off (sigmoid). R is true.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Auxin, gibberellin, cytokinin promote growth; ethylene and ABA mainly inhibit.
- Arithmetic L₀ + rt; geometric W₀ e^(rt); sigmoid = lag, log, stationary.
- SDP, LDP, DNP respond to the uninterrupted dark period. Vernalisation = cold treatment.

</div>
