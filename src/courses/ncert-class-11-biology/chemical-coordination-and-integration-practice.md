## 1-mark questions

1. Name the hormone that lowers blood glucose.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Insulin.</details>

2. Which gland is called the "master gland"?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The pituitary (it controls several other endocrine glands; it is itself controlled by the hypothalamus).</details>

3. Name the hormone that controls the sleep-wake cycle.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Melatonin, from the pineal gland.</details>

4. Which mineral is needed for the formation of thyroxine?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Iodine.</details>

5. Name the hormone secreted by the corpus luteum.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Progesterone.</details>

## 2-mark questions

6. Differentiate between endocrine and exocrine glands.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Endocrine glands have no ducts and release hormones directly into the blood (thyroid, pituitary). Exocrine glands have ducts that carry the secretion to the place where it acts (salivary glands, sweat glands).</details>

7. Name two hormones of the posterior pituitary and one function of each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Oxytocin: contraction of the uterus in childbirth and milk ejection. Vasopressin (ADH): makes the kidney tubules take back water, reducing the volume of urine.</details>

8. Why is iodised salt recommended?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Iodine is needed to make thyroxine. Without it the thyroid enlarges (goitre) and a baby may develop cretinism. A little iodine added to salt supplies the daily need.</details>

9. Explain the term "second messenger".

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A small molecule, such as cyclic AMP, made inside the cell when a hormone binds a receptor on the cell surface; it carries the message inside and starts the changes in the cell's enzymes.</details>

10. How does PTH differ from calcitonin in its effect?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>PTH from the parathyroid raises blood calcium (bone, kidney and gut); calcitonin from the thyroid lowers it by slowing the breakdown of bone.</details>

## 3-mark questions

11. Describe the control of the pituitary by the hypothalamus.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The hypothalamus makes releasing and inhibiting hormones (for example GnRH, somatostatin). These travel through a small portal blood system to the anterior pituitary and control the release of GH, TSH, ACTH, LH, FSH and prolactin. The posterior pituitary is not controlled by hormones: nerve fibres from the hypothalamus run into it and release oxytocin and vasopressin made in the hypothalamus.</details>

12. How do insulin and glucagon keep the blood glucose steady?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>After a meal glucose rises; beta cells release insulin, which helps cells take up glucose and makes the liver store it as glycogen, lowering the level. In a fast or exercise, glucose falls; alpha cells release glucagon, which makes the liver break down glycogen and make glucose, raising the level. This pair of opposite effects is negative feedback.</details>

13. A thyroid gland is enlarged and the person feels tired. Explain the cause using feedback.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>If iodine is lacking the thyroid cannot make enough thyroxine. Low thyroxine reduces the negative feedback on the pituitary, so TSH rises and keeps stimulating the gland, which grows (goitre). Because thyroxine is still low, the metabolic rate is low and the person feels tired.</details>

14. A hormone has a half-life of 7 days. How much of it remains after 14 days? Give your answer as a percentage.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Two half-lives have passed, so `$100 \times (1/2)^2 = 25$` %.

</details>

## 5-mark questions

15. Name the hormones of the anterior pituitary and give the function of each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Growth hormone: growth of tissues and bones. Prolactin: growth of the mammary glands and milk formation. TSH: stimulates the thyroid to release T3 and T4. ACTH: stimulates the adrenal cortex. LH: ovulation and the corpus luteum in females, androgen release in males. FSH: follicle growth in females, sperm formation in males.</details>

16. Describe the hormones of the adrenal gland and their functions.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The medulla makes adrenaline and noradrenaline, the emergency hormones: they raise the heart rate, breathing and blood glucose, widen the pupils and send more blood to the muscles. The cortex makes glucocorticoids (cortisol: raises blood glucose, reduces inflammation, helps in stress) and mineralocorticoids (aldosterone: the distal tubule keeps Na⁺ and water and loses K⁺), and a little androgen. Too little cortex hormone causes Addison's disease; too much cortisol, Cushing's syndrome.</details>

17. Compare how steroid hormones and peptide hormones act on a target cell.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Peptide and protein hormones (insulin, glucagon) cannot cross the membrane, so they bind receptors on the cell surface. The complex makes a second messenger such as cAMP inside, which changes enzyme activity; the response is fast. Steroid hormones (cortisol, oestrogen) and thyroid hormones pass through the membrane and bind receptors in the cytoplasm or nucleus. The hormone-receptor complex acts on the DNA and changes gene expression, so new proteins are made; the response is slower and lasts longer.</details>

18. Describe the disorders caused by too much or too little of growth hormone, thyroid hormone and insulin.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Growth hormone: too little in childhood gives pituitary dwarfism, too much gives gigantism, and too much in adults gives acromegaly. Thyroid hormone: too little gives cretinism in a child, and hypothyroidism in an adult; too much gives Graves' disease (exophthalmic goitre); lack of iodine gives simple goitre. Insulin: too little, or failure of cells to respond, gives diabetes mellitus (high blood sugar, glucose in urine, thirst), treated with insulin and by diet and exercise.</details>

## Case-based question

*A 12-year-old, Anil, is very tall for his age. His hands and feet are large, and his doctor found a small tumour in a gland at the base of his brain. His blood has a very high level of a hormone that stimulates growth.*

19. (a) Name the gland and the hormone. (b) Name the condition. (c) What would happen if the same tumour appeared in an adult? (d) Which brain region controls this gland?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) The anterior pituitary; growth hormone. (b) Gigantism. (c) The long bones can no longer lengthen, so the jaw, hands and feet thicken: acromegaly. (d) The hypothalamus, through releasing and inhibiting hormones such as somatostatin.</details>

## Assertion-reason

Choose: (a) both true and R explains A; (b) both true but R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

20. **A:** Adrenaline is called the emergency hormone. **R:** It increases the heart rate and blood sugar and sends more blood to the muscles.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a).</details>

21. **A:** Diabetes insipidus and diabetes mellitus have the same cause. **R:** Both produce a large volume of urine.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(d). A is false: one is a lack of ADH and the other a lack or failure of insulin. R is true.</details>

22. **A:** Steroid hormones can act on genes. **R:** They are lipid-soluble and pass through the cell membrane to receptors inside the cell.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a).</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Hormones: made by ductless glands, carried in blood, act on cells with specific receptors.
- Hypothalamus controls the pituitary: releasing and inhibiting hormones to the front lobe; nerve fibres to the back lobe (oxytocin, ADH).
- Thyroid: T3, T4 (need iodine), calcitonin. Parathyroid: PTH. Adrenal: adrenaline (medulla), cortisol and aldosterone (cortex). Pancreas: insulin and glucagon.
- Peptide hormones: surface receptor and second messenger (cAMP). Steroids and thyroid hormones: inside receptor, change gene expression.
- Negative feedback holds levels steady. Disorders: dwarfism, gigantism, acromegaly, goitre, cretinism, Graves', Addison's, Cushing's, diabetes insipidus and mellitus.
