**Practice · Class 11 Biology · Chapter 5.** Read the chapter lesson, [Morphology of Flowering Plants](/learn/courses/ncert-class-11-biology/morphology-of-flowering-plants/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">For "modification" questions answer in three parts: which organ it was, what it has become, and what job it does (for example: the stem of Opuntia has become a flat, green phylloclade that does photosynthesis). For floral questions go whorl by whorl: calyx, corolla, androecium, gynoecium.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For "differentiate", draw a table with at least three points.
2. For modifications, use a table: modification, job, example.
3. For a floral formula, write symmetry, sex, then K, C, A, G with brackets for fusion.
4. Draw neat labelled diagrams: the root tip, a dicot seed, a monocot grain.

## 1-mark questions

**Q1.** Name the part of the embryo which becomes the shoot.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The plumule.</details>

**Q2.** What is phyllotaxy?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The arrangement of leaves on a stem or branch: alternate, opposite or whorled.</details>

**Q3.** Name the type of placentation in pea.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Marginal placentation.</details>

**Q4.** What does the symbol ⊕ mean in a floral formula?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The flower is actinomorphic (radially symmetric).</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

**Q5.** Differentiate tap root and fibrous root systems.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

| Tap root | Fibrous root |
|---|---|
| Develops from the radicle | The radicle dies early; roots come from the base of the stem |
| One main root with branches | Many thin roots of equal size |
| Dicots (mustard, gram) | Monocots (wheat, grass) |

</details>

**Q6.** Why is the potato considered a stem and not a root?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It has nodes with scale leaves and buds (eyes) in their axils, and no root cap. Roots do not have buds.</details>

**Q7.** What is the difference between racemose and cymose inflorescences?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>In racemose the main axis grows without limit and flowers open from the base to the tip (acropetal); in cymose the axis ends in a flower and the flowers open from the top downwards (basipetal).</details>

**Q8.** Write the floral formula of a flower that is radially symmetrical and bisexual, with 4 free sepals, 4 free petals, 6 free stamens and 2 fused carpels forming a superior ovary.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

⊕ ⚥ K4 C4 A6 G̲(2)

</details>

**Q9.** Compare dicot and monocot seeds on three points.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

| | Dicot (bean) | Monocot (maize) |
|---|---|---|
| Cotyledons | two | one (scutellum) |
| Endosperm | absent in the mature seed | large, with aleurone layer |
| Seed coat | separate from the fruit wall | fused with the fruit wall |

</details>

**Q10.** A flower has the formula K5 C5 A10 G1, all free. How many floral organs does it have?

<div class="problem" data-problem="c11b-morph-parts"></div>

<details><summary>Answer</summary>5 + 5 + 10 + 1 = 21 organs.</details>

## 5-mark questions

**Q11.** Describe the regions of the root tip with a labelled diagram.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>From the tip: root cap (protects the tip); region of meristematic activity (cells divide rapidly); region of elongation (cells lengthen, pushing the root forward); region of maturation (cells differentiate; root hairs absorb water and minerals).</details>

**Q12.** Describe any five modifications of stem, root or leaf, with the job of each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Tuber of potato (stores food); rhizome of ginger (stores food, survives dry seasons); tendril of cucumber (a stem branch that climbs); prop roots of banyan (support); pneumatophores of mangrove (take in oxygen); pitcher of <i>Nepenthes</i> (a leaf that catches insects); spines of cactus (protection and less water loss).</details>

**Q13.** Describe aestivation and name its four types with an example of each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Aestivation is the arrangement of sepals or petals in a bud. Valvate: edges touch (<i>Calotropis</i>). Twisted: each overlaps the next (China rose, cotton). Imbricate: irregular overlap (cassia, gulmohur). Vexillary: the standard covers the wings, which cover the keel (pea, bean).</details>

## Case-based question

On a farm visit, a student sees a mustard plant in flower, a sugarcane field with slanting roots from the lower nodes, and a banyan tree with hanging roots. The mustard inflorescence has the oldest flowers at the bottom and buds at the top.

**(a)** What type of inflorescence is the mustard raceme, and in what order do its flowers open?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Racemose; acropetal (from the base upwards).</details>

**(b)** Name the modified roots of sugarcane and banyan, and what they do.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Sugarcane has stilt roots, banyan has prop roots; both support the plant.</details>

**(c)** Which root system does the mustard plant have, and why?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A tap root system: it is a dicot, and its radicle grows into the main root.</details>

## Assertion and reason

For each, choose: (a) both true and R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

**Q14.** **A:** A banana can form without fertilisation. **R:** Such a fruit is called parthenocarpic.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). Banana is a parthenocarpic fruit.</details>

**Q15.** **A:** Pneumatophores are found in mangrove plants. **R:** Mangroves grow in swampy soil with little oxygen.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). The roots grow upward to take in oxygen.</details>
