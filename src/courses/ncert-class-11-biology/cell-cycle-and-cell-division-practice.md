**Practice · Class 11 Biology · Chapter 10.** Read the chapter lesson, [Cell Cycle and Cell Division](/learn/courses/ncert-class-11-biology/cell-cycle-and-cell-division/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Always keep a running count of two numbers: chromosomes (n or 2n) and DNA (C). Draw a small cell for each stage, with 2n = 4 chromosomes, and you will not mix up mitosis and meiosis. For "differentiate" questions use a table with at least four rows.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For stages, give the name, what happens to the chromosomes and the nuclear envelope.
2. For comparisons, a table with the same row heads.
3. For diagrams, draw 2n = 4 cells, with the two homologue pairs in different colours.
4. Give the number of chromosomes and DNA amount at each stage when asked.

## 1-mark questions

**Q1.** In which phase is the DNA replicated?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The S phase of interphase.</details>

**Q2.** What is the name of a cell that has left the cycle and does not divide?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A cell in G0 (quiescent stage).</details>

**Q3.** At which stage of mitosis do the centromeres split?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Anaphase.</details>

**Q4.** What are chiasmata?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The X-shaped points where non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes have crossed over.</details>

**Q5.** What is the number of chromosomes in each cell after meiosis I in a human?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>23 (each with two chromatids).</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

**Q6.** What is the difference between G1, S and G2 phases?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>G1: growth and normal metabolism, DNA is 2C. S: DNA replication, 2C to 4C. G2: preparation for division (spindle proteins), DNA is 4C.</details>

**Q7.** Why is M phase the shortest part of the cycle, and how long is it in a 24 h cycle?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The actual division of the nucleus and cytoplasm is quick compared with growth and DNA copying; it takes about 1 hour of 24 hours (a bit over 4 %).</details>

**Q8.** Define synapsis and crossing over. In which stage does each occur?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Synapsis is the pairing of homologous chromosomes (zygotene). Crossing over is the exchange of segments between non-sister chromatids of the homologues (pachytene).</details>

**Q9.** How does cytokinesis differ in animal and plant cells?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>In animal cells a furrow appears and deepens until the cell is pinched in two. In plant cells a cell plate forms in the middle from Golgi vesicles and grows outward to the cell wall.</details>

**Q10.** Why is mitosis important for a plant growing a new leaf?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It makes new cells with the same number of chromosomes and the same genes as the parent cell, so the leaf is made of identical, normal cells. Meristem cells divide by mitosis throughout life.</details>

**Q11.** A cell has 2n = 16. Give the number of chromosomes and chromatids at (a) metaphase of mitosis, (b) anaphase of mitosis.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) 16 chromosomes, 32 chromatids. (b) 32 chromosomes (each chromatid is now a chromosome), 32 chromatids counted as separate chromosomes.

</details>

**Q12.** Explain two ways in which meiosis brings variation.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Crossing over in pachytene swaps segments between non-sister chromatids, giving new combinations of alleles. Independent assortment: each pair lines up in metaphase I at random, so 2ⁿ combinations of maternal and paternal chromosomes are possible.</details>

## 5-mark questions

**Q13.** Describe the stages of mitosis with the changes in chromosomes. Draw a diagram of metaphase and anaphase.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Prophase:** chromatin condenses into chromosomes of two chromatids; the nucleolus and nuclear envelope begin to vanish; the spindle forms. **Metaphase:** the chromosomes attach by their kinetochores to the spindle and line up on the equatorial plate. **Anaphase:** centromeres split and chromatids move to opposite poles; the number of chromosomes doubles for a moment. **Telophase:** chromosomes reach the poles and uncoil; the nuclear envelope and nucleolus re-form; **cytokinesis** then divides the cytoplasm into two identical diploid cells. (Draw: metaphase with chromosomes at the equator, anaphase with V-shaped chromatids moving to poles.)

</details>

**Q14.** Describe the five sub-stages of prophase I of meiosis.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Leptotene:** chromosomes condense into long thin threads. **Zygotene:** homologous chromosomes pair (synapsis), joined by the synaptonemal complex into bivalents. **Pachytene:** the bivalents are thicker; crossing over occurs at recombination nodules (enzyme recombinase). **Diplotene:** the synaptonemal complex dissolves and homologues begin to separate but stay joined at chiasmata. **Diakinesis:** chiasmata terminalise, the chromosomes are fully condensed, the nucleolus and nuclear envelope disappear and the spindle begins to form.

</details>

## Case-based question

**Read this.** A student counts the cells in a slide of an onion root tip, 200 cells in all: 170 in interphase, 14 in prophase, 6 in metaphase, 4 in anaphase and 6 in telophase. The cycle in this tissue takes 20 hours.

**Q15.** (i) What is the percentage of cells in M phase? (ii) Estimate the length of M phase. (iii) Which stage is the shortest and what does that tell us? (iv) Why is a root tip chosen?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(i) M phase = 14 + 6 + 4 + 6 = 30 cells out of 200 = 15 %. (ii) 15 % of 20 h = 3 h. (iii) Anaphase (4 cells, 2 %): it is the fastest stage, so the fewest cells are caught in it. (iv) The root tip has meristem cells which divide continuously by mitosis.

</details>

## Assertion-reason

Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

**Q16.** **A:** Meiosis II is called an equational division. **R:** Sister chromatids separate in it and the chromosome number stays n.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A.</details>

**Q17.** **A:** The DNA content doubles at anaphase of mitosis. **R:** The centromeres divide in anaphase.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(D) A is false: DNA doubles in S phase. At anaphase only the number of chromosomes (separated chromatids) doubles. R is true.</details>

**Q18.** **A:** Gametes of the same parent are genetically different. **R:** Crossing over and independent assortment occur during meiosis.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A.</details>
