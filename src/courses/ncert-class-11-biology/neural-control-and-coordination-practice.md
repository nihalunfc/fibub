## 1-mark questions

1. What is the resting potential of a typical neuron?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>About −70 mV (inside negative).</details>

2. Name the gap between two neurons.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The synaptic cleft (at a synapse).</details>

3. Which part of the brain connects the two cerebral hemispheres?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The corpus callosum.</details>

4. Name the photoreceptors that work in dim light.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Rods.</details>

5. What is the function of the Eustachian tube?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It joins the middle ear to the pharynx and equalises the air pressure on the two sides of the eardrum.</details>

## 2-mark questions

6. What is the difference between afferent and efferent neurons?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Afferent (sensory) neurons carry impulses from receptors to the central nervous system; efferent (motor) neurons carry impulses from the central nervous system to effectors such as muscles and glands.</details>

7. State the all-or-none law of the nerve impulse.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>If a stimulus is below the threshold, no action potential is produced; if it reaches the threshold, a full action potential of the same size is produced, whatever the strength of the stimulus.</details>

8. Why does the impulse in a myelinated nerve travel faster?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The myelin sheath insulates the axon, so the impulse jumps from one node of Ranvier to the next (saltatory conduction) instead of being regenerated along the whole membrane.</details>

9. Name two functions of the hypothalamus.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Control of body temperature, hunger, thirst and the sleep-wake cycle; production of hormones that control the pituitary gland (also emotions).</details>

10. What is the blind spot?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The place on the retina where the optic nerve leaves the eye. It has no rods or cones, so light falling there is not seen.</details>

## 3-mark questions

11. Explain how the resting potential is kept up.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The axon membrane is permeable to K⁺, which leaks out, and almost impermeable to Na⁺. Large negatively charged proteins cannot leave the cell. The Na⁺/K⁺ pump uses ATP to push 3 Na⁺ out for every 2 K⁺ taken in. The result is more K⁺ inside, more Na⁺ outside and a negative inside of about −70 mV.</details>

12. How is a nerve impulse passed across a chemical synapse?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The impulse arrives at the axon terminal and opens Ca²⁺ channels. Ca²⁺ makes synaptic vesicles fuse with the membrane and release neurotransmitter into the synaptic cleft. The transmitter binds receptors on the post-synaptic membrane, opens ion channels and starts a new potential. The transmitter is then broken down by enzymes or taken back, so the signal stops.</details>

13. A neuron at −70 mV receives 6 excitatory signals of +3 mV and 1 inhibitory signal of −4 mV. Will it fire if the threshold is −55 mV?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Net = 6 × 3 − 4 = 14 mV, so the potential is `$-70 + 14 = -56$` mV. This is 1 mV short of −55 mV, so it will not fire.

</details>

14. Give the difference between rods and cones.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Rods work in dim light, give black-and-white vision and contain rhodopsin (from vitamin A); they are spread over the retina away from the fovea. Cones work in bright light, give colour vision (red, green and blue types) and are concentrated at the fovea, where vision is sharpest.</details>

## 5-mark questions

15. Describe how an action potential is generated and travels along an axon.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>At rest the axon is polarised, about −70 mV inside, kept by the K⁺ leak and the Na⁺/K⁺ pump. A stimulus depolarises the membrane to the threshold (about −55 mV); voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open and Na⁺ rushes in, so the inside becomes positive (about +30 mV): depolarisation. Then the Na⁺ channels close and K⁺ channels open; K⁺ leaves and the inside becomes negative again: repolarisation, with a slight undershoot. The pump restores the ion balance. The depolarised patch triggers the next patch, so the action potential moves along the axon as a wave; in a myelinated fibre it jumps from node to node. It is all-or-none, and the refractory period makes it travel in one direction only.</details>

16. Describe the parts of the human brain and give one function of each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Forebrain: cerebrum (thought, memory, speech, voluntary movement; four lobes; two hemispheres joined by the corpus callosum), thalamus (relay of sensory signals), hypothalamus (temperature, hunger, thirst, hormones). Midbrain: visual and auditory reflexes. Hindbrain: pons (links parts of the brain, breathing), cerebellum (balance and coordination), medulla oblongata (breathing, heartbeat, swallowing, vomiting).</details>

17. Describe the structure of the eye and explain how an image is formed on the retina.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The eye wall has the sclera with the transparent cornea, the vascular choroid with the iris and ciliary body, and the retina with rods and cones. The lens divides the aqueous chamber from the vitreous chamber. Light enters through the cornea, aqueous humor, pupil (size set by the iris) and lens, which together focus a small inverted image on the retina. The ciliary muscle changes the thickness of the lens: thin for far objects, thick for near objects. Rods and cones convert light to impulses; the optic nerve carries them to the visual cortex in the occipital lobe, which interprets the image.</details>

18. Trace the path of sound from the air to the brain.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The pinna collects sound waves and the external canal carries them to the tympanic membrane, which vibrates. The vibrations pass along the three ear ossicles (malleus, incus, stapes), which amplify them, to the oval window. The movement sets the fluid in the cochlea in motion and moves the basilar membrane, which bends the hair cells of the organ of Corti. The hair cells generate impulses which travel in the auditory nerve to the auditory (temporal) cortex of the brain.</details>

## Case-based question

*Rohit touched a hot idli vessel and pulled his hand away before he felt any pain. A moment later he felt the burn and said "ouch". His doctor explained that the first movement was a reflex.*

19. (a) What is the reflex arc, and why was the movement so fast? (b) Name the receptor and the effector in this case. (c) Which part of the CNS processed the reflex? (d) Why did he feel pain a moment later?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) A reflex arc is the pathway receptor → sensory neuron → spinal cord → motor neuron → effector. It was fast because the signal took a short path and did not need the brain to decide. (b) Receptor: pain and temperature receptors in the skin of the hand. Effector: the muscles of the arm and hand. (c) The spinal cord. (d) At the same time, impulses were sent up the spinal cord to the brain (the sensory areas of the cerebral cortex), which produced the feeling of pain after the reflex had happened.</details>

## Assertion-reason

Choose: (a) both true and R explains A; (b) both true but R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

20. **A:** A stronger stimulus produces a larger action potential. **R:** The action potential follows the all-or-none law.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(d). A is false; R is true: the size is fixed. A stronger stimulus only increases the frequency of firing.</details>

21. **A:** Synaptic transmission in the human nervous system is mostly one-way. **R:** The neurotransmitter is stored only in vesicles in the pre-synaptic terminal.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). Only the pre-synaptic neuron can release transmitter, so the signal cannot go back.</details>

22. **A:** The cerebellum controls the heartbeat. **R:** The cerebellum is part of the hindbrain.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(d). A is false: the heartbeat centre is in the medulla; the cerebellum controls balance and coordination. R is true.</details>
