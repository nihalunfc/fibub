**Practice · Class 11 Biology · Chapter 16.** Read the chapter lesson, [Excretory Products and their Elimination](/learn/courses/ncert-class-11-biology/excretory-products-and-their-elimination/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Two habits win marks here. For any "where does it happen?" question, name the part of the nephron and then say what moves (water, salt, glucose) and how (active transport or osmosis). For any hormone question, give the trigger, the target and the result, for example: low blood pressure → renin → angiotensin II → aldosterone → distal tubule keeps Na⁺ and water.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For urine formation, use the three headings: filtration, reabsorption, secretion, and give a number (180 L filtered, 1.5 L urine).
2. For the nephron diagram, label the glomerulus, Bowman's capsule, PCT, loop of Henle (both limbs), DCT and collecting duct, and draw the afferent arteriole wider than the efferent one.
3. For hormones, use a small table: hormone, source, target, effect.
4. For numbers, write the formula first, then the substitution.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the nitrogenous waste excreted by birds.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Uric acid.</details>

2. What is the functional unit of the kidney?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The nephron.</details>

3. Which hormone, released by the heart, widens blood vessels and lowers blood pressure?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Atrial natriuretic factor (ANF).</details>

4. Name the process by which glucose in the glomerular filtrate is taken back into the blood.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Selective reabsorption (by active transport), mainly in the proximal convoluted tubule.</details>

5. What is micturition?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The release of urine from the urinary bladder through the urethra, by a nervous reflex.</details>

## 2-mark questions

6. Why is the efferent arteriole narrower than the afferent arteriole?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The narrow exit holds back the blood and raises the pressure in the glomerulus. This high pressure is what forces water and small solutes out into the Bowman's capsule (ultrafiltration).</details>

7. Why can a person with diabetes mellitus have glucose in the urine?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The blood sugar is so high that the filtrate carries more glucose than the proximal tubule can reabsorb. The rest passes on and appears in the urine.</details>

8. Name two differences between ureotelic and uricotelic animals.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Ureotelic animals (humans, frogs) excrete urea, which is moderately toxic and needs some water; uricotelic animals (birds, reptiles) excrete uric acid, which is almost non-toxic and leaves as a paste with very little water.</details>

9. What does the vasa recta do in the kidney?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It runs beside the loop of Henle with the blood flowing in the opposite direction and carries away water and solutes without washing out the medullary salt gradient. It acts as a counter-current exchanger.</details>

10. State the function of the JG cells.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>They sense blood pressure and flow in the afferent arteriole and release renin when these fall, starting the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone pathway.</details>

## 3-mark questions

11. Describe how the filtrate changes in the ascending limb of the loop of Henle.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The ascending limb is permeable to salts but impermeable to water. Na⁺ and Cl⁻ move out into the medulla (partly by active transport), so the filtrate becomes more dilute as it rises toward the cortex. The salt that leaves helps to build the high concentration in the medulla.</details>

12. How does ADH help to save water in the body?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>When the blood becomes more concentrated, osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus signal the posterior pituitary to release ADH. ADH makes the collecting duct more permeable to water, so water follows the salt gradient into the medulla and returns to the blood. The urine becomes small in volume and concentrated. When water is plentiful, ADH falls and dilute urine is passed.</details>

13. A kidney filters 125 mL per minute. How much filtrate is made in a day, and if 1.5 litres are passed as urine, what percentage is reabsorbed?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Filtrate per day = 125 × 1440 = 180 000 mL = 180 L. Reabsorbed = 180 − 1.5 = 178.5 L, which is `$178.5 \div 180 \times 100 = 99.2$` %.

</details>

14. List three ways in which organs other than the kidney help in excretion.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Lungs remove CO₂ and some water; the liver passes bile pigments (bilirubin, biliverdin), cholesterol and drugs into the bile; the skin's sweat glands remove water, NaCl, a little urea and lactic acid, and the sebaceous glands remove sterols and waxes.</details>

## 5-mark questions

15. Describe the structure of a nephron with a labelled sketch of its parts.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A nephron has a renal corpuscle and a renal tubule. The corpuscle is a knot of capillaries, the glomerulus, inside the cup-shaped Bowman's capsule; blood enters by a wider afferent arteriole and leaves by a narrower efferent arteriole. The tubule starts as the proximal convoluted tubule (with a brush border), continues as the loop of Henle (descending and ascending limb), then the distal convoluted tubule, which opens into a collecting duct shared by many nephrons. Peritubular capillaries surround the tubule and the vasa recta runs along the loop. Sketch: glomerulus and capsule at the top left, PCT, a U-shaped loop dipping down into the medulla, DCT and then the collecting duct, with cortex and medulla marked.</details>

16. Explain the steps in urine formation, with the part of the nephron where each happens.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. **Filtration** in the glomerulus: blood pressure forces water and small solutes through the capillary wall, basement membrane and podocyte slits into the Bowman's capsule. GFR is about 125 mL/min (180 L/day).
2. **Reabsorption**: the PCT takes back 65 to 70 % of the water and Na⁺ and nearly all glucose and amino acids; the descending limb lets out water; the ascending limb lets out salts; the DCT takes back Na⁺, water and HCO₃⁻; the collecting duct takes back water.
3. **Secretion**: H⁺, K⁺ and ammonia are added to the filtrate in the PCT, DCT and collecting duct to keep the blood pH and ion balance.

Only about 1.5 L of the 180 L becomes urine.

</details>

17. Explain the counter-current mechanism that makes concentrated urine.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The two limbs of the loop of Henle carry the filtrate in opposite directions. The descending limb is permeable to water but not salt; water leaves, and the filtrate becomes more concentrated. The ascending limb is permeable to salt but not water; salt leaves into the medulla. Together, a small 200 mOsm/L difference at each level is multiplied along the loop into a gradient of 300 mOsm/L in the cortex to about 1200 mOsm/L at the tip. The vasa recta, with flow in the opposite direction, carries away water and salt without destroying the gradient. The collecting duct passes through this salty zone, and under ADH water leaves it by osmosis, so urine as strong as 1200 mOsm/L (four times the blood) can be made.</details>

18. Describe how the kidney regulates blood pressure and volume through the juxtaglomerular apparatus and the hormones that follow.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A fall in blood pressure, blood volume or GFR is sensed by the JG cells, which release renin. Renin converts angiotensinogen to angiotensin I, which is changed to angiotensin II. Angiotensin II narrows blood vessels, so pressure and GFR rise, and it makes the adrenal cortex release aldosterone. Aldosterone makes the distal tubule take back Na⁺ and water, increasing blood volume. When the pressure rises again, renin release is reduced (negative feedback). If the pressure is too high, ANF from the atria widens the vessels and opposes the system.</details>

## Case-based question

*Meena visited a doctor for tiredness and swelling in her feet. A urine test showed protein and some blood cells, and her blood had a high level of urea. The doctor said that the filtering units of her kidneys were inflamed after an infection.*

19. (a) Name the condition and the structure that is damaged. (b) Why do proteins and blood cells normally not appear in urine? (c) What is the name for a high urea level in blood? (d) If her kidneys fail, name one treatment that cleans the blood.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Glomerulonephritis; the glomeruli. (b) The filtration barrier (capillary wall, basement membrane and podocyte slits) is too fine for blood cells and large proteins to pass. When it is damaged they leak into the filtrate. (c) Uremia. (d) Haemodialysis (or a kidney transplant for a lasting solution).</details>

## Assertion-reason

Choose: (a) both true and R explains A; (b) both true but R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

20. **A:** Birds excrete uric acid. **R:** Uric acid is highly soluble in water and needs a large volume of water to be removed.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(c). Uric acid is almost insoluble, which is why birds can remove it as a paste with little water.</details>

21. **A:** A person who has lost a lot of blood has less urine. **R:** The fall in blood pressure raises renin, angiotensin II and aldosterone, so the tubules keep more Na⁺ and water.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). R explains A.</details>

22. **A:** The filtrate in the proximal convoluted tubule is isotonic to blood. **R:** The PCT is impermeable to water.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(c). A is true; R is false. The PCT is permeable to water, and water follows the salts and glucose, so the concentration stays the same.</details>
