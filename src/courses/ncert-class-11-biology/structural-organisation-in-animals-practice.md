**Practice · Class 11 Biology · Chapter 7.** Read the chapter lesson, [Structural Organisation in Animals](/learn/courses/ncert-class-11-biology/structural-organisation-in-animals/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Tissue questions have a pattern: name the tissue, say how its structure suits the job, and give a place where it is found. For animal types draw a quick table (system, organ, one special fact). Never mix up the closed circulation of the earthworm and the frog with the open one of the cockroach.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. State the tissue, then its structure, then the function, then the location.
2. For "differentiate", write a two-column table.
3. For labelled figures use a pencil and straight label lines.
4. Name the animal and the system together: for example "cockroach, respiratory system: tracheae".

## 1-mark questions

**Q1.** Name the tissue that lines the alveoli of the lungs.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Simple squamous epithelium.</details>

**Q2.** What connects muscle to bone?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Tendons (dense regular connective tissue).</details>

**Q3.** Name the gland cell that makes mucus in the gut lining.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Goblet cell (a unicellular gland).</details>

**Q4.** Which segments carry the clitellum in an earthworm?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Segments 14, 15 and 16.</details>

**Q5.** What is the heart of a frog like?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Three-chambered: two atria and one ventricle.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

**Q6.** Differentiate between exocrine and endocrine glands with an example each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Exocrine glands release their secretion through a duct (salivary gland, sweat gland). Endocrine glands have no duct and release hormones directly into the blood (thyroid, pituitary).</details>

**Q7.** Why does blood count as a connective tissue?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It has cells (red cells, white cells, platelets) in a matrix, the plasma, and it connects the other tissues by carrying materials between them.</details>

**Q8.** Give the differences between skeletal and smooth muscle (any three).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Skeletal: striated, many nuclei, long cylinders, voluntary, joined to bones. Smooth: no stripes, one nucleus, spindle-shaped, involuntary, in the walls of hollow organs.</details>

**Q9.** What is the role of the typhlosole in the earthworm?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It is a fold of the intestinal wall that increases the surface area for digestion and absorption.</details>

**Q10.** Name the respiratory structures of a cockroach and a frog.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Cockroach: tracheae with tracheoles, opening to the outside through spiracles. Frog: skin, buccal cavity and lungs.</details>

**Q11.** What is the use of cilia in the lining of the windpipe?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The cilia beat in one direction and sweep mucus with trapped dust and germs up and out of the lungs.</details>

**Q12.** Explain tight, adhering and gap junctions in one line each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Tight junctions seal the gap between cells so fluids cannot leak across; adhering junctions fasten neighbouring cells like spot welds; gap junctions are channels that let ions and small molecules move from cell to cell.</details>

## 5-mark questions

**Q13.** Describe the structure of an open circulation and a closed circulation with an example of each, and state one advantage of the closed system.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In an **open** circulation (cockroach) the heart pumps haemolymph into body spaces (haemocoel), so organs lie directly in the fluid, which returns to the heart through holes (ostia). Pressure is low. In a **closed** circulation (earthworm, frog, humans) the blood stays in vessels; pumped by hearts, it flows at higher pressure and can be directed to the organs which need it. This control and speed is the advantage of the closed type. The cockroach does not use its blood to carry oxygen (tracheae do), but the earthworm and frog use blood (with haemoglobin) for oxygen.

</details>

**Q14.** Compare the three kinds of muscle tissue in a table and state where each is found.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

| | Skeletal | Smooth | Cardiac |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stripes | yes | no | yes |
| Nuclei per cell | many | one | one |
| Shape | long cylinders | spindle | branched |
| Control | voluntary | involuntary | involuntary |
| Found in | muscles on bones | gut, vessels, bladder | heart wall |

Cardiac cells are joined by intercalated discs.

</details>

## Case-based question

**Read this.** A student studying a thin slice of tissue under the microscope sees one layer of tall cells, with beating hair-like cilia on top and a few oval, empty-looking cells (goblet cells) between them. The slice came from the windpipe of a goat.

**Q15.** (i) Name the tissue. (ii) What are the goblet cells doing? (iii) What would happen to the dust in the air breathed in? (iv) Would the same tissue be a good lining of the skin? Why not?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(i) Ciliated columnar epithelium. (ii) They secrete mucus that traps dust and germs. (iii) The cilia sweep the mucus upwards to the throat, where it is swallowed or coughed out. (iv) No. The skin needs protection against wear: a stratified (many-layered) epithelium is stronger than a single layer of columnar cells.

</details>

## Assertion-reason

Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

**Q16.** **A:** Cartilage has no blood vessels in it. **R:** Its cells get food by diffusion through the matrix.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A.</details>

**Q17.** **A:** Earthworm is unisexual. **R:** It has both testes and ovaries.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(D) A is false: an earthworm is hermaphrodite. R is true.</details>

**Q18.** **A:** A cockroach's blood does not carry oxygen. **R:** Its blood has no haemoglobin and air tubes carry oxygen to the cells.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A.</details>
