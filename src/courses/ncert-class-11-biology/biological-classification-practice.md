**Practice · Class 11 Biology · Chapter 2.** Read the chapter lesson, [Biological Classification](/learn/courses/ncert-class-11-biology/biological-classification/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Most classification questions are solved by two clues: is there a nucleus, and what is the cell wall made of? Prokaryote means Monera; chitin means Fungi; cellulose and photosynthesis means Plantae; no wall and feeding on others means Animalia; single-celled eukaryote means Protista.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For "differentiate", draw a table with at least three points.
2. For classes of fungi or protozoans, give the group, the key feature and an example.
3. Draw and label simple diagrams: a virus, a bacteriophage, a lichen section.
4. Underline scientific names by hand.

## 1-mark questions

**Q1.** Name the kingdom that includes all the bacteria.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Monera.</details>

**Q2.** What is the cell wall of fungi made of?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Chitin.</details>

**Q3.** Who discovered viroids?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>T. O. Diener, in 1971.</details>

**Q4.** Which protozoan group does *Plasmodium* belong to?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Sporozoans.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

**Q5.** Why was the two-kingdom system of Linnaeus given up?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It put prokaryotes and eukaryotes together, and unicellular and multicellular forms, and it could not place organisms such as <i>Euglena</i> (plant-like and animal-like) or fungi (cell wall, but no chlorophyll). Whittaker used cell type, body organisation, nutrition, reproduction and relationships.</details>

**Q6.** Differentiate Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

| Gram-positive | Gram-negative |
|---|---|
| Thick peptidoglycan wall | Thin wall under an outer membrane |
| Keeps the violet dye | Loses the violet dye in alcohol |
| Appears violet | Appears pink with the counterstain |

</details>

**Q7.** What are archaebacteria? Name their three kinds.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Bacteria that live in extreme places, with a cell wall without peptidoglycan. Halophiles (salty places), thermoacidophiles (hot acidic springs) and methanogens (marshes, guts of cattle; make methane).</details>

**Q8.** What are the features of the protozoan group "sporozoans"? Give an example and the disease it causes.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Parasites with an infectious spore-like stage. <i>Plasmodium</i> causes malaria.</details>

**Q9.** Compare viruses and viroids.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A virus has DNA or RNA inside a protein coat (capsid). A viroid is only a small free RNA with no coat. Both are obligate parasites. Viruses cause many diseases; viroids cause diseases such as the potato spindle tuber.</details>

**Q10.** A phage releases 40 phages per cell. Starting from one phage, how many are there after 2 lytic cycles?

<div class="problem" data-problem="c11b-bclass-burst"></div>

<details><summary>Answer</summary>40 × 40 = 1600.</details>

## 5-mark questions

**Q11.** Describe the life cycle of a fungus with the terms plasmogamy, karyogamy and meiosis.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Haploid spores germinate into hyphae and form a mycelium (n). Two compatible hyphae meet and their cytoplasm fuses (plasmogamy), giving cells with two nuclei (n + n, dikaryon). Later the nuclei fuse (karyogamy) to give a diploid (2n) zygote nucleus. Meiosis follows quickly and makes haploid spores, which spread and start the cycle again.</details>

**Q12.** Describe the structure and multiplication of a bacteriophage.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Structure: head (capsid with double-stranded DNA), tail, tail fibres. Lytic cycle: attachment, injection of DNA, copying of phage DNA and proteins by the host machinery, assembly of new phages, lysis of the host. In the lysogenic cycle the DNA joins the host chromosome as a prophage and multiplies with the host.</details>

**Q13.** Write the features of the four classes of fungi with one example each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Phycomycetes: coenocytic mycelium, zygospores, <i>Rhizopus</i>. Ascomycetes: septate, ascospores in asci, <i>Penicillium</i>, yeast. Basidiomycetes: septate, basidiospores on basidia, mushrooms (<i>Agaricus</i>). Deuteromycetes: only asexual spores known, <i>Alternaria</i>.</details>

## Case-based question

A school group scrapes some grey-green crust from a tree trunk in a busy city street and compares it with a thicker, bushy growth from a hill station in the Western Ghats. Under a microscope the crust shows fungal threads wrapped around green cells.

**(a)** What is this organism?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A lichen: an association of a fungus (mycobiont) with an alga or a cyanobacterium (phycobiont).</details>

**(b)** What does each partner give the other?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The alga makes food by photosynthesis; the fungus gives water, minerals and shelter, and anchors it.</details>

**(c)** Why is there less of it on the city street?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Lichens absorb gases such as sulphur dioxide directly through their surface; polluted air damages them, so they are indicators of pollution.</details>

## Assertion and reason

For each, choose: (a) both true and R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

**Q14.** **A:** Mycoplasma can survive without a cell wall. **R:** Mycoplasma are the smallest living cells and can live without oxygen.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(b). Both are true, but being small does not explain the missing wall.</details>

**Q15.** **A:** Viruses are not considered living outside a host cell. **R:** They have no metabolism of their own and multiply only inside a host.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a).</details>
