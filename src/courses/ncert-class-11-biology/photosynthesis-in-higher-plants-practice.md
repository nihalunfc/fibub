**Practice · Class 11 Biology · Chapter 11.** Read the chapter lesson, [Photosynthesis in Higher Plants](/learn/courses/ncert-class-11-biology/photosynthesis-in-higher-plants/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Two habits help. First, keep the two stages apart: light reactions (thylakoid, make ATP, NADPH, O₂) and the Calvin cycle (stroma, use ATP and NADPH, make sugar). Second, for every "compare C3 and C4" question, answer with a table: anatomy, first product, enzyme, photorespiration, temperature.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Name the place (thylakoid or stroma) and the product of each step.
2. For numbers, write the cost per CO₂ first (3 ATP and 2 NADPH), then multiply.
3. Draw the Z scheme or the Calvin cycle as a simple labelled flow with arrows.

## 1-mark questions

1. Which gas released in photosynthesis comes from water?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Oxygen.</details>

2. Name the primary CO₂ acceptor in C3 plants.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>RuBP (ribulose 1,5-bisphosphate).</details>

3. What is the first stable product of the Calvin cycle?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>3-phosphoglyceric acid (PGA), a 3-carbon compound.</details>

4. Which pigment is the main one in the reaction centre?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Chlorophyll a (P680 in PS II and P700 in PS I).</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

5. Why are the light reactions and the Calvin cycle called "light" and "dark" reactions, and why is the second name misleading?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The light reactions need light to excite chlorophyll. The Calvin cycle does not use light directly. But it needs the ATP and NADPH from the light reactions, so it stops within minutes in the dark, and in many plants its enzymes are switched on by light. "Dark" does not mean it runs at night.</details>

6. State Blackman's law of limiting factors and give one example.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>When a process depends on several factors, its rate is limited by the one in shortest supply. Example: on a bright day with 0.03 % CO₂, raising the light does not raise photosynthesis, but adding CO₂ does.</details>

7. Differentiate between cyclic and non-cyclic photophosphorylation (any three points).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

| Cyclic | Non-cyclic |
|---|---|
| only PS I | PS II and PS I |
| electron returns to P700 | electron ends on NADP⁺ |
| ATP only | ATP, NADPH and O₂ |
| no water splitting | water is split |

</details>

8. How many ATP and NADPH are used to make 2 glucose molecules in a C3 plant? Show the working.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

2 glucose need 12 CO₂. ATP = 12 × 3 = **36**. NADPH = 12 × 2 = **24**.

</details>

9. Why is photorespiration almost absent in maize?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>PEP carboxylase in the mesophyll fixes CO₂ into OAA, which carries CO₂ to the bundle sheath. There the CO₂ is concentrated around RuBisCO, so RuBisCO rarely binds O₂.</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

10. Describe the light reactions with the help of the Z scheme.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Light excites PS II (P680). The electron goes to an acceptor and is replaced by electrons from the splitting of water (2H₂O → 4H⁺ + O₂ + 4e⁻), which also puts H⁺ in the lumen. The electron runs downhill through plastoquinone, the cytochrome b6f complex and plastocyanin; the energy pumps H⁺ into the lumen. PS I (P700) is excited, and its electron goes uphill to ferredoxin and then to NADP⁺, giving NADPH. The H⁺ gradient drives ATP synthase, making ATP (chemiosmosis). Products: ATP, NADPH and O₂. The graph of energy against the path looks like a "Z".

</details>

11. Explain the three stages of the Calvin cycle and state the total ATP and NADPH for one glucose.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. **Carboxylation:** RuBisCO joins CO₂ to RuBP, making 2 PGA.
2. **Reduction:** PGA becomes G3P using 2 ATP and 2 NADPH per CO₂.
3. **Regeneration:** RuBP is remade from G3P using 1 ATP per CO₂.

Per CO₂: 3 ATP and 2 NADPH. Six turns give one glucose: **18 ATP and 12 NADPH**.

</details>

12. Describe the C4 pathway and say why it suits hot climates.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Kranz anatomy: bundle sheath cells around the vascular bundle. In the mesophyll PEP carboxylase fixes CO₂ onto PEP to make OAA (4 C), which becomes malate. Malate goes to the bundle sheath cells and is split, releasing CO₂ for the Calvin cycle with RuBisCO; pyruvate returns and is changed to PEP using 2 ATP. Because CO₂ is concentrated, photorespiration is negligible; the stomata can stay partly closed in heat, so water is saved and the best temperature is higher (30 to 45 °C).

</details>

## Case-based question

A student grows two pots, one of wheat and one of maize, in a closed glasshouse. On a very hot afternoon the stomata of both close partly. She measures the CO₂ inside the leaves and the growth over two weeks.

13. (a) Which plant will keep growing better in this heat? (b) Why does the inner CO₂ fall? (c) Name the process that wastes energy in the other plant. (d) Name the first product in each plant.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Maize, a C4 plant. (b) Closed stomata let less CO₂ in while photosynthesis uses it up. (c) Photorespiration in wheat, because RuBisCO binds O₂ when CO₂ is low. (d) Wheat: PGA (3 C); maize: OAA (4 C).</details>

## Assertion and reason

For each, choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

14. **A:** The O₂ released in photosynthesis comes from water. **R:** Photosystem II takes electrons from water.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Water is split to replace the electrons lost by P680, and O₂ is a by-product.</details>

15. **A:** C4 plants need more ATP per glucose than C3 plants. **R:** The C4 pathway uses 2 ATP per CO₂ to regenerate PEP.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) 30 ATP against 18 per glucose; the extra 2 per CO₂ pays for PEP regeneration.</details>

16. **A:** Photorespiration is a wasteful process in C3 plants. **R:** RuBisCO binds O₂ instead of CO₂, no sugar is made and CO₂ is released.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both are true and R explains A: oxygenation of RuBP uses energy and gives no sugar.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Light reactions: thylakoid, give ATP, NADPH and O₂. Calvin cycle: stroma, 3 ATP + 2 NADPH per CO₂.
- C3 versus C4: Kranz anatomy, PEP carboxylase, OAA, no photorespiration, best in heat.
- Blackman: the scarcest factor limits the rate.
- Cyclic flow: PS I only, ATP only.

</div>
