## How to set out answers

1. Name the organelle, give its structure in a line, then the function.
2. Use tables for prokaryote versus eukaryote and plant versus animal.
3. Show the formula and the units for calculations on size and magnification.

## 1-mark questions

**Q1.** Who said that "all cells arise from pre-existing cells"?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Rudolf Virchow (1855).</details>

**Q2.** Name the smallest known cells.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Mycoplasma (about 0.3 µm).</details>

**Q3.** What is the unit used for the size of ribosomes?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The Svedberg unit (S), which depends on how fast a particle settles in a centrifuge.</details>

**Q4.** Which organelle is called the "suicide bag"?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The lysosome.</details>

**Q5.** What is the arrangement of microtubules in a cilium?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>"9 + 2": nine pairs around two single ones in the middle.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

**Q6.** Why is the plasma membrane called selectively permeable?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It lets some substances cross easily (small non-polar molecules, water), others only with the help of proteins (ions, sugars), and some not at all. It decides what enters and leaves.</details>

**Q7.** Give two differences between rough and smooth ER.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Rough ER has ribosomes on its surface and makes proteins; smooth ER has none and makes lipids and steroids, detoxifies drugs and stores calcium.</details>

**Q8.** What is the endosymbiotic theory? Give two pieces of evidence.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Mitochondria and chloroplasts began as free bacteria that were taken into a larger cell and lived there. Evidence: they have their own circular DNA, 70S ribosomes like bacteria, double membranes, and they divide by fission.</details>

**Q9.** Differentiate between plasmodesmata and the middle lamella.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Plasmodesmata are narrow channels through the cell walls that connect the cytoplasm of adjacent plant cells. The middle lamella is the layer of calcium pectate that cements the walls of neighbouring cells.</details>

**Q10.** What are the types of chromosomes by centromere position?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Metacentric (central centromere, two equal arms), sub-metacentric (slightly off centre), acrocentric (one very short arm), telocentric (centromere at the end).</details>

**Q11.** Distinguish chloroplasts, chromoplasts and leucoplasts.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Chloroplasts are green with chlorophyll and do photosynthesis. Chromoplasts have yellow, orange or red pigments (carotenoids) and colour flowers and fruits. Leucoplasts are colourless and store starch, oils or proteins.</details>

**Q12.** A cell appears 4.5 cm long in a photo magnified 1500 times. What is its real length?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Real length = image ÷ magnification = 45 mm ÷ 1500 = 0.03 mm = 30 µm.

</details>

## 5-mark questions

**Q13.** Describe the fluid mosaic model of the plasma membrane, with a labelled diagram.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The membrane is a **bilayer of phospholipids**: the hydrophilic heads (with phosphate) face the watery outside and inside, and the hydrophobic fatty-acid tails face each other in the middle. **Proteins** are mosaic-like pieces in this fluid layer: integral proteins cross the membrane (channels, carriers, pumps) and peripheral proteins sit on the surfaces (enzymes, receptors). **Cholesterol** between the tails adjusts the fluidity, and **sugar chains** on the outer face form the glycocalyx. The layer is fluid because lipids and proteins move sideways (flip-flops are rare). The model explains how the membrane lets some molecules through and not others. (Draw: two rows of circles with tails, with proteins crossing the middle and labels.)

</details>

**Q14.** Describe how a secretory protein travels from its synthesis to the outside of the cell.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

It is made by a ribosome on the **rough ER** and enters the ER lumen, where it folds. A **transport vesicle** buds off and fuses with the **cis face of the Golgi**. In the Golgi it is modified (sugars added) and sorted. At the **trans face** a **secretory vesicle** forms; it moves to the plasma membrane and fuses with it, releasing the protein by **exocytosis**. Some vesicles from the Golgi become lysosomes.

</details>

## Case-based question

**Read this.** A student measures a spherical cell of diameter 10 µm and another of diameter 20 µm. Take the surface of a sphere as 4πr² and the volume as 4πr³/3.

**Q15.** (i) What is the S/V of each cell? (ii) Which cell is more efficient at exchanging materials? (iii) How do some large cells (such as intestine lining cells) overcome the problem?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(i) S/V = 3/r. For r = 5 µm it is 0.6 µm⁻¹; for r = 10 µm it is 0.3 µm⁻¹. (ii) The smaller cell, with twice the S/V. (iii) By folding the membrane into microvilli, being flat or long, or having many small organelles.

</details>

## Assertion-reason

Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

**Q16.** **A:** Mitochondria can make some of their own proteins. **R:** They have their own DNA and ribosomes.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A.</details>

**Q17.** **A:** Prokaryotic ribosomes are larger than eukaryotic ones. **R:** They are 70S while eukaryotic ribosomes are 80S.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(D) A is false: 70S ribosomes are smaller than 80S. R is true.</details>

**Q18.** **A:** The lysosomal enzymes work best in acid. **R:** The inside of a lysosome is acidic.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A.</details>
