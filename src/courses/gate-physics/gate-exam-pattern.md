**Module 10 · about 25 minutes.** Modules 1 to 9 covered the physics. This lesson is about the paper: what is in it, how it is marked, how marks turn into a score, and how to spend three hours on it. If you have already read the JAM version of this lesson, [the JAM paper](/learn/courses/iit-jam-physics/jam-exam-pattern/), most of the marking logic carries over; the counts and the aptitude part are new.

<div class="bigidea">GATE PH is 100 marks in 180 minutes, so every mark is worth about 1.8 minutes. Only MCQs carry negative marks; MSQs and NATs never cost anything. Know the rules exactly, keep moving, and remember that 15 of the 100 marks are general aptitude, which this course does not teach but you should not neglect.</div>

**Check the official site first.** The pattern below follows the GATE papers of recent years. Each year a different institute organises GATE (for GATE 2027 it is IIT Madras), and details can change: read the current information brochure on the official GATE website before you plan. This course is not affiliated with the IITs, IISc or the organising institute.

## 1. The paper at a glance

GATE is a 3-hour computer-based test. The PH paper has **65 questions worth 100 marks**, all on screen, with an on-screen virtual scientific calculator (no physical calculator) and a scribble pad.

| Part | Questions | Marks | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| General aptitude (GA) | 10 (5 of 1 mark, 5 of 2 marks) | 15 | verbal, quantitative, analytical and spatial reasoning; common to every GATE paper |
| Physics | 55 (25 of 1 mark, 30 of 2 marks) | 85 | the PH syllabus, in MCQ, MSQ and NAT form |
| Total | 65 | 100 | 180 minutes |

The physics questions come from nine syllabus sections, which are the modules of this course: [mathematical physics](/learn/courses/gate-physics/linear-vector-spaces/), [classical mechanics](/learn/courses/gate-physics/lagrangian-mechanics/), [electromagnetic theory](/learn/courses/gate-physics/multipoles/), [quantum mechanics](/learn/courses/gate-physics/postulates-operators/), [thermodynamics and statistical physics](/learn/courses/gate-physics/ensembles/), [atomic and molecular physics](/learn/courses/gate-physics/fine-structure/), [solid state physics](/learn/courses/gate-physics/reciprocal-lattice/), nuclear and particle physics, and [electronics](/learn/courses/gate-physics/semiconductor-devices/). No official weighting by section is published; recent papers spread the marks over all nine, so no section can be skipped safely.

## 2. Question types and marking

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>MCQ</dt><dd>four options, exactly one correct; +1 or +2 if right, −1/3 or −2/3 if wrong, 0 if left blank</dd>
<dt>MSQ</dt><dd>four options, one or more correct; full marks only for exactly the right set; never negative, no partial credit</dd>
<dt>NAT</dt><dd>type a number (no options); right if inside the stated range; never negative. Watch the units and the number of decimal places asked for</dd>
<dt>Expected mark of an MCQ guess</dt><dd>`$p\,m - (1 - p)\,m/3 = \tfrac m3(4p - 1)$` for a question worth `$m$`, if `$p$` is your chance of being right: zero for a blind guess among four</dd>
</dl>

</div>

The split of the 55 physics questions into types varies from year to year. fibub's GATE mock test uses a fixed split that matches the marks exactly:

| Section of the fibub mock | Type | Questions | Marks each | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | MCQ | 15 | 1 | 15 |
| 2 | MCQ | 15 | 2 | 30 |
| 3 | MSQ | 3 | 1 | 3 |
| 4 | MSQ | 5 | 2 | 10 |
| 5 | NAT | 7 | 1 | 7 |
| 6 | NAT | 10 | 2 | 20 |
| | | **55** | | **85** |

That gives 25 one-mark and 30 two-mark questions, as in the real paper. The mock gives 150 minutes for these 85 marks, about the share of the 3 hours they deserve, leaving the rest for the aptitude part.

**Worked example.** A candidate's physics part: one-mark MCQs 10 right, 4 wrong; two-mark MCQs 8 right, 5 wrong; MSQs 2 one-mark and 3 two-mark right; NATs 5 one-mark and 6 two-mark right. Marks: `$10 - 4/3 = 8.67$`; `$16 - 10/3 = 12.67$`; MSQ `$2 + 6 = 8$`; NAT `$5 + 12 = 17$`. Total 46.33 out of 85. The nine wrong MCQs cost 4.67 marks; the wrong MSQs and NATs cost nothing.

## 3. From marks to a GATE score

Your result shows marks out of 100 and a **GATE score** out of 1000, which is what admissions and fellowships use; it is valid for three years. Recent brochures compute it as

```math
\text{score} = S_q + (S_t - S_q)\,\frac{M - M_q}{\bar M_t - M_q}
```

where `$M$` is your mark, `$M_q$` the qualifying mark for the general category, `$\bar M_t$` the mean mark of the top 0.1% of candidates (or the top 10, whichever is more), and `$S_q = 350$`, `$S_t = 900$` the scores assigned to those two marks. The qualifying mark has been the larger of 25 and `$\mu + \sigma$` (mean plus one standard deviation of all marks in the paper), with lower qualifying marks for reserved categories. Two consequences: your score depends on how everyone else did, so a hard paper does not hurt you by itself; and the score rises linearly with marks, so every mark counts the same wherever it comes from. Check the current brochure for the exact rules.

## 4. A plan for the 180 minutes

The going rate is **1.8 minutes per mark**: under 2 minutes for a 1-mark question, about 3.5 for a 2-mark one.

| Time | What to do |
|---|---|
| 0–25 min | **General aptitude.** 15 marks that are usually quicker than physics marks. Do them first while you are fresh, but stop at 25 to 30 minutes. |
| 25–100 min | **Physics, first pass.** Answer everything you can finish within its budget, in any order. Mark the rest for review. Do not guess yet. |
| 100–155 min | **Second pass.** Marked questions, easiest first: longer derivations, careful NATs, MSQs where each statement needs checking. |
| 155–170 min | **MSQ and NAT sweep.** Every blank MSQ and NAT gets your best reasoned answer: they cost nothing if wrong. |
| 170–180 min | **MCQ decisions.** For each unanswered MCQ, if you can rule out at least one option, guess among the rest ([lesson 10.2](/learn/courses/gate-physics/gate-strategy/) works out when that pays). Check every answer is saved. |

Habits that save marks:

- **Read the question stem to the end.** GATE likes a long set-up with a small final ask: a ratio, a sign, a value in different units.
- **Mind the NAT format.** The answer is checked against a range; round only at the end and give it in the units asked.
- **Click the calculator sparingly.** Estimate with powers of ten in your head and use the virtual calculator for the final digits.
- **Do a one-line check.** Dimensions, a limiting case or a symmetry catches most slips in seconds.

## 5. Preparing with this course

1. **Learn each lesson** and work its live problems (new numbers every time) until you can do them without the hint.
2. **Practise by topic** on the [practice pages](/learn/courses/gate-physics/practice/), filtering by level: GATE questions at levels 3 to 5 are the exam's own standard.
3. **Sit full mock papers** on the [GATE mock test page](/learn/courses/gate-physics/mock-test/): 55 physics questions in the pattern above, a 150-minute timer and GATE marking, including negative marks for MCQs. One sitting, no notes, on-screen calculator only.
4. **Review every mock**: for each lost mark, decide whether it was knowledge (back to the lesson), method (more practice questions) or exam craft (time, misreading, a bad guess). Track that split from mock to mock.
5. **Practise aptitude separately** from a source of your choice, under time: it is 15% of the paper.

<div class="myth">

**"GATE has negative marking, so leave anything you are unsure of."** Only MCQs have negative marks. A blank MSQ or NAT throws away a free chance, and even on MCQs a guess with one option ruled out has a positive expected value. The real risk is not guessing: it is spending ten minutes on one question while easier marks wait elsewhere.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- 65 questions, 100 marks, 180 minutes: GA 10 questions for 15 marks, physics 55 questions (25 one-mark, 30 two-mark) for 85 marks.
- MCQ: −1/3 or −2/3 if wrong. MSQ: all-or-nothing, never negative. NAT: within a range, never negative.
- A blind MCQ guess is worth zero on average; ruling out options makes it positive.
- GATE score: linear in marks between the qualifying mark (350) and the top 0.1% mean (900); valid three years.
- About 1.8 minutes per mark; two passes, then sweep MSQ and NAT, then decide on MCQs.
- Always check the current brochure on the official GATE website.

</div>
