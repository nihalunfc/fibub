**Module 5 · about 15 minutes.** By the end you will have one simple frame that fits any interview question, a way to plan it in a few seconds, and tricks for filling time well or recovering when your mind goes blank.

<div class="bigidea">A frame you know by heart frees your mind for ideas: Point, Reason, Example, Point.</div>

## 1. Why structure matters in a short answer

In Take an Interview, each answer is short. Without a plan, most people do one of two things: they say one sentence and stop, or they wander around the topic until time runs out. Both lose points on topic development, which is about answering the question with clear, connected support.

A fixed frame solves both. You never wonder "what next?", because the next step is always the same. Everyday analogy: a pilot uses the same checklist for every flight, not because they forget how to fly, but because the checklist leaves their attention free for the weather.

We practise with **5 to 15 seconds of preparation and 45 seconds of speaking**. These are fibub's practice times; the test screen shows the real ones.

Step through a body paragraph and watch each move (Point, Explain, Example, Link) add one sentence.

<div class="steps" data-fig="@lang-peel-steps">
<div class="step"><p><strong>Point.</strong> Open with one sentence that states the idea of the paragraph.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><strong>Explain.</strong> Say why it is true or how it works, in one or two sentences.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><strong>Example.</strong> Give one specific example that proves the point.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><strong>Link.</strong> Close by tying the paragraph back to the question.</p></div>
</div>

Notice that the Point and the Link say nearly the same thing; the Explain and Example in between do the proving.

## 2. The frame: Point, Reason, Example, Point

| Step | What it does | Typical length | Starter |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Point** | Answers the question directly | 1 sentence | *I'd say..., Personally, I...* |
| **Reason** | Explains why | 1 to 2 sentences | *The main reason is that...* |
| **Example** | Proves the reason with something real | 2 to 3 sentences | *For instance, last year...* |
| **Point** | Returns to your answer | 1 sentence | *So overall..., That's why...* |

Five to seven sentences at a calm pace fill about 45 seconds. If you have spare time, add a detail to the example or a short concession ("Of course..., but...") before the last point.

The frame also works for personal questions. "What do you do to relax?" becomes: what I do (point), why it helps (reason), last Sunday (example), so it is my reset (point).

## 3. The method: planning in a few seconds

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">

**1. Hear every part of the question.** Two-part questions ("where... and why") need both. Say the parts to yourself in two words each.

</div>
<div class="step">

**2. Pick a side in one second.** Choose the side with the easiest example, not the "right" one. There is no right one.

</div>
<div class="step">

**3. Find one example.** A real person, place, time or number from your life. Inventing a realistic small example is fine; it is a language test, not a lie detector.

</div>
<div class="step">

**4. Start with the point.** Do not plan more. Say your first sentence and let the frame carry you: reason, example, point.

</div>
<div class="step">

**5. Watch the clock, then close.** With about ten seconds left, move to your closing sentence. A clean finish a few seconds early is better than being cut off mid-word.

</div>
</div>

## 4. Worked example

**Question:** "Is it better to live in a big city or a small town when you are young?"

**Plan (about 5 seconds):** City. Jobs. Cousin in Chicago, three offers. Close.

**Answer:**

> **(Point)** When you're young, I'd say a big city is better, mainly because of the opportunities. **(Reason)** Most internships and first jobs are in cities, and at that age you need experience more than anything. **(Example)** My cousin is a good example. She moved to Chicago at twenty with no contacts, and within a month she had three internship offers, something that would never have happened in our home town. **(Concession)** Of course, cities are expensive, and she shared an apartment with three people. **(Point)** But for a few years, the chances you get are worth it.

Notice what the plan did not include: no full sentences, no vocabulary list. Just side, reason, example. The frame turned those into a 45-second answer.

## 5. Making the example specific

A general example ("Sometimes I study with friends") proves nothing. A specific one makes your reason believable and shows off vocabulary. Add any two of these:

- **Who:** my roommate, my chemistry teacher, four of us
- **When:** last spring, every Thursday night, in my first week
- **Where:** in the library, at a café near my apartment
- **A number:** thirty seconds, three offers, two thousand dollars
- **What happened:** and then I realised..., in the end...

Compare: "Studying with friends helps." → "Every Thursday night, four of us meet in the library and quiz each other before chemistry tests, and my grades went up a full letter."

## 6. Filling time and recovering

**Finished early with 10 to 15 seconds left?** Add one more detail to your example (what happened next, how you felt), or a concession and your final point. Do not start a brand new reason you cannot finish.

**Mind gone blank?** Use a bridge that sends you back to your point: *"What I mean is...", "Let me put it another way...", "The key thing for me is..."* Then give a new detail.

**Made a mistake?** Correct it quickly if it is easy ("She go... she went home"), otherwise keep going. Restarting the whole answer wastes time and breaks your flow.

**Running out of time mid-sentence?** Finish the sentence simply. "...so that's why I prefer it." A complete simple sentence sounds better than a rushed complex one.

<div class="strategy">

**Practise the frame until it is automatic.** For one week, answer three random questions a day with the timer below, always using Point, Reason, Example, Point. Record yourself, then check: Did my first sentence answer the question? Could you point to the reason and the example? Did I close? By day five, most learners stop thinking about the frame and start thinking only about ideas, which is exactly the goal.

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"A structured answer will sound robotic."** Only if you announce it ("My first point is... My second point is..."). Natural connectors ("The main reason is", "For instance", "So overall") give structure without sounding like a speech. Listeners hear it as clear, not mechanical.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- Point: *I'd say..., Personally, I prefer..., For me, the answer is..., I'm in favour of...*
- Reason: *The main reason is that..., This is mostly because..., What really matters is...*
- Example: *For instance..., A good example is..., Last year, I...*
- Extra detail: *On top of that..., What's more..., And then...*
- Concession: *Of course..., Admittedly..., That said...*
- Final point: *So overall..., That's why..., So for me...*
- Bridges: *What I mean is..., Let me put it another way..., The key thing is...*

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Use one frame for every answer: Point, Reason, Example, Point.
- Plan only your side and one example; let the frame build the sentences.
- Make examples specific with who, when, where, a number or what happened.
- With spare time, add a detail or a concession; never start a new reason you cannot finish.
- If you go blank, use a bridge back to your point; correct small mistakes and keep going.

</div>
