**Module 6 · about 15 minutes.** By the end you will be able to run a full, timed TOEFL iBT mock at home with the practice in this course, take a shorter mini mock on this page, and use the results to plan your last weeks.

<div class="bigidea">A mock test is a rehearsal under real rules: the same order, the same clock, one listen, no pausing. It tells you where your time and focus run out before the real test does.</div>

## 1. What a full mock looks like

The 2026 TOEFL iBT has four sections, about 90 minutes of tasks in all: **Reading** (about 30 minutes, about 50 items), **Listening** (about 29 minutes, about 47 items), **Writing** (about 23 minutes, 12 items) and **Speaking** (about 8 minutes, 11 items). The [test format lesson](/learn/courses/toefl/test-format/) has every task. Because the test is [adaptive](/learn/courses/toefl/adaptive-test/), times and item counts can vary a little.

A home mock is like a flight simulator. It cannot copy every detail of a real flight (our drills do not adapt to your answers), but it trains the things that matter most: following the order, keeping to time, and staying calm when something goes wrong.

**Set up first:**

- A quiet room, a computer with a keyboard and headphones. The listening drills are read aloud by your browser's voice.
- Paper and a pen, or a small whiteboard, for notes.
- Phone in another room. Close every other tab.
- No spell check: the writing pad on this site has none, and neither does the test.

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">

**Before you start.** Write the start time on your paper. Promise yourself: no pausing, no replaying, no looking things up.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Reading.** Set a 30-minute timer for the whole section. Move steadily; if an item is hard, choose your best answer and go on.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Listening.** Play each clip once, take short notes, answer, move on. Mark guesses with a "?" so you can check them later.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Writing.** Build a Sentence first, then the email and the discussion post with the writing pad timers. Keep the last minute of each for checking.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Speaking.** Do the repeat sentences and then interview questions back to back, recording yourself. Do not restart an answer.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Record.** Note your reading and listening accuracy, save your writing, keep your recordings for the review.

</div>
</div>

<div class="strategy">

**Practice at the hour of your real test.** If your test is at 8 a.m., take at least one mock at 8 a.m. Your focus and voice are different at different times of day, and the mock should match the day.

</div>

## 3. A worked example: Daniel's first mock

Daniel is aiming for an overall band of 5 for a graduate program. His notes after his first full mock:

- Reading: Complete the Words 90% right, but he finished the academic passage with 4 items guessed.
- Listening: Choose a Response 70%; everything else above 85%.
- Writing: email fine; discussion post only 85 words.
- Speaking: interview answers stopped after about 20 seconds.

**The thinking.** Daniel looks for patterns, not a general feeling. Reading is a **timing** problem: he spends too long on Complete the Words checking each blank. Choose a Response is a **skill** problem: the best replies are often indirect, and he picks options that repeat words. The short discussion post is a **planning** problem. The short interview answers are a **structure** problem.

**His next ten days:** a strict 8-minute limit for Complete the Words; daily lcr rounds with the [choose a response method](/learn/courses/toefl/choose-a-response/); one discussion post a day with a 1-minute plan; three interview questions a day using a view, reason, example frame from [answer structure](/learn/courses/toefl/answer-structure/). Then another full mock.

## 4. Mistakes to avoid in a home mock

<div class="myth">

**"My mock score tells me my exact TOEFL band."** Our items are original and fixed, and the real test adapts and is scored by ETS with its own methods. A mock gives a rough picture. Look at the pattern across sections and the trend across two or three mocks, not one number.

</div>

- **Pausing or replaying.** The real test plays each clip once. A replayed clip gives a false score.
- **Repeating drills you know by heart.** Use first rounds and items you have not seen.
- **Skipping Speaking.** It is only 8 minutes, but it often decides the overall band. Record it.
- **A mock every day.** Two or three full mocks over your last month, each with a careful review, help more than daily mocks with no review. See the [study plan](/learn/courses/toefl/study-plan/).

## 6. Review: where you actually improve

Spend at least as long reviewing as you spent on the mock. For every wrong or guessed item, write one line with a short reason label, then count the labels:

<div class="phrases">

- **Reading reasons:** *misspelled ending, wrong word form, ran out of time, true but not asked, matched words not meaning*
- **Listening reasons:** *chose the repeated word, missed the indirect reply, changed plan, missed the speaker's attitude, lost focus*
- **Writing reasons:** *word order, missing a request, too short, no new idea, verb errors*
- **Speaking reasons:** *dropped words in repeat, stopped early, no example, long pauses, unclear endings*

</div>

Any label that appears three times or more points to the lesson to revisit. Use the scoring lessons for [writing](/learn/courses/toefl/writing-scoring/) and [speaking](/learn/courses/toefl/speaking-scoring/) to judge your answers.

## 7. Check your mock judgment

<figure class="fig" data-fig="drill" data-set="m6-mock" data-n="8"><figcaption>Mock test decisions: what would you do? Original situations.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="keypoints">

- Keep the real order: Reading, Listening, Writing, Speaking, about 90 minutes of tasks.
- Use one round each of rcw, rdl, rap, lcr, lconv, lann, ltalk and w-bas, plus the writing pads and speaking timers.
- No pausing, no replaying, no restarting answers.
- A home mock does not adapt, so read it as a rough guide and watch the trend.
- Review for as long as the mock took and label each mistake with its reason.

</div>
