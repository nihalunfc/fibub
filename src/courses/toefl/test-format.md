**Module 1 · about 15 minutes.** By the end you will know every task in the 2026 TOEFL iBT: what it asks, how long its section is and what skill it really measures. Keep this page as your map.

<div class="bigidea">The 2026 TOEFL iBT has four sections, Reading, Listening, Writing and Speaking, built from short tasks. Quick tasks test the building blocks (words, sentences, sounds); longer tasks test whether you can use them for study.</div>

## 1. The whole test at a glance

<div class="steps" data-fig="testmap" data-mode="toefl">
<div class="step">

Four sections, about **90 minutes** of tasks in all. ETS says to allow **about two hours** including instructions, and because the test adapts, times and item counts can vary a little. Press Next to open each section.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Reading, about 30 minutes, about 50 items.** Complete the Words, Read in Daily Life (short real-world texts), and Read an Academic Passage.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Listening, about 29 minutes, about 47 items.** Choose a Response to one sentence, then a conversation, an announcement and an academic talk, each with questions.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Writing, about 23 minutes, about 12 items.** Build a Sentence (word order), Write an Email, and Write for an Academic Discussion.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Speaking, about 8 minutes, about 11 items.** Listen and Repeat, then Take an Interview.

</div>

## 2. Reading

| Task | What you see | What you do | What it really tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| Complete the Words | A short paragraph in which some words are cut off | Type the missing letters of each word | Vocabulary, spelling and grammar in context |
| Read in Daily Life | A notice, email, schedule, ad or post | Answer questions about it | Finding information quickly in real texts |
| Read an Academic Passage | A passage like a short textbook section | Answer questions on facts, inference, vocabulary and purpose | Understanding academic ideas and their logic |

## 3. Listening

| Task | What you hear | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Listen and Choose a Response | One short thing someone says | Pick the best reply |
| Listen to a Conversation | Two people talking, often on campus | Answer questions about it |
| Listen to an Announcement | A notice, for example from a professor or campus office | Answer questions about it |
| Listen to an Academic Talk | A short lecture or talk | Answer questions about the main idea, details and the speaker's purpose |

## 4. Writing

| Task | What you do |
|---|---|
| Build a Sentence | Put words and phrases in the right order to complete a reply in a short exchange |
| Write an Email | Write an email for a real situation (to a professor, a manager, a company), covering the points asked |
| Write for an Academic Discussion | Read a professor's question and two students' posts, then add your own post with your opinion and reasons |

## 5. Speaking

| Task | What you do |
|---|---|
| Listen and Repeat | Hear a sentence and say it back exactly, clearly and naturally |
| Take an Interview | Answer a series of questions from an interviewer on a topic, giving your view with reasons and examples |

<div class="myth">

**"The short tasks are easy marks, so I can skip practising them."** Quick tasks such as Complete the Words and Build a Sentence come in large numbers, so together they count for a lot. Small errors in spelling and word order add up. Modules 2 and 4 train them directly.

</div>
