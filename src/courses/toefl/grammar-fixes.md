**Module 4 · about 16 minutes.** By the end you will know the ten grammar mistakes that appear most often in learners' emails and discussion posts, a quick way to find them in your own writing, and how to fix each one. Then you will practice on more than forty original sentences.

<div class="bigidea">You do not need perfect grammar. You need to stop making the same few frequent mistakes, because a handful of errors repeated in every paragraph is what readers notice most.</div>

## 1. Why a few mistakes matter so much

In the email and discussion tasks, language use is one of the things raters look at (lesson 4.5). Two kinds of errors hurt the most:

1. **Errors that block meaning.** The reader cannot tell what you mean, or has to read twice.
2. **Frequent errors.** The same slip, such as a missing -s or a missing article, in almost every sentence.

The good news: researchers who study learner writing (for example Ferris 2011) find that most errors fall into a small number of patterns, and that learners who check for their own top patterns improve quickly. You do not have to learn all of English grammar before test day. You have to know your own top five mistakes and look for them.

An everyday picture: a doctor does not test you for every possible illness. They check the common ones first. Your proofreading should work the same way.

## 2. The ten mistakes, and the fix for each

**1. Subject-verb agreement.** The verb must match its real subject.
- ✗ She study biology. → ✓ She **studies** biology.
- ✗ The results of the survey was surprising. → ✓ The results ... **were**.
- Watch for: *everyone, each, one of* (singular); *there is / there are*.

**2. Wrong tense.** Use the time words as clues.
- ✗ Yesterday I go to the library. → ✓ Yesterday I **went**.
- ✗ I live here since 2023. → ✓ I **have lived** here since 2023.
- ✗ When I arrived, the lecture already started. → ✓ ... **had already started**.

**3. Conditionals.** After "if" about the future, use the present.
- ✗ If I will have time, I will join. → ✓ If I **have** time, I will join.
- ✗ If he took the course last year, he would not have this debt now. → ✓ If he **had taken** the course last year, he **would not have** this debt now. (The "if" part is about the past, so it needs "had taken".)

**4. Verb forms after other words.**
- ✗ I look forward to meet you. → ✓ ... to **meeting** you.
- ✗ She suggested to take the bus. → ✓ She suggested **taking** the bus.
- ✗ He can speaks. → ✓ He can **speak**.
- ✗ The rules were announce. → ✓ The rules were **announced**.

**5. Word form.** Right word family, wrong part of speech.
- ✗ I am interesting in biology. → ✓ I am **interested** in biology.
- ✗ She explained it very clear. → ✓ ... very **clearly**.
- ✗ Bike lanes are benefit. → ✓ Bike lanes are **beneficial**.

**6. Countable and uncountable nouns.**
- ✗ some advices, informations, furnitures → ✓ some **advice**, **information**, two **pieces of furniture**
- ✗ less students → ✓ **fewer** students (but **less** time, **less** money)

**7. Articles (a, an, the).**
- ✗ I want to become engineer. → ✓ ... become **an** engineer.
- ✗ She is a honest person. → ✓ **an** honest person (the h is silent).
- ✗ The education is important. → ✓ **Education** is important (a general idea, no article).

**8. Prepositions.** Learn them with their word, as a chunk.
- on Monday, at 9 a.m., in March, in 2026
- depend **on**, good **at**, interested **in**, responsible **for**, similar **to**

**9. Sentence boundaries.**
- Comma splice: ✗ I missed the bus, I was late. → ✓ I missed the bus**, so** I was late. / I missed the bus**.** I was late.
- Fragment: ✗ Because the bus was late. → ✓ Because the bus was late**, I missed the start.**
- Double linking: ✗ Although it rained, but we went. → ✓ Although it rained, we went.

**10. Relative clauses and comparisons.**
- ✗ My roommate, which is from Chile → ✓ My roommate, **who** is from Chile
- ✗ more long, most easiest → ✓ **longer**, **easiest**

## 3. Worked example: proofreading a post

Here is an original student post with errors. Read it, then see how the fixes were found.

> In my opinion, school uniform is good idea. Every students look the same, so nobody feel bad about there clothes. Also, parents spends less money, because they dont need buy many clothes. If my school will have uniforms, I will be happy.

**Pass 1, verbs:** "Every students look" → "Every student looks" (every + singular). "nobody feel" → "nobody feels". "parents spends" → "parents spend". "dont need buy" → "don't need to buy". "If my school will have" → "If my school had uniforms, I would be happy" (an imagined situation).

**Pass 2, nouns and articles:** "school uniform is good idea" → "school uniforms are a good idea".

**Pass 3, small words and spelling:** "there clothes" → "their clothes".

> In my opinion, school uniforms are a good idea. Every student looks the same, so nobody feels bad about their clothes. Also, parents spend less money, because they don't need to buy many clothes. If my school had uniforms, I would be happy.

Notice the method: three short passes, each looking for one kind of error, instead of one long read looking for everything.

## 4. The method: proofread in three passes

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">

**Pass 1. Verbs only.** Point at every verb. Does it agree with its subject? Is the tense right for the time words? Is the form right after "can", "to", "look forward to", "was"?

</div>
<div class="step">

**Pass 2. Nouns only.** Point at every noun. Singular or plural? Countable or not? Does a singular countable noun have a/an/the/my in front?

</div>
<div class="step">

**Pass 3. Small words and joins.** Prepositions, linking words, commas between full sentences, spelling of short words (their/there, its/it's, than/then).

</div>
<div class="step">

**Then: your personal list.** Finish with one quick look for your own top mistakes (see the strategy box below).

</div>
</div>

In timed practice you may have only a minute for this. Do pass 1 first: verb errors are the most frequent and the most visible.

<div class="strategy">

**Build your personal error list.** After each practice email or post, write down every grammar error you find (or a friend or teacher finds). After five tasks, count them by type. Your top three types are your personal list. Write them at the top of your practice page, and look for exactly those three in every final check.

- [ ] My mistake 1: ____________
- [ ] My mistake 2: ____________
- [ ] My mistake 3: ____________

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"I should use only very simple sentences so I make no mistakes."** Accuracy matters, but range matters too. A post made only of short, safe sentences shows a narrow range. The better plan is to use a few structures you control well (a "because" clause, a relative clause with "who" or "which", one conditional) and check them carefully. Safe and varied beats simple and repetitive.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

Chunks worth learning whole, because their grammar is fixed:

- *look forward to* + -ing; *suggest* + -ing; *be used to* + -ing
- *depend on*, *be good at*, *be interested in*, *be responsible for*
- *the number of* + plural noun + singular verb ("the number of students has grown")
- *one of the* + plural noun ("one of the best courses")
- *If I were you, I would...*; *If I had known, I would have...*
- *I would be grateful if you could...*

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Frequent errors and errors that block meaning cost the most; you do not need perfect grammar.
- The top patterns: agreement, tense, conditionals, verb forms, word form, countable nouns, articles, prepositions, sentence boundaries, relative clauses and comparisons.
- Proofread in three passes: verbs, nouns, small words and joins.
- Keep a personal list of your top three mistakes and check for them every time.
- Use a few structures you control well; safe and varied beats simple and repetitive.
