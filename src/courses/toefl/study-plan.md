**Module 1 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will have a personal week-by-week plan, a rough idea of your level, and daily habits that raise your bands.

<div class="bigidea">Your plan comes from three numbers: weeks until the test, hours a week, and your weakest section. Give the weakest section the most time, because it pulls your overall band down the most.</div>

## 1. Find your starting point

Take this short mixed check without a dictionary. It is not a TOEFL score, just a pointer to your weakest area.

## 3. A good study week

| Day | 45-60 minutes |
|---|---|
| Mon | One lesson in your focus section + its drill |
| Tue | Focus section drills + 15 minutes of academic listening (a lecture or podcast) |
| Wed | One lesson in another section + its drill |
| Thu | Focus section under time: speaking timer or writing pad |
| Fri | Word building: 20 academic words, their forms (-tion, -al, -ly) and spelling |
| Sat | Timed mixed practice; review every mistake |
| Sun | Rest, or light reading in English |

## 4. Daily habits that raise your band

- **Read academic English** for 15 minutes: popular science articles, university news pages, encyclopedia entries. Notice how words are spelled and built.
- **Listen to short lectures or podcasts**, then say the main idea and two details out loud.
- **Speak for one minute** on a simple question with the timer. Record, listen, fix one thing.
- **Write one short email or post** a day: to a professor, a classmate or a company.
- **Keep a mistake list** from every drill, with the reason. Read it each weekend.

<div class="myth">

**"I will just memorise templates."** Templates help you start, but the 2026 tasks are short and specific, and raters notice answers that do not fit the question. Learn flexible phrases, then practise using them in your own words.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Plan from three numbers: weeks left, hours a week, weakest section.
- The weakest section gets about double time; raising it moves the overall band fastest.
- Study most days in short sessions; take a full mock near the start and near the end.
- Daily: academic reading, short lectures, one minute of timed speaking, one short piece of writing, a mistake list.

</div>
