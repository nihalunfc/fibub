**Module 1 · about 12 minutes.** By the end you will know what the TOEFL iBT measures, how the 2026 version differs from the old one, and how to use this course to prepare on your own.

<div class="bigidea">The TOEFL iBT measures whether you can use English for study and everyday life at university. Since 21 January 2026 it is shorter, adaptive and scored from 1 to 6.</div>

## 1. What the name means

TOEFL stands for **Test of English as a Foreign Language**. "iBT" means internet-based test. It is made by **ETS**, a non-profit testing organisation in the USA. You take it on a computer, either at a test centre or at home with an online proctor (the **Home Edition**).

The test checks four skills: **Reading, Listening, Writing and Speaking**. It mixes academic English (a short lecture, a passage from a textbook-style article, a class discussion) with everyday campus and life English (an email to a professor, a notice, a conversation with a classmate).

## 2. What changed in 2026

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">

**Shorter.** About 90 minutes of tasks (allow about two hours with instructions), down from about two hours of tasks before.

</div>
<div class="step">

**New task types.** Short, quick tasks such as *Complete the Words* (fill in missing letters), *Build a Sentence* (put words in order) and *Listen and Repeat* sit alongside longer passages and talks.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Adaptive.** The test adjusts to your level as you go, so you see questions that fit you. Lesson 1.4 explains what that means for your strategy.

</div>
<div class="step">

**A new score scale.** Each section and the overall score are reported from **1 to 6**, lined up with the CEFR levels used in Europe and elsewhere. For two years after January 2026 a comparable score on the old **0 to 120** scale is also shown.

</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Old TOEFL books still work."** Much of the skill is the same, but most old task types (long integrated speaking tasks, independent essays, 700-word passages with ten questions) are gone. Practise the 2026 tasks; this whole course is built on them.

</div>

## 3. Who takes the TOEFL

- **Students** applying to universities and colleges in the USA, Canada, the UK, Australia, Europe and Asia. Most American universities accept it.
- **Graduate applicants**, often alongside the GRE or GMAT.
- **Scholarship and exchange** applicants.
- Some **professional and visa** uses; check whether your organisation accepts the TOEFL iBT, and which score it wants.

Moving to Canada as a permanent resident? Canada's immigration department uses other tests such as CELPIP; fibub has a free [CELPIP preparation course](/learn/courses/celpip/).
