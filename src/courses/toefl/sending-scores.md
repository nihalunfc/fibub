**Module 6 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will know where to find your TOEFL scores, how to send them to universities without paying more than you need to, how long they last, and how to decide whether to retake.

<div class="bigidea">Your score only helps when the right university receives it in time. Plan your recipients before the test, check each university's own rules, and treat a short score as a map for the next attempt.</div>

## 1. Getting your scores

After the test, Reading and Listening are scored and your Writing and Speaking answers are rated. Your scores then appear in your **ETS account**, the same account you used to book. ETS shows the current waiting time on ets.org; check it before you plan any deadline.

Your score report shows a **band from 1 to 6 for each section**, in half bands, and an **overall band**, which is the average of the four rounded to the nearest half band. For two years after January 2026, it also shows a comparable score on the old 0 to 120 scale. The [scores lesson](/learn/courses/toefl/scores-and-bands/) explains the bands, CEFR levels and the comparison.

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">

**Log in to your ETS account** and open your scores when they are ready.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Download a copy (PDF) for yourself.** It is useful for your own records and for some applications, but many universities accept only official reports sent by ETS.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Write down each section band** next to each university's requirement: the overall band and any section minimums.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Decide:** send scores now, add more recipients, or plan a retake.

</div>
</div>

## 2. Sending scores to universities

Think of score reports like posting a sealed letter. The university trusts the letter because it comes straight from ETS, not from you. That is why most admissions offices want an **official score report** sent by ETS, and why a screenshot or your own PDF is often not enough.

**Free recipients chosen before the test.** When you book, ETS lets you choose a number of institutions to receive your scores **free of charge**. You must choose them before a deadline close to your test date. After that, the free choice is gone. The number of free recipients and the deadline can change, so check ets.org when you book.

**Additional score reports for a fee.** After the test, you can order reports for more institutions from your ETS account, for a fee per report. Check ets.org for the current fee and delivery times.

**Institution codes.** Each university or program has a code in ETS's list. Search carefully: big universities may have several codes for different schools or campuses. If the university's admissions page lists a code, use that one.

<div class="strategy">

**Choose your free recipients early.** Most students know their top universities before the test. Naming them as free recipients saves money. If you are worried about sending a weak score, remember that a university sees the score you send it, and many set their own rules for multiple scores; read their admissions page.

</div>

### Deadlines

Order reports early. Delivery is not instant, and the university also needs time to match your report to your application. Use the same name and date of birth on your TOEFL booking and your application, so the two can be matched.

## 3. How long scores last

TOEFL iBT scores are valid for **two years from the test date**. After that, ETS no longer sends them. Some universities also ask that your scores are still valid on their application deadline or on your start date, so check each university's rule.

Like a carton of milk, your score has a date on it. A score from 23 months ago may be valid today but expired by the time the university reads your file.

## 4. If a score is short

First, look at the details calmly. Is it the **overall band** that is short, or **one section** below a minimum? Being half a band short in one section is common, and it is the easiest gap to close.

**Compare with your mocks.** If your [home mocks](/learn/courses/toefl/mock-tests/) were clearly higher in that section, nerves or a technical issue may have played a part. If they were close to the result, the result is probably fair, and you need more practice before another try.

**Score review.** ETS has offered a review of Writing or Speaking scores for a fee. Check ets.org for whether it is available for your test, the fee and the deadline. A review can confirm, raise or lower a score.

### Retaking

You can take the TOEFL iBT again. ETS sets rules about how soon you can retest; check ets.org for the current waiting period and fees. A new test gives a new score in **every section**, so keep all four warm.

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">

**Take a day or two** before you decide, so you plan with a clear head.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Choose a date that leaves time to change something:** often three to six weeks for half a band in one section, longer for bigger gaps. Make sure the new score can reach universities before their deadlines.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Spend most of your practice on the short section**, about 60% of your time, and keep the other three warm.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Take a full mock** about a week before, then follow the [final week plan](/learn/courses/toefl/final-week/).

</div>
</div>

## 5. A worked example: Sofia's scores

Sofia is applying to three graduate programs. Their requirements: Program A, overall 5 with no section below 4.5; Program B, overall 4.5; Program C, overall 5 with Speaking at least 5. Her scores: Reading 5.5, Listening 5, Writing 5, Speaking 4.5, overall 5.

**The thinking.** The average is (5.5 + 5 + 5 + 4.5) ÷ 4 = 5. Program A: overall 5, lowest section 4.5, so she **meets** it. Program B: she **meets** it easily. Program C: overall is fine, but Speaking is 4.5, below its minimum of 5, so she **does not meet** it yet.

**Her plan.** She chose A and B as free recipients before the test, so their reports are on the way. For C, she checks the deadline: seven weeks away. She books a retake in four weeks, spends most of her practice on Speaking with [answer structure](/learn/courses/toefl/answer-structure/) and daily recorded interview answers, keeps the other sections warm, and orders a report for C after the new scores arrive. She also emails C's admissions office to ask whether they accept scores from more than one test date.

<div class="myth">

**"If I retake right away, I will do better because I know the test."** Knowing the format helps a little, but your English is the same as last week. Without focused practice on the weak section, the most likely result is a similar score for another fee. Give yourself enough time to change something, and check that the new score can still arrive before the deadline.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- **Words on the report and in your account:** *section band, overall band, test date, score report, recipient, institution code, comparable score*
- **Words in university requirements:** *minimum overall, section minimum, valid at the time of application, official scores, sent directly from ETS*
- **Words for next steps:** *additional score report, score review, retest, waiting period, deadline, fee*

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Scores appear in your ETS account after rating; download a copy, but most universities want official reports from ETS.
- Choose free recipients before the test deadline; reports ordered later cost a fee. Check ets.org for current rules.
- Scores are valid for two years from the test date; check each university's own validity rule.
- Check section minimums as well as the overall band.
- A retake scores every section again: focus on the short one, and leave time for the new score to arrive.
