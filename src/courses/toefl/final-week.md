**Module 6 · about 14 minutes.** By the end you will have a day-by-day plan for your last seven days, a sleep routine, the extra checks for the Home Edition, and a 15-minute warm-up for test morning.

<div class="bigidea">The last week is not for building new skills. It is for keeping your English warm with short timed practice, removing every practical risk, and arriving rested.</div>

## 1. What the last week is for

By now, your level is mostly set. A week cannot add a full band, but tiredness, a technical problem or panic about time can easily cost you half a band. The final week protects your score more than it raises it.

Think of a musician before a concert. The pieces were learned months ago. In the last days they play through the hard passages slowly, check the instrument, and sleep well, so their hands do what they have trained them to do.

**What the final week can do:**

- Make timing automatic, especially in Reading and the two writing tasks.
- Fix one or two repeated mistakes from your mistake list.
- Keep your ear and voice used to English every day.
- Remove practical risks: ID, booking, room, equipment, route.

**What it cannot do:** teach you thousands of new words, change your accent, or replace months of practice. Do not try; it only adds stress.

## 2. The plan, day by day

Count back from your test day (Day 0). For what happens on the day itself, see [test day](/learn/courses/toefl/test-day/).

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">

**Day 7: last full mock.** Take it at the same time of day as your test, using the [mock test lesson](/learn/courses/toefl/mock-tests/).

</div>
<div class="step">

**Day 6: review.** Label every mistake with its reason. Judge your writing and speaking with the scoring lessons. Pick two "final fixes", for example "check word endings in Complete the Words" and "give an example in every interview answer".

</div>
<div class="step">

**Days 5 and 4: targeted practice.** About 60 minutes a day: one timed round of your weakest reading task and your weakest listening task, one email or discussion post with the timer, and four or five interview questions, recorded. Work on your two fixes only. Home Edition: run the system check now.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Day 3: mixed and lighter.** About 40 minutes across all four sections. Check your booking: date, time, time zone (for Home Edition), and the ID rules.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Day 2: light touch.** About 30 minutes: your mistake list, a few Listen and Repeat sentences, two interview questions. Test center: plan your route. Home Edition: set up the room as it will be on the day.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Day 1: rest.** Twenty minutes at most, then stop. ID ready. Home Edition: run the system check again, charge or plug in the computer, and tell everyone at home when you need quiet. Normal bedtime.

</div>
</div>

<div class="strategy">

**Practice at test pace.** In the final week, time every drill and every answer. You are not learning how to do the tasks any more; you are training to do them well inside the clock.

</div>

## 3. Home Edition: the extra checks

If you test at home, the room and the computer are part of your test. Use ETS's current rules on ets.org as your checklist. In general:

- **The computer.** Use the same computer, browser and network you checked. Turn off automatic updates for the day if you can, and close other programs.
- **The room.** A private room with a closed door, a clear desk and good light on your face. The proctor will ask you to show the room by webcam.
- **The people.** Nobody should come in or speak to you during the test. Tell your family or roommates the exact times.
- **The notes.** Only the note-taking materials ETS allows, ready on the desk.

A problem found on Day 4 is a small job. The same problem found at check-in can cost you the test.

## 4. A worked example: Aisha's week

Aisha's Home Edition test is on Thursday at 7 a.m. Her mock (the Thursday before, at 7 a.m.) showed: reading fine, listening fine, Write an Email missing one of the three requests, and interview answers that sounded rushed.

**Her final fixes:** number the three requests before writing the email; take a breath and say her view in one sentence before giving reasons.

**Her week:** Friday she reviews. Saturday and Sunday: one email a day with the requests numbered, five interview questions a day, recorded. She runs the system check Saturday and finds her webcam needs a driver update; she fixes it that day. Monday: a mixed 40 minutes and she confirms the time zone in her booking. Tuesday: light practice and she sets up her desk. Wednesday: twenty minutes, a second system check, and bed at her usual time. She wakes at 5:30 a.m. from Sunday on, so 7 a.m. feels normal.

## 5. Sleep, food and nerves

**Sleep.** Sleep helps your brain keep what you practiced, and a tired brain listens and reads more slowly. Keep the same bedtime and wake-up time all week. For an early test, start waking early from about Day 5.

**Food and drink.** A normal breakfast you know agrees with you. Your usual amount of coffee or tea, no more.

**Nerves.** A little nervousness keeps you sharp. If it grows, breathe in for 4 counts and out for 6, five times. Remember that the test is adaptive: harder items can be a sign you are doing well, so a hard question is no reason to panic (see [the adaptive test lesson](/learn/courses/toefl/adaptive-test/)).

<div class="myth">

**"I should study until midnight the night before, to have everything fresh."** Late cramming gives you a little more to remember and a lot less energy to use it. The Speaking section comes last, when you are most tired. Rest the night before, so you still have a clear voice at the end.

</div>

## 6. The 15-minute morning warm-up

Do it at least an hour before check-in. Easy, short, and only to get your English moving.

| Minutes | Do this |
|---|---|
| 0 to 5 | Read a short article aloud: a university news page or popular science. Clear endings, steady rhythm. |
| 5 to 8 | One short listening clip. Say the main idea and one detail out loud. |
| 8 to 12 | Two spoken answers with the timers below. |
| 12 to 15 | Read your two final fixes and a few favorite phrases. Then stop. |

<div class="phrases">

- **Calm sentences for yourself:** *I have done this in my mocks. One item at a time. A hard item may mean I am doing well.*
- **Interview starters to have ready:** *I would say that... ; In my opinion... ; The main reason is... ; For example, when I...*
- **Email openers to have ready:** *I am writing to ask about... ; Thank you for... ; Could you please let me know...*

<div class="keypoints">

- The final week sharpens and protects; it does not build new skills.
- Day 7 full mock, Day 6 review, Days 5 to 2 shorter timed practice, Day 1 rest.

- Home Edition: run the system check days ahead and again the day before; set up the room early.
- Same bedtime all week; a 15-minute warm-up on test morning.
