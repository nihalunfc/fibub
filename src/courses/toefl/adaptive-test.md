**Module 1 · about 11 minutes.** By the end you will understand what "adaptive" means, why it changes how you should pace yourself, and which old test-taking habits to drop.

<div class="bigidea">An adaptive test chooses what to show you next based on how you have done so far. It aims to find your level quickly, so stronger answers lead to harder questions, and harder questions can earn a higher band.</div>

## 1. What "adaptive" means

ETS says the 2026 TOEFL iBT "adjusts to your level as you go" and that "test time and items may vary". ETS does not publish every detail of its design, but adaptive language tests generally work in one of two ways:

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">

**Item by item.** After every answer, the computer estimates your level and picks the next question to match it. Right answer: a slightly harder question. Wrong answer: a slightly easier one.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Stage by stage (multistage).** You answer a first block of questions at a middle level. Your result on that block decides whether your second block is easier or harder.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Either way, the goal is the same:** spend most of the test on questions near your real level, because those tell the most about you. That is why an adaptive test can be shorter and still be accurate.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Your score depends on the difficulty, not only the count.** Answering harder questions correctly is worth more than answering easy ones. Two people with the same number right can get different bands.

</div>
</div>

## 2. What it means for your strategy

| Old habit | Better in an adaptive test |
|---|---|
| Rush the first questions to save time for later | Work **carefully** at the start of each section; it shapes what comes next |
| Skip hard questions and come back later | You may not be able to return; answer each one as you meet it |
| Count your right answers to guess your score | Ignore counting; harder questions mean you are doing well |
| Panic when a section feels hard | Read hardness as a good sign and keep your pace |
| Leave blanks when unsure | Never leave blanks: eliminate, then choose |

<div class="strategy">

**The three rules.** (1) Accuracy first, especially early in each section. (2) Never leave a blank. (3) Keep a steady pace; do not spend two minutes on one item.

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"If the questions get easier, I must be failing."** Not necessarily. Difficulty moves up and down as the test checks your level from both sides. Do not try to read your score from how questions feel; just answer each one well.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- The 2026 TOEFL iBT adapts to your level as you go, so times and items vary a little.
- Strong answers usually lead to harder questions, which can earn higher bands.
- Be most careful early in each section; your first answers shape what follows.
- Never leave a blank; eliminate wrong options and choose.
- Hard questions are usually a good sign: keep calm and keep your pace.
