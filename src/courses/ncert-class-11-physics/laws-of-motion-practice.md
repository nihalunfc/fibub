**Practice · Class 11 Physics · Chapter 4.** Read the chapter lesson, [Laws of Motion](/learn/courses/ncert-class-11-physics/laws-of-motion/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer. Marks go for the free-body diagram, the law quoted, the equation along each axis, the substitution with units and the answer.

<div class="bigidea">For every problem: pick one body, draw only the forces on it, choose axes (one along the acceleration), write ΣF = ma on each axis and solve. For a connected system write one equation per body and join them with what they share (T, a).</div>

## How to set out a numerical

1. Draw the body as a dot with every force on it as an arrow (weight, normal, tension, friction, applied).
2. Choose axes: along the slope for an incline, towards the centre for circular motion.
3. Write ΣF = ma along each axis for each body.
4. Solve, give the unit, and check that the sign and size make sense (N ≥ 0, T ≥ 0).

## 1-mark questions

1. State Newton’s first law of motion.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A body remains at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line unless a net external force acts on it.</details>

2. Define impulse and give its SI unit.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Impulse is the product of force and the time for which it acts (the area under the F–t graph), equal to the change in momentum. SI unit: N s (= kg m/s).</details>

3. Why does a passenger standing in a bus fall forward when the bus stops suddenly?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Inertia: the lower part of the body stops with the bus because of friction, while the upper part tends to continue moving forward.</details>

4. Can the force of friction ever accelerate a body?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Yes: the static friction of the floor on a walker pushes him forward, and the friction of a lorry floor accelerates the box lying on it.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

5. (2) A body of mass 5 kg is acted on by a net force of 20 N. Find its acceleration and its velocity after 3 s, from rest.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

a = F/m = 20/5 = **4 m/s²**; v = at = 4 × 3 = **12 m/s**.

</details>

6. (2) Why is it easier to pull a lawn roller than to push it?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>When you push along the handle, which is at an angle to the ground, part of the push is directed downward and adds to the weight, so the normal force and the friction grow. When you pull, the handle’s force has an upward component, which lowers the normal force and therefore the friction.</details>

7. (3) A cricket ball of 0.15 kg moving at 20 m/s is hit back along the same line at 30 m/s. If the contact lasts 0.01 s, find the change of momentum and the average force on the ball.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Take the original direction as positive: Δp = m(v − u) = 0.15(−30 − 20) = **−7.5 kg m/s** (7.5 kg m/s in the direction of the return). F = Δp/Δt = 7.5/0.01 = **750 N**.

</details>

8. (3) A 60 kg man stands on a weighing scale in a lift. What does the scale read when the lift (a) moves up at constant speed, (b) accelerates up at 2 m/s², (c) accelerates down at 2 m/s², (d) falls freely? (g = 10 m/s²)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) N = mg = **600 N**. (b) N = m(g + a) = 60 × 12 = **720 N**. (c) N = m(g − a) = 60 × 8 = **480 N**. (d) N = m(g − g) = **0** (weightlessness).

</details>

9. (3) A bullet of mass 20 g moving at 300 m/s is stopped by a wooden block and penetrates 6 cm. Find the average resisting force.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Deceleration: v² = u² + 2as gives 0 = 90000 − 2a(0.06), so a = 7.5 × 10⁵ m/s². Force F = ma = 0.02 × 7.5 × 10⁵ = **15 000 N**.

</details>

## 5-mark questions

10. (5) State the law of conservation of momentum and derive it from Newton’s second and third laws for two colliding bodies. Give two examples.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Statement.** If no net external force acts on a system, its total momentum remains constant.

**Derivation.** Two bodies A and B collide for a short time Δt. Force on A by B is F_AB and on B by A is F_BA = −F_AB (third law). By the second law, change of momentum of A is F_AB Δt and of B is F_BA Δt = −F_AB Δt. Adding: Δp_A + Δp_B = 0, so p_A + p_B (before) = p_A + p_B (after): m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂.

**Examples.** Recoil of a gun when it fires; a rocket moving forward as gas goes backward; two skaters pushing apart.

</details>

11. (5) Two blocks of masses 4 kg and 6 kg are joined by a light string passing over a smooth pulley. Find the acceleration of the system and the tension (g = 10 m/s²). Draw the free-body diagrams.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

FBD of each mass: weight down, tension up. For the heavier block (6 kg, accelerating down): 60 − T = 6a. For the lighter (4 kg, accelerating up): T − 40 = 4a.

Add: 20 = 10a, so **a = 2 m/s²**. Then T = 40 + 4 × 2 = **48 N**.

</details>

12. (5) A block of mass 10 kg rests on a rough slope of 30° (μ_s = 0.7, μ_k = 0.5; g = 10 m/s²). (a) Does it slide? (b) If a force pushes it just hard enough to start it sliding down and then removed, what is its acceleration afterwards?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Along the slope: mg sinθ = 100 × 0.5 = 50 N. N = mg cosθ = 100 × 0.866 = 86.6 N, f_max = 0.7 × 86.6 = 60.6 N. Since 50 N < 60.6 N, the block **does not slide**.

(b) Once it slides, friction is kinetic: f_k = 0.5 × 86.6 = 43.3 N. Net force down the slope = 50 − 43.3 = 6.7 N, a = 6.7/10 ≈ **0.67 m/s²** down the slope.

</details>

## Case-based question

A car of mass 1000 kg takes a flat circular bend of radius 50 m on a dry road (μ = 0.8) and then on a wet stretch (μ = 0.4). Take g = 10 m/s².

(a) What force provides the centripetal force? (b) Find the greatest speed on the dry road. (c) Find the greatest speed on the wet road. (d) Does the mass of the car affect these speeds?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) The force of friction between the tyres and the road, directed towards the centre.

(b) v = √(μrg) = √(0.8 × 50 × 10) = √400 = **20 m/s** (72 km/h).

(c) v = √(0.4 × 50 × 10) = √200 = **14.1 m/s** (51 km/h).

(d) **No.** The mass cancels from μmg = mv²/r.

</details>

## Assertion-reason questions

Choose: (A) both A and R true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

13. **A:** It is easier to stop a slowly moving truck than a fast one with the same brakes. **R:** The momentum of the faster truck is greater.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). More momentum needs a larger impulse to remove it, so for the same force it takes longer or farther.</details>

14. **A:** Action and reaction forces never cancel. **R:** They act on different bodies.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). Forces can cancel only if they act on the same body.</details>

15. **A:** A person in a freely falling lift feels weightless. **R:** The normal force of the floor on the person is zero.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). In free fall a = g, so N = m(g − a) = 0; the feeling of weight is the normal force from the floor, which vanishes.</details>

- ΣF = ma on each axis; draw a free-body diagram for each body, showing only the forces on it.

- Connected bodies: one equation each, joined by the shared tension and acceleration.
- Friction: f_s ≤ μ_sN, f_k = μ_kN; on a slope N = mg cosθ.
- Centripetal force mv²/r comes from real forces (friction, tension, normal component); v_max = √(μrg).
