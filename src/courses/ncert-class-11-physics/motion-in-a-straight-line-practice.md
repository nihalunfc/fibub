**Practice · Class 11 Physics · Chapter 2.** Read the chapter lesson, [Motion in a Straight Line](/learn/courses/ncert-class-11-physics/motion-in-a-straight-line/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer. Marks go for the sign convention, the equation chosen, the substitution with units and the final answer.

<div class="bigidea">Most problems here need three habits: choose a positive direction and keep to it, list what you know (u, v, a, s, t) before choosing an equation, and for graphs think “slope = rate, area = total”.</div>

## How to set out a numerical

1. Draw the line with the origin and the positive direction marked.
2. Write the known and unknown quantities with signs (a = −g for upward-positive free fall).
3. Pick the equation that has exactly one unknown.
4. Substitute with units and check that the sign and the size are sensible.

## 1-mark questions

1. Can the displacement of a body be zero when the distance travelled is not?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Yes, if the body returns to its starting point (for example, one full lap of a track).</details>

2. What does the area under an acceleration–time graph represent?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The change in velocity.</details>

3. What is the acceleration of a freely falling body at the highest point of its path?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>g = 9.8 m/s², directed downward (even though the velocity is momentarily zero).</details>

4. Write the SI unit and dimensions of acceleration.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>m/s²; [L T⁻²].</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

5. (2) Distinguish between average speed and average velocity with an example.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Average speed = total path length/time (a scalar); average velocity = displacement/time (a vector). A car that drives 5 km to a shop and 5 km back in 20 min has an average speed of 30 km/h and an average velocity of zero.</details>

6. (2) A train slows from 90 km/h to 54 km/h in 10 s. Find its acceleration.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

u = 25 m/s, v = 15 m/s. a = (v − u)/t = (15 − 25)/10 = **−1 m/s²** (a retardation of 1 m/s²).

</details>

7. (3) Derive the relation v² = u² + 2as using a velocity–time graph.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

For constant a the v–t graph is a straight line from u to v. Displacement s = area of the trapezoid = ½(u + v)t. Slope gives a = (v − u)/t, so t = (v − u)/a. Substitute: s = ½(u + v)(v − u)/a = (v² − u²)/2a. Hence **v² = u² + 2as**.

</details>

8. (3) A ball is thrown vertically upward at 25 m/s from a cliff 30 m high. With g = 10 m/s², find the time it takes to reach the ground at the bottom of the cliff.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Take upward positive; displacement at landing is −30 m. s = ut − ½gt²: −30 = 25t − 5t², so t² − 5t − 6 = 0, which gives (t − 6)(t + 1) = 0. Time **t = 6 s**.

</details>

9. (3) A car starting from rest has the acceleration shown: 2 m/s² for 5 s, then 0 for 10 s, then −5 m/s² until it stops. Find the total distance.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

After 5 s: v = 10 m/s, s₁ = ½ × 2 × 25 = 25 m. Next 10 s at 10 m/s: s₂ = 100 m. Stopping: t = 10/5 = 2 s, s₃ = ½ × 10 × 2 = 10 m. Total = 25 + 100 + 10 = **135 m**.

</details>

## 5-mark questions

10. (5) Derive the three equations of uniformly accelerated motion using a velocity–time graph, and state when each is the most useful.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Let the velocity change from u to v in time t with constant a.

1. Slope = a = (v − u)/t, so **v = u + at**. Use it when s is not involved.
2. Area under the graph = rectangle ut + triangle ½(v − u)t = ut + ½at², so **s = ut + ½at²**. Use it when v is not involved.
3. Eliminate t: **v² = u² + 2as**. Use it when t is not involved.

</details>

11. (5) A stone is thrown vertically up at 30 m/s. Taking g = 10 m/s², find (a) the maximum height, (b) the time to reach it, (c) the time to return to the thrower’s hand, (d) its velocity at 2 s and at 4 s, (e) its displacement after 5 s.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) H = u²/2g = 900/20 = **45 m**. (b) t = u/g = **3 s**. (c) 2u/g = **6 s**. (d) v = u − gt: at 2 s, 30 − 20 = **+10 m/s** (still rising); at 4 s, 30 − 40 = **−10 m/s** (falling). (e) s = 30(5) − 5(25) = 150 − 125 = **+25 m** (it is 25 m above the hand, coming down).

</details>

12. (5) Two trains A and B are on the same track, A ahead at 20 m/s and B behind at 30 m/s, 200 m apart. B’s driver sees A and applies the brakes with a retardation of 1 m/s². Show by calculation whether a collision occurs.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Take B’s velocity relative to A: initial 10 m/s, retardation 1 m/s². The relative distance needed to bring this relative velocity to zero: s = u²/2a = 100/2 = 50 m, taking 10 s. Since 50 m < 200 m, the relative gap never closes: **no collision** (the minimum gap is 150 m).

</details>

## Case-based question

A school bus on a straight road follows this schedule: it speeds up uniformly from rest to 15 m/s in 10 s, moves at 15 m/s for 40 s, then brakes to rest uniformly in 5 s.

(a) Find the acceleration in the first stage. (b) Find the total distance. (c) Find the average speed. (d) Which part of the v–t graph has area equal to the braking distance?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) a = 15/10 = **1.5 m/s²**.

(b) Areas: ½ × 15 × 10 = 75 m; 15 × 40 = 600 m; ½ × 15 × 5 = 37.5 m. Total = **712.5 m**.

(c) Total time = 55 s; average speed = 712.5/55 = **12.95 m/s** (about 13 m/s).

(d) The final triangle, 37.5 m.

</details>

## Assertion-reason questions

Choose: (A) both A and R true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

13. **A:** A body can have a constant speed but a changing velocity. **R:** Velocity includes direction, speed does not.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). (In a straight line this can happen only by reversing, but along a circle it happens all the time, as in the next chapters.)</details>

14. **A:** The distance covered in the n-th second by a body starting from rest and accelerating uniformly is proportional to (2n − 1). **R:** Distance covered in the first n seconds is proportional to n².

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). s_n = ½a n² − ½a(n − 1)² = ½a(2n − 1), the difference of the two n² distances.</details>

15. **A:** A heavier body falls faster than a lighter one in a vacuum. **R:** The force of gravity on a heavier body is greater.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(D). A is false: in a vacuum both fall with the same g. R is true (weight = mg) but a heavier body also has a larger inertia, and the two effects cancel.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Equations for constant a: v = u + at; s = ut + ½at²; v² = u² + 2as. Choose the one with a single unknown.
- Use a = −g for upward-positive free-fall problems; displacement can be negative.
- Slope of x–t = v; slope of v–t = a; area under v–t = displacement.
- Relative velocity: v_AB = v_A − v_B; for a chase, time = gap ÷ relative speed.
