## How to set out a numerical

1. List the data in SI units; convert °C to K for gas and radiation problems.
2. Draw the stages (warm, melt, warm) on a line if a substance changes state.
3. Write heat lost = heat gained for mixing problems, substitute with units and state the answer with its unit.

## 1-mark questions

1. What is absolute zero on the Celsius scale?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>−273.15 °C.</details>

2. Which has the larger specific heat: water or iron?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Water (4186 J/kg K against about 450).</details>

3. State Wien’s displacement law.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The wavelength at which a black body emits most strongly is inversely proportional to its absolute temperature: λ<sub>m</sub>T = constant (2.898 × 10⁻³ m K).</details>

4. What is the temperature of water at its maximum density?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>4 °C.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

5. (2) Why do we put a lid on a pan when we want water to boil faster?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The lid reduces heat loss by convection and evaporation and raises the pressure slightly, so the water gains heat faster and reaches its boiling point sooner.</details>

6. (2) Distinguish between heat and temperature.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference (joule). Temperature is a measure of hotness, related to the average kinetic energy of the molecules (kelvin). A bucket of warm water holds more heat than a cup of boiling water, though its temperature is lower.</details>

7. (3) A copper block of mass 0.5 kg at 100 °C is dropped into 1 kg of water at 20 °C. Find the final temperature. (c of copper 390, water 4200 J/kg K.)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

0.5 × 390 × (100 − T) = 1 × 4200 × (T − 20)

19 500 − 195T = 4200T − 84 000, so T = 103 500/4395 = **23.5 °C**.

</details>

8. (3) A wall of area 10 m² and thickness 20 cm has k = 0.8 W/m K. The inside is 25 °C and the outside 5 °C. Find the heat lost per second.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

H = kAΔT/d = 0.8 × 10 × 20 / 0.2 = **800 W**.

</details>

## 5-mark questions

9. (5) Define the coefficients of linear, area and volume expansion and show that γ ≈ 3α.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

α = ΔL/(LΔT), β = ΔA/(AΔT), γ = ΔV/(VΔT).

For a cube of side L, V = L³. After heating, the side is L(1 + αΔT) and

`$V' = L^3(1+\alpha\Delta T)^3 \approx L^3(1 + 3\alpha\Delta T)$`

since α is tiny, so terms in α² and α³ can be dropped. Then ΔV = 3αVΔT and **γ = 3α**. Similarly β = 2α.

</details>

10. (5) Describe the three modes of heat transfer with an example of each. Write the law for conduction and Newton’s law of cooling.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Conduction:** the transfer of heat through matter without bulk motion, e.g. a hot spoon in tea. H = kA(T₁ − T₂)/d.

**Convection:** the transfer by the moving fluid itself, e.g. sea breeze or a boiling pot. Warmer fluid rises, cooler sinks.

**Radiation:** transfer by electromagnetic waves, which needs no medium, e.g. the Sun’s heat reaching Earth.

**Newton’s law of cooling:** the rate of loss of heat of a body is proportional to its excess temperature over the surroundings (for small differences): dT/dt = −k(T − Tₛ), so T = Tₛ + (T₀ − Tₛ)e^(−kt).

</details>

## Case-based question

A student heats 200 g of ice at −10 °C with a steady 500 W heater. Specific heat of ice = 2100 J/kg K, latent heat of fusion = 3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg, specific heat of water = 4186 J/kg K.

(a) How much heat warms the ice to 0 °C? (b) How much heat melts it? (c) How much heat then warms the water to 50 °C? (d) How long does the whole job take?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Q₁ = 0.2 × 2100 × 10 = 4200 J.

(b) Q₂ = 0.2 × 3.34 × 10⁵ = 66 800 J.

(c) Q₃ = 0.2 × 4186 × 50 = 41 860 J.

(d) Total = 112 860 J; time = 112 860/500 = **226 s** (about 3.8 minutes). The melting stage takes more than half of the time while the temperature stays at 0 °C.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

Choose: (a) both true and R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

11. **A:** Steam at 100 °C causes more severe burns than boiling water at 100 °C. **R:** Steam has a large latent heat of vaporisation.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). Condensing steam gives up 2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg to the skin before even starting to cool.</details>

12. **A:** Doubling the kelvin temperature of a black body increases the power it radiates 16 times. **R:** The power radiated per unit area is proportional to T⁴.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). Both are true and R explains A: 2⁴ = 16.</details>

13. **A:** Metals feel colder than wood at the same temperature. **R:** Metals have a higher thermal conductivity than wood.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). Metal conducts heat away from your hand faster, so your skin cools faster and it feels colder.</details>
