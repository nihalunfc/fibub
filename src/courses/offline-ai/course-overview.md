**Module 1 · about 15 minutes · read first: nothing.** By the end you will know what the course covers, what you will build, and which lessons to take first.

## 1. Why this matters

Most people meet AI through a website: you type, a company's computers think, an answer comes back. That is fine until you have private files you cannot upload, no internet, a monthly limit you keep hitting, or a job that asks you to build AI into a workflow. This course teaches the other way: download a model and run it on a machine you control.

## 2. What you will be able to do

By the end of the course you will be able to:

- explain in plain words what a model is, what a token is, and why memory decides what you can run;
- choose hardware for your budget in India, Canada, the USA or the UK, and know what it costs to run;
- download a model such as Google's Gemma 4, install it, and use it with the internet switched off;
- call it from small Python programs, so it can work through your files while you do something else;
- search your own documents with it, privately;
- share one machine with a family or a team;
- compare the real cost with Claude, ChatGPT and Google plans, and know where each wins.

## 3. How the course is built

There are 5 parts, 18 modules and about 117 lessons. Every lesson follows the same shape:

1. **Why this matters**, in two to four sentences.
2. **Numbered sections**, each a plain explanation first.
3. **A "Maths and principle" box** with the rule behind it, one equation, an analogy and a tiny calculation. You can skip the box and still follow the lesson.
4. **A worked example** with made-up numbers.
5. **Practice**, with answers hidden until you open them.
6. **Check yourself**: three quick questions at the end.

Some lessons have **simulations**: small things to play with, such as a slider that shows whether a model fits in your memory. They run in your browser and send nothing anywhere.

<summary>Maths and principle</summary>

**Principle:** learning sticks when you do something with it within a day of reading it.

**Equation:** total time ≈ lessons × (reading + practice) = 117 × (20 + 10) minutes ≈ 58 hours.

**Analogy:** the course is a sum of small terms. Each lesson adds a little; skipping the practice is like dropping terms from the sum and hoping the total stays the same. It does not.

**Try it:** at 45 minutes a day, about how many weeks is 58 hours? (58 × 60 ÷ 45 ≈ 77 days, about 11 weeks.)

## 4. Which order to take it in

The course is written to be taken in order, but you do not have to. If your first goal is "get a model running on my laptop and use it from code", take this short path first:

1. Module 1 (this module) and Module 3, lessons 3.1 to 3.5 (tokens).
2. Lesson 4.1 (memory) and lessons 8.1 and 8.5 (shrinking a model to fit).
3. Module 9 (install, then test with the internet off).
4. Module 11 (call the model from Python).

That path is about 20 lessons. After it you can use local AI for real work, and the rest of the course makes you better at it. Lesson 1.4 has a short quiz that suggests a path for your own situation.

## 5. Worked example

*Made-up numbers.* Priya works in a small accounts office. She has a laptop with 16 GB of memory and about 40 minutes at lunch. She takes one lesson a day. After about four weeks of the short path she has a model running offline and a Python script that summarises each client's PDF statements into a one-page note. She has spent nothing on hardware. She then reads Module 16 to decide whether a desktop would pay for itself.

## 6. Practice

1. Write down the one job you most want local AI to do for you.
2. Find out how much memory (RAM) your computer has. On Windows: Settings, System, About. On a Mac: Apple menu, About This Mac.
3. Decide whether you will take the course in order or the short path first.

<details>
<summary>Answers and tips</summary>

1. Keep it concrete: "summarise my meeting notes" beats "use AI more". You will come back to this in the capstone.
2. Write the number down. 8 GB, 16 GB or 32 GB decides which models you can run, as Module 4 explains.
3. If you want results this month, take the short path; if you want the full picture first, go in order.
</details>

**New words:** *model* (the file that holds what the AI learned), *local* or *on-device* (runs on your own machine), *cloud* (runs on a company's computers over the internet).
