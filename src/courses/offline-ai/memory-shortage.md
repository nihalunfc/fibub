**Module 5 · about 15 minutes · read first: 5.2 and 5.6.** You'll be able to read the market and decide when to buy.

<div class="bigidea">In a shortage, judge a price against what is normal in your own country lately, not against launch news, and wait for a fair price if you can.</div>

## 1. Why this matters

In 2026, memory prices rose sharply. Graphics cards sold far above list price, Apple raised Mac prices in several countries, and laptops with more memory cost more. Buyers who understand why can avoid the worst prices.

## 2. What happened

AI data centres need enormous amounts of **high-bandwidth memory (HBM)**, a special stacked memory for AI chips. Memory makers moved factory lines to make it, so ordinary memory (in PCs, phones and graphics cards) became scarce. Prices of memory sticks, SSDs, graphics cards and high-memory computers all rose.

Two examples from one card, the RTX 5060 Ti 16 GB, read on 18 November 2026:

- **India:** lowest ₹44,767 today against a 12-month median of ₹69,000. It has become cheaper.
- **Canada:** lowest CA$1,170 today against a 12-month median of CA$674. It has become far dearer.

Same card, opposite directions. **Your local market is what counts.**

<div class="myth">

**"World news tells me what I will pay."** Headlines describe the global market. Your price depends on local stock, import costs, taxes and shops. The same card fell in price in India while it rose in Canada over the same year. Always check a price history for your own country.

</div>

## 3. Is today's price fair?

A **median** is the middle price over a period: half the days were cheaper, half dearer. Comparing today with the recent median is like checking the usual fare for a route before deciding a flight is a bargain.

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><b>Today's price.</b> In Canada the lowest price for the card is CA$1,170.</div>
<div class="step"><b>The recent normal.</b> The 3-month median is CA$1,073. Today ÷ median = 1,170 ÷ 1,073 ≈ 1.09.</div>
<div class="step"><b>The premium.</b> 1.09 − 1 = 0.09: today is about 9% above the recent normal.</div>
<div class="step"><b>The longer view.</b> Against the 12-month median of CA$674, today is about 74% higher. The shortage hit Canada hard this year.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Decide.</b> Set a price alert at about the 3-month median and keep using what you have. Buy when it is met, or sooner only if a real job is blocked.</div>
</div>

```math
\text{premium} = \frac{\text{today's price}}{\text{median price}} - 1
```

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Today's price</dt><dd>The lowest price you can actually buy at now, from a shop with clear returns.</dd>
<dt>Median price</dt><dd>The middle price over the last 3 or 12 months, from a price-tracking site for your country.</dd>
<dt>Premium</dt><dd>How far today is above (or, if negative, below) that normal, as a percentage.</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>The premium is the extra you pay for buying today rather than on an ordinary day. A big premium is the shortage charging you for impatience.</dd>
</dl>

## 4. How to buy well

1. Look up the price history for your country (price-tracking sites list 3- and 12-month medians).
2. Set a target near the 3-month median, and wait for it.
3. Consider the option that the shortage hits least: a used card, a previous-generation Mac, or a smaller model on what you own.
4. Buy memory you will need for years, once, rather than upgrading twice.
5. Do not buy on credit at a peak price to chase a model you have not tested (Lesson 5.1).

<details class="maths" open>
<summary>Maths and principle</summary>

**Principle:** in a volatile market, compare today's price with the recent median, not with launch news.

**Equation:** premium = (today's price ÷ 3-month median) − 1.

**Analogy:** buying flights: check the usual fare for the route before you decide a price is a bargain.

**Try it:** Canada: CA$1,170 against a 3-month median of CA$1,073? (1,170 ÷ 1,073 − 1 ≈ 9% above the recent median.)
</details>

## 5. Worked example

*Made-up numbers.* Mei in Vancouver wants a 16 GB card. Today's price is 9% above the 3-month median. She sets a price alert at the median, keeps using Gemma 4 E4B on her laptop, and buys five weeks later when a shop runs a sale.
