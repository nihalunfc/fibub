## 1. Why this matters

For most people who outgrow their laptop, the choice is between a 16 GB graphics card for a desktop and a Mac mini with 24 to 32 GB. One is fast, the other fits bigger models. Picking the right one saves a second purchase.

## 2. The two choices

**16 GB graphics card (RTX 5060 Ti 16 GB class):** 448 GB/s, fits models up to about 14B fully, fast prompt reading, about 180 W. Needs a desktop PC (Lesson 4.4).

**Mac mini M6 with 24 or 32 GB:** fits 27B to 31B models at 4-bit, quiet, about 25 to 40 W under load. Slower writing on the same model, and slower to read long prompts.

## 4. Who should pick which

- Already have a desktop and want speed on small to medium models: the **card**.
- Want bigger models, silence and low power, or do not own a desktop: the **Mac mini with 24 or 32 GB**. Skip 16 GB if you can: it is the size you are leaving behind.

<details class="maths" open>
<summary>Maths and principle</summary>

**Principle:** compare price per GB of fast memory and price per token per second, not price alone.

**Equation:** price per GB = price ÷ usable GB. Speed per money = (bandwidth ÷ model GB) ÷ price.

**Analogy:** buying rice by the kilo, not by the bag: a bigger bag can be the better deal.

**Try it:** US$429 for 15 usable GB versus US$1,299 for about 24 usable GB? (About $29 per GB versus about $54 per GB. The card is cheaper per GB but needs a PC; the Mac is a whole computer.)
</details>

## 5. Worked example

*Made-up numbers.* Priya in Pune has a three-year-old desktop with a 650 W supply. A 16 GB card at about ₹46,000 runs Gemma 4 12B at 40 to 50 tokens per second. A 32 GB Mac mini would cost well over twice that. She buys the card and keeps the Mac idea for when she needs 27B models.
