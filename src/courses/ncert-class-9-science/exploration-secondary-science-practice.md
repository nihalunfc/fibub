**Practice · Class 9 Science · Chapter 1.** Read the chapter lesson, [Exploration: Entering the World of Secondary Science](/learn/courses/ncert-class-9-science/exploration-secondary-science/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Nearly every question in this chapter asks one of four things: is the test fair, are the units and prefixes right, are the significant figures right, or what does the graph say? Check these four in order and you will rarely lose marks.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For a numerical, write what is given **with units**, the formula, the substitution, and the answer with its unit and the right number of significant figures.
2. For "why" questions, give a reason in a full sentence, with one example.
3. For a graph, label both axes with quantity and unit, choose a scale, plot with crosses, draw a best-fit line.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the SI unit of time and its symbol.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The second, s.</details>

2. How many significant figures are there in 0.0705?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>3 (the digits 7, 0 and 5; the zeros before 7 do not count).</details>

3. Convert 4.5 km to metres.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>4.5 × 1000 = 4500 m.</details>

4. What name is given to an error that shifts every reading the same way?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A systematic error (for example, a zero error).</details>

5. In a fair test, what is the variable that is deliberately changed called?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The independent variable.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

6. Why should a hypothesis be testable? Give one example of a hypothesis that is not. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Only a testable hypothesis can be supported or shown wrong by evidence, which is how science progresses. "Ghosts are shy" cannot be tested, because nothing could ever prove it wrong.</details>

7. A student measures a pencil as 14.0 cm using a ruler with 1 mm divisions. What is the least count of the ruler, and what is the reading in millimetres? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Least count = 1 mm. The length is 14.0 cm = 140 mm.</details>

8. Add 12.11 cm and 0.8 cm and give the answer with the correct number of decimal places. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>12.11 + 0.8 = 12.91. The least precise number (0.8) has 1 decimal place, so the answer is 12.9 cm.</details>

9. Explain the difference between accuracy and precision with an example. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Accuracy is how close a result is to the true value; precision is how close repeated results are to each other. A weighing scale that always shows 2.00 kg for a true 2.50 kg load is precise (the readings agree) but not accurate (it is 0.5 kg off, a zero error).</details>

10. To find the time period of a pendulum, a student times 30 oscillations as 54.6 s. Find the time period, and explain why timing 30 oscillations is better than timing one. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>T = 54.6 ÷ 30 = 1.82 s. A person's reaction time (about 0.2 s) is a large fraction of one swing, but small compared with 54.6 s. Dividing by 30 spreads the same timing error over 30 swings, so the error in T becomes about 0.2 ÷ 30 ≈ 0.007 s.</details>

## 5-mark questions

11. (a) List the steps of the scientific method. (b) A student wants to find out whether the colour of a surface affects how fast it warms in sunshine. State the independent, dependent and two controlled variables. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>(a) Observe, ask a question, make a testable hypothesis, test fairly, analyse the results, conclude and repeat or share.

(b) Independent: the colour of the surface (black, white, silver). Dependent: the temperature rise in a fixed time. Controlled variables: the size and material of the plates, the time in the sun, the position and angle to the sun, the starting temperature, the same thermometer.</details>

12. The readings of the length of a table are 120.4, 120.6, 120.3, 120.5 and 120.7 cm. (a) Find the mean. (b) Write the result as mean ± half the range. (c) If the true length is 120.0 cm, find the percentage error of the mean. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) Mean = (120.4 + 120.6 + 120.3 + 120.5 + 120.7) ÷ 5 = 602.5 ÷ 5 = 120.5 cm.

(b) Range = 120.7 − 120.3 = 0.4 cm, half = 0.2 cm, so L = 120.5 ± 0.2 cm.

(c) Percentage error = |120.5 − 120.0| ÷ 120.0 × 100 = 0.42 %. All readings are above 120.0, which suggests a systematic error (for example, a stretched or zero-error tape).

</details>

13. The table gives the stretch of a spring: load 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 N and stretch 2.1, 4.0, 6.2, 7.9, 10.1 cm. (a) State which variable goes on each axis. (b) Describe the graph you expect. (c) Find the slope using the points (1 N, 2.0 cm) and (5 N, 10.0 cm) of the best-fit line and say what it means. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) Load (the variable you change) on the x-axis, stretch (what you measure) on the y-axis, both labelled with units.

(b) The points lie close to a straight line through (or very near) the origin, so the stretch is proportional to the load. Draw a best-fit straight line, not a zig-zag.

(c) Slope = (10.0 − 2.0) ÷ (5 − 1) = 8.0 ÷ 4 = 2.0 cm/N. It means the spring stretches 2.0 cm for each newton of load.

</details>

## Case-based question

Arjun wants to know which of three paper aeroplane designs flies furthest. He throws each design five times from the same spot with a gentle push and measures how far it lands, using a tape marked in centimetres. The tape starts at the 2 cm mark, and he did not notice. Design A gives 410, 395, 402, 418, 405 cm (all read on the tape).

1. Name one controlled variable that Arjun should keep the same. *(1)*
2. Find the mean for design A. *(1)*
3. What kind of error does the tape's starting mark cause, and what should Arjun do to every reading? *(1)*
4. How could Arjun make his conclusion more reliable? *(1)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. The strength and angle of the throw (or the same paper, or the same thrower).
2. (410 + 395 + 402 + 418 + 405) ÷ 5 = 2030 ÷ 5 = 406 cm.
3. A zero (systematic) error: every reading is 2 cm too large. He should subtract 2 cm from every reading, giving 404 cm.
4. Throw more times, use the same thrower or a launcher, and keep the conditions (wind) the same.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

Choose: (a) both A and R are true and R explains A; (b) both true but R does not explain A; (c) A is true, R is false; (d) A is false, R is true.

14. **A:** Taking the mean of many readings does not remove a zero error. **R:** A zero error affects every reading in the same way.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). Because every reading shifts by the same amount, the average shifts by it too.</details>

15. **A:** The number 3.000 has four significant figures. **R:** Trailing zeros that come after a decimal point are significant.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). The zeros after the decimal point show the number was measured to that precision, so all four digits count and R explains A.</details>

16. **A:** A graph of stretch against load should be drawn by joining the dots. **R:** The scatter of points is caused by measurement error.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(d). A is false: draw a smooth best-fit line instead. R is true: the scatter is measurement error, which is exactly why joining the dots is wrong.</details>
