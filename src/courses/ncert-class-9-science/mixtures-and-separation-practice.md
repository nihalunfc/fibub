## How to set out your answers

For definitions give the property that makes the difference. For separation, name the method, the property it uses, and the steps in order. For calculations write the formula, put in the numbers with units, and box the answer. Draw neat labelled diagrams of distillation and chromatography.

## 1-mark questions

**1.** Name the process in which a solid changes directly into a gas on heating.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Sublimation.</details>

**2.** What is the name of the effect that makes a torch beam visible in fog?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The Tyndall effect.</details>

**3.** What is the solvent in a sugar solution made with water?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Water.</details>

**4.** Name the method used to separate cream from milk.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Centrifugation.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

**5.** Give three differences between a true solution and a suspension.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A true solution has particles under 1 nm, is clear, does not settle and passes through filter paper. A suspension has particles over 1000 nm, is cloudy or has visible particles, settles on standing and is held back by filter paper.</details>

**6.** Why is crystallisation preferred to evaporation for obtaining pure copper sulphate from its solution?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Evaporating to dryness can overheat and break down the crystals, and leaves soluble impurities mixed in. In crystallisation the solution is only concentrated and then cooled; pure crystals form while the impurities stay in the solution.</details>

**7.** Calculate the mass percent of a solution containing 25 g of sugar in 175 g of water.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Mass of solution = 25 + 175 = 200 g. Mass percent = 25 ÷ 200 × 100 = 12.5%.

</details>

**8.** A spot moved 3.6 cm while the solvent front moved 9 cm. Find its Rf value.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Rf = 3.6 ÷ 9 = 0.4.

</details>

**9.** Name the method of separation for each: (a) iron filings from sand, (b) oil from water, (c) pure water from sea water.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Magnet. (b) Separating funnel. (c) Distillation (the salt does not evaporate).</details>

## 5-mark questions

**10.** Describe how you would separate a mixture of sand, salt and iron filings. Name the property used at each step.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. Pass a magnet over the mixture: iron is magnetic and sticks to it.
2. Add water to the remaining sand and salt and stir: salt dissolves, sand does not (solubility).
3. Filter: sand stays on the paper (residue), salt solution passes through (filtrate).
4. Heat the filtrate to evaporate the water and leave the salt (volatility), or crystallise it.

</details>

**11.** Explain with a labelled diagram how fractional distillation separates ethanol (78 °C) and water (100 °C).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Heat the mixture in a flask fitted with a fractionating column filled with glass beads, a thermometer at the top of the column, and a condenser leading to a receiver. Both liquids evaporate but ethanol is more volatile. In the cooler column the water vapour condenses and runs back; ethanol vapour rises, reaches the top at 78 °C, is cooled in the condenser and is collected. When the thermometer reaches about 100 °C, change the receiver to collect water.

</details>

**12.** A saturated solution made with 250 g of water at 80 °C holds 169 g of salt per 100 g of water. When cooled to 20 °C only 32 g can dissolve per 100 g. How much salt crystallises out?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Loss per 100 g of water = 169 − 32 = 137 g. For 250 g of water: 137 × 250 ÷ 100 = 342.5 g of crystals.

</details>

## Case-based question

Anil sets up paper chromatography with a black marker ink. After 10 minutes the solvent front has moved 10 cm. He sees three spots: a yellow one 8 cm up, a red one 5 cm up and a blue one 2.5 cm up.

**13a.** Why do the dyes separate?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The solvent carries them up, but each clings to the paper by a different amount, so they travel at different speeds.</details>

**13b.** Find the Rf value of the red spot.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Rf = 5 ÷ 10 = 0.5.</details>

**13c.** Which dye is held most strongly by the paper?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The blue one, which travelled the shortest distance (Rf = 0.25).</details>

**13d.** What would Anil learn if the marker ink gave only one spot?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It probably contains only one dye (or dyes that travel together in this solvent).</details>

## Assertion and reason

Choose: (a) both true, R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

**14.** A: Milk shows the Tyndall effect. R: Milk is a colloid whose particles scatter light.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). Both are true and R is the explanation.</details>

**15.** A: Simple distillation separates alcohol and water completely. R: Their boiling points are very close.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(d). A is false: with close boiling points, simple distillation does not separate them well, so fractional distillation is needed. R is true.</details>

**16.** A: Dissolving sugar in water is a physical change. R: No new substance is formed.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). Sugar is still sugar and can be recovered by evaporation.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Mass percent = solute ÷ solution × 100; Rf = spot distance ÷ solvent distance.
- Choose the method from the property that differs: magnetism, size, solubility, density, boiling point, sublimation, speed on paper.
- Solution: no Tyndall effect, does not settle. Colloid: Tyndall effect, does not settle. Suspension: settles, filtered out.
- Fractional distillation for liquids with close boiling points; simple distillation for a solid dissolved in a liquid.

</div>
