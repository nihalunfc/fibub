**Practice · Class 9 Science · Chapter 10.** Read the chapter lesson, [Sound Waves: Characteristics and Applications](/learn/courses/ncert-class-9-science/sound-waves/), first. Try each question before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Two formulas do most of the work: v = f × λ for any wave, and d = v × t ÷ 2 for echoes and SONAR (the sound goes there and back).</div>

## How to answer

1. Write the formula, then the numbers with units, then the answer with a unit.
2. For echo questions, say that the time covers the journey there and back.
3. In theory answers, name the quantity (frequency, amplitude) that explains each difference.

## 1-mark questions

1. What type of wave is sound in air?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A longitudinal wave.</details>

2. Name the unit of frequency.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Hertz (Hz), one vibration per second.</details>

3. What is the approximate speed of sound in air at 20 °C?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>About 343 m/s.</details>

4. What does SONAR stand for?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Sound Navigation And Ranging.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Differentiate between a compression and a rarefaction. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A compression is a region where air particles are pushed close together, so the pressure is higher than normal. A rarefaction is a region where the particles are spread apart, so the pressure is lower.</details>

6. A bell rings in a jar. Air is pumped out and the sound fades. What does this show? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Sound needs a material medium to travel. With less air there are fewer particles to carry the vibration, so the sound fades and would not travel at all in a vacuum.</details>

7. A tuning fork vibrates 1200 times in 3 s. Find its frequency and time period. *(3 marks)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Frequency `$f = 1200 / 3 = 400$` Hz. Time period `$T = 1/f = 1/400 = 0.0025$` s (2.5 ms).

</details>

8. Why does thunder reach you several seconds after the lightning flash? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Light travels extremely fast (about 3 × 10⁸ m/s) so you see the flash almost at once. Sound travels only about 343 m/s, so it takes a few seconds to cover the distance.</details>

9. A boy hears the echo of his shout 1.5 s later. How far is the reflecting wall? (Speed of sound 343 m/s.) *(3 marks)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Total distance = `$v \times t = 343 \times 1.5 = 514.5$` m, there and back. The wall is `$514.5 / 2 \approx 257$` m away.

</details>

10. Why is the stage of a big auditorium often lined with curtains and the ceiling made rough? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Soft and rough materials absorb sound and scatter it, so they reduce reverberation. That keeps speech and music clear.</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

11. Explain how a SONAR finds the depth of the sea. A pulse returns after 3.4 s; find the depth if the speed of sound in sea water is 1500 m/s. *(5 marks)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

A ship sends an ultrasonic pulse down to the sea bed. The pulse is reflected and the receiver on the ship detects the echo. The time between sending and receiving is measured.

The pulse travels down and back, so the depth is `$d = v t / 2 = 1500 \times 3.4 / 2 = 2550$` m.

Ultrasound is used because it travels in a narrow beam and is not confused with ordinary sea and ship noises.

</details>

12. (a) Define wavelength, frequency and amplitude. (b) State how pitch and loudness depend on them. (c) A note of 680 Hz travels in air at 340 m/s: find its wavelength. *(5 marks)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Wavelength: distance between two consecutive compressions (or rarefactions). Frequency: number of vibrations per second. Amplitude: maximum displacement of a particle from its mean position.

(b) Pitch increases with frequency. Loudness increases with amplitude.

(c) `$\lambda = v/f = 340/680 = 0.5$` m.

</details>

13. Describe three uses of ultrasound. Why can an echo not be heard if the wall is only 5 m away? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Uses: (1) cleaning small parts in an ultrasonic bath, (2) medical scans (sonography) of organs and unborn babies, (3) finding cracks in metal blocks and railway tracks. For the wall 5 m away, the echo returns after 2 × 5 ÷ 343 ≈ 0.03 s, less than the 0.1 s the ear holds a sound, so it merges with the original and is not heard as a separate echo (it adds to reverberation).</details>

## Case-based question

14. A student claps in front of a large hill and hears the echo 1.2 s later. She then moves to the middle of a big empty hall with bare walls and hears a long ringing sound after a clap. Use 343 m/s for sound in air.

(a) How far is the hill? (b) Why did she hear a ringing sound in the hall and not a separate echo? (c) How could the hall be made clearer for speech? (d) Would the echo from the hill come back sooner or later on a colder day?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) `$d = vt/2 = 343 \times 1.2 / 2 \approx 206$` m.

(b) The walls reflect again and again and the reflections arrive less than 0.1 s apart, so they merge with the clap: reverberation.

(c) Add soft, sound-absorbing materials such as curtains, carpets and cushioned seats, or rough panels.

(d) Later. Sound is slower in colder air, so the round trip takes longer.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

15. **Assertion (A):** Sound cannot travel through a vacuum. **Reason (R):** Sound is a longitudinal wave that needs a material medium.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true, and R explains A.</details>

16. **Assertion (A):** A sound of higher frequency always has a larger wavelength in the same medium. **Reason (R):** Wavelength = speed ÷ frequency.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false, R is true. In one medium the speed is fixed, so a higher frequency gives a smaller wavelength.</details>

17. **Assertion (A):** The speed of sound is greater in steel than in air. **Reason (R):** Particles in a solid are closer and pass on vibrations faster.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true, and R explains A.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Sound is a longitudinal wave with compressions and rarefactions; it needs a medium.
- v = f × λ; T = 1/f; pitch depends on frequency, loudness on amplitude.
- Echo and SONAR use d = v × t ÷ 2 because the sound goes there and back.
- An echo needs a wall at least about 17 m away; closer reflections give reverberation.
- Ultrasound is above 20 kHz and is used in cleaning, scans, flaw finding and SONAR.
