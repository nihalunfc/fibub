**Practice · Class 9 Science · Chapter 7.** Read the chapter lesson, [Work, Energy, and Simple Machines](/learn/courses/ncert-class-9-science/work-energy-and-simple-machines/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Nearly every question here is one of five formulas: W = F s cos θ, KE = ½ m v², PE = m g h, P = W/t and effort × effort arm = load × load arm. Write the formula, put in numbers with units, and give the answer with its unit.</div>

## How to set out a numerical

1. Write what is given, converted to SI (kg, m, s, N).
2. Write the formula in symbols.
3. Substitute and calculate.
4. State the answer with its unit.

## 1-mark questions

1. Write the SI unit of power.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The watt (W), equal to 1 joule per second.</details>

2. A bus moves at constant speed along a straight road. The road’s normal reaction is at 90° to the motion. What is the work done by it?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Zero, because cos 90° = 0.</details>

3. Which has more kinetic energy: a 1 kg body at 4 m/s or a 4 kg body at 1 m/s?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The first: ½ × 1 × 16 = 8 J, against ½ × 4 × 1 = 2 J.</details>

4. Name the class of lever to which a pair of tweezers belongs.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Class 3: the effort is applied between the fulcrum and the load.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. A girl lifts a 5 kg bag through 2 m. Find the work she does (g = 10 m/s²). *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>W = m g h = 5 × 10 × 2 = 100 J.</details>

6. State the law of conservation of energy. A swing slows down and stops: has the law failed? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it only changes from one form to another. The law has not failed: the swing’s energy has turned into heat and sound through friction and air drag.</details>

7. Why does a longer ramp make it easier to push a barrel up? Does it reduce the work done? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The force needed is W × h/L, so for the same height h a larger length L means a smaller force. The work (force × length of ramp) stays the same without friction; it is spread over a longer distance. With friction the work is even a little more.</details>

8. A 60 W fan runs for 8 hours a day. How many units does it use in 30 days, and what does that cost at ₹7 per unit? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Energy per day = 0.06 kW × 8 h = 0.48 kWh. In 30 days = 14.4 kWh (units). Cost = 14.4 × 7 = ₹100.80.</details>

## 5-mark questions

9. (a) Show that the kinetic energy of a body of mass m moving at speed v is ½ m v². (b) Find the work needed to take a 1000 kg car from rest to 20 m/s. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) A constant force F gives a body starting from rest an acceleration a = F/m. Over a distance s it reaches speed v with v² = 2 a s. Work done W = F s = m a × v²/(2a) = ½ m v². This work is stored as kinetic energy.

(b) W = ½ × 1000 × 20² = 200 000 J = 200 kJ.

</details>

10. A pendulum bob of mass 0.5 kg is released from a height of 0.2 m above its lowest point (g = 10 m/s²). (a) What is its potential energy at the release point, taking the lowest point as zero? (b) What is its kinetic energy at the lowest point? (c) What is its speed there? Ignore friction. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) PE = m g h = 0.5 × 10 × 0.2 = 1 J.

(b) By conservation of energy all of it becomes kinetic energy: KE = 1 J.

(c) ½ m v² = 1, so v² = 2/0.5 = 4 and v = 2 m/s.

</details>

## Case-based question

A village builds a small rope-and-pulley hoist to raise 120 N sacks of grain 3 m to a loft. The hoist has 3 rope strands supporting the load. A farmer pulls with an effort and takes 12 s per sack.

11. (a) What effort is needed ideally? (b) How much rope must she pull per sack? (c) What work does she do on a sack, and what is her power? (d) Why is her real effort a bit more than the ideal value?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Effort = 120 ÷ 3 = 40 N.

(b) Rope pulled = 3 × 3 m = 9 m.

(c) Work = 40 × 9 = 360 J (same as 120 × 3). Power = 360 ÷ 12 = 30 W.

(d) Friction in the pulleys and the weight of the lower block add extra work, so the real effort is more than 40 N.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

12. **A:** A person carrying a load up a staircase does work, but carrying it along level ground does not (on the load, in the line of the force). **R:** Work depends on the displacement along the direction of the force.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A. Up the stairs the displacement has a part along the upward force; on level ground it is at 90°.</details>

13. **A:** A machine with MA = 5 does five times as much work as the effort put in. **R:** A machine can multiply force.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false: work out cannot exceed work in. R is true: the machine multiplies force, but the effort moves five times as far.</details>

- W = F s cos θ; KE = ½ m v²; PE = m g h; P = W/t.

- Falling from rest through h: v = √(2 g h), whatever the mass.
- Lever: effort × effort arm = load × load arm. Pulley with n strands: MA = n. Ramp: MA = L/h.
- Machines trade force for distance; they never create energy.
