**Practice · Class 9 Science · Chapter 11.** Read the chapter lesson, [Reproduction: How Life Continues](/learn/courses/ncert-class-9-science/reproduction-how-life-continues/), first. Try each question before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Sort every question into “one parent, copies” (asexual) or “two gametes fuse, variety” (sexual), then name the exact method or part. Always put the steps of a process in order.</div>

## How to answer

1. For a method, name it, say what happens in two or three steps, and give an example organism.
2. For diagrams, draw large, label with straight lines, and give the figure a title.
3. For “why” questions, link the feature to its job (for example, bright petals attract insects).

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the type of reproduction in which only one parent is involved.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Asexual reproduction.</details>

2. What is the female part of a flower called?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The carpel (pistil).</details>

3. Name the structure that grows from the pollen grain and carries the male gametes to the ovule.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The pollen tube.</details>

4. After fertilisation, what does the ovary develop into?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The fruit.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Give one difference between budding and fragmentation. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>In budding, a small bud grows out from the parent and breaks off (yeast, Hydra). In fragmentation the whole body breaks into pieces and each piece grows into a new organism (Spirogyra).</details>

6. Why is bread mould reproduction by spores so successful in the rainy season? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Spores are very light and have a tough coat, so many are blown around. In the humid, damp monsoon air they land on moist food and germinate quickly.</details>

7. Differentiate between self-pollination and cross-pollination. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>In self-pollination, pollen reaches the stigma of the same flower (or another flower of the same plant). In cross-pollination, pollen goes to a flower of a different plant of the same kind, giving more variation.</details>

8. A farmer wants banana plants that are exactly like the best parent plant. Which kind of reproduction should be used and why? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Vegetative propagation (asexual), such as using suckers or tissue culture. It gives offspring identical to the parent, so the good qualities are kept, and the plants flower and fruit earlier than plants grown from seed.</details>

9. Name the parts of the carpel and say what each does. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The stigma is sticky and catches pollen. The style is a tube that holds up the stigma and through which the pollen tube grows. The ovary contains ovules, each holding an egg cell; after fertilisation it becomes the fruit.</details>

10. A bacterium divides every 30 minutes. How many bacteria will there be from one after 3 hours? *(3 marks)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

3 hours = 180 min, so there are 180 ÷ 30 = 6 rounds of division. Cells = `$2^6 = 64$`.

</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

11. Describe, with the help of a labelled sketch, what happens from pollination to the formation of the zygote in a flower. *(5 marks)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Pollen lands on the stigma (pollination). The pollen grain germinates and a pollen tube grows down the style to the ovary. The tube enters an ovule and carries the male gametes. One male gamete fuses with the egg cell (fertilisation) to form the zygote.

A good sketch shows stigma, style, ovary, ovule, pollen grain, pollen tube and egg cell, all labelled.

</details>

12. Explain why sexual reproduction leads to variation and why that helps a species. Give two examples of animals with external and internal fertilisation. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>In sexual reproduction, gametes from two parents fuse, so the zygote gets a mixture of features from both, and every offspring is a different combination. If conditions change or a new disease arrives, some varied individuals are likely to survive and carry on the species. External fertilisation: frog, fish (in water). Internal fertilisation: hen, cow or human (in the female body).</details>

13. Describe four methods of asexual reproduction with one example each. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of asexual reproduction. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Binary fission: amoeba, bacteria. Budding: yeast, Hydra. Fragmentation: Spirogyra. Spores: bread mould, ferns. Vegetative propagation: potato eyes, bryophyllum leaf. Advantage: fast, with one parent. Disadvantage: no variation, so a single disease can wipe out all the offspring.</details>

## Case-based question

14. A gardener plants pieces of a potato, each with an eye, and a rose stem cutting with a node. Next to them he sows seeds of a pea plant. The pea flowers are pollinated within the same flower. Later, the pea ovary swells into a pod with five seeds, though the flower had ten ovules.

(a) Which two of the plantings use vegetative propagation? (b) What kind of pollination do the pea flowers show? (c) Why are there only five seeds from ten ovules? (d) Will pea seedlings be exactly like the parent? Give a reason.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) The potato pieces and the rose cutting.

(b) Self-pollination.

(c) Only fertilised ovules become seeds, so five of the ten ovules must have been fertilised.

(d) They will be very similar, but not necessarily identical: they come from fusion of gametes, which can mix traits. The potato and rose plants, by contrast, will be copies of the parent.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

15. **Assertion (A):** A potato piece without an eye does not grow into a plant. **Reason (R):** Buds are present in the eyes of a potato.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true, and R explains A.</details>

16. **Assertion (A):** Offspring formed by binary fission are genetically varied. **Reason (R):** Two parents take part in binary fission.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both false: binary fission has one parent and gives identical offspring.</details>

17. **Assertion (A):** Wind-pollinated flowers make a large amount of pollen. **Reason (R):** Most of the pollen grains carried by wind do not reach a stigma.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true, and R explains A: much pollen is wasted, so a lot must be made.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Asexual: one parent, clones (fission, budding, fragmentation, spores, vegetative propagation).
- Sexual: gametes fuse to a zygote; offspring vary.
- Flower: sepals, petals, stamens (male), carpel (female).
- Pollination, then pollen tube, then fertilisation, then seed (from ovule) and fruit (from ovary).
- External fertilisation (frog) needs water and many eggs; internal fertilisation (hen, mammals) needs fewer.

</div>
