**Practice · Class 9 Science · Chapter 9.** Read the chapter lesson, [Atomic Foundations of Matter](/learn/courses/ncert-class-9-science/atomic-foundations-of-matter/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Three habits win marks here: check charges add to zero when writing a formula, add atomic masses with every subscript counted, and always go through the mole: grams ÷ molar mass = moles, moles × 6.022 × 10²³ = particles.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Formulae: write the ions with charges, criss-cross, cancel, check.
2. Masses: write each atom’s mass × its subscript, then add.
3. Mole sums: write n = m/M with the numbers, then N = n × N<sub>A</sub>. Give units (g, mol, molecules).

Atomic masses: H = 1, C = 12, N = 14, O = 16, Na = 23, Mg = 24, Al = 27, S = 32, Cl = 35.5, K = 39, Ca = 40.

## 1-mark questions

1. State the law of conservation of mass.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>In a chemical reaction mass is neither created nor destroyed: total mass of reactants = total mass of products.</details>

2. What is the ratio by mass of hydrogen to oxygen in water?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>1 : 8.</details>

3. Write the formula of sodium sulphide (Na⁺, S²⁻).
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Na₂S.</details>

4. What is the mass of 1 mole of carbon atoms?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>12 g.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. 5.3 g of sodium carbonate reacts with 6 g of ethanoic acid and gives 8.2 g of sodium ethanoate, 0.9 g of water and 2.2 g of carbon dioxide. Show that this agrees with the law of conservation of mass. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Reactants: 5.3 + 6 = 11.3 g. Products: 8.2 + 0.9 + 2.2 = 11.3 g. They are equal, so mass is conserved.</details>

6. Write the formulae of (a) magnesium chloride and (b) aluminium oxide. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Mg²⁺, Cl⁻: MgCl₂. (b) Al³⁺, O²⁻: Al₂O₃.</details>

7. Calculate the molecular mass of ammonium sulphate, (NH₄)₂SO₄. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>N: 2 × 14 = 28; H: 8 × 1 = 8; S: 32; O: 4 × 16 = 64. Total = 28 + 8 + 32 + 64 = 132 u.</details>

8. Explain how Dalton’s theory accounts for the law of constant proportions. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Atoms of an element have a fixed mass, and atoms combine in fixed whole-number ratios in a compound. So the ratio of the masses of the elements in the compound is fixed. For water, 2 H atoms (2 u) and 1 O atom (16 u) always give 2 : 16 = 1 : 8.</details>

## 5-mark questions

9. (a) How many moles of water are in 54 g? (b) How many molecules is that? (c) How many atoms of hydrogen are there? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) M(H₂O) = 18 g/mol, so n = 54/18 = 3 mol.

(b) N = 3 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 1.807 × 10²⁴ molecules.

(c) Each molecule has 2 H atoms: 2 × 1.807 × 10²⁴ = 3.613 × 10²⁴ hydrogen atoms.

</details>

10. Write the criss-cross steps to get the formula of calcium phosphate, Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻. Check the charge. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

1. Write Ca²⁺ PO₄³⁻.
2. Criss-cross: Ca gets 3 (the number in phosphate’s charge), PO₄ gets 2 (the number in calcium’s charge).
3. The polyatomic ion is put in brackets: Ca₃(PO₄)₂.
4. Check the charge: 3 × (+2) = +6 and 2 × (−3) = −6, so the total is 0.

</details>

## Case-based question

A chemistry club weighs 4.0 g of hydrogen gas (H₂) and 32 g of oxygen gas (O₂) and burns them together under a closed bell jar. All the gas is used up and 36 g of water is formed.

11. (a) How many grams of hydrogen reacted? (b) Which law is shown by 36 g of water forming? (c) How many moles of water formed? (d) What would the total mass be before and after the reaction?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Water is 1 : 8 by mass, so 36 g contains 4 g of hydrogen: all 4 g reacted. Check: 32 g of oxygen needs 4 g of hydrogen.

(b) The law of constant proportions (1 : 8). Closed-jar masses before and after also show conservation of mass.

(c) 36 ÷ 18 = 2 mol.

(d) 4 + 32 = 36 g before and 36 g after: mass is conserved.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

12. **A:** The mass of an open beaker of vinegar and baking soda falls during the reaction. **R:** The law of conservation of mass does not apply to reactions that produce gases.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is true. R is false: the law applies to all reactions; the mass fell because the gas escaped into the air.</details>

13. **A:** 1 mole of water and 1 mole of carbon dioxide contain the same number of molecules. **R:** One mole of any substance contains 6.022 × 10²³ particles.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A. (Their masses differ: 18 g and 44 g.)</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Total mass is conserved; compounds have fixed mass ratios (water 1 : 8).
- Formula: criss-cross the charges, cancel common factors, bracket polyatomic ions, check total charge 0.
- Molecular or formula unit mass = sum of atomic masses (H₂O 18 u, CO₂ 44 u, NaCl 58.5 u).
- n = m/M; N = n × 6.022 × 10²³; the mass of 1 mole in grams is the mass in u.

</div>
