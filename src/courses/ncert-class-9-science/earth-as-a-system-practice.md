## How to set out answers

1. For a cycle, write the stages in order, and name the process at each stage (evaporation, photosynthesis, fixation...).
2. For a numerical, write what is given with units, the rule in words or symbols, the working, and the answer with its unit.
3. For “explain why”, give the cause first and then the result, in two or three short sentences.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the four spheres of the Earth.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Lithosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere and biosphere.</details>

2. What is meant by albedo?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The fraction of the incoming sunlight that a surface reflects.</details>

3. Name two greenhouse gases.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Carbon dioxide and methane (also water vapour and nitrous oxide).</details>

4. Which process puts water vapour into the air from the leaves of plants?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Transpiration.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Why is the natural greenhouse effect necessary for life on Earth? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Without greenhouse gases the average temperature of the Earth would be about −18 °C and the oceans would be frozen. The gases absorb heat sent out by the ground and send some of it back down, keeping the average at about 15 °C.</details>

6. Explain why growing a pulse crop in rotation with wheat helps the soil. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Bacteria in the root nodules of pulses fix nitrogen gas from the air into nitrogen compounds in the soil. The next crop of wheat uses these compounds to make proteins, so less fertiliser is needed.</details>

7. A sea breeze blows during the day, but a land breeze blows at night. Explain. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Land heats up faster than the sea. In the day the land is hotter, air above it rises, and cooler air flows in from the sea: a sea breeze. At night the land cools faster, so the sea is warmer: air rises over the sea and cool air flows from the land to the sea: a land breeze.</details>

8. Why is the amount of CO₂ in the air rising even though plants take in CO₂ by photosynthesis? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Burning fossil fuels, clearing forests and some farming add CO₂ faster than plants and the oceans can remove it. About half of the extra CO₂ stays in the air, so the amount keeps rising. Natural flows used to be balanced; human additions have broken the balance.</details>

## 5-mark questions

9. (a) Name the stages of the water cycle. (b) Say what powers it. (c) Explain why the amount of water on Earth does not run out. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>(a) Evaporation (and transpiration), condensation into clouds, precipitation as rain or snow, then runoff to rivers and the sea or storage as groundwater and ice. (b) The Sun’s heat. (c) Water is not used up but only changes form and place. It goes round the cycle again and again, so the total stays the same, although the amount of <i>fresh, easy-to-reach</i> water is small and can be polluted or overused.</details>

10. Describe the nitrogen cycle with the help of a flow chart. Name the type of bacteria at each stage. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>Nitrogen gas in the air → (fixation by <i>Rhizobium</i> in root nodules and free-living bacteria, lightning, and fertiliser factories) → nitrates and ammonia in the soil → (absorbed by roots) → plant proteins → (eaten) → animal proteins → (waste and death, decomposers) → ammonia → (nitrifying bacteria) → nitrates → (denitrifying bacteria) → nitrogen gas in the air. Lightning and the Haber process also fix nitrogen without bacteria.</details>

## Case-based question

A school in Pune plants 200 trees on its ground. A student reads that a full-grown tree takes in about 22 kg of carbon dioxide a year, and that carbon makes up 12/44 of the mass of CO₂. The school’s electricity use is 400 units a month, and the grid releases about 0.75 kg of CO₂ per unit.

11. (a) How much CO₂ do the 200 trees take in each year? (b) How much carbon is that? (c) How much CO₂ does the school’s electricity release in a year? (d) Do the trees cancel it? Suggest one more step.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) 200 × 22 = 4400 kg of CO₂ a year.

(b) Carbon = 4400 × 12/44 = 1200 kg.

(c) 400 × 0.75 = 300 kg a month, so 300 × 12 = 3600 kg a year.

(d) The full-grown trees (4400 kg) would absorb a little more than the school releases (3600 kg), but young trees absorb much less for the first years, and the school is still adding CO₂. The school should also use less electricity (LED lights, switching off fans) or install solar panels.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

12. **A:** Snow-covered mountains reflect most of the sunlight that falls on them. **R:** Snow has a high albedo.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.</details>

13. **A:** Nitrogen makes up 78% of the air, so plants can use it directly for making proteins. **R:** Nitrogen is a very important part of proteins.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false, R is true. Plants cannot use N₂ gas directly, because its atoms are held by a very strong triple bond. It must first be fixed into nitrates or ammonia.</details>

14. **A:** The summer monsoon winds blow from the sea to the land in India. **R:** In summer the land is hotter than the sea, so air over the land rises.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A. The rising air over the hot land leaves low pressure and draws in moist air from the ocean.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Four spheres: lithosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere, biosphere; they exchange energy and matter.
- Absorbed sunlight = 340 × (1 − albedo) W/m²; energy in = energy out at a steady temperature.
- Natural greenhouse effect: −18 °C becomes 15 °C. Extra gas from human activity warms the planet further.
- Cycles: water (evaporation, condensation, precipitation), carbon (photosynthesis versus respiration, decay and burning), nitrogen (fixation to denitrification).
- Wind is air moving from high to low pressure; uneven heating makes the sea and land breezes and the monsoon.
- Weather is short-term; climate is the average pattern over about 30 years.

</div>
