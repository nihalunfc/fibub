**Science · Class 9 · Chapter 3 · about 50 minutes.** By the end you will be able to name the main plant and animal tissues, say where each is found, and match the shape of its cells to its job.

<div class="bigidea">A body is not a heap of identical cells. Similar cells team up into tissues, and each tissue has a shape and structure that suits its job: hollow tubes for carrying, thick walls for strength, flat cells for quick exchange, long fibres for pulling.</div>

## 1. Why we need tissues

A single-celled *Amoeba* does everything inside one cell. A tree or a human has trillions of cells, so the work is divided, just as a school has teachers, cooks and drivers. A **tissue** is a group of similar cells that work together on one job. Tissues join to make **organs** (a leaf, a heart), and organs make **organ systems**.

## 2. Plant tissues

Plants are fixed in one place, so they need strong, mostly dead support tissue and few moving parts. Their tissues are of two types: **meristematic** (dividing) and **permanent** (grown and settled).

### Meristematic tissue

Cells in a meristem are small, boxy and packed together, with thin walls, a large nucleus and almost no vacuole. They **keep dividing** to make new cells.

- **Apical meristem** at the tips of roots and shoots: growth in **length**.
- **Lateral meristem** (cambium) in a ring in stems and roots: growth in **thickness**. In trees a ring is added each year; counting the rings gives the age.
- **Intercalary meristem** at the base of leaves or the joints of grass and bamboo: this is why grass grows back after mowing.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c9-tis-meristem"><figcaption>Pick a meristem and press play. Apical: the tips lengthen. Lateral: the stem widens and the cross-section gains rings. Intercalary: the grass regrows from the base after the mower has cut off the tops.</figcaption></figure>

### Permanent tissue

When cells stop dividing and take on a shape and a job, they form permanent tissue. There are **simple** permanent tissues (one kind of cell) and **complex** ones (several kinds working together).

**Simple tissues**

- **Parenchyma:** thin-walled living cells with a large vacuole and gaps between them. They store food and water, and when they have chloroplasts (**chlorenchyma**) they make food. In water plants like lotus and *Hydrilla* they hold air (**aerenchyma**) and help the plant float.
- **Collenchyma:** living cells with extra cellulose at the **corners**. It supports and also lets parts bend: a leaf stalk or the stem of a young plant.
- **Sclerenchyma:** dead cells with very thick walls of **lignin** and almost no space inside. It gives hardness: the coconut shell, the husk, the stone of a mango, jute and coir fibres.

**Complex tissues**

- **Xylem:** carries **water and minerals** from roots up to leaves. It has vessels and tracheids (dead hollow tubes with thick walls), fibres and living parenchyma.
- **Phloem:** carries **food** (sugar made in the leaves) to all parts, up and down. It has living **sieve tubes** with perforated end walls, **companion cells** that help them, parenchyma and fibres.

**Protective tissue: epidermis.** The outer layer of leaves, stems and roots is a skin of flat cells without gaps. On aerial parts it is covered with a waxy **cuticle** that cuts water loss. Leaves have tiny pores called **stomata**; the roots have **root hairs**, long thin extensions that soak up water. In older stems the epidermis is replaced by **cork** (bark), layers of dead cells with a waterproof substance (suberin).

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c9-tis-plant"><figcaption>Choose any plant tissue to see its cells drawn, where it is found and what its structure is good for. Notice how the structure matches the job: rings of lignin keep xylem tubes open against suction; sieve plates let sugar pass but keep the tubes joined.</figcaption></figure>

Water goes up the xylem because water evaporates from the leaves (**transpiration**), pulling more water up behind it like a drink through a straw. Sugar moves in the phloem to wherever it is needed, using the plant’s energy.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c9-tis-flow"><figcaption>Blue dots (water) move up only, in dead tubes; orange dots (sugar) move both ways in living tubes. Raise the sunshine and wind and transpiration speeds up, so water moves up faster.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="myth">

**“Phloem carries food down and xylem carries water up, always.”** Xylem is one-way, upward. Phloem is two-way: sugar goes from the leaves (where it is made) to wherever it is used or stored, which may be the roots, a growing fruit or a new shoot.

</div>

**Stomata.** The two guard cells of a stoma have chloroplasts. In sunlight they take up water by osmosis (from the Cell chapter), swell and bend apart, so the pore opens and CO₂ comes in for photosynthesis while water vapour leaves. At night, or when the plant is short of water, they lose water and the pore shuts.

## 3. Animal tissues

Animals move and respond, so they need tissues for covering, joining, moving and sensing. There are four main types: epithelial, connective, muscular and nervous.

### Epithelial tissue

Epithelial tissue forms the covering of the body and the lining of organs and cavities. Its cells are packed tightly with **very little space** between them, and rest on a thin basement membrane. Its jobs are protection, absorption and secretion.

- **Squamous:** thin flat cells; lining of the mouth, blood vessels, air sacs of the lungs. Gases and liquids pass easily.
- **Cuboidal:** cube-shaped cells; kidney tubes, glands. They secrete and absorb.
- **Columnar:** tall, narrow cells; the lining of the stomach and intestines. They absorb and secrete, and **goblet cells** make mucus.
- **Ciliated columnar:** with hair-like cilia that sweep; the windpipe and nose.
- **Stratified squamous:** many layers; the skin and the mouth lining; protects against rubbing.
- **Glandular epithelium:** part of the epithelium turns into a gland that secretes (sweat gland, salivary gland).

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c9-tis-epith"><figcaption>Choose a type. Look how the shape fits the job: flat for quick exchange, tall for absorbing, ciliated for sweeping, layered for protection. In the ciliated type the hairs beat in a wave.</figcaption></figure>

### Connective tissue

Connective tissue joins, supports and carries. Its cells sit in a non-living **matrix** (jelly, fibres, hard salts or a liquid), which gives each type its character.

- **Areolar tissue:** loose and soft; fills spaces between organs, under the skin; helps repair.
- **Adipose tissue:** cells filled with fat; stores energy, insulates and cushions.
- **Tendon:** dense and strong; muscle to bone.
- **Ligament:** elastic and strong; bone to bone.
- **Cartilage:** firm but flexible matrix with cells in small pockets; ear flap, nose tip, the ends of bones, and the rings of the windpipe; cushions joints. It has no blood vessels, so it heals slowly.
- **Bone:** hard matrix of calcium and phosphorus salts laid down in rings; supports, protects (skull protects the brain, ribs the lungs) and anchors muscles. Bone marrow makes blood cells.
- **Blood:** a **liquid matrix (plasma)** with cells. **Red blood cells** carry oxygen using haemoglobin; **white blood cells** fight germs; **platelets** help blood clot. Plasma also carries food, hormones and wastes.

### Muscular tissue

Muscle cells (fibres) are long and contain proteins that slide past each other to **contract** (shorten), then relax. Muscles move the body because they can pull, never push.

- **Skeletal (striated) muscle:** long cylindrical cells with **many nuclei** and light and dark stripes. **Voluntary**: you decide to move. Attached to bones by tendons; quick but tires.
- **Smooth (unstriated) muscle:** spindle-shaped cells with **one nucleus** and no stripes. **Involuntary**: the walls of the food pipe, stomach, blood vessels, iris of the eye. Slow and steady.
- **Cardiac muscle:** short, **branched** striated cells joined by dark **intercalated discs**; one nucleus. **Involuntary** and never tires: the heart beats about 70 times a minute all your life.

### Nervous tissue

Nervous tissue is made of **neurons** (nerve cells), which carry messages as electrical impulses. A neuron has a **cell body** with the nucleus, short branched **dendrites** that receive impulses, and a long **axon** that carries the impulse away. The gap between one neuron and the next is the **synapse**, crossed by chemicals. Nerve cells in the brain, spinal cord and nerves let you feel, think, and react: pull your hand from a hot tava before you even think “hot”.

## 4. Structure fits function: a quick map

| Tissue | Structure | Function it suits |
|---|---|---|
| Xylem vessel | dead hollow tube, lignin rings | carries water up without collapsing |
| Phloem sieve tube | living tube with sieve plates | moves sugar both ways |
| Sclerenchyma | thick lignin wall, tiny hollow | rigid strength |
| Collenchyma | thick corners | flexible support |
| Squamous epithelium | one thin flat layer | fast diffusion |
| Ciliated epithelium | cilia on tall cells | sweeps mucus |
| Bone | hard calcium matrix | strong support |
| Blood | liquid matrix | transport |
| Skeletal muscle | long striped fibres | strong quick pulling |
| Neuron | long axon with branches | carries impulses |

A mixture of tissues forms an organ: a leaf has epidermis, parenchyma, xylem and phloem; the heart has cardiac muscle, connective tissue, epithelium and nerves.

<div class="keypoints">

- A tissue is a group of similar cells doing one job; tissues form organs.
- Plant tissues: meristematic (apical, lateral, intercalary) and permanent (parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma, xylem, phloem, epidermis).
- Xylem carries water up (dead tubes); phloem carries food up and down (living tubes).
- Guard cells open stomata when turgid and close them when flaccid.
- Animal tissues: epithelial (cover and line), connective (join, support, carry), muscular (move), nervous (send messages).
- Connective tissues have cells in a matrix: areolar, adipose, tendon, ligament, cartilage, bone, blood. Tendon joins muscle to bone; ligament joins bone to bone.
- Skeletal muscle: striated, voluntary. Smooth: unstriated, involuntary. Cardiac: striated, branched, involuntary.
- Neurons: dendrites receive, axon carries, synapse passes the message on.

</div>
