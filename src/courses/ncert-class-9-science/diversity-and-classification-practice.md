**Practice · Class 9 Science · Chapter 12.** Read the chapter lesson, [Patterns in Life: Diversity and Classification](/learn/courses/ncert-class-9-science/diversity-and-classification/), first. Try each question before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">For any organism ask four questions in order: Does it have a nucleus? One cell or many? A cell wall? How does it feed? Those four answers give its kingdom, and the rest of the groups follow.</div>

## How to answer

1. For classification questions, give the group and the feature that decides it.
2. Write scientific names with capital genus and small species, underlined or in italics.
3. For keys, say what each question separates.

## 1-mark questions

1. Who introduced the binomial system of naming?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Carl Linnaeus.</details>

2. Name the five kingdoms.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae and Animalia.</details>

3. Which group of plants does not have vascular tissue and lives in damp places, like moss?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Bryophytes.</details>

4. Name the vertebrate class whose members have feathers.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Birds (Aves).</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Why do we need scientific names when every region has common names? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Common names differ from place to place and language to language, and the same name may refer to different species. A scientific name is the same worldwide and belongs to only one species, so everyone knows which organism is meant.</details>

6. Write the rules for writing a scientific name, with an example. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>It has two parts, genus then species. The genus starts with a capital letter and the species with a small letter. It is printed in italics, or each word is underlined separately when handwritten. Example: <i>Mangifera indica</i>.</details>

7. A cell has a nucleus, a cell wall and no chlorophyll, and the organism absorbs food from dead matter. Name its kingdom and give two examples. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Fungi, for example yeast and mushroom.</details>

8. Distinguish between monocots and dicots on three points. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Monocots have one seed leaf, parallel veins and fibrous roots (maize, wheat). Dicots have two seed leaves, net-like veins and a taproot (gram, mango).</details>

9. Why are bats and birds both able to fly but placed in different classes? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Classification uses basic features, not just one feature like flight. Bats have hair and feed milk to their young, so they are mammals; birds have feathers and lay hard-shelled eggs.</details>

10. A key has 4 yes/no questions along every path. How many organisms can it identify at most? *(2 marks)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Each question gives 2 outcomes, so the key can separate `$2^4 = 16$` organisms at most.

</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

11. Describe the classification hierarchy, from kingdom to species. Why is the species the basic unit? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The ranks from largest to smallest are kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus and species. Each rank contains groups that are more and more alike. For example, the tiger belongs to the animal kingdom, the phylum Chordata, the class Mammalia, the order Carnivora, the family Felidae, the genus Panthera and the species Panthera tigris. A species is the basic unit because its members are so alike that they can breed with each other and produce fertile offspring.</details>

12. State the four features used to separate the five kingdoms, and compare Fungi and Plantae using them. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The features: (1) a nucleus present or not, (2) one cell or many, (3) a cell wall present or not, (4) the mode of nutrition. Fungi and plants both have a nucleus and a cell wall, and many cells (some fungi are single-celled). But plants have chlorophyll and make food by photosynthesis, and the wall is made of cellulose, whereas fungi have no chlorophyll and absorb food from dead matter.</details>

13. Name the five classes of vertebrates and compare them on body covering and way of reproduction. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Fishes: scales, eggs laid in water. Amphibians: moist skin without scales, eggs laid in water. Reptiles: dry scales, eggs with leathery shells on land. Birds: feathers, hard-shelled eggs. Mammals: hair or fur, most give birth to live young and feed them milk.</details>

## Case-based question

14. A student finds an unknown plant. It has parallel veins on a long narrow leaf, no thorns, a flower and a fruit with seeds, and each seed has one seed leaf. A second plant has no flowers and no seeds, but has true roots, stems, leaves and tubes carrying water. A third has a body that is not divided into root, stem and leaf, and it lives in water.

(a) Name the group of each plant. (b) Is the first plant a monocot or a dicot? (c) What feature separates the second plant from a moss? (d) What question in a key would separate the second plant from a pine tree?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) The first is an angiosperm, the second a pteridophyte (fern), the third an alga (thallophyte).

(b) A monocot: parallel veins and one seed leaf.

(c) The fern has vascular tissue (tubes for water and food), which moss lacks.

(d) “Does it make seeds?” The fern does not; the pine tree does.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

15. **Assertion (A):** Whales are classified as mammals, not as fishes. **Reason (R):** Whales have hair, breathe with lungs and feed milk to their young.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true, and R explains A (whales have only sparse hair, but lungs and milk are enough).</details>

16. **Assertion (A):** Mushrooms are classified as fungi, not plants. **Reason (R):** Mushrooms contain chlorophyll and make their own food.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is true, R is false: mushrooms have no chlorophyll and absorb food from dead matter.</details>

17. **Assertion (A):** <i>Panthera tigris</i> and <i>Panthera leo</i> belong to the same genus. **Reason (R):** Both names begin with the same first word.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true, and R explains A: the first word of a binomial name is the genus.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Ranks: kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species. A species can breed and make fertile young.
- Binomial name: genus (capital), species (small), italic or underlined.
- Five kingdoms: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia, decided by nucleus, cell number, cell wall and nutrition.
- Plants: algae, bryophytes, pteridophytes, gymnosperms, angiosperms. Animals: invertebrate groups and five vertebrate classes.
- A dichotomous key with q questions separates up to 2<sup>q</sup> organisms.
