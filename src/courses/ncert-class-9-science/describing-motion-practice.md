**Practice · Class 9 Science · Chapter 4.** Read the chapter lesson, [Describing Motion Around Us](/learn/courses/ncert-class-9-science/describing-motion/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Every motion question is one of three kinds: pick the right quantity (distance or displacement, speed or velocity), read a graph (slope is rate, area is total), or choose the one equation of motion that has your three knowns and your one unknown. Write down u, v, a, t, s first, with signs.</div>

## How to set out a numerical

1. List what is given and what is needed, with units. Convert km/h to m/s (divide by 3.6).
2. Choose the equation: v = u + at (no s), s = ut + ½at² (no v), v² = u² + 2as (no t).
3. Substitute, calculate, give the unit. A body that stops has v = 0; one that starts from rest has u = 0; slowing down gives a negative a.

## 1-mark questions

1. What does the area under a velocity-time graph represent?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The displacement of the body.</details>

2. Write the SI unit of acceleration.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Metre per second squared, m/s².</details>

3. Can displacement be zero when the distance is not zero? Give an example.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Yes. A runner who completes one lap of a track returns to the start: displacement 0, distance one lap.</details>

4. What is the shape of the position-time graph of a body at rest?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A horizontal straight line (its position does not change with time).</details>

5. A car's speedometer shows 54 km/h. What is its speed in m/s?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>54 ÷ 3.6 = 15 m/s.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

6. Distinguish between speed and velocity. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Speed is distance per unit time and is a scalar (size only). Velocity is displacement per unit time and is a vector (size and direction). A car going round a circle at a steady 40 km/h has constant speed but changing velocity.</details>

7. A ball is thrown vertically up with a speed of 19.6 m/s. Taking g = 9.8 m/s², find the time to reach the highest point. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>At the top, v = 0, a = −9.8 m/s². From v = u + at: 0 = 19.6 − 9.8t, so t = 2 s.</details>

8. A train slows from 72 km/h to 36 km/h in 20 s. Find the acceleration. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>u = 72/3.6 = 20 m/s, v = 36/3.6 = 10 m/s. a = (10 − 20)/20 = −0.5 m/s², a retardation of 0.5 m/s².</details>

9. A boy walks 30 m east, then 40 m north. Find his distance and the magnitude of his displacement. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Distance = 30 + 40 = 70 m. Displacement is the hypotenuse of the right triangle: √(30² + 40²) = √2500 = 50 m (at about 53° north of east).</details>

10. Why is a body moving in a circle at constant speed said to be accelerating? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity, and velocity has both size and direction. In a circle the direction of motion keeps changing, so the velocity changes even though the speed is constant. The acceleration points towards the centre.</details>

## 5-mark questions

11. Derive s = ut + ½at² using a velocity-time graph. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Draw a v-t graph: a straight line from u at t = 0 to v at time t. The area under it is the displacement s. The area is a rectangle of height u and width t, which is ut, plus a triangle of base t and height (v − u). Since v − u = at (from the slope, a = (v − u)/t), the triangle has area ½ × t × at = ½at².

So s = ut + ½at².

</details>

12. A car starting from rest accelerates uniformly at 2 m/s² for 10 s, then moves at constant speed for 20 s, and then brakes uniformly to rest in 5 s. (a) Find the maximum speed. (b) Draw (describe) the v-t graph. (c) Find the total distance using the area. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) v = u + at = 0 + 2 × 10 = 20 m/s.

(b) A line rising from (0, 0) to (10 s, 20 m/s); a horizontal line to (30 s, 20 m/s); a line falling to (35 s, 0).

(c) Area = ½ × 10 × 20 + 20 × 20 + ½ × 5 × 20 = 100 + 400 + 50 = 550 m.

</details>

13. A motorbike moving at 15 m/s brakes with a retardation of 3 m/s². Find (a) the time it takes to stop and (b) the distance it covers. Check (b) using the average speed. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) 0 = 15 − 3t, so t = 5 s.

(b) v² = u² + 2as: 0 = 15² − 2 × 3 × s, so s = 225/6 = 37.5 m.

Check: average speed = (15 + 0)/2 = 7.5 m/s, and 7.5 × 5 = 37.5 m. ✓

</details>

## Case-based question

Kavya is in a lift in a tall building. A graph on her phone shows the lift's speed: it rises from 0 to 3 m/s in 3 s, moves steadily at 3 m/s for 12 s, then slows to a stop in 3 s.

1. Find the acceleration in the first 3 s. *(1)*
2. How high does the lift go in the steady part? *(1)*
3. Find the total height travelled using the area under the graph. *(1)*
4. Is the lift accelerating during the steady part? Explain. *(1)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. a = (3 − 0)/3 = 1 m/s².
2. 3 m/s × 12 s = 36 m.
3. ½ × 3 × 3 + 36 + ½ × 3 × 3 = 4.5 + 36 + 4.5 = 45 m.
4. No: the speed and direction do not change, so the acceleration is zero (the graph is a horizontal line, slope 0).

</details>

## Assertion and reason

Choose: (a) both A and R are true and R explains A; (b) both true but R does not explain A; (c) A is true, R is false; (d) A is false, R is true.

14. **A:** The distance travelled by a body can be greater than its displacement. **R:** Displacement is the shortest path between the start and the end.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). The path length is at least as long as the shortest straight path, and equal only for straight-line motion.</details>

15. **A:** A body moving with constant speed can be accelerating. **R:** Acceleration depends only on the change of speed.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(c). A is true (e.g. in a circle). R is false: acceleration is the change of velocity, which includes direction.</details>

16. **A:** The area under a velocity-time graph gives the displacement. **R:** Area = velocity × time.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). Velocity × time is displacement, which is exactly what the area of the graph is.</details>

- In circular motion at constant speed the velocity still changes, so there is acceleration.
