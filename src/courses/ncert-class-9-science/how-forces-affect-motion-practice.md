**Practice · Class 9 Science · Chapter 6.** Read the chapter lesson, [How Forces Affect Motion](/learn/courses/ncert-class-9-science/how-forces-affect-motion/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">For force questions: draw the body, mark every force with its direction, add them to get the net force, then use F = ma. For collisions, recoils and explosions: write momentum before = momentum after, with a sign for direction.</div>

## How to set out a numerical

1. Write the given values with units (grams to kg first), and what you must find.
2. For one body: net force = sum of forces (with signs), then F = ma.
3. For two bodies interacting: total momentum before = total momentum after, with right as positive and left as negative.
4. Give the answer with its unit and, for a vector, its direction.

## 1-mark questions

1. State Newton's first law of motion.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A body stays at rest or keeps moving in a straight line at constant speed unless an unbalanced (net) force acts on it.</details>

2. What is the SI unit of momentum?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>kg m/s.</details>

3. Which has more inertia: a 2 kg stone or a 5 kg stone?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The 5 kg stone, because inertia is measured by mass.</details>

4. A body is acted on by balanced forces. What is its acceleration?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Zero, because the net force is zero.</details>

5. A boy has a mass of 40 kg. What is his weight on Earth (g = 9.8 m/s²)?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>W = mg = 40 × 9.8 = 392 N.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

6. A force of 20 N acts on a 4 kg trolley. Find the acceleration. If the force acts for 3 s from rest, what is the final speed? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>a = F/m = 20/4 = 5 m/s². v = u + at = 0 + 5 × 3 = 15 m/s.</details>

7. Why does a fielder pull her hands back when catching a fast cricket ball? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The ball's momentum must go to zero. Pulling the hands back lengthens the time of stopping. Since F = change in momentum ÷ time, the force on the hands is smaller and the catch does not hurt.</details>

8. A truck of mass 3000 kg and a car of mass 1000 kg are travelling at the same speed. Which has greater momentum, and by how many times? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The truck. Momentum is mass × velocity and the speeds are equal, so the truck has 3000/1000 = 3 times the car's momentum.</details>

9. Why does a gun recoil when it fires a bullet? Explain using conservation of momentum. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Before firing the gun and bullet are at rest, so the total momentum is zero. After firing, the bullet has forward momentum mv. The total must still be zero, so the gun gets an equal momentum backwards, MV = mv. The gun is heavy so its velocity V = mv/M is small, but it is still the kick we feel.</details>

10. A bullet of 10 g leaves a rifle at 500 m/s. The rifle has a mass of 4 kg. Find its recoil speed. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>MV = mv, so V = (0.01 × 500)/4 = 5/4 = 1.25 m/s backwards.</details>

## 5-mark questions

11. State Newton's second law and derive F = ma from it. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

The rate of change of momentum of a body is proportional to the unbalanced force applied, and takes place in the direction of the force.

Let a body of mass m change its velocity from u to v in time t. Initial momentum = mu, final momentum = mv. Rate of change of momentum = (mv − mu)/t = m(v − u)/t. By the law, F ∝ m(v − u)/t. Since (v − u)/t = a, F ∝ ma, so F = kma. The newton is defined so that k = 1: F = ma.

</details>

12. A 1200 kg car travelling at 20 m/s is brought to rest in 8 s. (a) Find the retardation. (b) Find the average braking force. (c) How would the force change if it took 16 s? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) a = (0 − 20)/8 = −2.5 m/s², a retardation of 2.5 m/s².

(b) F = ma = 1200 × 2.5 = 3000 N (backwards, opposite to the motion).

(c) The change in momentum is the same, 1200 × 20 = 24 000 kg m/s, but the time is doubled, so the force halves to 1500 N.

</details>

13. A 5 kg trolley at 4 m/s collides with a 3 kg trolley at rest on a smooth track. They stick together. Find (a) the speed after the collision and (b) the momentum before and after. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v: 5 × 4 + 0 = 8v, so v = 20/8 = 2.5 m/s.

(b) Before: 5 × 4 + 3 × 0 = 20 kg m/s. After: 8 × 2.5 = 20 kg m/s. The total momentum is conserved.

</details>

## Case-based question

Rahul is learning to catch in a school cricket practice. A 0.15 kg ball arrives at 24 m/s. First he catches it with stiff hands and stops it in 0.01 s. Next he lets his hands move back and stops it in 0.06 s.

1. Find the momentum of the ball before it is caught. *(1)*
2. Find the average force in the stiff-hands catch. *(1)*
3. Find the average force with soft hands. *(1)*
4. Explain why the second catch hurts less. *(1)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. p = mv = 0.15 × 24 = 3.6 kg m/s.
2. F = Δp/t = 3.6/0.01 = 360 N.
3. F = 3.6/0.06 = 60 N.
4. Both catches remove the same momentum, but the longer stopping time in the second catch gives a six times smaller force.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

Choose: (a) both A and R are true and R explains A; (b) both true but R does not explain A; (c) A is true, R is false; (d) A is false, R is true.

14. **A:** A passenger leans backwards when a bus suddenly starts. **R:** A body at rest tends to stay at rest.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). Inertia of rest keeps her upper body behind while the bus moves forward.</details>

15. **A:** Action and reaction forces cancel each other. **R:** They are equal and opposite.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(d). A is false: they act on different bodies, so they cannot cancel. R is true.</details>

16. **A:** A heavy and a light stone dropped together (no air) reach the ground together. **R:** The acceleration due to gravity does not depend on the mass of the falling body.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). W = mg and a = W/m = g for every mass.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Net force: add forces with signs; F = ma; unit N = kg m/s².
- Momentum p = mv; F = change in momentum ÷ time, so a longer stopping time gives a smaller force.
- Action and reaction are equal, opposite, simultaneous and on different bodies, so they never cancel.
- In an isolated system total momentum before = after; gun recoil MV = mv; stick together v = (m₁u₁ + m₂u₂)/(m₁ + m₂).
- Weight = mg; mass does not change with place.
