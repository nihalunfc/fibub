**Practice · Class 9 Science · Chapter 8.** Read the chapter lesson, [Journey Inside the Atom](/learn/courses/ncert-class-9-science/journey-inside-the-atom/), first. Try each question before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Four moves solve most atom questions: protons = Z, neutrons = A − Z, electrons = Z for a neutral atom (adjust for an ion), and fill shells 2, 8, 8. Valency comes from the outer shell.</div>

## How to answer

1. For numericals, write Z, A and the charge first, then count p, n and e.
2. For electron arrangements, show the shells as numbers separated by commas.
3. In model questions, say what the experiment showed, then what it meant.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the particle discovered by J. J. Thomson.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The electron.</details>

2. What is the charge on an alpha particle?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Positive (+2): it is a helium nucleus.</details>

3. Write the electron arrangement of oxygen (Z = 8).
<details><summary>Answer</summary>2, 6.</details>

4. What is the valency of aluminium (Z = 13)?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Arrangement 2, 8, 3: it loses 3 electrons, so valency 3.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. How many protons, neutrons and electrons does ³⁷Cl (Z = 17) have? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Protons 17, neutrons 37 − 17 = 20, electrons 17 (neutral atom).</details>

6. State two things the gold-foil experiment showed about the atom. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(1) Most of the atom is empty space, since most alpha particles went straight through. (2) There is a small, dense, positive nucleus, because a few particles bounced back or were deflected through large angles.</details>

7. Why are the chemical properties of isotopes almost the same? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Chemical properties depend on the electrons, mainly the outer shell. Isotopes have the same number of protons, so the same number of electrons and the same arrangement; they differ only in neutrons.</details>

8. Bromine has two isotopes of mass 79 and 81, in about equal numbers. What is its average atomic mass? Why is the answer not a whole number in the periodic table? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Average = (50 × 79 + 50 × 81)/100 = 80. The table shows the weighted average over the natural mixture of isotopes, so it need not be whole (chlorine, 75 : 25, gives 35.5).</details>

## 5-mark questions

9. (a) Name the model of the atom that said electrons move in fixed shells. (b) What difficulty with Rutherford’s model did it remove? (c) Give two limitations of Thomson’s model. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) Bohr’s model.

(b) A circling electron should lose energy and spiral into the nucleus. Bohr said that in fixed shells electrons do not lose energy, so atoms are stable.

(c) It could not explain the gold-foil result (large deflections), and it had no nucleus: it said the positive charge is spread through the whole atom.

</details>

10. Write the electron arrangements of Na, Na⁺, Cl and Cl⁻ and explain why Na⁺ and Cl⁻ are stable. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Na: 2, 8, 1. Na⁺: 2, 8 (lost 1 electron). Cl: 2, 8, 7. Cl⁻: 2, 8, 8 (gained 1).

Both ions now have a full outer shell (8 electrons), like the unreactive noble gases neon and argon, which makes them stable. The number of protons does not change in forming an ion.

</details>

## Case-based question

A school lab has four labelled bottles of gas and a table of atoms: X has Z = 10, A = 20; Y has Z = 11, A = 23; Z has Z = 18, A = 40; W has Z = 19, A = 40.

11. (a) Write the electron arrangement of X and Y. (b) Which two atoms are isobars? (c) Which of X and Z are likely to be unreactive? Why? (d) How many neutrons does Y have?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) X: 2, 8. Y: 2, 8, 1.

(b) Z (argon-40) and W (potassium-40): same A = 40, different Z.

(c) X (neon) and Z (argon): their outer shells are full (8), so they have no tendency to gain or lose electrons.

(d) 23 − 11 = 12 neutrons.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

12. **A:** Most alpha particles passed straight through the gold foil. **R:** The atom is mostly empty space.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.</details>

13. **A:** Isotopes of an element have different chemical properties. **R:** They have different numbers of neutrons.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false: their chemistry is nearly identical because they have the same electrons. R is true (they differ in neutrons), but it does not make their chemistry differ.</details>
