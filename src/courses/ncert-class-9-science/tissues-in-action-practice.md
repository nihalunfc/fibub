## How to set out your answers

Name the tissue, say where it is found, describe its cells, then state how the structure suits the function. A table works well for differences. Draw and label tissue diagrams neatly.

## 1-mark questions

**1.** Name the tissue whose cells keep dividing.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Meristematic tissue.</details>

**2.** Which tissue forms the husk of a coconut?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Sclerenchyma.</details>

**3.** What do we call the tissue that joins bone to bone?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Ligament.</details>

**4.** Which muscle is found in the heart wall?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Cardiac muscle.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

**5.** Give two differences between xylem and phloem.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Xylem carries water and minerals; phloem carries food. Xylem vessels are dead and hollow; phloem sieve tubes are living. Xylem moves materials upward only; phloem moves them up and down.</details>

**6.** Why do guard cells open and close a stoma?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>In light, guard cells take in water by osmosis and become turgid; their thin inner walls and thick outer walls make them curve apart and open the pore. When they lose water they become flaccid and the pore closes.</details>

**7.** Write one feature of squamous epithelium and say how it suits the job in the lungs.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It is made of very thin flat cells. In the air sacs this lets oxygen and carbon dioxide diffuse across quickly.</details>

**8.** What is the difference between a tendon and a ligament?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A tendon is strong and hardly stretches and joins muscle to bone. A ligament is elastic and joins bone to bone at a joint.</details>

**9.** A heart beats 75 times a minute. How many beats are there in 12 hours?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Minutes in 12 hours = 12 × 60 = 720. Beats = 75 × 720 = 54 000.

</details>

## 5-mark questions

**10.** Describe the three types of muscle tissue, with one location each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. Skeletal muscle: long cylindrical cells with many nuclei and stripes; voluntary; arms and legs.
2. Smooth muscle: spindle-shaped, one nucleus, no stripes; involuntary; wall of the intestine.
3. Cardiac muscle: short branched striped cells with discs, one nucleus; involuntary; the heart.

</details>

**11.** Describe the structure and functions of blood.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Blood is a connective tissue with a liquid matrix, plasma (about 55%), in which cells float. Red cells carry oxygen with haemoglobin; white cells fight germs; platelets clot blood at wounds. Plasma carries digested food, hormones, carbon dioxide and wastes.

</details>

**12.** Explain how each of the following is suited to its function: (a) xylem vessel, (b) sclerenchyma fibre, (c) a neuron.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Xylem vessel: dead, hollow tube with thick lignin walls; water flows freely and the tube does not collapse. (b) Sclerenchyma fibre: dead, with very thick lignin walls and a narrow cavity; gives hardness and strength. (c) Neuron: long axon carries impulses over long distances; many dendrites collect signals; the synapse passes them on.

</details>

## Case-based question

A nurse shows Ravi a drop of blood under a microscope. He sees many small red disc-shaped cells, a few larger cells with a visible nucleus and some tiny fragments, all in a yellowish fluid.

**13a.** Name the red cells and say what they do.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Red blood cells (erythrocytes); they carry oxygen using haemoglobin.</details>

**13b.** Which cells are the larger ones with a nucleus?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>White blood cells; they defend the body against germs.</details>

**13c.** What are the tiny fragments and what is their job?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Platelets; they help the blood clot at a cut.</details>

**13d.** What is the yellowish fluid and why is blood called a connective tissue?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Plasma. Blood has cells in a matrix (plasma) and connects the parts of the body by carrying materials.</details>

## Assertion and reason

Choose: (a) both true, R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

**14.** A: Xylem tissue carries water upward. R: Xylem vessels are hollow, dead tubes.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). Hollow tubes let water move easily.</details>

**15.** A: Cardiac muscle works throughout life without resting. R: Cardiac muscle is involuntary.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(b). Both are true, but being involuntary (not under our control) does not by itself explain why it does not tire; cardiac cells are specially built with many mitochondria and a good blood supply.</details>

**16.** A: Cartilage heals slowly. R: Cartilage has no blood vessels.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a). Without a blood supply, healing materials arrive slowly.</details>
