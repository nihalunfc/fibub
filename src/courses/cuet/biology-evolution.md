**Module 9 · about 22 minutes.** By the end you will give evidence for evolution, explain natural selection, use the Hardy-Weinberg equation and outline human evolution. Read first: [molecular inheritance](/learn/courses/cuet/biology-molecular-inheritance/).

<div class="bigidea">Living things vary, some variations help survival and reproduction, and those get passed on more often. Over many generations this natural selection changes a population and can split it into new species.</div>

**Everyday picture:** antibiotic resistance. Take an antibiotic and most bacteria die, but a rare few with a resistant mutation survive and multiply. Stop the course early and you have selected for a resistant population.

## 1. Origin of life and evidence

Oparin and Haldane proposed that life began from simple chemicals in a hot early atmosphere with no free oxygen. Miller (1953) passed sparks through CH₄, NH₃, H₂ and water vapour and got amino acids.

| Evidence | Example |
|---|---|
| Fossils | rock layers of different ages hold different life forms |
| Homologous organs (divergent evolution) | forelimbs of whale, bat, cheetah, human: same bones, different jobs |
| Analogous organs (convergent evolution) | wings of bird and butterfly; eyes of octopus and mammal |
| Natural selection seen | peppered moths in industrial England; antibiotic and pesticide resistance |
| Adaptive radiation | Darwin's finches; Australian marsupials |

## 2. How evolution works

<div class="steps" data-title="Natural selection in four steps">
<div class="step"><b>Variation:</b> individuals differ, partly because of mutation and recombination.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Over-production:</b> more offspring are born than can survive.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Struggle and selection:</b> those better suited to the environment survive and reproduce more (fitness = reproductive success).</div>
<div class="step"><b>Inheritance:</b> the useful variations become more common in each generation.</div>
</div>

```math
p + q = 1 \qquad p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1
```

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>p, q</dt><dd>frequencies of the two alleles A and a in the gene pool.</dd>
<dt>p², 2pq, q²</dt><dd>frequencies of AA, Aa and aa when the population is in equilibrium.</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>allele frequencies stay constant unless something disturbs them: gene flow, genetic drift (founder effect), mutation, recombination or natural selection. A change means evolution is happening.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

## 3. Human evolution

Dryopithecus and Ramapithecus (~15 million years ago) → Australopithecines (East Africa, ~2 million years ago) → Homo habilis (first stone tools, ~650-800 cc brain) → Homo erectus (~900 cc, fire) → Neanderthals (~1,400 cc, buried their dead) → Homo sapiens (Africa, ~75,000-10,000 years ago, spread across continents; cave art ~18,000 years ago; farming ~10,000 years ago).

<div class="myth">

**"Humans evolved from chimpanzees."** Humans and chimpanzees share a common ancestor that lived millions of years ago. Both lines have been evolving ever since.

</div>
