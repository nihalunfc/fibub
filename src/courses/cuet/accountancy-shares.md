**Module 10 · about 18 minutes.** By the end you will pass the entries for issuing, forfeiting and reissuing shares and work out the capital reserve.

<div class="bigidea">Share capital records what has been called up; cash records what has been received. Forfeiture cancels the shares of a shareholder who did not pay a call, keeping what was paid; reissue sells them again.</div>

<div class="steps" data-title="From application to reissue">
<div class="step"><b>Application and allotment:</b> Bank debited for money received; Share Application, then Share Capital, credited. A premium is credited to Securities Premium, usually with allotment.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Calls:</b> each call is debited to a call account and credited to share capital; unpaid amounts sit in Calls in Arrears.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Forfeiture:</b> debit Share Capital with the called-up amount, credit the unpaid calls, credit Share Forfeiture with the amount received. (If the premium was not received, debit Securities Premium too.)</div>
<div class="step"><b>Reissue:</b> credit Share Capital at the paid-up value; any discount is debited to Share Forfeiture, and may not exceed the forfeited amount.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Capital reserve:</b> what is left in Share Forfeiture for the reissued shares is a capital profit, transferred to Capital Reserve.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"All of the share forfeiture balance goes to capital reserve after any reissue."** Only the part relating to the shares reissued, after the reissue discount. The rest stays in Share Forfeiture until those shares too are reissued.

</div>

## Practice

<div class="qbank" data-bank="cuet-accountancy" data-topics="share-capital" data-n="10" data-mode="practice" data-type="mcq"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Share capital is debited on forfeiture with the called-up amount.
- Share forfeiture is credited with what was received.
- Reissue discount ≤ forfeited amount on those shares.
- Gain on reissued shares → capital reserve.
- Securities premium uses are limited by Section 52 (no dividends).
