**Module 9 · about 24 minutes.** The highest-scoring Biology chapter. By the end you will solve monohybrid and dihybrid crosses, blood group problems and pedigrees.

<div class="bigidea">Every trait is controlled by a pair of factors (alleles), one from each parent. They separate when gametes form and, for genes on different chromosomes, sort independently. That simple idea gives the 3 : 1 and 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 ratios.</div>

**Everyday picture:** drawing one card from each of two shuffled decks, where each deck holds one "tall" card and one "dwarf" card. Four equally likely pairings; three of them contain at least one tall card.

## 1. Mendel's crosses

<figure class="fig" data-fig="punnett"><figcaption>Choose each parent. Every box is one equally likely fertilisation: Tt × Tt gives 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt, and 3 tall : 1 dwarf.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="steps" data-title="Mendel's three laws">
<div class="step"><b>Dominance:</b> in a Tt plant only T shows; t is hidden, not lost.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Segregation:</b> the two alleles separate into different gametes, so each gamete carries one.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Independent assortment:</b> genes for different traits on different chromosomes sort independently, giving 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 in a dihybrid F₂.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Test cross</b> with the recessive parent: 1 : 1 means the unknown was heterozygous; all dominant means homozygous.</div>
</div>

## 2. Beyond Mendel

| Pattern | Example | F₂ ratio |
|---|---|---|
| Incomplete dominance | snapdragon flower colour: red, pink, white | 1 : 2 : 1 (phenotype = genotype) |
| Codominance | Iᴬ and Iᴮ both expressed in blood group AB | |
| Multiple alleles | ABO: Iᴬ, Iᴮ, i give 6 genotypes, 4 groups | |
| Pleiotropy | one gene, many effects (phenylketonuria) | |
| Polygenic | skin colour, height: many genes add up | a bell curve |
| Linkage | genes close on one chromosome tend to stay together; recombination separates them | parental types more common |

## 3. Sex and disorders

Humans: XX female, XY male (the father decides the child's sex). Birds: ZW female, ZZ male. Grasshopper: XO male.

| Disorder | Type |
|---|---|
| Haemophilia, colour blindness | X-linked recessive |
| Sickle-cell anaemia | autosomal recessive (one base change, Glu → Val) |
| Phenylketonuria, thalassaemia | autosomal recessive |
| Down's syndrome | trisomy 21 |
| Klinefelter's (XXY), Turner's (XO) | sex chromosome number |

<div class="myth">

**"A dominant trait is the most common one in a population."** Dominant only means it shows in a heterozygote. Polydactyly (extra fingers) is dominant yet rare.

</div>
