**Module 10 · about 17 minutes.** By the end you will classify cash flows and build cash from operating activities from net profit.

<div class="bigidea">Profit is not cash. The cash flow statement shows where cash actually came from and went, in three buckets: running the business, buying and selling long-term assets, and dealing with owners and lenders.</div>

<figure class="fig" data-fig="cash-buckets"><figcaption>Sort each item into its activity. The bars add up the ones you sorted correctly.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="steps" data-title="Cash from operations, indirect method">
<div class="step">Start with net profit before tax and extraordinary items.</div>
<div class="step">Add back non-cash and non-operating charges: depreciation, goodwill written off, loss on sale of assets, interest paid. Subtract non-operating income: profit on sale of assets, interest and dividend received.</div>
<div class="step">Adjust for working capital: increases in current assets are subtracted, decreases added; increases in current liabilities are added, decreases subtracted.</div>
<div class="step">Subtract income tax paid. The result is net cash from operating activities.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Interest received is always operating."** For a non-finance company it is an investing inflow (and interest paid is financing). For a bank or finance company, both are operating.

</div>

## Practice

<div class="qbank" data-bank="cuet-accountancy" data-topics="cash-flow" data-n="10" data-mode="practice" data-type="mcq"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Operating: the main business. Investing: long-term assets and investments. Financing: owners and lenders.
- Indirect method: net profit, add back non-cash charges, remove non-operating items, adjust working capital, subtract tax paid.
- Current asset up → cash down; current liability up → cash up.
- For non-finance firms: interest and dividends received are investing; interest and dividends paid are financing.

</div>
