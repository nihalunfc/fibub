**Module 10 · about 15 minutes.** By the end you will value goodwill by three methods and know when each is used.

<div class="bigidea">Goodwill is the value of a firm's ability to earn more than a normal business with the same capital. Every method measures that extra earning power in a different way.</div>

## 1. Three methods

<div class="steps" data-title="Average profit ₹80,000; capital ₹5,00,000; normal rate 12%">
<div class="step"><b>Average profit method:</b> goodwill = average profit × years' purchase. At 2 years: ₹1,60,000.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Super profit method:</b> normal profit = 12% of 5,00,000 = ₹60,000; super profit = 80,000 − 60,000 = ₹20,000; at 3 years' purchase, goodwill = ₹60,000.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Capitalisation of average profit:</b> capitalised value = 80,000 × 100/12 = ₹6,66,667; goodwill = 6,66,667 − 5,00,000 = ₹1,66,667.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Capitalisation of super profit:</b> 20,000 × 100/12 = ₹1,66,667, the same answer, as it must be.</div>
</div>

```math
\text{Goodwill} = (\bar{P} - C \times r) \times n
```

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>P̄</dt><dd>average (or weighted average) profit, after removing abnormal items</dd>
<dt>C × r</dt><dd>normal profit: what the same capital would earn at the normal rate in the industry</dd>
<dt>n</dt><dd>number of years' purchase: how many years the extra profit is expected to last</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>if the firm earns only a normal return, it has no goodwill, however big it is.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<div class="problem" data-problem="super-profit"></div>

<div class="myth">

**"A big firm always has big goodwill."** Goodwill comes from earning more than normal on the capital used. A large firm earning a normal return has none.

</div>

## 2. Practice

<div class="qbank" data-bank="cuet-accountancy" data-topics="goodwill" data-n="10" data-mode="practice" data-type="mcq"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Average profit method: average profit × years' purchase.
- Super profit = average profit − normal profit; goodwill = super profit × years.
- Capitalisation: average profit × 100/rate − capital employed.
- Only purchased goodwill is recorded in the books.
