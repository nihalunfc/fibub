**Module 9 · about 24 minutes.** By the end you will describe population growth and species interactions, and trace energy and matter through an ecosystem.

<div class="bigidea">A population grows until its surroundings cannot support more. Species affect each other (helping, harming, or neither), and every ecosystem runs on sunlight that flows one way up the food chain while nutrients go round and round.</div>

**Everyday picture:** a new phone game. Downloads shoot up at first, then level off as most people who want it already have it. Populations behave the same way, levelling off at the carrying capacity.

## 1. Populations

| Idea | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Density | numbers per unit area (or % cover for plants) |
| Growth | Nₜ₊₁ = Nₜ + [(births + immigration) − (deaths + emigration)] |
| Exponential (J curve) | dN/dt = rN, when resources are unlimited |
| Logistic (S curve) | dN/dt = rN(K − N)/K, levelling at carrying capacity K |
| Age pyramids | broad base = growing; bell = stable; narrow base = declining |

<div class="steps" data-title="Interactions between species">
<div class="step"><b>Mutualism (+ +):</b> lichens (alga + fungus), mycorrhiza, fig and its wasp.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Competition (− −):</b> species fighting for the same resource; Gause's principle says two cannot share exactly the same niche for long.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Predation (+ −):</b> tiger and deer; plants defend with thorns and chemicals (Calotropis).</div>
<div class="step"><b>Parasitism (+ −):</b> Cuscuta on hedge plants; brood parasitism of the cuckoo laying eggs in a crow's nest.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Commensalism (+ 0):</b> orchid on a mango branch; barnacles on a whale; cattle egret and cattle.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Amensalism (− 0):</b> Penicillium releasing a chemical that kills nearby bacteria.</div>
</div>

## 2. Ecosystems

Gross primary productivity is the rate plants fix energy; net primary productivity = GPP − respiration, the energy available to herbivores. Decomposition runs through fragmentation, leaching, catabolism, humification and mineralisation, fastest when warm and moist.

```math
\text{NPP} = \text{GPP} - R \qquad E_{n} \approx E_1 \times 0.1^{\,n-1}
```

<div class="problem" data-problem="ten-percent"></div>

| Pyramid | Shape |
|---|---|
| Energy | always upright |
| Numbers | usually upright; inverted for one tree with many insects |
| Biomass | upright on land; inverted in the sea (small phytoplankton biomass supports more fish) |

Succession builds communities over time: on bare rock it starts with lichens (pioneers) and ends with a stable climax community. Phosphorus cycles mainly through rocks and has no gas phase; carbon cycles through the air.

<div class="myth">

**"Energy is recycled in an ecosystem like nutrients."** Nutrients cycle; energy does not. It enters as sunlight and leaves as heat at every level, which is why food chains rarely have more than four or five links.

</div>
