**Module 11 · about 18 minutes.** By the end you will find equilibrium income and use the multiplier.

<div class="bigidea">In the short run, output adjusts to what people plan to spend. A rise in investment raises income by more than itself, because each rupee spent becomes income that is partly spent again.</div>

## 1. Consumption and saving

C = a + bY, where a is autonomous consumption and b is the MPC, the share of extra income spent. MPS = 1 − MPC is the share saved. Equilibrium is where aggregate demand (C + I) equals income, or equivalently where saving equals investment.

## 2. The multiplier

<figure class="fig" data-fig="multiplier-rounds"><figcaption>₹100 crore of new investment, spent and re-spent. A higher MPC means taller later bars and a bigger total.</figcaption></figure>

```math
k = \frac{\Delta Y}{\Delta I} = \frac{1}{1 - MPC} = \frac{1}{MPS}
```

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>k</dt><dd>the multiplier: how many rupees of income each rupee of new investment creates</dd>
<dt>MPC</dt><dd>marginal propensity to consume: the share of each extra rupee spent</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>the more people spend out of extra income, the further each round travels. If nothing were spent again (MPC = 0), k would be 1.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

## 3. Excess and deficient demand

**Excess demand** (spending above full-employment output) raises prices: an inflationary gap. **Deficient demand** leaves output and jobs below full employment: a deflationary gap. Governments and central banks use fiscal and monetary policy to close the gaps.

<div class="myth">

**"A bigger saving rate always grows income faster in the short run."** In this model, higher MPS means a smaller multiplier: if everyone saves more at once, demand falls and income can fall (the paradox of thrift).

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- C = a + bY; MPC + MPS = 1.
- Equilibrium: AD = Y, or S = I.
- k = 1/(1 − MPC) = 1/MPS; ΔY = k × ΔI.
- Excess demand → inflationary gap; deficient demand → deflationary gap.
