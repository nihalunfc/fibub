**Module 10 · about 14 minutes.** By the end you will prepare the realisation account and know the order in which money is paid out.

<div class="bigidea">The realisation account is a temporary account that turns every asset into cash and every liability into a payment; its balance is the profit or loss on closing the firm, shared in the profit-sharing ratio.</div>

<div class="steps" data-title="Closing a firm, in order">
<div class="step"><b>Transfer.</b> Assets (except cash, bank and fictitious assets) to the debit of realisation at book value; outside liabilities to its credit.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Sell and pay.</b> Cash from selling assets is credited to realisation; liabilities paid are debited. Assets taken by partners are debited to their capital.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Expenses.</b> Realisation expenses are debited to realisation (or to a partner if the partner bears them).</div>
<div class="step"><b>Share the result.</b> The balance is the profit or loss on realisation, shared in the profit-sharing ratio.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Settle.</b> Pay outsiders first, then partners' loans, then partners' capital, until cash is nil.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Partners' loans go to the realisation account."** They do not: a partner's loan is paid separately after outsiders and before capitals.

</div>

## Practice

<div class="keypoints">

- Assets at book value on the debit side; outside liabilities on the credit side.
- Sale proceeds are credited; payments to creditors debited.
- Order of payment: outsiders, partners' loans, partners' capitals.
- Realisation profit or loss is shared in the profit-sharing ratio.
