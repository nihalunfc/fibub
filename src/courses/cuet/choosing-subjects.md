**Module 1 · about 12 minutes.** By the end you will have a short list of the five tests that keep the most courses open for you.

<div class="bigidea">Choose your tests backwards: start from the courses you want, read what each one asks for, and pick the five tests that cover the most of them.</div>

## 1. The four-step method

<div class="steps" data-title="Pick your five tests">
<div class="step"><b>Step 1: list 6 to 10 courses you would happily join</b>, at several universities: for example B.Sc. (Hons) Physics at three universities, B.Sc. (Hons) Electronics at two, B.Tech at one that admits through CUET.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Step 2: for each one, write down the CUET tests it asks for.</b> Every university publishes this in its admission bulletin (Delhi University calls it the programme-wise list). Many say things like "one language + any three domain subjects" or "a language + Physics + Chemistry + Mathematics".</div>
<div class="step"><b>Step 3: count how often each test appears.</b> The tests that unlock the most courses go in first. A language (usually English) almost always makes the list.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Step 4: fill the remaining slots with your strongest subjects</b>, or the General Test if several of your courses ask for it. Never pick a test only because you think it is easy: a test no course asks for is a wasted slot.</div>
</div>

## 2. Common combinations

These are typical patterns, not rules. Confirm every course on its own university's list for your year.

| You want | Tests that often fit |
|---|---|
| B.Sc. physical sciences, B.Tech through CUET | English + Physics + Chemistry + Mathematics (+ General Test) |
| B.Sc. life sciences, biotechnology | English + Physics + Chemistry + Biology (+ General Test) |
| B.Com (Hons), economics, BBA | English + Mathematics or Applied Mathematics + Accountancy + Economics (+ General Test) |
| B.A. programmes | English + two or three humanities subjects (+ General Test) |
| A wide safety net across many courses | English + General Test + your three strongest domain subjects |

<div class="myth">

**"The General Test is optional, so skip it."** It is optional for you, but many universities use it for B.A., BBA, BMS, B.Voc and similar courses, and some use it when a course has no matching domain subject. If even two of your target courses ask for it, it is worth a slot, and this course teaches it from zero in Part C.

</div>

## 3. Where to go next

Now you know your tests. Go to Lesson 1.4 to build a timetable, then take only the parts of this course you need: English is Module 2, the General Test is Modules 3 to 5, the science subjects are Modules 6 to 9 and commerce subjects Modules 10 and 11.

<div class="keypoints">

- You can take at most five tests: languages, domain subjects and the General Test together.
- Work backwards from 6 to 10 target courses and their published subject lists.
- Pick the tests that unlock the most courses first, then your strongest subjects.
- A language test (usually English) is needed by almost every course.
- Add the General Test if two or more of your courses use it.

</div>
