**Module 9 · about 26 minutes.** By the end you will explain how DNA copies itself and how a gene becomes a protein. Read first: [inheritance](/learn/courses/cuet/biology-inheritance/) and, for the chemistry of nucleotides, [biomolecules](/learn/courses/cuet/chemistry-biomolecules/).

<div class="bigidea">DNA stores instructions as a sequence of four bases. The cell copies DNA to DNA (replication), reads DNA into RNA (transcription), and turns RNA into protein three bases at a time (translation). That flow is the central dogma.</div>

**Everyday picture:** a library's master cookbook (DNA) never leaves the reading room. You photocopy one recipe (mRNA), take it to the kitchen (ribosome), and cook the dish (protein) by following it word by word.

## 1. DNA and its proof

Watson and Crick's double helix: two antiparallel strands, sugar-phosphate backbone outside, bases inside, A pairs with T (2 hydrogen bonds), G with C (3). One turn is 3.4 nm with 10 base pairs. In eukaryotes DNA wraps around histone octamers to form nucleosomes (beads on a string).

<div class="problem" data-problem="chargaff"></div>

| Experiment | Showed |
|---|---|
| Griffith (1928), pneumococcus in mice | a "transforming principle" can change harmless bacteria into deadly ones |
| Avery, MacLeod, McCarty | that principle is DNA (DNase destroyed it; protease and RNase did not) |
| Hershey and Chase (1952), phages with ³²P and ³⁵S | the DNA, not the protein coat, enters the bacterium |
| Meselson and Stahl (1958), ¹⁵N | replication is semi-conservative |

## 2. From gene to protein

<div class="steps" data-title="Replication, transcription, translation">
<div class="step"><b>Replication:</b> helicase opens the helix; DNA polymerase builds 5′ → 3′ only, so one strand is made continuously (leading) and the other in Okazaki fragments joined by ligase (lagging).</div>
<div class="step"><b>Transcription:</b> RNA polymerase copies the template strand of a gene into mRNA, from promoter to terminator.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Processing (eukaryotes):</b> introns are cut out (splicing), a cap and a poly-A tail are added.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Translation:</b> the ribosome reads mRNA codons; each tRNA brings the amino acid matching its anticodon; peptide bonds link them.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Stop:</b> at UAA, UAG or UGA the chain is released.</div>
</div>

The genetic code is a triplet, nearly universal, degenerate (most amino acids have several codons), non-overlapping and has no commas. 64 codons: 61 code for amino acids, 3 stop.

## 3. Control and applications

**Lac operon (E. coli):** with no lactose the repressor sits on the operator and blocks transcription; lactose binds the repressor and switches on the genes for digesting it. **Human Genome Project** (completed 2003): about 3 × 10⁹ bp and roughly 20,000-25,000 genes; under 2% codes for proteins. **DNA fingerprinting** compares repetitive DNA (VNTRs) that differs between people: used in forensics and paternity tests.

<div class="myth">

**"Every bit of DNA is a gene."** Most human DNA does not code for protein. It includes control regions, repeats and sequences whose roles are still being worked out.

</div>
