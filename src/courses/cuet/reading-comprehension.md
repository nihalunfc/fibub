**Module 2 · about 18 minutes.** By the end you will have a method for any passage and know how each question type is set and solved.

<div class="bigidea">Every answer in reading comprehension is in the passage or follows directly from it. Your job is to find the evidence, not to remember facts or guess the author's mind.</div>

## 1. The three kinds of passage

CUET's English test uses **factual** passages (science, society, environment, reports), **narrative** passages (a story or a memory) and **literary** passages (descriptive prose or a short poem). The method is the same; literary passages need more attention to tone and figures of speech.

## 2. The five-step method

<div class="steps" data-title="Read a passage in five steps">
<div class="step"><b>1. Questions first (20 seconds).</b> Skim the question stems, not the options. Note key words to hunt for: a name, a number, "the author suggests", "the word X means".</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Read for the map (2 minutes).</b> Read the passage once at a steady speed. After each paragraph, give it a 3-word label in your head: "problem: plastic", "solution tried", "author doubts it".</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Answer detail questions from the text.</b> Go back to the paragraph your map points to and read two lines around the key word. Pick the option that says the same thing in different words.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Main idea and title last.</b> By now you have read the whole passage carefully. The main idea covers all of it; options that cover only one paragraph are too narrow, and options bigger than the passage are too broad.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Eliminate before you choose.</b> Cross out options that are (a) not in the passage, (b) too extreme ("always", "never", "all"), (c) true but not what was asked. Pick the best of what is left.</div>
</div>

## 3. Question types and how to beat them

| Type | It asks | The trap |
|---|---|---|
| Detail | What does the passage say about X? | An option that copies words from the passage but changes the meaning |
| Main idea / title | What is the passage mostly about? | Too narrow (one paragraph) or too broad |
| Inference | What can be concluded? | An outside fact, or something that goes further than the text |
| Vocabulary in context | What does a word mean *here*? | The word's most common meaning, which may not fit this sentence |
| Tone / purpose | How does the author feel, why was it written? | Extreme tones (furious, ecstatic) when the text is measured |

<div class="myth">

**"I should read slowly and understand every word."** You need to understand the argument, not every word. When you meet an unknown word, read the sentence around it and carry on; if a question asks about it, the context is usually enough.

<div class="keypoints">

- Read the question stems first, then the passage once, labelling each paragraph.
- Every answer must be supported by the passage; outside facts are traps.
- Main idea is neither one paragraph nor bigger than the passage.
- Vocabulary questions ask what the word means in this sentence.
- Eliminate extreme, unsupported and off-question options first.
