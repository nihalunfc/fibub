**Module 4 · about 15 minutes.** By the end you will test any conclusion by drawing every possible diagram for the statements.

<div class="bigidea">Treat the statements as true even if they are silly ("All cats are trees"). A conclusion follows only if it is true in every diagram the statements allow, not just the one you drew first.</div>

## 1. Four statements, four pictures

<figure class="fig" data-fig="venn"><figcaption>Pick a statement type to see how its circles must sit, and what follows from it.</figcaption></figure>

## 2. The method

<div class="steps" data-title="Statements: Some books are pens. All pens are rulers.">
<div class="step">Draw the simplest picture: books overlapping pens, and the pen circle inside the ruler circle.</div>
<div class="step">Conclusion I, "Some books are rulers": the overlap of books and pens lies inside rulers, so it is true in every picture. <b>Follows.</b></div>
<div class="step">Conclusion II, "All rulers are books": try to draw a picture where it fails. Put part of the ruler circle outside books. The statements still hold, so II <b>does not follow</b>.</div>
<div class="step">Answer: "Only I follows". Always try to break a conclusion: if you can draw a picture where it is false, it does not follow.</div>
</div>

## 3. The "either-or" case

Two conclusions about the same two groups, such as "Some A are B" and "No A is B", where one of them must be true but neither is certain, give the answer **"Either I or II follows"**. Use it only when the pair covers every possibility and each alone is uncertain.

<div class="myth">

**"Use what I know about the real world."** If the statement says "All fish are birds", accept it. Syllogism questions test logic, not facts. A conclusion that is true in real life but does not follow from the statements is wrong.

</div>

## 4. Practice

<div class="qbank" data-bank="cuet-general-test" data-topics="syllogisms" data-n="10" data-mode="practice" data-type="mcq"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- All A are B: A inside B. Some A are B: overlap. No A is B: apart. Some A are not B: part of A outside B.
- A conclusion follows only if it holds in every possible diagram.
- Try to draw a picture that breaks the conclusion.
- "Some" reverses ("Some B are A"); "All" does not.
- Either-or: a complementary pair, neither certain on its own.

</div>
