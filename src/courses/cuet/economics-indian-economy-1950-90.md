**Module 11 · about 16 minutes.** By the end you will explain the plan goals, the farm and industry policies of 1950 to 1990, and their results.

<div class="bigidea">Independent India chose planned development: the state would build heavy industry, reform land and protect home industry, aiming at growth with self-reliance and fairness.</div>

<div class="steps" data-title="From 1947 to 1990">
<div class="step"><b>The starting point (1947):</b> a farm economy with low productivity, little industry, poor infrastructure and an economy shaped to serve colonial trade.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Planning:</b> five-year plans from 1951 with four goals: growth, modernisation, self-reliance and equity.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Agriculture:</b> land reforms (abolishing intermediaries, land ceilings), then the Green Revolution from the mid-1960s: high-yielding seeds, fertiliser, irrigation, and food self-sufficiency.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Industry:</b> the 1956 resolution gave the public sector the commanding heights; private firms needed licences; small-scale industry was protected; imports were limited to protect home producers.</div>
<div class="step"><b>The result by 1990:</b> a diversified industry and food security, but inefficient public firms, a slow-moving licence system and a growing need for reform.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"The Green Revolution helped all farmers equally."** At first it mostly helped farmers with irrigation and money for inputs, and mainly in wheat-growing regions. Government credit and subsidies later spread its benefits to smaller farmers.
