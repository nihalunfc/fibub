**Module 10 · about 15 minutes.** By the end you will pass entries for the six ways a debenture can be issued and redeemed, and handle the loss on issue.

<div class="bigidea">A debenture is a loan to the company, not a share in it. The holder gets interest whether or not there is a profit, and is repaid on the agreed terms.</div>

<div class="steps" data-title="Six combinations">
<div class="step"><b>At par, redeemable at par:</b> Bank Dr, Debentures Cr at face value.</div>
<div class="step"><b>At a discount, redeemable at par:</b> Bank Dr with cash, Discount on Issue Dr with the shortfall, Debentures Cr at face value.</div>
<div class="step"><b>At a premium, redeemable at par:</b> Bank Dr, Debentures Cr, Securities Premium Cr.</div>
<div class="step"><b>At par or discount, redeemable at a premium:</b> also Loss on Issue Dr and Premium on Redemption Cr (a liability) for the future premium.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Writing off:</b> the discount or loss on issue is written off from securities premium first, then from the statement of profit and loss, over the life of the debentures.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Debenture holders are owners."** They are creditors. They have no voting rights and their interest is an expense; shareholders are the owners.

</div>

## Practice

<div class="qbank" data-bank="cuet-accountancy" data-topics="debentures" data-n="10" data-mode="practice" data-type="mcq"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Debentures are borrowed capital; interest is a charge against profit.
- Loss on issue = discount on issue + premium on redemption.
- Premium on redemption is a liability recorded at issue.
- Write off losses from securities premium first, then profit and loss.
- Collateral debentures bring in no cash.

</div>
