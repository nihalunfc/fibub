**Module 11 · about 15 minutes.** By the end you will explain human capital formation and the main paths of rural development.

<div class="bigidea">People are capital too: education, health and training raise what a person can produce. Rural development aims to raise that capacity and income in villages through credit, markets and new kinds of work.</div>

<div class="steps" data-title="Human capital">
<div class="step"><b>Sources:</b> education, health, on-the-job training, migration to better jobs, and information about jobs.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Why it matters:</b> skilled, healthy workers produce more and adopt new technology faster, which raises growth.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Human capital vs human development:</b> human capital treats education and health as means to more output; human development treats them as goals in themselves.</div>
</div>

<div class="steps" data-title="Rural development">
<div class="step"><b>Credit:</b> cooperatives, regional rural banks, NABARD and self-help groups replaced dependence on moneylenders.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Marketing:</b> regulated markets, cooperative marketing and better roads and storage help farmers get fair prices.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Diversification:</b> animal husbandry, fisheries, horticulture and non-farm jobs reduce risk from relying on one crop.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Sustainable farming:</b> organic farming avoids chemical inputs, can fetch better prices and protects soil.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Rural development means only farming."** It also includes health, education, roads, electricity, credit and non-farm jobs in villages.

</div>
