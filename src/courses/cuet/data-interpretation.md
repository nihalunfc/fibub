**Module 3 · about 15 minutes.** By the end you will read a table, bar chart or pie chart and answer its questions with quick, safe approximation.

<div class="bigidea">Data interpretation is percentages and averages on a chart. Spend 30 seconds understanding the chart (units, years, what is being counted) before you touch a question.</div>

## 1. Read before you calculate

<div class="steps" data-title="30 seconds on the chart">
<div class="step"><b>Title and units.</b> Thousands or lakhs? Rupees or units sold? Percent or actual numbers?</div>
<div class="step"><b>What changes along each axis.</b> Years along the bottom, categories in the legend.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Notice the obvious.</b> The biggest and smallest values, any big jump. Many questions ask exactly about these.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Now the questions,</b> one at a time, with the chart beside you.</div>
</div>

<figure class="fig" data-fig="di-chart"><figcaption>The same made-up sales data as a bar chart and a pie chart. Pie charts show shares; bar charts show sizes and changes.</figcaption></figure>

## 2. The five calculations

| Asked | Do |
|---|---|
| Percent change | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 |
| Share of total | part ÷ total × 100 (pie: angle ÷ 360 × 100) |
| Ratio | divide both by a common factor |
| Average | total ÷ number of items |
| Pie angle | share × 360° |

**Approximate when options are far apart.** 187 ÷ 412 is a little under 190 ÷ 400 = 47.5%; if the options are 35%, 45%, 55%, 65%, you are done.

<div class="myth">

**"A taller bar always means a bigger percentage increase."** Percent increase depends on the starting value. A rise from 20 to 30 (50%) is a bigger percentage than a rise from 150 to 200 (33%), even though the second bar grew more.

</div>

## 3. Practice

<div class="qbank" data-bank="cuet-general-test" data-topics="data-interpretation" data-n="10" data-mode="practice" data-type="mcq"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Read the title, units and axes before any question.
- Percent change divides by the starting value.
- Pie charts: share = angle ÷ 360.
- Approximate when options are far apart; calculate exactly only when they are close.
