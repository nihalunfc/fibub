**Module 7 · about 20 minutes.** By the end you will rank amine basicity, tell 1°, 2° and 3° amines apart, and use diazonium salts. Read first: [carbonyls](/learn/courses/cuet/chemistry-carbonyls/).

<div class="bigidea">An amine is ammonia with carbon groups in place of hydrogens. The lone pair on nitrogen makes it a base and a nucleophile, and everything about amines comes down to how available that lone pair is.</div>

**Everyday picture:** the smell of rotting fish is trimethylamine. A squeeze of lemon (an acid) turns it into a salt that does not evaporate, which is why lemon helps.

## 1. Basicity

<div class="steps" data-title="Ranking basicity">
<div class="step"><b>Alkyl groups push electrons</b> onto N (+I), so alkylamines are stronger bases than ammonia.</div>
<div class="step"><b>In the gas phase</b> only that matters: 3° &gt; 2° &gt; 1° &gt; NH₃.</div>
<div class="step"><b>In water</b> solvation also matters (more N-H to hydrogen bond with water). For methyl: 2° &gt; 1° &gt; 3° &gt; NH₃.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Aryl amines</b> are much weaker: the lone pair is shared with the ring. Electron-pulling groups (−NO₂) weaken them further.</div>
</div>

## 2. Making and testing amines

| Method | Gives |
|---|---|
| Reduce nitro compounds (Sn/HCl or H₂/Pd) | aniline from nitrobenzene |
| Reduce nitriles or amides (LiAlH₄) | primary amines |
| Gabriel phthalimide synthesis | pure primary aliphatic amines (not aniline) |
| Hoffmann bromamide (Br₂ + NaOH on an amide) | primary amine with one carbon fewer |

| Test | 1° | 2° | 3° |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hinsberg (C₆H₅SO₂Cl) | product dissolves in KOH | solid, insoluble in KOH | no reaction |
| Carbylamine | foul-smelling isocyanide | none | none |
| HNO₂ (aliphatic) | alcohol + N₂ bubbles | yellow oily nitrosamine | soluble salt |

## 3. Diazonium salts

Aniline + NaNO₂/HCl at 273-278 K gives benzenediazonium chloride, stable only when cold. N₂ is an excellent leaving group, so it swaps for many groups: Cl or Br (Sandmeyer, CuCl/CuBr), CN (CuCN), I (KI), OH (warm water), F (Balz-Schiemann), H (H₃PO₂). Coupling with phenol or aniline gives bright azo dyes such as methyl orange.

<div class="myth">

**"More alkyl groups always means a stronger base."** True in the gas phase, but in water tertiary amines are crowded and poorly solvated, so dimethylamine beats trimethylamine.

</div>
