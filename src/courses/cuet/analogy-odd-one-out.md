**Module 4 · about 12 minutes.** By the end you will solve analogies by naming the link and find the odd one out by finding what the others share.

<div class="bigidea">An analogy is a relationship copied to a new pair. Say the relationship as a short sentence ("A works in B"), then use exactly that sentence on the options.</div>

## 1. Make a bridge sentence

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: Pen : Write :: Knife : ?">
<div class="step">Bridge sentence: "A pen is used to write."</div>
<div class="step">Copy it: "A knife is used to ___."</div>
<div class="step">Options: cut, sharp, kitchen. Only <b>cut</b> fits the sentence; "sharp" describes a knife, "kitchen" is where it is.</div>
<div class="step">If two options fit, make the sentence more exact ("is the main tool used to").</div>
</div>

**Common links:** worker and workplace, tool and use, part and whole, young one and adult (calf : cow), product and raw material, synonym and antonym, unit and quantity (ampere : current), country and capital.

**Number analogies:** check squares, cubes, × and +, and digit sums. 4 : 18 :: 6 : 38 (n² + 2).

## 2. Odd one out

Find the property that **all but one** share: all primes, all multiples of 7, all vowels, all instruments with strings, all rivers that flow west. If you find a property that splits the items 2–2, it is the wrong property.

<div class="myth">

**"The odd one is the one that looks different."** Looks mislead: in 21, 35, 49, 63, 74 all but 74 are multiples of 7, even though 21 and 63 "look" alike. Find a rule, then check every item against it.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Make a bridge sentence for the first pair and apply it word for word.
