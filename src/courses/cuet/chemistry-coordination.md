**Module 7 · about 22 minutes.** By the end you will name complexes, find oxidation state and coordination number, spot isomers and predict magnetism with crystal field theory. Read first: [d- and f-block elements](/learn/courses/cuet/chemistry-d-f-block/).

<div class="bigidea">A complex is a metal ion holding a set of ligands, each donating a pair of electrons. The ligands' strength splits the metal's d orbitals, and the size of that split decides colour and magnetism.</div>

**Everyday picture:** haemoglobin in your blood is an iron complex, and chlorophyll is a magnesium complex. The ligands around the metal tune exactly what it does: carry oxygen, or catch sunlight.

## 1. The vocabulary

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Ligand | electron-pair donor | NH₃, H₂O, Cl⁻, CN⁻ |
| Denticity | number of donor atoms per ligand | en bidentate, EDTA⁴⁻ hexadentate |
| Coordination number | number of donor atoms bound to the metal | 6 in [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ |
| Chelate | ring formed by a polydentate ligand, extra stable | [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ |
| Ambidentate | can bind through either of two atoms | NO₂⁻ (N or O), SCN⁻ (S or N) |

<div class="steps" data-title="Naming K₃[Fe(C₂O₄)₃]">
<div class="step"><b>Cation first:</b> potassium.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Ligands in alphabetical order</b>, with prefixes di-, tri- (or bis-, tris- for complex names): trioxalato.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Metal:</b> anionic complexes end in -ate (iron → ferrate).</div>
<div class="step"><b>Oxidation state</b> in Roman numerals: x + 3(−2) = −3, so x = +3.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Full name:</b> potassium trioxalatoferrate(III).</div>

## 2. Isomerism

**Structural:** ionisation (ions swap in and out of the bracket), linkage (ambidentate ligand binds differently), coordination (ligands swap between cation and anion complexes), solvate/hydrate. **Stereo:** geometrical (cis and trans in square planar MA₂B₂ and octahedral MA₄B₂; fac and mer in MA₃B₃) and optical (non-superimposable mirror images, e.g. [Co(en)₃]³⁺).

## 3. Bonding

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Valence bond theory</dt><dd>hybridisation sets the shape: sp³ tetrahedral, dsp² square planar, d²sp³ (inner orbital, low spin) or sp³d² (outer orbital, high spin) octahedral.</dd>
<dt>Crystal field theory</dt><dd>in an octahedral field the five d orbitals split into t₂g (lower 3) and e_g (upper 2) by Δ₀. If Δ₀ is bigger than the pairing energy, electrons pair up (low spin).</dd>
<dt>Spectrochemical series</dt><dd>I⁻ &lt; Br⁻ &lt; Cl⁻ &lt; F⁻ &lt; OH⁻ &lt; H₂O &lt; NH₃ &lt; en &lt; CN⁻ &lt; CO. Left: weak field (high spin). Right: strong field (low spin).</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"A complex's colour is the colour of light it absorbs."** We see the complementary colour. [Ti(H₂O)₆]³⁺ absorbs green-yellow light and so looks violet.

</div>
