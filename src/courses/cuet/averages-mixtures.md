**Module 3 · about 16 minutes.** By the end you will use totals to solve average questions, write age puzzles as two equations and solve mixtures by alligation.

<div class="bigidea">An average is a total shared equally. Most average questions become easy as soon as you turn averages back into totals.</div>

## 1. Work with totals

```math
\text{total} = \text{average} \times \text{number}
```

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>average</dt><dd>the equal share each member would get if the total were shared out</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>the average is the balancing point: values above it exactly balance values below it. When someone joins and the average rises by d, the newcomer had to give d to every old member and still have the new average left.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

## 2. Ages: two sentences, two equations

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: father and son">
<div class="step">"A father is three times as old as his son": F = 3S.</div>
<div class="step">"In 10 years he will be twice as old": F + 10 = 2(S + 10). Both ages grow by 10.</div>
<div class="step">Substitute: 3S + 10 = 2S + 20, so <b>S = 10</b> and F = 30.</div>
<div class="step">Check in words: in 10 years they are 40 and 20. Twice. Done.</div>
</div>

## 3. Mixtures by alligation

<figure class="fig" data-fig="alligation"><figcaption>Mixing two prices (or two strengths) to hit a target. The gaps to the target, crossed over, give the ratio.</figcaption></figure>

The same rule works for concentrations (milk and water, acid solutions), speeds and marks: whenever two groups with different averages combine, the ratio of the groups is the inverse ratio of their distances from the combined average.

<div class="myth">

**"The average of two averages is the overall average."** Only if the groups are the same size. A class of 40 averaging 60 and a class of 10 averaging 80 together average (2,400 + 800) ÷ 50 = 64, not 70.

</div>

**Try it:** Change the two strengths and the litres, press Pour, then try Hit a target.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="@tp-mixture-vessel"><figcaption>The mixture always lands between the two strengths, nearer the one you used more of.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="keypoints">

- Turn averages into totals: total = average × number.
- Newcomer = new average + rise × old number.
- Ages: write each sentence as an equation; every age changes by the same years.
- Alligation: cheaper : dearer = (dearer − target) : (target − cheaper).
- Averages of groups of different sizes must be weighted.
