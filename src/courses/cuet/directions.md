**Module 4 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will sketch any walk, find how far and in which direction it ends, and solve shadow questions.

<div class="bigidea">Draw every walk on paper with north at the top. A right turn is always clockwise from the way you are facing, whatever direction that is.</div>

## 1. Walk it on a grid

<figure class="fig" data-fig="direction-walk"><figcaption>Each walk drawn on a grid. The dashed line is the straight path back to the start.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="problem" data-problem="walk-distance"></div>

<div class="steps" data-title="Four rules">
<div class="step"><b>Turns:</b> right = 90° clockwise from where you face; left = 90° anticlockwise. Facing west, a right turn faces north.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Net movement:</b> add up north minus south and east minus west separately.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Distance:</b> √(net north² + net east²). Learn the triples 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 6-8-10, 8-15-17.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Direction:</b> name the end point from the start: if it is north and east, it is north-east of the start (and the start is south-west of it).</div>
</div>

## 2. Shadows

In the **morning** the Sun is in the east, so shadows point **west**. In the **evening** shadows point **east**. At noon in India, shadows are short and point roughly north. Then use your sketch: if your shadow is in front of you in the morning, you are facing west.

<div class="myth">

**"Right always means east."** Only if you are facing north. Turn the drawing in your head, or better, draw an arrow for the way you face before each turn.

</div>
