**Module 2 · about 18 minutes.** By the end you will check any sentence against ten rules in the order examiners use them most.

<div class="bigidea">Error spotting is not about "sounding right". Check each part of the sentence against a short list of rules, starting with the subject and its verb.</div>

## 1. The checking order

<div class="steps" data-title="Check a sentence in this order">
<div class="step"><b>Find the real subject and its verb.</b> Ignore phrases in between: "The box of chocolates (is / are) on the table" → the subject is "box", so <b>is</b>.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Check the tense.</b> Do the time words agree? "Yesterday he has gone" is wrong: "yesterday" needs the simple past, "went".</div>
<div class="step"><b>Check pronouns.</b> Does each pronoun agree with its noun, and is the case right? "Between you and I" → "between you and <b>me</b>".</div>
<div class="step"><b>Check articles and prepositions.</b> "An university" → "a university" (the sound is "yoo"). "Discuss about" → "discuss" (no "about").</div>
<div class="step"><b>Check the shape of the sentence.</b> Modifiers next to what they describe, comparisons complete, lists in parallel form.</div>
</div>

## 2. The ten rules

| Rule | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Subject-verb agreement | Neither of them **are** ready. | Neither of them **is** ready. |
| 2. Tense consistency | He **has finished** it last week. | He **finished** it last week. |
| 3. Articles | She is **an** European writer. | She is **a** European writer. |
| 4. Prepositions | He is good **in** maths. | He is good **at** maths. |
| 5. Pronoun case and agreement | Every student must bring **their** own pen *(formal tests prefer)* | Every student must bring **his or her** own pen |
| 6. Modifiers | **Walking to school,** the rain began. | **Walking to school,** I was caught in the rain. |
| 7. Comparisons | Her marks are higher than **her brother**. | Her marks are higher than **her brother's**. |
| 8. Parallel structure | She likes reading, **to swim** and cycling. | She likes reading, **swimming** and cycling. |
| 9. Conditionals | If I **would have** known, I would have come. | If I **had** known, I would have come. |
| 10. Word order and redundancy | He **returned back** home. | He **returned** home. |

<div class="myth">

**"If it sounds fine when I say it, it is correct."** Spoken English allows many things formal written English does not, such as "less people" for "fewer people" or "between you and I". CUET tests formal written English, so trust the rules over your ear.

</div>

## 3. Practice

<div class="qbank" data-bank="cuet-english" data-topics="error-spotting" data-n="15" data-mode="practice" data-type="mcq"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Start with the real subject and its verb; ignore phrases in between.
- Then tense, pronouns, articles and prepositions, then the sentence's shape.
- "Each", "every", "either", "neither" and "one of" take singular verbs.
- Past conditionals: if + had + past participle, then would have + past participle.
- CUET tests formal written English, not casual speech.

</div>
