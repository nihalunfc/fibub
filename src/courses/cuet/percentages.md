**Module 3 · about 18 minutes.** By the end you will turn any percentage into a multiplier and solve change, successive-change and reverse questions in one line.

<div class="bigidea">"Increase by r%" means "multiply by (1 + r/100)" and "decrease by r%" means "multiply by (1 − r/100)". Once you think in multipliers, two changes in a row are just two multiplications.</div>

## 1. A percentage is a fraction of 100

*Per cent* means "for every hundred". 15% of 240 is 15/100 × 240 = 36. Fast mental route: 10% of 240 is 24, 5% is 12, so 15% is 36.

Learn these by heart; they turn long division into a glance:

| Fraction | 1/2 | 1/3 | 1/4 | 1/5 | 1/6 | 1/8 | 1/9 | 1/10 | 1/12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Percent | 50% | 33⅓% | 25% | 20% | 16⅔% | 12.5% | 11.1% | 10% | 8⅓% |

A useful flip: **x% of y = y% of x**. So 8% of 50 = 50% of 8 = 4.

## 2. Percent change always divides by the old value

```math
\text{percent change} = \frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}} \times 100
```

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>old</dt><dd>the starting value: the base the change is measured from</dd>
<dt>new − old</dt><dd>the change itself, positive for a rise, negative for a fall</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>a ₹60 rise on ₹400 is 15%, but the same ₹60 fall back from ₹460 is only about 13%. The base changed, so the percentage changed.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

## 3. Successive changes: multiply

<figure class="fig" data-fig="pct-bar"><figcaption>Two changes in a row. Try +20% then −20%, or −50% then +100%. The net change is never just the sum.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: +25% then −20%">
<div class="step">Write each change as a multiplier: +25% → × 1.25, −20% → × 0.80.</div>
<div class="step">Multiply them: 1.25 × 0.80 = 1.00.</div>
<div class="step">A multiplier of 1.00 means <b>no change at all</b>. That is why a shop that raises prices 25% and then offers "20% off" is back where it started.</div>
<div class="step">Shortcut for two changes a% and b% (use minus signs for falls): net = a + b + ab/100. Here 25 − 20 − 500/100 = 0%.</div>
</div>

## 4. Reverse percentages: divide by the multiplier

If you know the value **after** a change, divide by the multiplier to get back. After a 25% rise a salary is ₹30,000, so before it was 30,000 ÷ 1.25 = ₹24,000.

<div class="myth">

**"To undo a 25% rise, take off 25%."** Taking 25% off ₹30,000 gives ₹22,500, which is wrong, because the 25% was 25% of the <em>old</em> salary. To undo × 1.25, divide by 1.25: that is a 20% fall. In general, to undo a rise of r%, fall by r ÷ (100 + r) × 100 %.

</div>

## 5. The question types CUET likes

- **More than / less than.** If A is r% more than B, B is r ÷ (100 + r) × 100 % less than A (25% more → 20% less).
- **Spending stays the same.** If price rises r%, use must fall r ÷ (100 + r) × 100 % (Spending = price × quantity).
- **Exams.** "Needs 40% to pass, scores 178, fails by 22" → pass mark 200 = 40% of the maximum → 500.
- **Elections.** The winner's margin is the *difference* in percentages: 58% − 42% = 16% of the votes.
- **Mixtures.** Keep track of the part that does not change (Lesson 3.6 does more).
