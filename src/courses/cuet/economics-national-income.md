**Module 11 · about 18 minutes.** By the end you will move between the eight national income aggregates and know the three methods of measuring it.

<div class="bigidea">Every rupee spent on a final good is a rupee of income for someone. So national income can be counted three ways (production, income, spending) and, done properly, all three give the same answer.</div>

## 1. Three ways to count

<div class="steps" data-title="The same economy, counted three ways">
<div class="step"><b>Value added (product) method:</b> add each firm's output minus the inputs it bought from other firms, so nothing is counted twice.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Income method:</b> add wages, rent, interest, profit and mixed income of the self-employed.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Expenditure method:</b> add final consumption, investment, government spending and net exports.</div>
<div class="step">All three measure the same flow round the circular flow of income: households sell factor services to firms and buy their output.</div>
</div>

## 2. From GDP to national income

```math
\text{NNP}_{FC} = \text{GDP}_{MP} - \text{depreciation} - \text{NIT} + \text{NFIA}
```

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Gross → Net</dt><dd>subtract depreciation (the wear of machines during the year)</dd>
<dt>Domestic → National</dt><dd>add net factor income from abroad (NFIA): what residents earn abroad minus what non-residents earn here</dd>
<dt>MP → FC</dt><dd>subtract net indirect taxes (indirect taxes − subsidies)</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>each word in an aggregate's name tells you one adjustment. Change one word, make one adjustment.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

## 3. Real and nominal GDP

Nominal GDP uses this year's prices; real GDP uses base-year prices, so it shows only changes in output. The GDP deflator = nominal ÷ real × 100.

<div class="myth">

**"Higher GDP always means people are better off."** GDP ignores how income is shared, unpaid work at home, and harm to the environment. It measures output, not welfare.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Three methods: value added, income, expenditure.
- Gross − depreciation = net; domestic + NFIA = national; MP − NIT = FC.
- National income = NNP at factor cost.
- Second-hand sales and transfer payments are not part of GDP.
- Real GDP uses base-year prices; deflator = nominal ÷ real × 100.
