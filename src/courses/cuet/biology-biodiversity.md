**Module 9 · about 18 minutes.** By the end you will describe patterns of biodiversity, its losses and how India protects it. Read first: [organisms, populations and ecosystems](/learn/courses/cuet/biology-ecology/).

<div class="bigidea">Biodiversity is the variety of life at three levels: genes, species and ecosystems. It is richest in the tropics, is being lost fast mostly through human action, and is protected both in the wild and in safe places outside it.</div>

**Everyday picture:** rivets on an aeroplane wing. Losing one or two may not matter, but keep pulling rivets (species) out and at some point the whole wing (the ecosystem) fails. That is Paul Ehrlich's "rivet popper" idea.

## 1. Patterns

| Level | Example from India |
|---|---|
| Genetic | Rauwolfia vomitoria varying in its medicinal chemical; 50,000+ rice varieties |
| Species | Western Ghats have more amphibian species than the Eastern Ghats |
| Ecosystem | deserts, rainforests, mangroves, coral reefs, wetlands, alpine meadows |

India has about 2.4% of the world's land but roughly 8% of its recorded species, and is one of 12 mega-diversity countries.

```math
\log S = \log C + Z\log A \qquad (S = C A^Z)
```

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>S, A</dt><dd>number of species and area.</dd>
<dt>Z</dt><dd>the slope on a log-log graph: about 0.1-0.2 for smaller regions, 0.6-1.2 for very large areas such as whole continents.</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>bigger areas hold more species, but each doubling of area adds fewer new ones.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

## 2. Loss and conservation

<div class="steps" data-title="The evil quartet: four causes of loss">
<div class="step"><b>Habitat loss and fragmentation:</b> rainforests once covered 14% of land, now about 6%.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Over-exploitation:</b> Steller's sea cow and the passenger pigeon were hunted to extinction.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Alien species invasions:</b> Nile perch in Lake Victoria wiped out over 200 cichlid fish species; Parthenium, Lantana and water hyacinth in India.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Co-extinctions:</b> when a host dies out, its specialist parasites and pollinators go too.</div>
</div>

| Conservation | Examples |
|---|---|
| In situ (in the wild) | biodiversity hotspots (India has parts of 4: Western Ghats and Sri Lanka, Indo-Burma, Himalaya, Sundaland), biosphere reserves, national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, sacred groves (Khasi and Jaintia hills, Aravalli, Western Ghats) |
| Ex situ (outside) | zoos, botanical gardens, seed banks, cryopreservation of gametes, tissue culture |
| International | Earth Summit, Rio de Janeiro (1992); World Summit, Johannesburg (2002) |

<div class="myth">

**"Extinction is only natural, so it does not matter."** Species have always gone extinct, but the current rate is estimated at 100 to 1,000 times the natural background rate, driven by human activity.

- Three levels: genetic, species, ecosystem; richest near the equator.
- log S = log C + Z log A; Z ~0.1-0.2 (regions), 0.6-1.2 (continents).
- Evil quartet: habitat loss, over-exploitation, alien species, co-extinction.
- In situ: hotspots, reserves, parks, sacred groves; ex situ: zoos, seed banks, cryopreservation.
