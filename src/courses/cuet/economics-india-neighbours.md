**Module 11 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will compare the development paths of India, China and Pakistan.

<div class="bigidea">Three neighbours with similar starting points made different choices: the timing and type of reform, more than the starting point, explains much of the difference in their paths.</div>

<div class="steps" data-title="Three paths">
<div class="step"><b>Around 1950:</b> all three were poor, agricultural economies; India began planning in 1951, Pakistan in 1956, and the People's Republic of China was founded in 1949.</div>
<div class="step"><b>China:</b> the Great Leap Forward (1958) and the Cultural Revolution set it back; reforms from 1978 (farm reforms, special economic zones, foreign investment) brought very fast growth.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Pakistan:</b> a mixed economy with public and private sectors; growth was uneven, often relying on remittances and foreign aid, with political instability.</div>
<div class="step"><b>India:</b> steady planned growth, then faster growth after 1991, led strongly by services.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Comparing fairly:</b> look at growth, sector shares and human development (health, education, income per head), not one number.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Faster GDP growth settles which path was best."** Growth matters, but so do health, education, freedom and how gains are shared. That is why the Human Development Index is used alongside GDP.

</div>
