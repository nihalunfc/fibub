**Module 4 · about 14 minutes.** By the end you will find the rule of a series in a set order and handle letter series with alphabet positions.

<div class="bigidea">Don't stare at a series hoping to see the rule. Check in a fixed order: differences, second differences, ratios, squares and cubes, then two series mixed together.</div>

## 2. The checking order

<div class="steps" data-title="Five checks, in order">
<div class="step"><b>1. Differences.</b> Constant gaps (5, 8, 11, 14): add 3. Gaps that grow steadily (4, 6, 8, 10): second differences constant.</div>
<div class="step"><b>2. Ratios.</b> If terms grow fast, divide: 3, 6, 12, 24 (× 2); 2, 5, 11, 23 (× 2 + 1).</div>
<div class="step"><b>3. Squares and cubes.</b> Know squares to 30 and cubes to 15. 2, 5, 10, 17 is n² + 1; 0, 7, 26, 63 is n³ − 1.</div>
<div class="step"><b>4. Alternating series.</b> If nothing fits, split odd and even positions: 1, 10, 3, 20, 5, 30 is 1, 3, 5 and 10, 20, 30 woven together.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5. Primes and special numbers.</b> 2, 3, 5, 7, 11 (primes); 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 (each is the sum of the two before).</div>
</div>

## 3. Letter series

Turn letters into numbers: A = 1 ... Z = 26. Memorise **E J O T Y** = 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 to find any position fast. Then letter series are number series: B, E, H, K = 2, 5, 8, 11, so the next is 14 = N. Past Z, wrap round to A (27 → 1).

<div class="myth">

**"There is always one obvious rule."** Some series fit two rules for the first few terms. Check your rule on every term given, not just the first three, and check that it gives exactly one of the options.

</div>
