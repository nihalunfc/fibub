**Module 1 · about 12 minutes.** By the end you will know what CUET is for, how each paper is built, how your marks are counted and what happens after the result.

<div class="bigidea">CUET replaces dozens of separate entrance tests and board-mark cut-offs with one computer test: you pick up to five papers, each one is 50 questions in 60 minutes, and universities use your scores to rank you.</div>

## 1. What CUET is for

**CUET (UG)**, the Common University Entrance Test for undergraduate courses, is run every year by the **National Testing Agency (NTA)**. All central universities, such as Delhi University, BHU, JNU, Jamia Millia Islamia and Hyderabad University, admit undergraduates through it, and so do many state, deemed and private universities. Instead of chasing a 99% board cut-off, you are ranked on the CUET papers the course asks for.

It is a **computer-based test** taken at an exam centre, usually in May or June, with registration in about February and March and results in about July. Dates change every year, so always check [cuet.nta.nic.in](https://cuet.nta.nic.in/).

## 2. The three kinds of test

<div class="steps" data-title="How your exam is put together">
<div class="step"><b>Languages (13).</b> English, Hindi and 11 more Indian languages. A language test checks reading comprehension, vocabulary and grammar. Most courses ask for one language, very often English.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Domain subjects (23).</b> Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics, Biology, Accountancy, Economics, History, Political Science and more. Each follows the NCERT Class 12 syllabus.</div>
<div class="step"><b>The General Test (1).</b> General knowledge and current affairs, numerical ability (arithmetic up to about Class 8 level), and logical and analytical reasoning. Many courses ask for it; some do not.</div>
<div class="step"><b>You choose up to 5 tests in total.</b> The right five depend on the courses and universities you want. Lesson 1.3 shows how to choose.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Every test looks the same:</b> 50 compulsory multiple-choice questions, 4 options each, 60 minutes, +5 for a right answer and −1 for a wrong one. That sameness is your friend: one strategy works for all five papers.</div>
</div>

## 3. How your marks are counted

Every question is worth the same. A right answer earns **5 marks**, a wrong answer loses **1 mark**, and a question you leave blank (or only mark for review) earns **0**. The best possible score in one test is 50 × 5 = **250**.

```math
\text{score} = 5R - W
```

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>R</dt><dd>the number of questions you got right</dd>
<dt>W</dt><dd>the number you got wrong (blanks are not counted at all)</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>one right answer pays for five wrong ones. A wrong answer hurts, but far less than a right one helps, which is why Lesson 1.2 shows guessing is often worth it.</dd>
</dl>

## 4. After the test

NTA publishes an answer key, gives a few days to challenge it, then declares results. When a paper runs in more than one shift, NTA may adjust raw scores so that an easier or harder shift does not decide your rank (the method is in each year's bulletin). Then **each university runs its own admission**: you apply on its portal (for example, Delhi University's CSAS), it ranks you on the CUET papers that course needs, and seats are offered in rounds.

<div class="myth">

**"I must take the subjects I studied in Class 12."** Not any more. Since 2025 you may take any domain subject, but the university can still set its own rules for a course, such as needing Mathematics in Class 12 for B.Sc. (Hons) Mathematics. Check the course's eligibility before choosing.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- CUET (UG) is one computer-based entrance test run by NTA and used by central, state, deemed and private universities.
- You choose up to 5 tests from 13 languages, 23 domain subjects and the General Test.
- Every test: 50 compulsory questions, 60 minutes, +5 right, −1 wrong, 0 blank, 250 marks in all.
- Domain subjects follow the NCERT Class 12 syllabus.
- Each university then admits students through its own portal using the CUET scores it asks for.

</div>
