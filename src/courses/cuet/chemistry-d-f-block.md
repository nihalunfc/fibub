**Module 7 · about 18 minutes.** By the end you will explain the typical properties of transition metals and answer KMnO₄, K₂Cr₂O₇ and lanthanoid questions.

<div class="bigidea">Transition metals have a partly filled d subshell. That one fact explains almost everything about them: many oxidation states, colour, magnetism, catalysis and complex formation.</div>

**Everyday picture:** stained glass. A dash of cobalt makes glass blue and chromium makes it green, because the d electrons of those ions soak up some colours of light and let the rest through.

## 1. Typical properties and why

<div class="steps" data-title="Five properties from one partly filled d shell">
<div class="step"><b>Variable oxidation states:</b> (n−1)d and ns energies are close, so different numbers of electrons can be lost. Mn: +2 to +7.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Colour:</b> ligands split the d levels; light of the matching energy lifts an electron (d-d transition). We see the complementary colour. d⁰ and d¹⁰ ions are colourless.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Paramagnetism:</b> unpaired d electrons. Spin-only μ = √(n(n + 2)) BM.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Catalysis:</b> variable oxidation states and surfaces that hold reactants. Fe in the Haber process, V₂O₅ in the contact process, Ni for hydrogenation.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Complexes and alloys:</b> small, highly charged ions with empty d orbitals attract ligands; similar atomic sizes mix into alloys (brass, steel).</div>
</div>

## 2. Two oxidising agents you must know

| Compound | Made from | Key half-equation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| K₂Cr₂O₇ (orange) | chromite ore FeCr₂O₄ | Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O | turns yellow (CrO₄²⁻) in base: the pH changes the colour |
| KMnO₄ (purple) | pyrolusite MnO₂ | acid: MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O | neutral/weakly basic: gains 3e⁻ to MnO₂; self-indicator in titrations |

## 3. The f-block

Lanthanoids (Ce to Lu) fill 4f and mostly show +3. Across the series the atomic and ionic radii shrink steadily: the **lanthanoid contraction**. Its effects: Zr and Hf (4d and 5d) are almost the same size and so are very hard to separate, and lanthanoid basicity falls from La(OH)₃ to Lu(OH)₃. Actinoids (Th to Lr) fill 5f, show more oxidation states, and are all radioactive.

<div class="myth">

**"All transition metal compounds are coloured."** Sc³⁺ (3d⁰), Ti⁴⁺ (3d⁰), Zn²⁺ and Cu⁺ (3d¹⁰) compounds are usually white, because no d-d jump is possible.

</div>
