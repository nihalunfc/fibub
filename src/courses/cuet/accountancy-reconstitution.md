**Module 10 · about 18 minutes.** By the end you will find new and sacrificing ratios and pass the entries for goodwill, revaluation and reserves on admission.

<div class="bigidea">When a partner joins, the old partners give up part of their share. Everything that happens on admission compensates them fairly for that sacrifice and for value built up before the newcomer arrived.</div>

## 1. Ratios

<div class="steps" data-title="A and B share 3 : 2; C joins for 1/5">
<div class="step">Left for A and B: 1 − 1/5 = 4/5.</div>
<div class="step">If they give up in their old ratio, A keeps 3/5 × 4/5 = 12/25 and B 2/5 × 4/5 = 8/25. C has 5/25.</div>
<div class="step">New ratio A : B : C = 12 : 8 : 5.</div>
<div class="step">Sacrifice: A 3/5 − 12/25 = 3/25, B 2/5 − 8/25 = 2/25, so the sacrificing ratio is 3 : 2, the same as the old ratio, as expected.</div>
</div>

<div class="problem" data-problem="sacrifice-share"></div>

## 2. The three adjustments

- **Goodwill:** the new partner's share of goodwill (premium) goes to the old partners in the **sacrificing ratio**. Existing goodwill in the books is first written off in the old ratio.
- **Revaluation:** assets and liabilities are revalued; the profit or loss on the **revaluation account** goes to the old partners in the **old ratio**.
- **Reserves and accumulated profits:** shared among the old partners in the **old ratio**.

<div class="myth">

**"The new partner shares in the revaluation profit."** The rise in value happened before he joined, so it belongs to the old partners alone, in the old ratio.

</div>

## 3. Practice

<div class="keypoints">

- Sacrificing ratio = old − new; gaining ratio = new − old.
- Premium for goodwill: to old partners in the sacrificing ratio.
- Revaluation profit or loss and reserves: to old partners in the old ratio.
- If old partners give up in their old ratio, the sacrificing ratio equals the old ratio.
