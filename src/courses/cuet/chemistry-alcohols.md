**Module 7 · about 22 minutes.** By the end you will prepare and tell apart alcohols, phenols and ethers and predict their main reactions. Read first: [haloalkanes](/learn/courses/cuet/chemistry-haloalkanes/).

<div class="bigidea">All three have a C-O bond. In alcohols O-H sits on an ordinary carbon, in phenols on a benzene ring (which makes it more acidic), and in ethers the oxygen sits between two carbons with no O-H at all.</div>

**Everyday picture:** hand sanitiser is mostly ethanol or propan-2-ol; Dettol-type antiseptics are phenol derivatives; and diethyl ether was one of the first surgical anaesthetics.

## 1. Making alcohols and phenols

| From | Reagent | Gives |
|---|---|---|
| Alkene | dil. H₂SO₄ (Markovnikov) | alcohol |
| Alkene | B₂H₆ then H₂O₂/OH⁻ | anti-Markovnikov alcohol |
| Aldehyde / ketone | NaBH₄ or LiAlH₄ | 1° / 2° alcohol |
| Carbonyl + Grignard | RMgX then H₃O⁺ | HCHO → 1°, other aldehydes → 2°, ketones → 3° |
| Cumene | O₂ then H⁺ | phenol + acetone (industrial) |
| Benzene diazonium salt | warm water | phenol |

## 2. Acidity and reactions

<div class="steps" data-title="Why phenol is acidic and ethanol hardly at all">
<div class="step"><b>Lose H⁺:</b> ethanol gives ethoxide, phenol gives phenoxide.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Ethoxide:</b> the − charge sits on one O, and the ethyl group pushes more electron density onto it. Unstable, so ethanol barely ionises.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Phenoxide:</b> the − charge spreads into the ring by resonance. More stable, so phenol ionises more (pKa ~10 against ~16).</div>
<div class="step"><b>Nitro groups</b> at ortho and para pull charge further and make phenols much more acidic (picric acid is a strong acid).</div>
</div>

**Alcohols:** dehydration with conc. H₂SO₄ (443 K gives alkene, 413 K gives ether), oxidation (1° → aldehyde → acid, 2° → ketone, 3° resists), Lucas test (3° at once, 2° in minutes, 1° not at room temperature). **Phenols:** strongly ortho-para directing; bromine water gives a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol; Kolbe's reaction (CO₂ + NaOH) gives salicylic acid, and Reimer-Tiemann (CHCl₃ + NaOH) gives salicylaldehyde. **Ethers:** Williamson synthesis RO⁻ + R'X (R'X primary); HI splits them.

<div class="problem" data-problem="alcohol-combustion"></div>

<div class="myth">

**"Alcohols are acids because they have an O-H group."** Alcohols are weaker acids than water. They react with sodium metal, but not with NaOH. Phenol does react with NaOH, but not with NaHCO₃ (carboxylic acids do).

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Grignard + HCHO → 1°, + RCHO → 2°, + ketone → 3° alcohol.
- Acidity: carboxylic acid &gt; phenol &gt; water &gt; alcohol.
- Lucas test: 3° at once, 2° in minutes, 1° no turbidity.
- Kolbe → salicylic acid; Reimer-Tiemann → salicylaldehyde.
- Williamson: alkoxide + primary halide.
