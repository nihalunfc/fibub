**Module 4 · about 16 minutes.** By the end you will solve row, circle and ranking puzzles in a fixed order of clues.

<div class="bigidea">Place clues in order of certainty: fixed positions first, then pairs that link two people, then "not" clues to rule things out. Draw the seats before you read the second clue.</div>

## 1. A circle, solved clue by clue

Six friends sit round a table facing the centre. (1) Asha sits at the top. (2) Charu sits opposite Asha. (3) Bilal sits next to Asha, one seat anticlockwise. (4) Dev sits next to Asha. (5) Farah sits next to Dev. Who sits between Charu and Bilal?

<div class="steps" data-fig="seat-circle" data-title="Solve it one clue at a time">
<div class="step">Clue 1: Asha takes the top seat. Every other seat is placed relative to her.</div>
<div class="step">Clue 2: opposite in a circle of six is three seats away. Charu takes the bottom seat.</div>
<div class="step">Clue 3: Bilal is Asha's anticlockwise neighbour (on the drawing, just left of the top).</div>
<div class="step">Clue 4: Asha's only other neighbour seat is clockwise of her, so Dev sits there.</div>
<div class="step">Clue 5: Farah is next to Dev, so she takes the seat after him; Esha takes the last seat, between Charu and Bilal. <b>Answer: Esha.</b></div>
</div>

## 2. Left and right

- **Row facing north (towards you as you read):** their right is your right.
- **Row facing south:** their right is your left. Draw an arrow for the way they face.
- **Circle facing the centre:** a person's left is the clockwise neighbour, their right the anticlockwise one.
- **Circle facing outwards:** the other way round.

## 3. Ranking and rows

Total in a row = position from left + position from right − 1. Between two people = difference of positions − 1. For "taller than / heavier than" chains, write the names in a line from biggest to smallest as you read each clue.

<div class="myth">

**"I'll keep the arrangement in my head."** Puzzles are designed to overflow memory. Draw the seats as blanks first and fill them in pencil; when a clue allows two places, draw both cases side by side and let the next clue kill one.

</div>
