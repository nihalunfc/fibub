**Module 10 · about 16 minutes.** By the end you will find the gaining ratio and the amount due to a retiring partner or a deceased partner's executors.

<div class="bigidea">Retirement is admission in reverse: the remaining partners gain a share, so they pay the outgoing partner for it, and everything built up while the outgoing partner was there is shared in the old ratio.</div>

## 1. Gaining ratio

Gaining ratio = new ratio − old ratio. If nothing is said, the remaining partners take over the share in their old ratio, so the gaining ratio is their old ratio.

## 2. Settling the account

<div class="steps" data-title="Amount due to a retiring partner">
<div class="step">Start with the partner's capital (and current account) balance.</div>
<div class="step">Add their share of reserves and revaluation profit (old ratio); subtract any revaluation loss.</div>
<div class="step">Add their share of goodwill, paid by the gaining partners in the gaining ratio.</div>
<div class="step">On death, also add their share of profit to the date of death (on last year's profit or on sales, as the question says) and interest on capital to that date. The total goes to their executors.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Goodwill on retirement is credited to all partners."** Only the retiring partner is credited; the remaining partners who gain are debited in the gaining ratio.

</div>

## 3. Practice

<div class="qbank" data-bank="cuet-accountancy" data-topics="retirement-death" data-n="10" data-mode="practice" data-type="mcq"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Gaining ratio = new − old; gaining partners pay for goodwill.
- Reserves and revaluation are shared in the old ratio, including the outgoing partner.
- On death add profit to the date of death: last year's profit × months/12 × share.
- The balance goes to the retiring partner, or to a loan account, or to the executors.

</div>
