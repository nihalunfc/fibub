**Module 5 · about 18 minutes.** By the end you will know the parts of the Constitution, the main articles and how laws are made, which together cover most polity questions.

<div class="bigidea">Polity questions are about a small set of stable facts: what the Constitution guarantees, who holds which power, and how a law is made. Learn the structure and the facts hang on it.</div>

## 1. The Constitution at a glance

Adopted on **26 November 1949**, in force from **26 January 1950**. Dr B. R. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee; Dr Rajendra Prasad presided over the Constituent Assembly. The **Preamble** calls India a sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic (the words socialist and secular were added by the 42nd Amendment in 1976) and promises justice, liberty, equality and fraternity.

| Part | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Part III (Articles 12 to 35) | Fundamental Rights, enforceable in court |
| Part IV (Articles 36 to 51) | Directive Principles of State Policy, guidelines for governments, not enforceable in court |
| Part IVA (Article 51A) | Fundamental Duties (added in 1976) |
| Article 368 | How the Constitution is amended |

## 2. Rights worth knowing by number

- **Article 14** equality before law · **15** no discrimination by religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth · **17** untouchability abolished
- **Article 19** six freedoms (speech, assembly, association, movement, residence, profession) · **21** life and personal liberty · **21A** free education for children aged 6 to 14
- **Article 32** right to go to the Supreme Court to enforce rights (Dr Ambedkar called it the heart and soul of the Constitution)

## 3. How a bill becomes a law

<div class="steps" data-title="From bill to Act">
<div class="step"><b>Introduction.</b> A bill is introduced in either House (a money bill only in the Lok Sabha).</div>
<div class="step"><b>Debate and committee.</b> It is debated, often sent to a committee, changed, and voted on.</div>
<div class="step"><b>The other House.</b> It goes through the same steps. If the Houses disagree on an ordinary bill, the President can call a joint sitting.</div>
<div class="step"><b>The President's assent.</b> The President can sign it, or return a non-money bill once for reconsideration. Once signed, it becomes an Act.</div>
</div>

## 4. Who does what

The **President** is head of state and acts on the advice of the Council of Ministers led by the **Prime Minister**, who must have the support of the Lok Sabha. The **Lok Sabha** is directly elected (term five years); the **Rajya Sabha** is a permanent house whose members are elected by state legislatures, one third retiring every two years. The **Supreme Court** is the highest court and guardian of the Constitution; the Election Commission, the Comptroller and Auditor General and the Union Public Service Commission are constitutional bodies.

<div class="myth">

**"Directive Principles can be enforced in court like rights."** They cannot. They guide laws and policies (equal pay for equal work, village panchayats, protecting the environment), but a citizen cannot sue to enforce them. Fundamental Rights can be enforced.

</div>
