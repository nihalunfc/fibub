**Module 3 · about 17 minutes.** By the end you will handle unit conversion, average speed, relative speed, trains and boats.

<div class="bigidea">Everything comes from distance = speed × time. Trains, boats and races only change what "distance" and "speed" mean.</div>

## 1. One formula, one conversion

```math
d = s \times t \qquad 1\ \text{km/h} = \tfrac{5}{18}\ \text{m/s}
```

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>d, s, t</dt><dd>distance, speed and time, in matching units (km with hours, metres with seconds)</dd>
<dt>5/18</dt><dd>1,000 m ÷ 3,600 s. Going from m/s to km/h, multiply by 18/5.</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>for a fixed distance, speed and time are inversely proportional: go 25% faster and the time falls to 1/1.25 = 80% of what it was.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

## 2. Average speed is not the average of speeds

For a round trip at speeds a and b, more time is spent at the slower speed, so the average is lower than (a + b)/2: it is **2ab ÷ (a + b)**. Always use **total distance ÷ total time**.

## 3. Trains: add the lengths

<figure class="fig" data-fig="train-cross"><figcaption>To pass a pole the train covers its own length; to clear a platform it covers its length plus the platform's.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="problem" data-problem="train-platform"></div>

<div class="steps" data-title="Relative speed">
<div class="step"><b>Same direction:</b> subtract speeds. A 60 km/h train overtaking a 40 km/h train gains at 20 km/h.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Opposite directions:</b> add speeds. Two trains at 60 and 40 km/h approach each other at 100 km/h.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Two trains passing:</b> the distance is the sum of both lengths, at the relative speed.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Boats:</b> downstream = boat + stream, upstream = boat − stream. So boat = (down + up)/2 and stream = (down − up)/2.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Mixing units is fine if I'm careful."** Most wrong answers in this topic come from km/h meeting metres and seconds. Convert everything to m and s (or km and h) before you start.

</div>
