**Module 2 · about 15 minutes.** By the end you will have three ways to crack a word you half-know and a routine to learn new ones.

<div class="bigidea">A synonym has the same direction and about the same strength of meaning; an antonym has the opposite direction. Ask both questions of every option.</div>

## 1. Direction and strength

<figure class="fig" data-fig="word-scale"><figcaption>Words in a family sit on a scale. The best synonym is close on the same side; the best antonym is the mirror image on the other side.</figcaption></figure>

If the word is "delighted", "happy" is a synonym but "ecstatic" is closer in strength. CUET usually offers only one option on the right side, but when it offers two, pick the one closest in strength.

## 2. Three ways to crack a half-known word

<div class="steps" data-title="When you are not sure of the word">
<div class="step"><b>Sense its charge.</b> Does the word feel positive or negative? "Malicious", "callous", "obstinate" feel negative. That alone often rules out two options.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Break it into roots.</b> bene/mal (good/bad), ante/post (before/after), circum (around), contra (against), omni (all), philo (love), -phobia (fear), -cide (killing). "Omnipotent" = all-powerful.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Test it in a sentence.</b> Put each option into a simple sentence where the original fits: "She was ___ with money, never wasting a rupee." Only "thrifty" fits as well as "frugal".</div>
<div class="step"><b>Watch for look-alikes.</b> Examiners put in words that look or sound similar: frugal / fragile, eminent / imminent, affect / effect. Similar spelling is not similar meaning.</div>
</div>

## 3. A 10-minute daily routine

Read one good-quality editorial or long article a day. Write down five words you could not define, with the sentence they came in. Next day, cover the meaning and test yourself before adding five more. In 12 weeks that is about 400 words met in context, which is far more than most CUET papers test.

<div class="myth">

**"Learning long word lists is the best way."** Lists fade quickly because the words have no context. Words met in a sentence, written down and tested a day later stay much longer: testing yourself is one of the best-proven ways to learn.

</div>

## 4. Practice

<div class="keypoints">

- Synonym: same direction, similar strength. Antonym: opposite direction.
- Sense the word's charge, break it into roots, and test options in a sentence.
- Beware of look-alike words.
- Build vocabulary from daily reading: 5 words a day, tested the next day.
