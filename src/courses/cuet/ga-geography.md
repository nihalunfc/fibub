**Module 5 · about 17 minutes.** By the end you will have a mental map of India's physical features and the world basics that general awareness questions use.

<div class="bigidea">Hang geography facts on a map. Learn India from north to south (mountains, plains, plateau, coasts, islands) and each river, park and crop has a place to sit.</div>

## 1. India from north to south

<div class="steps" data-title="A tour from the Himalaya to the islands">
<div class="step"><b>The Himalaya.</b> Young fold mountains in three ranges: Himadri (highest), Himachal, Shiwaliks. Mount Everest, the world's highest peak, is in the Nepal Himalaya; Kangchenjunga stands on the Sikkim-Nepal border.</div>
<div class="step"><b>The Northern Plains.</b> Built by the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra and their tributaries: some of the most fertile land in the world.</div>
<div class="step"><b>The Peninsular Plateau.</b> Very old, hard rock. The Western Ghats are higher and continuous; the Eastern Ghats lower and broken by rivers. Anamudi (Kerala) is the highest peak of the south.</div>
<div class="step"><b>The coasts and islands.</b> The western coast is narrow (Konkan, Malabar); the eastern coast is wider, with deltas of the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri. Andaman and Nicobar in the Bay of Bengal; Lakshadweep (coral islands) in the Arabian Sea.</div>
</div>

## 2. Rivers, climate and soils

- **Himalayan rivers** are fed by snow and rain and flow all year. **Peninsular rivers** are rain-fed; most flow east, but the **Narmada** and **Tapi** flow west.
- **Monsoon:** the south-west monsoon brings most of India's rain from June to September; the north-east monsoon gives Tamil Nadu much of its rain in October to December.
- **Soils:** alluvial (northern plains, most widespread), black or regur (Deccan, good for cotton), red and laterite (south and east), desert (Rajasthan).

## 3. World basics

Seven continents (Asia is the largest, Australia the smallest) and five oceans (the Pacific is the largest and deepest). The Equator is 0° latitude; the Tropic of Cancer is about 23.5° N and passes through India. Each 15° of longitude is one hour of time; India uses one time zone, UTC + 5:30, based on 82.5° E.

<div class="myth">

**"All Indian rivers flow into the Bay of Bengal."** The Indus system, the Narmada, the Tapi, the Mahi and the Sabarmati flow into the Arabian Sea. The Narmada and Tapi do not form deltas; they end in estuaries.

</div>
