**Module 2 · about 14 minutes.** By the end you will order four or five jumbled sentences in about a minute.

<div class="bigidea">A paragraph is a chain: each sentence hooks onto the one before through a pronoun, a connector, a time word or a repeated idea. Find the first link and follow the chain.</div>

## 1. Try it

<figure class="fig" data-fig="para-order"><figcaption>Tap the sentences in the order you think is right, then see why.</figcaption></figure>

## 2. The four kinds of link

<div class="steps" data-title="Links that fix the order">
<div class="step"><b>Pronouns point back.</b> "She", "they", "it", "this", "these", "such" need a noun earlier. A sentence that starts with one is never first.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Connectors show the relationship.</b> "However", "but" turn against the previous sentence; "so", "therefore" give its result; "also", "moreover" add to it; "for example" illustrates it.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Time and sequence words.</b> "First", "then", "later", "by evening", "in 2020", "finally" put events in order.</div>
<div class="step"><b>New idea before its details.</b> "a new vaccine" (new, with "a") comes before "the vaccine" (already known, with "the").</div>
</div>

## 3. Use the options

Do not solve from scratch. Find the opening sentence, then look at the options: usually only one or two start with it. Then fix one certain pair (for example "R must follow Q because of 'She'") and check which option has that pair. You often do not need to place every sentence.

<div class="myth">

**"I need to read every option in full."** Usually one certain opening sentence and one certain pair rule out all but one option in seconds. Spend the time on finding links, not comparing letter strings.

</div>
