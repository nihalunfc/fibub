**Module 11 · about 15 minutes.** By the end you will explain the 1991 crisis and the three parts of the reforms.

<div class="bigidea">In 1991 India changed course: fewer controls at home (liberalisation), a smaller role for the state as owner (privatisation), and closer links with the world economy (globalisation).</div>

<div class="steps" data-title="Crisis to reform">
<div class="step"><b>The crisis:</b> high government deficits, rising foreign debt and falling foreign exchange reserves left India able to pay for only a few weeks of imports.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Liberalisation:</b> industrial licensing abolished for most industries, financial sector reforms, tax reforms, a market-led exchange rate and freer trade.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Privatisation:</b> disinvestment in public sector enterprises and opening sectors reserved for the state to private firms.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Globalisation:</b> lower tariffs, welcome to foreign investment, outsourcing of services and membership of the World Trade Organization from 1995.</div>
<div class="step"><b>The appraisal:</b> faster growth, more foreign investment and a boom in services, but critics point to slower growth in agriculture and uneven gains across people and regions.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Globalisation means only that foreign goods come in."** It is two-way: Indian firms and workers also sell services and goods abroad, which is how the IT and outsourcing industries grew.

</div>
