**Module 11 · about 16 minutes.** By the end you will explain credit creation and the RBI's tools of monetary policy.

<div class="bigidea">Banks keep only a fraction of deposits as reserves and lend the rest. Each loan becomes someone's deposit, so a little new money becomes much more credit, and the RBI controls how much by setting reserves and interest rates.</div>

## 1. Credit creation

<div class="steps" data-title="₹1,000 deposited, legal reserve ratio 20%">
<div class="step">The bank keeps ₹200 as reserves and lends ₹800.</div>
<div class="step">The ₹800 is spent and deposited in another bank, which keeps ₹160 and lends ₹640.</div>
<div class="step">This repeats: 1,000 + 800 + 640 + 512 + ... deposits.</div>
<div class="step">The total is 1,000 × 1/0.2 = ₹5,000. The money multiplier is 1 ÷ LRR.</div>
</div>

<div class="problem" data-problem="money-multiplier"></div>

## 2. The RBI's tools

| Tool | To reduce money supply (fight inflation) | To increase it |
|---|---|---|
| Repo rate | raise | cut |
| Cash reserve ratio (CRR) | raise | cut |
| Statutory liquidity ratio (SLR) | raise | cut |
| Open market operations | sell government securities | buy them |

<div class="myth">

**"Banks lend out only the cash people deposit."** Through the multiplier, the banking system as a whole creates deposits several times the cash it holds. That is why reserve ratios matter.

</div>
