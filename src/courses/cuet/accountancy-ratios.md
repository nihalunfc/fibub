**Module 10 · about 18 minutes.** By the end you will compute the main ratios and predict how a transaction changes them.

<div class="bigidea">A ratio is one number from the statements divided by another, chosen to answer one question: can the firm pay its bills, is it borrowing too much, how fast does its stock move, how much does it earn?</div>

| Group | Ratio | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Liquidity | Current ratio | current assets ÷ current liabilities |
| Liquidity | Quick ratio | (current assets − inventory − prepaid expenses) ÷ current liabilities |
| Solvency | Debt-equity | long-term debts ÷ shareholders' funds |
| Solvency | Interest coverage | profit before interest and tax ÷ interest on long-term debt |
| Activity | Inventory turnover | cost of revenue from operations ÷ average inventory |
| Activity | Trade receivables turnover | net credit revenue ÷ average trade receivables |
| Profitability | Net profit ratio | net profit ÷ revenue from operations × 100 |
| Profitability | Return on investment | profit before interest and tax ÷ capital employed × 100 |

<div class="steps" data-title="How a transaction moves the current ratio (2 : 1 to start)">
<div class="step"><b>Pay a creditor in cash:</b> both sides fall by the same amount, so the ratio <b>rises</b> above 2.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Buy goods on credit:</b> both sides rise by the same amount, so the ratio <b>falls</b> towards 1.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Collect from a debtor:</b> one current asset becomes another, so <b>no change</b>.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Buy machinery for cash:</b> current assets fall, liabilities do not, so the ratio <b>falls</b>.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"A higher current ratio is always better."** Too high can mean idle cash or slow-moving stock. A ratio is a question to investigate, not a grade.

## Practice

<div class="qbank" data-bank="cuet-accountancy" data-topics="ratios" data-n="10" data-mode="practice" data-type="mcq"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Liquidity: current and quick ratios. Solvency: debt-equity, interest coverage.
- Activity: inventory and receivables turnover. Profitability: net profit ratio, return on investment.
- Equal changes to both sides move a ratio towards 1 if it is above 1 (and away when they fall).
- Interpret ratios in context; compare across years and firms.

</div>
