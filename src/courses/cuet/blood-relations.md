**Module 4 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will turn any chain of relations into a small drawing and read off the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Draw, don't think. One symbol per person, one line per relation, one clue at a time, and generations in rows.</div>

## 1. The symbols

Square = man, circle = woman (a dot or "?" if you do not know yet). A double line joins a married couple; a single horizontal line joins brothers and sisters; a vertical line goes down a generation to the children.

<div class="steps" data-fig="family-tree" data-title="Build the tree, one clue at a time">
<div class="step">"Arjun is Meena's husband." Two people on the top row, joined by a double line.</div>
<div class="step">"Ravi is Arjun's son." A line down to the next generation: Ravi.</div>
<div class="step">"Priya is Ravi's sister." Priya joins Ravi on the same row, as a woman.</div>
<div class="step">"Kiran is Priya's son." A line down from Priya to the third row.</div>
<div class="step">"How is Kiran related to Meena?" Two rows down, through a daughter: <b>grandson</b> (daughter's son).</div>
</div>

## 2. Phrases to decode first

| Phrase | Means |
|---|---|
| my father's or mother's only son | me (if I am male) or my brother |
| my father's wife | my mother (unless the question says step-mother) |
| my mother's brother | my maternal uncle |
| my brother's wife | my sister-in-law |
| my son's daughter | my granddaughter |

Work from the **end** of a long phrase back to the speaker: "my father's sister's son" = father → his sister (aunt) → her son (cousin).

<div class="myth">

**"Every name tells you the gender."** In exam questions a name is not proof: if the question never says whether a person is male or female, the answer may be "cannot be determined". Use only what the clues state.

</div>
