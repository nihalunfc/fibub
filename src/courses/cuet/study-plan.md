**Module 1 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will have a week-by-week plan that fits your time and ends with full mock tests.

<div class="bigidea">A good plan has three phases: learn and practise each topic, revise the weak ones, then sit full timed mocks. The mocks are not optional: they are where knowledge becomes marks.</div>

## 1. Build your plan

<figure class="fig" data-fig="plan-builder"><figcaption>Set your weeks, tests and hours a day. About 60% of the time goes to learning, 20% to revision and 20% to full mocks.</figcaption></figure>

```math
\text{hours per test} = \frac{\text{learning weeks} \times 7 \times \text{hours a day}}{\text{number of tests}}
```

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>learning weeks</dt><dd>about 60% of the weeks you have left (the rest is revision and mocks)</dd>
<dt>number of tests</dt><dd>how many CUET papers you chose in Lesson 1.3 (at most five)</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>if this comes out under about 25 hours for a domain subject you have not studied well, cut a test or add an hour a day now, not in the last week.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

## 2. Three ready plans

**12 weeks (the comfortable plan).** Weeks 1 to 7: one module a week per subject, rotating subjects daily. Weeks 8 to 9: revise every topic where your practice score was under 80%. Weeks 10 to 12: one full mock per test every two days, and a full review of every mistake the day after.

**8 weeks (the usual plan, after board exams).** Weeks 1 to 5: lessons and practice, two subjects a day. Week 6: revision. Weeks 7 to 8: daily mocks, rotating tests.

**4 weeks (the sprint).** You do not have time for everything, so go for marks: in each subject do the lessons with the most questions first (this course lists them in order of importance within each module), practise with the solutions open, and start mocks in week 3.

## 3. One study day

<div class="steps" data-title="A 3-hour study session">
<div class="step"><b>10 minutes: warm-up.</b> A 10-question practice set from yesterday's topic. It is the fastest way to make learning stick.</div>
<div class="step"><b>60 minutes: one new lesson.</b> Read it, play with every figure and step through every animation. Write the key points in your own words.</div>
<div class="step"><b>50 minutes: practice.</b> Two or three practice sets on the new lesson. Read the full solution of every question you got wrong <em>and</em> of every one you guessed.</div>
<div class="step"><b>50 minutes: a second subject.</b> Same pattern, shorter: one lesson or one practice block.</div>
<div class="step"><b>10 minutes: log it.</b> Mark the lesson done and note your practice score. Anything under 80% goes on the revision list.</div>
</div>

<div class="problem" data-problem="cuet-target"></div>

<div class="myth">

**"I will do mocks once I have finished everything."** You will never feel finished. Take your first mock by the middle of your plan even if some chapters are missing: it shows which topics cost the most marks, which tells you what to study next.

</div>

## 4. Keep your progress safe

This course remembers which lessons you marked done **in this browser only**. Tick "Remember my progress on this device" on the [course home](/learn/courses/cuet/#progress) so it stays when you close the tab, and download your progress file every week: if you change phones or clear your browser, upload it to carry on exactly where you were.

<div class="keypoints">

- Split your weeks about 60% learning, 20% revision, 20% full mocks.
- Hours per test = learning weeks × 7 × hours a day ÷ number of tests: aim for 25+ for each domain subject.
- Each day: warm-up questions, one new lesson, practice with full solutions, then log your score.
- Anything under 80% practice accuracy goes on the revision list.
- Start full mocks by the middle of your plan, and save your progress file weekly.

</div>
