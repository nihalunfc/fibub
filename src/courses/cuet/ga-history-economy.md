**Module 5 · about 17 minutes.** By the end you will place the main events of Indian history in order and use the basic economy terms correctly.

<div class="bigidea">History questions reward order: if you know the sequence of eras and of the freedom movement, a date question becomes a matter of placing it on the line.</div>

## 1. The long timeline

<ul class="timeline">
<li><b>c. 2600-1900 BCE</b> Mature Harappan (Indus Valley) civilisation: planned cities such as Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Dholavira</li>
<li><b>c. 1500-500 BCE</b> Vedic period</li>
<li><b>c. 321-185 BCE</b> Maurya empire; Ashoka's edicts after the Kalinga war</li>
<li><b>c. 320-550 CE</b> Gupta period: advances in mathematics and astronomy (Aryabhata)</li>
<li><b>1206-1526</b> Delhi Sultanate</li>
<li><b>1526-1857</b> Mughal empire, from Babur's victory at Panipat</li>
<li><b>1857</b> The Revolt of 1857; Crown rule follows in 1858</li>
</ul>

## 2. The freedom movement in order

<div class="steps" data-title="From 1885 to 1947">
<div class="step"><b>1885</b> Indian National Congress founded. <b>1905</b> Partition of Bengal and the Swadeshi movement.</div>
<div class="step"><b>1919</b> Jallianwala Bagh massacre. <b>1920</b> Non-Cooperation Movement.</div>
<div class="step"><b>1930</b> Salt March to Dandi and Civil Disobedience. <b>1935</b> Government of India Act.</div>
<div class="step"><b>1942</b> Quit India Movement. <b>1947</b> Independence on 15 August.</div>

## 3. Economy terms

- **GDP:** the value of all final goods and services produced in a country in a year.
- **Inflation:** a general rise in prices; it lowers what a rupee can buy.
- **Repo rate:** the rate at which the RBI lends to banks. Raising it fights inflation; cutting it encourages borrowing.
- **GST:** a single indirect tax on the supply of goods and services, introduced on 1 July 2017.
- **Fiscal deficit:** how much more the government spends than it earns, other than by borrowing.

<div class="myth">

**"The RBI and the Finance Ministry are the same thing."** The RBI is India's central bank: it issues currency and sets interest rates. The Finance Ministry is part of the government: it prepares the budget and collects taxes.

</div>
