**Practice · Class 12 Physics · Chapter 8.** Read the chapter lesson, [Electromagnetic Waves](/learn/courses/ncert-class-12-physics/electromagnetic-waves/), first. Try each question before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Four relations carry this chapter: c = fλ, E₀ = cB₀, intensity I = ½ε₀cE₀² (and P/4πr² from a point source), and I<sub>d</sub> = ε₀ dΦ<sub>E</sub>/dt. For the spectrum, remember the order and one use of each band.</div>

## How to set out a numerical

1. Write the data in SI units (MHz to Hz, nm to m, cm² to m²).
2. Write the relation (c = fλ, B₀ = E₀/c, I = ½ε₀cE₀², E = hc/λ).
3. Substitute with units and give the answer with its unit.

## 1-mark questions

1. What is the direction of propagation of an EM wave in terms of the fields E and B?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Along the direction of E × B (perpendicular to both).</details>

2. Name the part of the electromagnetic spectrum used in a microwave oven.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Microwaves, about 2.45 GHz.</details>

3. What is the speed of EM waves in vacuum in terms of μ₀ and ε₀?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀) ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s.</details>

4. Which radiation is produced when fast electrons strike a metal target?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>X-rays.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Why is it necessary to add displacement current to Ampère’s law? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Between the plates of a charging capacitor no conduction current flows, so Ampère’s law gives different answers for a flat surface cut by the wire and for a surface passing through the gap. Adding I<sub>d</sub> = ε₀ dΦ<sub>E</sub>/dt makes the total current the same through every surface.</details>

6. State two properties of electromagnetic waves that show they are transverse and need no medium. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The electric and magnetic fields are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of travel (transverse, and the waves can be polarised). They travel through a vacuum, as sunlight does through space, so they need no material medium.</details>

7. A radio station broadcasts at 91.1 MHz. Find the wavelength. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>λ = c/f = 3 × 10⁸/(91.1 × 10⁶) = 3.29 m.</details>

8. The electric field of a plane EM wave in vacuum is `$E_y = 30\sin(2\times10^{7}x - 6\times10^{15}t)$` V/m (x in m, t in s). Find the wavelength, frequency and the amplitude of B. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

k = 2 × 10⁷ rad/m, so λ = 2π/k = 3.14 × 10⁻⁷ m = 314 nm (ultraviolet). ω = 6 × 10¹⁵ rad/s, so f = ω/2π = 9.55 × 10¹⁴ Hz. B₀ = E₀/c = 30/(3 × 10⁸) = 10⁻⁷ T = 100 nT. Check: ω/k = 3 × 10⁸ m/s.

</details>

9. Arrange in increasing order of frequency: X-rays, radio waves, visible light, microwaves, gamma rays, infrared, ultraviolet. Give one use of the lowest and the highest. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Radio waves &lt; microwaves &lt; infrared &lt; visible &lt; ultraviolet &lt; X-rays &lt; gamma rays. Lowest: radio waves for broadcasting. Highest: gamma rays for treating cancer.</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

10. A parallel-plate capacitor is being charged. Show that the displacement current between the plates equals the conduction current in the wires. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Let the plates have area A and charge Q. The field between them is `$E = Q/(\varepsilon_0 A)$`, so the electric flux is `$\Phi_E = EA = Q/\varepsilon_0$`. The displacement current is

`$I_d = \varepsilon_0\,\dfrac{d\Phi_E}{dt} = \varepsilon_0\,\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{dQ}{dt} = \dfrac{dQ}{dt}$`

and dQ/dt is exactly the conduction current I in the wires. So I<sub>d</sub> = I: the current is continuous round the circuit, and the Ampère–Maxwell law gives the same B for any surface.

</details>

11. A capacitor with circular plates of area 0.01 m² is charged so that the electric field between the plates changes at 3 × 10¹² V/m per second. Find the displacement current. *(5 marks, with the physics)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

`$I_d = \varepsilon_0 A\,dE/dt = 8.85\times10^{-12}\times0.01\times3\times10^{12} = 0.266$` A.

The same current flows in the connecting wires. The magnetic field between the plates, at a distance r from the axis inside the plates, is found from `$B\,(2\pi r) = \mu_0\varepsilon_0\,(dE/dt)\,\pi r^2$`, so `$B = \tfrac12\mu_0\varepsilon_0 r\,dE/dt$`.

</details>

12. Describe how an electromagnetic wave is produced, and give the relation between the amplitudes of E and B. Derive the speed from μ₀ and ε₀ numerically. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

An accelerated charge (for example, charges oscillating in an antenna) produces a changing electric field, which produces a changing magnetic field, which in turn produces a changing electric field; the pair propagates outward as a transverse wave with E ⟂ B ⟂ direction of travel. Their amplitudes are related by E₀/B₀ = c. Numerically

`$c = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\times10^{-7}\times8.85\times10^{-12}}} = 3.0\times10^{8}$` m/s.

</details>

## Case-based question

13. **Sunlight on a solar panel.** The intensity of sunlight at the Earth is about 1360 W/m². Assume it is a plane wave. Take c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F/m.

(a) Find the amplitude of the electric field.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

E₀ = √(2I/ε₀c) = √(2 × 1360/(8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 3 × 10⁸)) = √(1.02 × 10⁶) ≈ 1010 V/m.

</details>

(b) Find the amplitude of the magnetic field.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>B₀ = E₀/c = 1010/(3 × 10⁸) ≈ 3.4 × 10⁻⁶ T.</details>

(c) Find the radiation pressure on a perfectly absorbing surface.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>p = I/c = 1360/(3 × 10⁸) = 4.5 × 10⁻⁶ Pa.</details>

(d) The panel has an area of 2 m². What force does the sunlight exert on it?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>F = pA = 4.5 × 10⁻⁶ × 2 = 9 × 10⁻⁶ N: tiny, so it has no effect on the roof.</details>

## Assertion-reason questions

Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

14. **Assertion:** Electromagnetic waves can travel through a vacuum. **Reason:** A changing electric field produces a changing magnetic field and vice versa.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). The fields regenerate each other, so no material is needed.</details>

15. **Assertion:** Microwaves are used in radar. **Reason:** Microwaves have a longer wavelength than radio waves.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(C). A is true. R is false: microwaves have a <i>shorter</i> wavelength than radio waves, and that is what lets them form narrow beams.</details>

16. **Assertion:** A static charge does not produce an electromagnetic wave. **Reason:** Only accelerated charges radiate.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). A static charge has no changing field, so nothing is radiated.</details>
