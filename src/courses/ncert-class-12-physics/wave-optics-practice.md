**Practice · Class 12 Physics · Chapter 10.** Read the chapter lesson, [Wave Optics](/learn/courses/ncert-class-12-physics/wave-optics/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer. Board answers are marked in steps, so write the law or formula in symbols, then the numbers with units, then the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Almost every question in this chapter comes from three ideas: the path difference (Δ = nλ is bright, Δ = (n + ½)λ is dark), the fringe-width formula β = λD/d for two slits and the minimum condition a sin θ = nλ for one slit, and Malus’s law I = I₀cos²θ. Find the path difference first and the rest follows.</div>

## How to set out a numerical

1. Write what is given in SI units (nm to 10⁻⁹ m, mm to 10⁻³ m).
2. Write the formula in symbols: β = λD/d, y = nλD/d, a sin θ = nλ, I = I₀cos²θ.
3. Put in the numbers with units and work out the answer.
4. Check the size: fringe widths are usually fractions of a millimetre to a few mm.

## 1-mark questions

1. What is the shape of the wavefront of light from a distant star on reaching the Earth?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Plane. The star is so far away that a small part of its spherical wavefront looks flat.</details>

2. What happens to the frequency of light when it goes from air into glass?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It does not change. Only the speed and the wavelength change.</details>

3. State the condition for two sources to be coherent.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>They must have the same frequency and a constant phase difference with time.</details>

4. What happens to the central maximum of a single-slit pattern when the slit width is halved?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Its width doubles, because the width is 2λD/a.</details>

5. Which property of light does polarisation show?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>That light is a transverse wave.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

6. (2) State Huygens’ principle.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Every point on a wavefront is a source of secondary wavelets that spread in all directions with the speed of the wave. The new wavefront at a later time is the forward envelope (common tangent) of these wavelets.</details>

7. (2) In Young’s experiment the whole set-up is immersed in water of refractive index 4/3. How does the fringe width change?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In water the wavelength becomes λ' = λ/μ = 3λ/4. Since β = λD/d, the fringe width becomes `$\beta' = 3\beta/4$`. The fringes become narrower.

</details>

8. (2) Two coherent waves of equal intensity I₀ meet in phase. What is the resultant intensity? Does this violate conservation of energy?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The amplitudes add: 2a, so the intensity is 4I₀, not 2I₀. There is no violation: at the dark places the intensity is zero, and the average over the whole pattern is still 2I₀.

</details>

9. (3) A beam of unpolarised light of intensity 160 W/m² passes through two polaroids with axes at 30° to each other. Find the intensity of the emerging light.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

After the first polaroid: 160/2 = 80 W/m². Malus’s law: `$I = 80\cos^2 30° = 80\times\tfrac34 = 60$` W/m².

</details>

10. (3) In a double-slit experiment the slits are 0.5 mm apart and the screen is 1.2 m away. Light of wavelength 600 nm is used. Find (a) the fringe width and (b) the distance of the third dark fringe from the centre.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) `$\beta = \lambda D/d = 600\times10^{-9}\times1.2/(0.5\times10^{-3}) = 1.44\times10^{-3}$` m = 1.44 mm.

(b) The third dark fringe is at n = 2 in (n + ½)β: `$y = 2.5\beta = 3.6$` mm. (The first dark fringe is at 0.5β, the second at 1.5β, the third at 2.5β.)

</details>

11. (3) Light of wavelength 500 nm falls on a slit 0.2 mm wide. A screen is 2 m away. Find the width of the central bright fringe.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

`$2\lambda D/a = 2\times500\times10^{-9}\times2/(0.2\times10^{-3}) = 1\times10^{-2}$` m = 10 mm = 1 cm.

</details>

## 5-mark questions

12. (5) Using Huygens’ principle, prove the law of refraction at a plane surface. Draw the figure.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Draw the surface PP' with air (speed v₁) above and glass (speed v₂) below. A plane wavefront AB meets the surface at an angle i (A touches first). In the time t that B takes to reach the surface at C, BC = v₁t.

In the same time the secondary wavelet from A spreads in glass to a radius AE = v₂t. The tangent from C to this wavelet is the refracted front CE.

In right triangle ABC: `$\sin i = BC/AC = v_1t/AC$`.

In right triangle AEC (right angle at E): `$\sin r = AE/AC = v_2t/AC$`.

Dividing: `$\dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r} = \dfrac{v_1}{v_2} = n$`, a constant for the two media. This is Snell’s law. Also, because v₂ is smaller than v₁ for glass, r < i: the ray bends towards the normal.

</details>

13. (5) Derive the expression for the fringe width in Young’s double-slit experiment. State two ways of making the fringes wider.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Let the slits S₁, S₂ be a distance d apart and the screen a distance D away. For a point P at height y above the centre O, the path difference is `$\Delta = S_2P - S_1P$`. Draw S₁N perpendicular to S₂P; for D ≫ d, `$\Delta \approx d\sin\theta \approx d\tan\theta = dy/D$`.

Bright fringe: Δ = nλ, so `$y_n = n\lambda D/d$`. Dark fringe: Δ = (n + ½)λ, so `$y_n = (n+\tfrac12)\lambda D/d$`.

The distance between two successive bright (or dark) fringes is `$\beta = y_{n+1} - y_n = \dfrac{\lambda D}{d}$`.

To widen the fringes: use light of longer wavelength (red instead of blue), move the screen further away (larger D), or bring the slits closer (smaller d).

</details>

## Case-based question

**Reading a hair with a laser.** A student shines a red laser pointer (wavelength 650 nm) on a thin human hair stretched across the beam. A pattern of bright and dark spots appears on a wall 3.0 m away. She finds that the central bright band is 3.9 cm wide. (A hair acts like a single slit in the same way as an opening of the same width, as far as the dark fringes go.)

(a) Name the phenomenon that produces the pattern.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Diffraction of light by the hair.</details>

(b) Write the condition for the first dark fringe.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>`$a\sin\theta = \lambda$`, where a is the width of the hair (the obstacle acts like a slit of width a).</details>

(c) Find the thickness of the hair.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Central width `$= 2\lambda D/a$`, so `$a = 2\lambda D/w = 2\times650\times10^{-9}\times3.0/(3.9\times10^{-2}) = 1.0\times10^{-4}$` m = 0.1 mm.

</details>

(d) What happens to the pattern if she uses a blue laser (450 nm) instead?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The pattern shrinks, because the width of the central band is proportional to the wavelength. It becomes about 450/650 = 0.69 of its earlier size.</details>

## Assertion and reason

Choose: (A) both A and R are true and R explains A; (B) both true, R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

14. **Assertion:** Two ordinary bulbs behind two slits do not give interference fringes. **Reason:** The two bulbs are not coherent sources.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). The phase difference between independent bulbs changes randomly, so no stable pattern forms.</details>

15. **Assertion:** In the single-slit pattern, the central maximum is twice as wide as the other bright fringes. **Reason:** The first minima occur at a sin θ = ±λ, and the other minima are spaced λ/a apart in sin θ.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). The central band lies between ±λ/a (width 2λ/a), while the neighbouring bands are only λ/a wide.</details>

16. **Assertion:** Sound waves cannot be polarised. **Reason:** Sound waves are longitudinal.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). In a longitudinal wave the vibration is along the direction of travel, so there is no sideways direction to select.</details>

17. **Assertion:** The central fringe in Young’s experiment is always bright. **Reason:** The path difference at the centre is zero.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) for equal slits and a symmetrical set-up: both sources are equally far from O, so the waves arrive in phase.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Wavefront: surface of equal phase; rays are perpendicular to it. Huygens: every point is a source of wavelets; the new front is the forward envelope.
- Refraction: sin i / sin r = v₁/v₂ = n. Reflection: i = r. Frequency never changes; λₙ = λ/n.
- I = I₁ + I₂ + 2√(I₁I₂) cos φ. Equal waves: I = 4I₀cos²(φ/2). I_max/I_min = ((a₁ + a₂)/(a₁ − a₂))².
- Young: Δ = dy/D, β = λD/d, bright at nβ, dark at (n + ½)β. In a medium β' = β/μ.
- Single slit: minima at a sin θ = nλ; central maximum 2λD/a wide.
- Malus: I = I₀cos²θ; unpolarised light halves on the first polaroid.

</div>
