**Practice · Class 12 Physics · Chapter 9.** Read the chapter lesson, [Ray Optics and Optical Instruments](/learn/courses/ncert-class-12-physics/ray-optics-and-optical-instruments/), first. Draw a ray diagram for every question that has one before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Set up the sign convention before you calculate: pole or optical centre at the origin, light travelling left to right, object distance u negative. Then one formula (1/v + 1/u = 1/f for mirrors, 1/v − 1/u = 1/f for lenses) handles real and virtual images, convex and concave alike.</div>

## How to set out a numerical

1. Draw a rough diagram with the axis and the direction of the light.
2. Write u, v and f with their signs (concave mirror and concave lens: f negative for the mirror, convex lens f positive).
3. Put into the formula, solve for the unknown, then read the sign: v tells you the side (real or virtual) and m the size and orientation.
4. State the nature of the image.

## 1-mark questions

1. What is the relation between the focal length and the radius of curvature of a spherical mirror?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>f = R/2.</details>

2. Which colour of white light has the largest deviation in a prism?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Violet, since it has the largest refractive index.</details>

3. Write the SI unit of the power of a lens.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Dioptre (D), the reciprocal of the focal length in metres.</details>

4. State the condition for total internal reflection.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Light must go from a denser to a rarer medium and the angle of incidence must exceed the critical angle.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. A concave mirror has a focal length of 15 cm. An object is placed 10 cm in front of it. Find the image position and nature. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

u = −10 cm, f = −15 cm. 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = −1/15 + 1/10 = 1/30, so v = +30 cm: the image is **behind** the mirror (virtual). m = −v/u = −30/(−10) = +3: erect, three times bigger.

</details>

6. Why does a pool of water look shallower than it really is? Find the apparent depth of a 2.0 m deep tank filled with water (n = 4/3). *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Rays from the bottom bend away from the normal on leaving the water, so they seem to come from a higher point. Apparent depth = 2.0/(4/3) = 1.5 m.</details>

7. Find the critical angle for light going from glass (n = 1.5) to water (n = 1.33). *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>sin θ<sub>c</sub> = n₂/n₁ = 1.33/1.5 = 0.887, so θ<sub>c</sub> = 62.5°.</details>

8. A convex lens of focal length 10 cm and a concave lens of focal length 25 cm are in contact. Find the power and nature of the combination. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>P = 100/10 − 100/25 = 10 − 4 = +6 D. f = 1/6 m = 16.7 cm: a converging lens.</details>

9. State two advantages of a reflecting telescope over a refracting one. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(1) No chromatic aberration (a mirror does not disperse light). (2) A large mirror can be supported from behind and made much bigger, so it collects more light and gives better resolution.</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

10. Derive the mirror formula for a concave mirror, 1/v + 1/u = 1/f, using the figure of an object beyond the centre of curvature. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Let the object AB have height h and the real image A′B′ height h′. The ray AP reflects at the same angle, so ΔABP ~ ΔA′B′P and `$h'/h = v/u$`. The ray AM, parallel to the axis with MP = h, reflects through F; ΔMPF ~ ΔA′B′F gives `$h'/h = (v-f)/f$`. Equating, `$v/u = (v-f)/f$`, so `$uf = uv - fv$`. Dividing by uvf, `$1/v = 1/f - 1/u$`, i.e. `$1/f = 1/u + 1/v$`. With the New Cartesian convention u, v, f are negative and the formula has the same form.

</details>

11. Derive the lens maker’s formula for a thin lens of refractive index n in air. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

For one refracting surface (radius R, from n₁ to n₂): `$\dfrac{n_2}{v}-\dfrac{n_1}{u}=\dfrac{n_2-n_1}{R}$`. The first surface of the lens (radius R₁, air to glass) forms an image I₁ at v₁: `$\dfrac{n}{v_1}-\dfrac{1}{u}=\dfrac{n-1}{R_1}$`. I₁ is the object for the second surface (R₂, glass to air): `$\dfrac{1}{v}-\dfrac{n}{v_1}=\dfrac{1-n}{R_2}$`. Adding: `$\dfrac1v-\dfrac1u=(n-1)\left(\dfrac1{R_1}-\dfrac1{R_2}\right)$`. For an object at infinity v = f, so `$\dfrac1f=(n-1)\left(\dfrac1{R_1}-\dfrac1{R_2}\right)$`.

</details>

12. Derive the relation between n, the angle of the prism A and the angle of minimum deviation D<sub>m</sub>. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In the quadrilateral formed by the faces and the two normals, r₁ + r₂ = A. The deviation is δ = (i − r₁) + (e − r₂) = i + e − A. δ is least when the ray passes symmetrically: i = e, so r₁ = r₂ = A/2, and D<sub>m</sub> = 2i − A, i.e. i = (A + D<sub>m</sub>)/2. By Snell's law `$n = \dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r_1} = \dfrac{\sin\frac{A+D_m}{2}}{\sin\frac A2}$`.

</details>

## Case-based question

13. **A small telescope and a magnifier.** A student builds an astronomical telescope from a lens of focal length 80 cm (objective) and a lens of focal length 4 cm (eyepiece). She also uses the 4 cm lens as a magnifying glass to read small print, with the image at 25 cm.

(a) What is the magnification of the telescope in normal adjustment?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>m = f<sub>o</sub>/f<sub>e</sub> = 80/4 = 20.</details>

(b) How far apart are the lenses?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>L = f<sub>o</sub> + f<sub>e</sub> = 84 cm.</details>

(c) What is the magnification when the 4 cm lens is a magnifier with the image at 25 cm?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>m = 1 + D/f = 1 + 25/4 = 7.25.</details>

(d) Why is a large objective better for observing faint stars?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The light collected is proportional to the area of the objective, so a larger diameter gives a brighter image and also finer resolution.</details>

## Assertion-reason questions

Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

14. **Assertion:** A diamond sparkles. **Reason:** Its critical angle is small, so light inside undergoes total internal reflection many times.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). sin θ<sub>c</sub> = 1/2.42, θ<sub>c</sub> = 24.4°, so most rays inside strike a face beyond θ<sub>c</sub> and are totally reflected.</details>

15. **Assertion:** A convex mirror is used as a rear-view mirror. **Reason:** A convex mirror forms a real and magnified image.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(C). A is true (wide field of view). R is false: a convex mirror always forms a virtual, erect and diminished image.</details>

16. **Assertion:** A glass convex lens placed in a liquid of higher refractive index than glass behaves as a diverging lens. **Reason:** Then n<sub>lens</sub>/n<sub>medium</sub> &lt; 1, so (n − 1) is negative.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). The sign of f depends on (n<sub>l</sub>/n<sub>m</sub> − 1); when it is negative a biconvex lens has negative f.</details>
