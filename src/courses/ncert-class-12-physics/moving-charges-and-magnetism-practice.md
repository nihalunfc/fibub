<div class="bigidea">Almost every question in this chapter is one of five moves: F = qvB sin θ with r = mv/qB for a particle; B = μ₀I/2πr, μ₀NI/2a or μ₀nI for a source; F/L = μ₀I₁I₂/2πd for two wires; τ = NIAB sin θ for a coil; and shunt or series resistance for a meter. ## How to set out a numerical

1. Write what is given, converted to SI units (cm to m, µC to C, mA to A), and what you must find.
2. Name the law and write it in symbols. For directions, say which hand rule you used.
3. Substitute with units, then give the answer with its unit.
4. Check: does the direction agree with the right-hand rule? Is a radius or a resistance a sensible size?

## 1-mark questions

1. State the SI unit of the magnetic field and give its definition in terms of force.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The tesla (T). 1 T is the field in which a wire 1 m long carrying 1 A at right angles to the field feels a force of 1 N.</details>

2. What is the work done by a magnetic field on a moving charge?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Zero. The force is at right angles to the velocity at every instant.</details>

3. Write the formula for the field at the centre of a circular coil of N turns and radius a, carrying current I.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>B = μ₀NI/2a.</details>

4. Why does a moving-coil galvanometer have a soft iron core between curved pole pieces?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>To make the field radial, so that the coil's plane is always parallel to B and the deflection is proportional to the current (an evenly spaced scale). The core also strengthens the field.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. An electron moves at 3 × 10⁶ m/s at right angles to a field of 0.01 T. Find the radius of its path. (m = 9.1 × 10⁻³¹ kg, e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C) *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>r = mv/qB = 9.1×10⁻³¹ × 3×10⁶ ÷ (1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 0.01) = 1.7×10⁻³ m, about 1.7 mm.</details>

6. A charged particle enters a uniform magnetic field at 30° to it. Describe its path, and say what happens to its kinetic energy. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A helix with axis along the field: the component of velocity across B gives a circle, and the component along B carries it forward. The magnetic force does no work, so the kinetic energy stays constant.</details>

7. Show that the time period of a charged particle moving in a circle in a uniform magnetic field does not depend on its speed. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The magnetic force provides the centripetal force: qvB = mv²/r, so r = mv/qB. The time for one circle is T = 2πr/v = 2π(mv/qB)/v = 2πm/(qB). The speed v has cancelled, so T depends only on m, q and B.

</details>

8. Two long parallel wires carry 10 A and 20 A in the same direction, 5 cm apart. Find the force per metre and say whether it attracts or repels. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

F/L = μ₀I₁I₂/2πd = 2×10⁻⁷ × 10 × 20 ÷ 0.05 = 8×10⁻⁴ N per metre. Currents in the same direction attract.

</details>

9. A galvanometer of resistance 40 Ω gives full-scale deflection for 5 mA. How would you convert it into an ammeter of range 0 to 2 A? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Connect a shunt S in parallel. I_g G = (I − I_g) S, so S = 0.005 × 40 ÷ (2 − 0.005) = 0.2/1.995 = 0.100 Ω.

</details>

10. Why is a voltmeter's resistance made very high? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>It is connected in parallel across the part being measured. A high resistance means it draws almost no current, so it does not change the voltage it is measuring.</details>

## 5-mark questions

11. (a) State Ampère's circuital law. (b) Use it to find the magnetic field inside a long solenoid with n turns per unit length carrying current I. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) The line integral of B·dl round any closed path equals μ₀ times the total current passing through the surface bounded by the path: ∮B·dl = μ₀I_enc.

(b) Take a rectangle abcd with side ab of length h inside the solenoid, parallel to the axis, and side cd outside, where B is negligible for a long solenoid. On bc and da the field is perpendicular to dl (or zero), so they contribute nothing. Thus ∮B·dl = Bh. The rectangle encloses nh turns, so I_enc = nhI. Hence Bh = μ₀nhI, so B = μ₀nI, uniform inside.

</details>

12. Derive the magnetic field at a point on the axis of a circular current loop of radius a, a distance x from the centre. Deduce the field at the centre. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Take an element dl of the loop. Its distance from the point P is r = √(a² + x²), and dl is perpendicular to r, so dB = (μ₀/4π) I dl/r², perpendicular to r. Resolve dB into a component along the axis, dB sin α with sin α = a/r, and one across the axis. The across-axis components of opposite elements cancel. So B = ∫dB sin α = (μ₀I/4πr²)(a/r)(2πa) = μ₀Ia²/2(a² + x²)^{3/2}.

At the centre x = 0, so B = μ₀I/2a.

</details>

13. (a) Derive an expression for the torque on a rectangular current loop in a uniform magnetic field. (b) What is the torque when the plane of the loop is parallel to the field? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) Let the loop have sides a and b with N turns, and let the normal make an angle θ with B. The forces on the sides of length a (parallel to the axis of rotation) are F = NIaB, equal and opposite, at a perpendicular distance b sin θ apart. Torque τ = F × b sin θ = NIabB sin θ = NIAB sin θ, i.e. τ = m B sin θ with m = NIA. The forces on the other two sides are equal, opposite and along the same line, so they give no torque.

(b) With the plane parallel to the field, the normal is at 90° to B, so sin θ = 1 and the torque is the maximum, NIAB.

</details>

14. With a diagram in mind, explain the principle, construction and working of a moving-coil galvanometer, and explain how to make it a voltmeter of range V. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Principle: a current-carrying coil in a magnetic field feels a torque. Construction: a rectangular coil of many turns on a soft iron core, free to rotate between curved pole pieces of a strong magnet (a radial field), held by a hair spring with a pointer on a scale. Working: the torque NIAB is balanced by the spring's restoring torque kφ, so φ = (NAB/k)I: the deflection is proportional to the current.

To make a voltmeter, join a high resistance R in series: V = I_g(G + R) with I_g the full-scale current, so R = V/I_g − G.

</details>

## Case-based question

A school lab has a long solenoid of 500 turns on a tube 25 cm long, carrying 2 A. A small coil of 50 turns and radius 2 cm, carrying 0.5 A, is placed at the centre of the solenoid with the plane of the coil perpendicular to the axis. A proton is fired along the axis of the solenoid.

15. (a) Find the field inside the solenoid. (b) What is the force on the proton moving along the axis? (c) The proton is now fired at right angles to the axis at 2 × 10⁵ m/s. Find the radius of its path (m = 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg, e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C). (d) What torque acts on the small coil?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) n = 500/0.25 = 2000 turns per metre, so B = μ₀nI = 4π×10⁻⁷ × 2000 × 2 = 5.0×10⁻³ T = 5 mT.

(b) Zero. The proton moves along B, so sin θ = 0.

(c) r = mv/qB = 1.67×10⁻²⁷ × 2×10⁵ ÷ (1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 5.0×10⁻³) = 0.42 m, about 42 cm.

(d) The normal of the coil is along the axis, parallel to B, so θ = 0 and τ = NIAB sin θ = 0.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

16. **A:** A charged particle moving in a uniform magnetic field has a constant speed. **R:** The magnetic force is always perpendicular to the velocity.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A. With F ⟂ v the force does no work, so the kinetic energy and speed do not change.</details>

17. **A:** An ammeter is connected in parallel with the circuit element whose current is measured. **R:** An ammeter has a very low resistance.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false, R is true. An ammeter is connected in series. Its low resistance is exactly why it must be in series: so that it does not change the current.</details>

18. **A:** The magnetic field inside a long solenoid is uniform. **R:** The field inside a long solenoid depends only on the current and on the number of turns per unit length.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation: B = μ₀nI has no position or radius in it, so it is the same everywhere inside.</details>

- Torque on a loop: τ = NIAB sin θ = mB sin θ.
