**Practice · Class 12 Physics · Chapter 5.** Read the chapter lesson, [Magnetism and Matter](/learn/courses/ncert-class-12-physics/magnetism-and-matter/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">A magnet is a dipole of moment m. Three formulas cover almost every numerical: the field B = (μ₀/4π)·2m/r³ (axis) or m/r³ (equator), the torque τ = mB sin θ, and the energy U = −mB cos θ. For materials, the key is to tell dia, para and ferro apart by the sign and size of χ, and soft from hard by the hysteresis loop.</div>

## How to set out a numerical

1. Write m, B, r or θ with SI units (cm to m).
2. Say which point you are asked about (axial or equatorial) and write the formula in symbols with μ₀/4π = 10⁻⁷.
3. Substitute and give the answer with its unit and direction.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the SI unit of magnetic dipole moment.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Ampere metre squared (A m²), which is the same as J/T.</details>

2. Which pole of a freely suspended magnet points towards the geographic north?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Its north (north-seeking) pole.</details>

3. What is the net magnetic flux through a closed surface?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Zero (Gauss's law for magnetism).</details>

4. What sort of material is used for making the core of a transformer?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Soft iron (or silicon steel): high permeability and a narrow hysteresis loop.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. State two properties of magnetic field lines. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>They form closed loops (from N to S outside a magnet and from S to N inside), and two lines never cross. (Also: the tangent gives the direction of B; closer lines mean a stronger field.)</details>

6. A small magnet has a moment of 3 A m². Find the field on its axis at 60 cm. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>B = 10⁻⁷ × 2m/r³ = 10⁻⁷ × 6 ÷ 0.216 = 2.8×10⁻⁶ T = 2.8 µT.</details>

7. A magnet of moment 4 A m² is in a field of 0.5 T at 30° to it. Find the torque on it, and the energy released as it turns to lie along the field. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

τ = mB sin θ = 4 × 0.5 × sin 30° = 1 N m. Energy at 30°: U = −mB cos 30° = −1.73 J. Aligned: U = −mB = −2 J. Energy released as it turns = 2 − 1.73 = 0.27 J.

</details>

8. Distinguish between diamagnetic, paramagnetic and ferromagnetic substances using the susceptibility and one example each. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Diamagnetic: χ small and negative, weakly repelled (bismuth, copper). Paramagnetic: χ small and positive, weakly attracted, χ = C/T (aluminium, oxygen). Ferromagnetic: χ large and positive and dependent on H, strongly attracted, can be permanently magnetised (iron, nickel, cobalt).</details>

9. Why does a paramagnetic substance lose its magnetisation when it is heated? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Heat makes the atomic magnets move more violently, which knocks them out of line with the field. So fewer are aligned, M falls and χ falls as 1/T (Curie's law).</details>

10. Why is soft iron used for electromagnets and steel for permanent magnets? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>An electromagnet's core must gain and lose its magnetism quickly when the current is switched on and off: soft iron has low retentivity and low coercivity (and high permeability). A permanent magnet must keep its magnetism and resist demagnetising: steel has high retentivity and high coercivity.</details>

## 5-mark questions

11. (a) Show that a current loop behaves as a magnetic dipole and find its dipole moment. (b) Write the expression for the magnetic field on the axis of a short bar magnet. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) The field of a loop of radius a on its axis at a large distance x is B ≈ μ₀Ia²/2x³ = (μ₀/4π)(2 × Iπa²)/x³. This has exactly the form of the axial field of a bar magnet, (μ₀/4π)(2m/x³), with m = IA. So the loop is a dipole of moment m = IA (NIA for N turns), directed along the normal by the right-hand rule. (b) B_axial = (μ₀/4π)(2m/r³) along the direction of m, for r much larger than the magnet's length.

</details>

12. Derive the expression for the torque on a bar magnet in a uniform magnetic field and the potential energy of the magnet. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

The magnet has poles ±q_m a distance 2l apart, at angle θ to B. Each pole feels a force q_mB: equal and opposite, so no net force. They form a couple with the perpendicular distance between them 2l sin θ, so τ = q_mB × 2l sin θ = mB sin θ, with m = q_m·2l.

The work done in turning the magnet from θ₀ to θ is W = ∫mB sin θ dθ = mB(cos θ₀ − cos θ). Taking U = 0 at θ₀ = 90°, U(θ) = −mB cos θ.

</details>

13. (a) State Gauss's law for magnetism. (b) Explain why the net flux is zero in the case of a bar magnet. (c) Compare it with Gauss's law for electric fields. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) The net magnetic flux through any closed surface is zero: ∮B·dA = 0. (b) Magnetic field lines are closed loops: those that leave the surface near the north pole must enter it again, so the flux out equals the flux in. Cutting a magnet produces two magnets, never an isolated pole. (c) For the electric field, ∮E·dA = q_enc/ε₀, because electric field lines begin and end on charges, so there can be a net flux. The magnetic counterpart has no "magnetic charge" on the right-hand side because monopoles do not exist.

</details>

14. Draw (describe) a hysteresis loop. Define retentivity and coercivity, and state what the area of the loop represents. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Plot B (vertical) against H (horizontal). Starting from saturation, as H is reduced to zero, B falls to a non-zero value B_r on the vertical axis: the **retentivity**. H must be reversed to a value −H_c to bring B to zero: the **coercivity**. Carrying on to negative saturation and back gives a closed loop symmetrical about the origin. The area enclosed equals the energy lost as heat per unit volume per cycle of magnetisation.

</details>

## Case-based question

Anil keeps a small bar magnet (m = 1.5 A m²) on his desk. He hangs it in a uniform field of 0.2 T, and notices that when displaced slightly it oscillates with a period of 1.2 s. The compass needle on a shelf is at 30 cm from the magnet along its axis.

15. (a) Find the torque on the magnet when it is held at 60° to the field. (b) Find the moment of inertia of the magnet. (c) Find the field of the magnet at the compass on its axis. (d) Is the magnet's field at that point, on the axis, stronger or weaker than at an equal distance on the equator? By what factor?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) τ = mB sin θ = 1.5 × 0.2 × sin 60° = 0.26 N m.

(b) T = 2π√(J/mB), so J = mB T²/4π² = 1.5 × 0.2 × 1.44/39.48 = 0.011 kg m².

(c) B = 10⁻⁷ × 2m/r³ = 10⁻⁷ × 3 ÷ 0.027 = 1.1×10⁻⁵ T = 11 µT.

(d) Stronger on the axis, by a factor of 2 (the equatorial value is 5.6 µT, in the opposite direction).

</details>

## Assertion and reason

16. **A:** A bar magnet cut into two pieces gives two separate poles. **R:** Magnetic monopoles do not exist.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false, R is true. Cutting gives two complete magnets, each with a north and a south pole, because isolated poles do not exist.</details>

17. **A:** A paramagnetic substance has a weaker magnetisation at higher temperature. **R:** Heat disturbs the alignment of the atomic magnetic moments.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A (Curie's law, χ = C/T).</details>

18. **A:** The core of a transformer is made of soft iron. **R:** Soft iron has a high retentivity.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is true, R is false. Soft iron has low retentivity and coercivity, which gives a narrow loop and little energy loss per cycle.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- m = NIA (loop); axial B = 10⁻⁷·2m/r³; equatorial B = 10⁻⁷·m/r³ (opposite). Axial = 2 × equatorial.
- τ = mB sin θ, U = −mB cos θ; stable at 0°, unstable at 180°; T = 2π√(J/mB).
- ∮B·dA = 0: no monopoles; poles come in pairs.
- B = μ₀(H + M), M = χH, μr = 1 + χ. Dia: χ < 0, small. Para: χ > 0, small, ∝ 1/T. Ferro: χ large; Curie temperature.
- Hysteresis loop: retentivity, coercivity, area = energy loss. Soft iron: electromagnets and cores; steel: permanent magnets.

</div>
