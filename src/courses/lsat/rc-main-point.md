**Module 6 · about 14 minutes · read first: [6.1 Mapping a passage](/learn/courses/lsat/rc-passage-mapping/) and [2.2 Main conclusion](/learn/courses/lsat/main-conclusion/).** By the end you will match the main point and purpose to the author's position every time.

<div class="bigidea">The main point of a passage is the author's conclusion, just as in Logical Reasoning. If the author takes a side, the answer takes that side; if the author only describes, the answer only describes.</div>

## 1. From map to answer

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">Look at the last line of your map: usually the author's position.</div>
<div class="step">Say the main point in one sentence: "Streets can do more than one job; manage night markets, don't ban them."</div>
<div class="step">Check the verb of each purpose answer: does the author <i>argue</i>, <i>evaluate</i>, <i>describe</i>, <i>defend</i>? A passage that takes a side is not merely "describing".</div>
<div class="step">Eliminate: one-paragraph answers (too narrow), answers broader than the passage (too broad), and views the author rejects.</div>
</div>

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Main point</dt><dd>the author's central claim, which the whole passage supports</dd>
<dt>Primary purpose</dt><dd>what the author is doing with that claim: arguing for it, explaining it, comparing views</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>If a friend asked "what was that article saying?", your one-sentence answer is the main point.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<details class="try"><summary>Try it: a passage reports a common view, gives new evidence against it, and ends "It is time historians described her as what the letters show she was." Is the purpose "to describe a painter's life"?</summary>

No. The author argues that an accepted label is wrong and should change. "To challenge a widely held characterisation using new evidence" fits.

</details>

<div class="myth">

**"The main point is in the first paragraph."** On the LSAT the first paragraph is often the view the author goes on to reject.

</div>
