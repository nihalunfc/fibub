**Module 5 · about 14 minutes · read first: [5.1](/learn/courses/lsat/inference-must-be-true/).** By the end you will choose the answer the passage makes most likely, without overreaching.

<div class="bigidea">Same job as Must Be True, with a little more room: the right answer is the one the passage supports best. It is almost always modest and tied tightly to the text.</div>

## 1. The question stem

*"The statements above, if true, most strongly support which one of the following?"* or *"...provide the most support for..."*.

## 2. Support levels

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><b>Passage:</b> "In a survey of hospital nurses, those who worked mostly night shifts reported sleeping on average 1.5 hours less per day than those who worked days. Night-shift nurses also reported more headaches."</div>
<div class="step"><b>Strongly supported:</b> "Among the surveyed nurses, working mostly night shifts was associated with less sleep." It restates a finding modestly.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Weakly supported:</b> "Lack of sleep causes headaches in nurses." The passage shows two things together, not a cause.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Not supported:</b> "Hospitals should end night shifts." A recommendation the passage never makes.</div>
</div>

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Most strongly supported</dt><dd>the answer that would be hardest to deny given the passage</dd>
<dt>Hedged wording</dt><dd>"some", "at least one", "can", "is associated with" make answers easier to support</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Think of a jury instruction: pick the claim the evidence backs most firmly, not the one that tells the most exciting story.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<details class="try">
<summary>Try it: a full question</summary>

"When a café near the university added a quiet study room, its weekday afternoon sales rose by a third, while its weekend sales stayed the same. Most of the café's weekday afternoon customers are students."

Which one of the following is most strongly supported by the information above?

(A) The study room probably drew more weekday afternoon customers, many of them students.
(B) Every café near a university should add a study room.
(C) Students prefer quiet rooms to libraries.
(D) The café's weekend customers dislike the study room.
(E) The café's profits doubled.

**Answer: (A).** It is hedged ("probably", "many") and follows the passage closely. (B) generalises to every café. (C) brings in libraries. (D) is not supported by steady weekend sales. (E) is a different, bigger claim (profits, doubled).

</details>

<div class="myth">

**"If the answer is probably true in real life, pick it."** Only the passage counts. Real-world plausibility without support in the text is a classic trap.

</div>

## Practice set

<div class="keypoints">

- Pick the answer best supported by the passage; it need not be strictly proven.
- Prefer modest, hedged answers tied to the text.
- Correlation in the passage does not support causal answers.
- Eliminate recommendations, generalisations and new topics.
