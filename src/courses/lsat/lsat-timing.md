**Module 7 · about 15 minutes · read first: [1.1 How the LSAT works](/learn/courses/lsat/how-the-lsat-works/).** By the end you will have a pacing plan for each section and a rule for when to move on.

<div class="bigidea">Every LSAT question is worth the same. A hard question that eats three minutes costs you two easy ones. Pace is not about rushing; it is about spending time where it earns points.</div>

## 1. The numbers

| Section (current format) | Time | Questions | Per question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Logical Reasoning | 35 min | about 25 | about 84 s |
| Reading Comprehension | 35 min | about 27 (4 passages) | about 8½ min per passage |

From August 2027, sections get longer (LR 45 minutes for 32 questions, RC 52 minutes for 6 passages), but the time per question stays about the same.

## 2. Two passes in Logical Reasoning

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">First pass: answer every question you can in about a minute. If one passes two minutes, pick your best guess, mark it and move on.</div>
<div class="step">Check the clock at question 13: about 17 minutes should be gone.</div>
<div class="step">Second pass: return to marked questions with the time you saved.</div>
<div class="step">Last minute: make sure every question has an answer. Blank and wrong both score zero.</div>
</div>

## 3. Pacing Reading Comprehension

Give each passage about 3 minutes of reading and mapping and 5 to 6 minutes for its questions. If one passage is very hard, do it last.

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Pace</dt><dd>seconds per question, which you can measure on every timed practice</dd>
<dt>Opportunity cost</dt><dd>the easy questions you do not reach because a hard one took too long</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Pacing is a budget. Spending three minutes on one question is like spending a week's money on one meal.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<details class="try"><summary>Try it: at question 13 you have used 22 minutes. What now?</summary>

You are about 5 minutes behind. Switch to strict two-minute limits, guess and mark faster, and keep the last minute to fill in any blanks.

</details>

<div class="myth">

**"Skipping is giving up."** Skipping for now is strategy. Many students get marked questions right on the second look, with a fresh mind.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- About 84 seconds per LR question; about 8½ minutes per RC passage.
- Two passes: answer, mark and move on, then return.
- Check the clock at the halfway point.
- Never leave a blank.

</div>
