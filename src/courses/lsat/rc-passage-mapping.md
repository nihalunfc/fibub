**Module 6 · about 16 minutes · read first: [2.1 Argument anatomy](/learn/courses/lsat/argument-anatomy/).** By the end you will turn a 450-word passage into a four-line map you can answer questions from.

<div class="bigidea">An LSAT passage is an argument stretched over several paragraphs. Read it the way you read a Logical Reasoning argument: find the views, find the author's view, and note what each paragraph is for. Details can wait until a question asks for them.</div>

## 1. What to track

- **Voices:** whose claims are these? Older scholars, critics, supporters, the author.
- **The author's position:** often marked by words like "but", "however", "is incomplete", "overlooks".
- **Paragraph jobs:** background, a view, an objection, evidence, the author's answer.

## 2. Build a map

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">Paragraph 1 of the night-markets passage: planners saw streets as channels for movement; codes cleared anything in the way. Map: <b>old view: street = channel</b>.</div>
<div class="step">Paragraph 2: night markets challenge that; supporters list benefits. Map: <b>challenge + benefits</b>.</div>
<div class="step">Paragraph 3: two objections, practical and legal. Map: <b>critics: noise; legal "privatising"</b>.</div>
<div class="step">Paragraph 4: author weighs them; legal one weak, practical one calls for management; streets can do several jobs. Map: <b>AUTHOR: manage, don't ban; rewrite codes</b>.</div>
</div>

With that map you can answer the main point, the organisation and the attitude questions without rereading.

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Passage map</dt><dd>a few words per paragraph naming its job and any view in it</dd>
<dt>Viewpoint</dt><dd>a position held by someone in the passage; the author's is the one that matters most</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Lawyers read cases the same way: who argued what, what the court decided, and why. The LSAT is testing that habit.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<details class="try"><summary>Try it: a paragraph says "Historians have long described X as self-taught. This account is not exactly false, but it is badly incomplete." What goes on the map?</summary>

"Old view: self-taught. AUTHOR: incomplete." The second sentence is where the author steps in.

</details>

<div class="myth">

**"I must understand every sentence before moving on."** Stopping on hard sentences costs time you need for questions. Get the structure, then return to the exact lines a question points to.

</div>

**Try it:** Tap each paragraph to label its job, then press Check.

<div class="keypoints">

- Track voices, the author's position and each paragraph's job.
- Keep the map to a few words per paragraph.
- Turning words mark where the author steps in.
- Go back to the text for details only when a question asks.
