**Module 5 · about 14 minutes · read first: [3.3](/learn/courses/lsat/strengthen/).** By the end you will explain how two clashing facts can both be true.

<div class="bigidea">A paradox is two true facts that seem not to fit. The answer is a third fact that shows they fit after all, usually by revealing a difference the passage did not mention.</div>

## 1. The question stem

*"Which one of the following, if true, most helps to resolve the apparent discrepancy described above?"*, *"...explain..."*, *"...reconcile..."*

## 2. Name the clash first

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><b>Passage:</b> "After the town lowered the speed limit on Oak Road, the number of accidents there rose."</div>
<div class="step">Fact 1: slower limit. Fact 2: more accidents. The clash: slower should mean safer.</div>
<div class="step">What could make both true? Maybe many more cars now use Oak Road, or the lower limit was introduced at the same time as road works.</div>
<div class="step">An answer like "Oak Road became the main detour while a nearby highway was closed for the whole period" explains both facts: more traffic, more accidents, despite the lower limit.</div>
</div>

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Discrepancy</dt><dd>two facts that seem to conflict</dd>
<dt>Resolution</dt><dd>a new fact that shows how both can be true</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Paradoxes on the LSAT are never real contradictions. Something changed, or two groups differ, in a way the passage did not tell you. Find that hidden difference.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

## 3. Wrong answers

- **Explains only one fact.** "Lower limits make drivers more careful" deepens the puzzle.
- **Makes it worse.** Adds a reason the second fact should not have happened.
- **Irrelevant.** About a different road, time or group.

<details class="try">
<summary>Try it: a full question</summary>

"A supermarket chain cut the price of its own-brand bread by 20%, yet its total bread revenue rose."

Which one of the following, if true, most helps to resolve the apparent discrepancy?

(A) The cheaper bread drew so many new buyers that far more loaves were sold.
(B) The chain's competitors also sell bread.
(C) Bread is one of the chain's least profitable products.
(D) Some customers prefer more expensive bread.
(E) The chain also cut the price of milk.

**Answer: (A).** Lower price per loaf, but many more loaves: revenue can rise. (B) and (E) do not explain the rise. (C) is about profit, not revenue. (D) suggests people might buy less of the cheaper bread, which makes the puzzle worse.

</details>

<div class="myth">

**"The answer should show one of the facts is wrong."** Both facts are given as true. The answer explains them; it never disputes them.

<div class="keypoints">

- Name the two facts and why they seem to clash.
- The right answer is a new fact that lets both be true, often a hidden difference or change.
- Eliminate answers that explain only one side, deepen the puzzle, or are off topic.
