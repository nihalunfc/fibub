**Module 6 · about 15 minutes · read first: [5.1 Must be true](/learn/courses/lsat/inference-must-be-true/).** By the end you will confirm every detail answer in the text and judge inferences by their support.

<div class="bigidea">Detail questions reward going back to the passage; inference questions reward caution. In both, the right answer can be pointed to in the text.</div>

## 1. Details: go back

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">Question: "What do the fungi receive from trees?"</div>
<div class="step">Your map says paragraph 1 describes the exchange. Go there.</div>
<div class="step">The line: "The fungi ... receive sugars that trees produce through photosynthesis."</div>
<div class="step">Answer: sugars. "Water" and "phosphorus" appear nearby but go the other way: a reversal trap.</div>
</div>

## 2. Inferences: support, not possibility

An inference answer is something the author or passage would accept from what is written. If you need to add an outside fact, or if the answer is stronger than the passage ("always", "never", "deliberately"), it is wrong.

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Detail</dt><dd>stated in the passage, perhaps in different words</dd>
<dt>Inference</dt><dd>not stated but strongly supported by what is</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Scientists separate what a study showed from what people claim it showed. The forest passage is about exactly that gap.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<details class="try"><summary>Try it: the passage says it is "not always clear whether [carbon] travels through fungal threads or through the soil". Can you infer that the reviewers deny fungal networks exist?</summary>

No. The same paragraph says "The underground network is real". You can infer only that some carbon may move by other routes.

</details>

<div class="myth">

**"I remember it, so I don't need to check."** Memory blurs who did what. Ten seconds of checking saves the question.

</div>
