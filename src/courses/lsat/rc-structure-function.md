**Module 6 · about 14 minutes · read first: [2.3 The role a statement plays](/learn/courses/lsat/role-of-statement/).** By the end you will name the job of any sentence or paragraph in a passage.

<div class="bigidea">Function questions are Role questions stretched to passage size. Ask: what would the author's argument lose if this part were removed? That loss is its function.</div>

## 1. The method

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">Find the part the question names (a paragraph, an example, a word).</div>
<div class="step">Read the sentence before it: what claim is on the table?</div>
<div class="step">Ask what this part does for that claim: illustrates it, supports it, qualifies it, objects to it, or answers an objection.</div>
<div class="step">Example: "email" comes right after "Once that principle was stated, it applied to telephones". It shows the principle reaching a later technology.</div>
</div>

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Function</dt><dd>the job a part does in the author's argument</dd>
<dt>Illustrate</dt><dd>give an example of a general claim</dd>
<dt>Qualify</dt><dd>limit a claim ("slow to adapt, but...")</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Editors ask exactly this when they cut: if a paragraph does no job, it goes.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<details class="try"><summary>Try it: what is the function of the telephone paragraph in the precedent passage?</summary>

It gives an example of the problem the first paragraph sets up: new technology strains reasoning by precedent.

</details>

<div class="myth">

**"Function answers describe content."** "The author mentions email to discuss email privacy" restates content. Function answers say what the content does for the argument.
