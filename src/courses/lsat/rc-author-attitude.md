**Module 6 · about 14 minutes · read first: [6.2 Main point](/learn/courses/lsat/rc-main-point/).** By the end you will name the author's attitude in two words and back it with a phrase.

<div class="bigidea">Tone lives in small words: "badly incomplete", "less forceful than it first appears", "more serious". Collect them while you read, and the attitude answer will be the one that matches their strength.</div>

## 1. Strength matters

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">Collect the tone words: "not exactly false", "badly incomplete", "none of this diminishes", "it enlarges it".</div>
<div class="step">Toward the old label: critical, but conceding it is partly true.</div>
<div class="step">Toward the painter: admiring.</div>
<div class="step">Pick answers that match both direction (for or against) and strength (mild or strong).</div>
</div>

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Qualified</dt><dd>with conditions or limits: "generally right, but..."</dd>
<dt>Ambivalent</dt><dd>pulled both ways</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Lawyers choose words like these with care, because "the argument is weak" and "the argument is wrong" commit you to different things.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<details class="try"><summary>Try it: "The legal objection is less forceful than it first appears. The practical objection is more serious." Attitude toward the practical objection?</summary>

Takes it seriously, more than the legal one, but (from the rest of the passage) thinks it calls for management, not a ban: qualified acceptance.

</details>

<div class="myth">

**"Neutral is a safe guess."** LSAT authors usually take a position. Neutral is right only when the author truly only reports.

</div>
