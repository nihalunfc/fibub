**Module 6 · about 15 minutes · read first: [4.4 Point at issue](/learn/courses/lsat/point-at-issue/) and [6.1 Mapping](/learn/courses/lsat/rc-passage-mapping/).** By the end you will place two authors side by side and see where they meet.

<div class="bigidea">Each LSAT Reading Comprehension section has one pair of shorter passages on the same topic. Map each in one line, then compare: where do they agree, where do they disagree, and how does B respond to A?</div>

## 1. Compare two maps

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">Passage A: taken by force → default should be return; burden on museums. Map: <b>general rule: return</b>.</div>
<div class="step">Passage B: real histories are messy → judge each claim on its facts. Map: <b>no general rule; case by case</b>.</div>
<div class="step">Agree: museums should not simply keep everything.</div>
<div class="step">Disagree: whether a general presumption of return is right.</div>
</div>

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Relationship</dt><dd>how one passage bears on the other: supports, challenges, complicates, applies</dd>
<dt>Agreement</dt><dd>a point both passages support, even if they disagree on others</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Courts compare arguments this way: both sides often agree on the facts and differ on the rule.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<details class="try"><summary>Try it: does Passage B think every object should stay in museums?</summary>

No: "None of this means that museums should keep everything." That shared ground is a likely "both agree" answer.

</details>

<div class="myth">

**"The two passages always disagree."** They often share a lot. Questions about agreement are as common as questions about disagreement.

</div>
