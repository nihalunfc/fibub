**Module 4 · about 18 minutes · read first: [2.6](/learn/courses/lsat/common-flaws/).** By the end you will describe the error in an argument and match it to the precise abstract wording of the right answer. Flaw questions are among the most common on the LSAT.

<div class="bigidea">A flaw answer is a description of a mistake. Name the mistake in your own words first, then find the answer that describes exactly that mistake in this argument, not a mistake in general.</div>

## 1. The question stem

*"The reasoning in the argument is flawed because the argument..."*, *"...is most vulnerable to criticism on the grounds that it..."*, *"Which one of the following identifies a reasoning error in the argument?"*

## 2. Method

<div class="steps" data-fig="arg-map" data-p1="Every member of the winning debate team took Professor Ruiz's logic course." data-c="Taking Professor Ruiz's logic course is enough to make someone an excellent debater." data-a="Taking the course guarantees excellence (but the evidence only shows the winners took it).">
<div class="step" data-show="">"Every member of the winning debate team took Professor Ruiz's logic course. So taking that course is enough to make someone an excellent debater."</div>
<div class="step" data-show="p1">Evidence: all winners took the course (winner → course).</div>
<div class="step" data-show="p1 c">Claim: course → excellent debater. The arrow has been flipped.</div>
<div class="step" data-show="p1 c a">Name it: <b>it confuses a condition shared by the winners with one that guarantees winning</b> (necessary versus sufficient). Many students took the course and never won anything.</div>
</div>

1. Find premises and conclusion.
2. Ask: why might the premises be true and the conclusion still false? That scenario is the flaw.
3. Name it (use the catalogue in Lesson 2.6).
4. Match: the right answer describes your flaw <i>as it happens in this argument</i>.

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>"Presumes, without providing justification, that..."</dt><dd>the argument assumes what follows; check that it really does assume it</dd>
<dt>"Fails to consider the possibility that..."</dt><dd>the argument ignores a scenario; check that the scenario would hurt the argument</dd>
<dt>"Takes for granted"</dt><dd>same as "presumes"</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Every right flaw answer passes two checks: the argument really does this, and doing it really is a problem here.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<details class="try">
<summary>Try it: a full question</summary>

"Critics say the new stadium will cause traffic jams. But the critics include several people who live near the site and simply do not want a stadium next door. So their claim about traffic can be dismissed."

The argument's reasoning is flawed because it

(A) rejects a claim by attacking the motives of those who make it rather than the claim itself.
(B) relies on a sample that is too small.
(C) confuses a necessary condition with a sufficient one.
(D) assumes that what is true of a group is true of each member.
(E) uses the word "traffic" in two different senses.

**Answer: (A).** Whether the critics have a personal interest says nothing about whether the stadium will cause traffic jams. That is an attack on the person (ad hominem). The other answers name real flaws, but not flaws this argument commits.

</details>

<div class="myth">

**"If an answer names a real flaw, it could be right."** It must be the flaw <i>in this argument</i>. The four wrong answers usually describe real flaws that simply do not happen here.

</div>

## Practice set

Original questions in the LSAT's own five-option format, from warm-up to test level. Read the argument, predict the answer, then choose; every question has a full explanation of why the right answer is right and the others are not.

<div class="keypoints">

- Premises are true; the flaw is how they are used.
- Imagine the premises true and the conclusion false: that scenario reveals the flaw.
- Name it in your own words, then match the abstract answer.
- Check each answer: does the argument do this, and is it a problem here?
