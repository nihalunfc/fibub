**Module 1 · about 12 minutes · read first: nothing, start here.** By the end you will know exactly what is on the LSAT, how it is scored, and which version of the test you will sit.

<div class="bigidea">The LSAT does not test what you know about law. It tests how well you read and reason: whether you can see what an argument claims, what it takes for granted, and where it goes wrong. Those are skills, and skills can be trained.</div>

## 1. The test today (through June 2027)

Law schools in the USA and Canada use the LSAT, along with your grades and application, to decide on admission. LSAC, the organisation that writes it, describes the current test as four 35-minute multiple-choice sections:

| Section | Counts? | Questions |
|---|---|---|
| Logical Reasoning (LR) | yes | short arguments, one question each |
| Logical Reasoning (LR) | yes | short arguments, one question each |
| Reading Comprehension (RC) | yes | four passages, about 6 to 8 questions each |
| Variable section (LR or RC) | **no** | tries out new questions |

There is a 10-minute break after the second section. You cannot tell which section is unscored, so treat every section as real. In all, **50 Logical Reasoning and 27 Reading Comprehension questions** count. Logical Reasoning is about two thirds of your score, which is why most of this course is about it.

Since August 2026, most test takers sit the multiple-choice part at a test centre, on a computer (LSAC allows limited exceptions; check its website).

## 2. From August 2027: three sections

LSAC has announced that from the August 2027 test, the unscored questions will be mixed into the scored sections instead of having a section of their own:

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><b>Reading Comprehension:</b> 52 minutes, 6 passages, 40 questions (27 scored).</div>
<div class="step"><b>Break</b> of 10 minutes.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Logical Reasoning 1:</b> 45 minutes, 32 questions (25 scored).</div>
<div class="step"><b>Break</b> of 10 minutes.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Logical Reasoning 2:</b> 45 minutes, 32 questions (25 scored). The question types, the number of scored questions, the time per question and the 120 to 180 scale do not change.</div>
</div>

Everything this course teaches works for both versions. The only difference is pacing: longer sections need steadier stamina (Lesson 7.2).

## 3. Scores

Your number of correct answers (the "raw score") is converted to a scale from **120 to 180**. There is no penalty for wrong answers, so answer every question. Each test form has its own conversion, so the same raw score can give slightly different scaled scores on different dates. Most schools publish the middle 50% range of LSAT scores of the students they admit: use that to set your target.

## 4. LSAT Argumentative Writing

You also write an essay, taken separately online at home with remote supervision. You get a debatable issue and some perspectives on it: **15 minutes of prewriting analysis** (you may move on after 5) and **35 minutes to write** an argument for a position. It is not scored, but schools read it, and LSAC releases your score only once an essay is on file. Lesson 7.1 covers it.

<div class="myth">

**"I need to study law or memorise logic symbols."** No legal knowledge is needed or rewarded, and the passages are written for any educated reader. You need a handful of ideas (premise, conclusion, assumption, conditional) used quickly and carefully. This course teaches them from scratch.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Through June 2027: four 35-minute sections, of which two Logical Reasoning and one Reading Comprehension count; one is unscored.
- From August 2027: three sections (RC 52 min, LR 45 min, LR 45 min) with unscored questions mixed in; same question types and scale.
- Scores run from 120 to 180; no penalty for guessing.
- Logical Reasoning is about two thirds of the score; there are no Logic Games any more.
- Argumentative Writing is unscored but required: 15 minutes prewriting, 35 minutes writing.
