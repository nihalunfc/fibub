**Module 4 · about 16 minutes · read first: [2.3](/learn/courses/lsat/role-of-statement/).** By the end you will describe how any argument makes its case and match that description to the abstract answer choices.

<div class="bigidea">Method questions ask for a description of the argument's strategy, not its topic. Strip away the subject matter and describe the moves: "it gives an example that contradicts a general claim", "it shows two cases are alike".</div>

## 1. Common methods

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><b>Counterexample.</b> "Some say no film from that studio has made a profit. But its 2019 film did." One case refutes an "all" or "no".</div>
<div class="step"><b>Analogy.</b> "Requiring a licence to drive is sensible; flying a large drone is just as risky, so it should need a licence too."</div>
<div class="step"><b>Eliminating alternatives.</b> "The leak is not from the roof or the windows, so it must be from the pipes."</div>
<div class="step"><b>Unacceptable consequence.</b> "If we accept your rule, then no one could ever borrow a book, which is absurd." (Also called reductio.)</div>
<div class="step"><b>Questioning the evidence.</b> "The survey supporting your view only asked people already using the service."</div>
<div class="step"><b>General principle applied to a case.</b> "Public money should fund only public goods. This stadium benefits one private team. So it should not get public money."</div>
</div>

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Method</dt><dd>the strategy the argument uses, described without its topic</dd>
<dt>Abstract wording</dt><dd>"a general claim", "a particular case", "an analogous situation", "a consequence of the opponent's position"</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>If you could swap "drones" for "boats" and "licence" for "permit" and the answer still fits, you are describing the method, not the topic.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<details class="try">
<summary>Try it: a full question</summary>

"Dana: Our school should drop its uniform rule, because uniforms do not improve learning. Lee: Improving learning was never the point of the rule. It was adopted so students from poorer families would not feel singled out by their clothes, and it still does that."

Lee responds to Dana by

(A) arguing that Dana has misidentified the purpose of the rule.
(B) offering a counterexample to Dana's claim about learning.
(C) showing that Dana's argument leads to an absurd consequence.
(D) agreeing with Dana's conclusion for a different reason.
(E) questioning the evidence Dana gives about learning.

**Answer: (A).** Lee does not dispute whether uniforms improve learning; Lee says that was never the rule's purpose. (B) and (E) would challenge the learning claim, which Lee leaves alone. (D) is wrong: Lee disagrees with dropping the rule.

</details>

<div class="myth">

**"Most of the answer fits, so it's right."** A method answer must fit completely. If one clause describes a move the argument does not make (say, "cites an expert"), the whole answer is wrong.

</div>

## Practice set

Original questions in the LSAT's own five-option format, from warm-up to test level. Read the argument, predict the answer, then choose; every question has a full explanation of why the right answer is right and the others are not.

<div class="keypoints">

- Describe the argument's moves, without the topic.
- Know the common methods: counterexample, analogy, elimination, unacceptable consequence, questioning evidence, principle applied.
- Check every clause of an answer against the passage.
- Method answers are neutral: the argument may be good or flawed.
