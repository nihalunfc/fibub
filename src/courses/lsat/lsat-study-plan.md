**Module 1 · about 10 minutes · read first: [1.1](/learn/courses/lsat/how-the-lsat-works/).** By the end you will have a week-by-week plan and a review routine.

<div class="bigidea">The LSAT rewards a small set of reasoning habits used fast and without errors. Learn the habit, practise it untimed until it is accurate, then add the clock.</div>

## 1. The 12-week plan

About an hour a day, six days a week.

| Weeks | Focus | Lessons |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Starting point: take full practice test 1 (Lesson 7.3), timed | 1.1, 1.2 |
| 2 to 3 | How arguments work: premises, conclusions, conditional logic, quantifiers, flaws | 2.1 to 2.6 |
| 4 to 5 | The assumption family | 3.1 to 3.6 |
| 6 | Describing arguments | 4.1 to 4.4 |
| 7 | Drawing conclusions | 5.1 to 5.3 |
| 3 to 8 | One Reading Comprehension lesson or passage set every other day | 6.1 to 6.6 |
| 9 | Timing and writing | 7.1, 7.2 |
| 10 | Full practice test 2, timed, then a full review | 7.4 |
| 11 to 12 | Your weakest question types again, plus official LawHub practice tests | |

LSAC's LawHub has free official practice tests: use them in the last weeks, after the skills are in place.

**Try it:** Press Play to run the clock and set your own seconds per question.

## 2. Untimed, then timed

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><b>Learn:</b> read the lesson and follow every worked example to the end, including why each wrong answer is wrong.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Accurate:</b> do the lesson's practice untimed. Aim for nearly every answer right, and be able to say why.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Fast:</b> only then add a timer: about 1 minute 25 seconds per Logical Reasoning question.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Mixed:</b> finally, mix question types as the real test does, so you learn to recognise each type from its question stem.</div>
</div>

## 3. Blind review

After a timed set, before checking answers, go back through every question you were not sure of and decide again with no clock. Then check. If your blind-review score is much higher than your timed score, the problem is speed; if both are low, it is understanding: go back to the lesson.

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Timed score</dt><dd>what you got under real time</dd>
<dt>Blind-review score</dt><dd>what you got after rethinking, untimed, before seeing the answers</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>The gap between the two tells you whether to work on method or on speed, so you spend each study hour on the thing that will raise your score.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Doing thousands of questions is the way to improve."** Doing questions without careful review mostly repeats your mistakes. Twenty questions reviewed deeply beat a hundred rushed ones.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Start with a timed practice test, learn argument structure first, then the question families.
- Practise untimed until accurate, then timed, then mixed.
- Blind-review every uncertain question before checking answers.
- Book the test when timed practice scores sit steadily near your target.

</div>
