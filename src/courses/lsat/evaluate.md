**Module 3 · about 14 minutes · read first: [3.3](/learn/courses/lsat/strengthen/), [3.4](/learn/courses/lsat/weaken/).** By the end you will pick the question that matters most for judging an argument.

<div class="bigidea">To evaluate an argument, ask about its gap. The right answer is a question whose answer could go either way, and each way would change how good the argument looks.</div>

## 1. The question stem

*"The answer to which one of the following questions would be most useful in evaluating the argument?"* or *"...would it be most helpful to know..."*. The answers are questions or "whether..." clauses.

## 2. The variance test

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><b>Argument:</b> "Since the library started opening on Sundays, more books are borrowed each week. So Sunday opening increased borrowing."</div>
<div class="step"><b>Candidate:</b> "Did borrowing on the other six days stay the same?"</div>
<div class="step">Answer <b>yes</b>: the extra borrowing is on Sundays, so Sunday opening likely caused it. The argument gets <b>stronger</b>.</div>
<div class="step">Answer <b>no</b> (it rose on every day): something else, like a new collection, may have caused the rise. The argument gets <b>weaker</b>. One answer helps, the other hurts: this is the right choice.</div>
</div>

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Variance test</dt><dd>imagine two opposite answers to the question; a relevant question makes the argument better one way and worse the other</dd>
<dt>Irrelevant question</dt><dd>both answers leave the argument where it was</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Evaluate questions ask "what experiment would tell us if the author is right?" The useful experiment is one that could come out either way.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<details class="try">
<summary>Try it: a full question</summary>

"A company found that employees who work from home two days a week report higher job satisfaction. The company concludes that letting everyone work from home two days a week will raise job satisfaction across the company."

The answer to which one of the following would be most useful in evaluating the argument?

(A) Do the employees who already work from home have jobs that suit home working better than other jobs at the company?
(B) How many employees does the company have?
(C) Does the company plan to open a new office?
(D) Do managers also work from home?
(E) Is job satisfaction measured by a survey?

**Answer: (A).** If yes, the home workers may be happier because of their kind of job, and extending home working to everyone may not help (weaker). If no, the comparison is fair (stronger). The others leave the argument unchanged whichever way they are answered.

</details>

<div class="myth">

**"Pick the question I would most like to know the answer to."** Curiosity is not the test. Some interesting questions do not affect the argument at all. Use the variance test on each answer.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Evaluate questions ask which question would best show whether the argument works.
- Find the gap, then pick the question about it.
- Variance test: one answer should strengthen, the opposite answer weaken.
- Reject questions whose answers would not change the argument either way.
