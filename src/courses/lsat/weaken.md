**Module 3 · about 20 minutes · read first: [3.3](/learn/courses/lsat/strengthen/).** By the end you will find the fact that most damages an argument, and steer around the answers built to look damaging.

<div class="bigidea">Weaken is strengthen in reverse. Find the gap between evidence and claim, then pick the fact that widens it: an alternative cause, a reason the assumption fails, or a flaw in a comparison.</div>

## 1. The question stem

*"Which one of the following, if true, most seriously weakens the argument?"*, *"...casts the most doubt on..."*, *"...undermines..."*. Accept each answer as true; ask which one most reduces the support for the conclusion.

## 2. The three most common weakeners

<div class="steps" data-fig="arg-map" data-p1="Employees at firms that offer free gym membership take fewer sick days." data-c="Offering free gym membership would reduce sick days at our firm." data-a="Gym membership itself causes the difference, not the kind of firm or employee." data-x="Firms that offer gym membership mostly employ young office staff, who take fewer sick days at any firm." data-xs="-">
<div class="step" data-show="">"Employees at firms that offer free gym membership take fewer sick days. So offering free gym membership would reduce sick days at our firm."</div>
<div class="step" data-show="p1 c">Evidence: a correlation between gym perks and fewer sick days. Claim: the perk would cause fewer sick days here.</div>
<div class="step" data-show="p1 c a">Assumption: the gym membership is the cause, not something else about those firms.</div>
<div class="step" data-show="p1 c a x"><b>Alternative cause:</b> those firms employ younger office staff, who take fewer sick days anyway. That explains the correlation without the gym, so the argument is badly weakened.</div>
</div>

1. **Alternative cause** (as above).
2. **The assumption fails**: "Most employees given free membership never use it."
3. **Bad comparison**: the two groups or places differ in an important way ("our firm's employees work night shifts in a warehouse").

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Weaken</dt><dd>makes the conclusion less likely to follow from the evidence</dd>
<dt>Out of scope</dt><dd>an answer about a different group, time or issue than the conclusion</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>An argument is only as strong as its weakest link. A good weakener puts pressure on exactly the link the author did not defend.</dd>

## 3. Traps

- **Attacks a premise.** "Some of the sick-day data was estimated" questions the facts, which the LSAT treats as given. Usually wrong.
- **Out of scope.** About gym costs, or about people outside the comparison.
- **Wrong direction.** Strengthens instead (it happens under time pressure: reread the stem).
- **Too weak to matter.** "Some employees prefer swimming" barely touches the conclusion.

<details class="try">
<summary>Try it: a full question</summary>

"Since the town banned cars from the market square, shops on the square report 20% higher sales. The car ban therefore helped local shops."

Which one of the following, if true, most seriously weakens the argument?

(A) Some shoppers drive to the edge of the square and walk in.
(B) In the same period, a large office building opened beside the square, bringing hundreds of workers.
(C) Shops on the square sell mostly clothing and food.
(D) The town council voted for the ban by a narrow margin.
(E) Sales at shops on the square vary from month to month.

**Answer: (B).** The new office workers are an alternative cause for higher sales. (A) is consistent with the ban helping. (C) and (D) are irrelevant. (E) is too vague to explain a 20% rise.

</details>

<div class="myth">

**"The best weakener proves the conclusion false."** That is rarely available. Pick the answer that does the most damage to the support; it may only show the conclusion is less likely.

</div>
