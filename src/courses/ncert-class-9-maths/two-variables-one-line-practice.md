**Practice · Class 9 Maths · Chapter 13.** Read the lesson, [Two Variables, One Line](/learn/courses/ncert-class-9-maths/two-variables-one-line/), first. Try each question on paper before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Almost every question here comes down to one move: put the numbers into the equation. A point is on the line if the equation stays true.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Write the equation in the form ax + by = c.
2. To test a point, substitute and show both sides.
3. To graph, make a table of at least three (x, y) pairs, plot them, and draw the line with a ruler.
4. Label the axes and write the equation on the line.

## 1-mark questions

1. Is (1, 2) a solution of x + y = 3?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Yes: 1 + 2 = 3.</details>

2. Write x = 5 as an equation in two variables.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>x + 0·y = 5.</details>

3. Where does the line y = 0 lie on the plane?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>It is the x-axis.</details>

4. Find y if x = 2 in the equation 3x − y = 4.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>6 − y = 4, so y = 2.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Write four solutions of 2x + y = 7. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Take x = 0, 1, 2, 3: y = 7, 5, 3, 1. The solutions are (0, 7), (1, 5), (2, 3), (3, 1).</details>

6. Does (4, −1) lie on the line 2x + 3y = 5? Give the working. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>2(4) + 3(−1) = 8 − 3 = 5, equal to the right side. Yes, it lies on the line.</details>

7. The point (3, 2) is on the line kx + 4y = 17. Find k. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

3k + 4(2) = 17, so 3k = 9 and k = 3.

</details>

8. Find where the line 4x + 3y = 24 cuts the two axes. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Put y = 0: 4x = 24, x = 6, so it cuts the x-axis at (6, 0). Put x = 0: 3y = 24, y = 8, so it cuts the y-axis at (0, 8).

</details>

9. Draw the lines x = 4 and y = −3 on one plane. Where do they cross? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>x = 4 is the vertical line through 4 on the x-axis. y = −3 is the horizontal line through −3 on the y-axis. They cross at (4, −3).</details>

Fresh numbers every time. Try until you get three right in a row.

## 5-mark questions

10. Draw the graph of 3x + 2y = 12. From the graph, read the value of y when x = 2, and find the area of the triangle formed by the line and the two axes. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Table: x = 0, y = 6; x = 2, y = 3; x = 4, y = 0 (and x = 6, y = −3 as a check). Plot (0, 6), (2, 3), (4, 0) and join them. When x = 2, y = 3 (check: 6 + 6 = 12). The line cuts the axes at (4, 0) and (0, 6), so the triangle is right-angled at the origin with legs 4 and 6: area = ½ × 4 × 6 = 12 square units.

</details>

11. A taxi charges ₹ 40 fixed and ₹ 12 per km. Write the equation for the fare F in rupees for d km, find the fare for 9 km, and the distance for a fare of ₹ 220. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

F = 40 + 12d. For 9 km: F = 40 + 108 = ₹ 148. For ₹ 220: 220 = 40 + 12d, so 12d = 180 and d = 15 km. Points such as (0, 40), (1, 52), (2, 64) lie on a straight line.

</details>

## Case-based question

Anil sells notebooks at ₹ 20 each and pens at ₹ 10 each from a stall near his school in Pune. One day his total sales are ₹ 200. Let x be the number of notebooks and y the number of pens sold.

12. (a) Write an equation linking x and y. (b) Find the y-intercept and the x-intercept of its graph. (c) Is selling 6 notebooks and 8 pens possible? (d) Why are only some points of the line real answers?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) 20x + 10y = 200, or 2x + y = 20. (b) x = 0 gives y = 20, so the y-intercept is (0, 20). y = 0 gives x = 10, so the x-intercept is (10, 0). (c) 2(6) + 8 = 20, so yes. (d) You cannot sell a part of a notebook, so only the points with whole, non-negative coordinates count: (0, 20), (1, 18), …, (10, 0).

</details>

## Assertion and reason

13. **Assertion (A):** The equation x = 7 has a graph that is a vertical line. **Reason (R):** In x = 7 the value of y can be anything.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both are true and R explains A.</details>

14. **Assertion (A):** (2, 3) and (3, 2) are both solutions of x + y = 5. **Reason (R):** Both pairs add up to 5.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both are true and R explains A.</details>

15. **Assertion (A):** A linear equation in two variables has exactly one solution. **Reason (R):** You can choose x in many ways.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false: there are endless solutions. R is true, and it is the reason why A is false.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- A solution of `$ax + by = c$` is a pair (x, y) that makes it true; there are endless solutions.
- The graph is a straight line; two points draw it, a third checks it.
- x-intercept: put y = 0. y-intercept: put x = 0.
- x = k is vertical; y = k is horizontal.
- A point is on the line only if its coordinates satisfy the equation.

</div>
