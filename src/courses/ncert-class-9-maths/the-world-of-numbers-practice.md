**Practice · Class 9 Maths · Chapter 3.** Read the lesson, [The World of Numbers](/learn/courses/ncert-class-9-maths/the-world-of-numbers/), first. Try each question on paper before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Before you answer a “rational or irrational?” question, simplify first: √16 and √(9/4) are rational. For decimals ask whether the digits stop or repeat. For roots in a denominator reach for the conjugate.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Simplify the number as far as you can before you classify it.
2. For a recurring decimal write x = …, multiply by 10 or 100, and subtract.
3. For rationalising show the conjugate and the identity (a + b)(a − b) = a² − b².
4. For exponents write the root first, then the power.

## 1-mark questions

1. Is √25 rational or irrational?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Rational: √25 = 5.</details>

2. Write 5/8 as a decimal.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>0.625 (terminating).</details>

3. Is 0.1010010001… (a new zero each time) rational or irrational?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Irrational: it never ends and never repeats a block.</details>

4. Find the value of 16^(1/4).
<details><summary>Answer</summary>2, because 2⁴ = 16.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Write 0.3636… as a fraction in lowest terms. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Let x = 0.3636… Then 100x = 36.3636… and 99x = 36, so x = 36/99 = 4/11.

</details>

6. Find two irrational numbers between 2 and 3. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>√5 ≈ 2.236 and √7 ≈ 2.646 (since 4 < 5 < 7 < 9). Others: 2.101001000…</details>

7. Rationalise 6/√3. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>6/√3 × √3/√3 = 6√3/3 = 2√3.</details>

8. Simplify (√5 + √2)(√5 − √2) and say whether the result is rational. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Use (a + b)(a − b) = a² − b²: (√5)² − (√2)² = 5 − 2 = 3, a rational number.

</details>

9. Simplify 2^(1/3) × 2^(2/3) ÷ 2^(1/2). *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Add the first two exponents: 1/3 + 2/3 = 1, so 2¹ ÷ 2^(1/2) = 2^(1 − 1/2) = 2^(1/2) = √2.

</details>

Fresh numbers every time. Try until you get three right in a row.

## 5-mark questions

10. Rationalise 1/(3 + 2√2) and use √2 = 1.414 to find its value to three decimal places. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Multiply by the conjugate 3 − 2√2: the bottom is 3² − (2√2)² = 9 − 8 = 1, so the result is 3 − 2√2. With √2 = 1.414: 3 − 2.828 = 0.172. Check: 1/(3 + 2.828) = 1/5.828 = 0.1716.

</details>

11. (a) Show 0.9999… = 1. (b) Write 0.2̅7̅ as a fraction. (c) Is 0.12345678910111213… rational? Give a reason. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) x = 0.999…, 10x = 9.999…, so 9x = 9 and x = 1. (b) x = 0.2727…: 100x − x = 27, so x = 27/99 = 3/11. (c) It has no repeating block (the numbers grow in length), so it is irrational.

</details>

## Case-based question

A square tile has side 1 m. A carpenter wants to place a straight wooden strip along the diagonal of the tile and another along the diagonal of a square with side 2 m.

12. (a) What is the exact length of the first diagonal? (b) Is it rational? (c) Write the second diagonal in simplest form. (d) What is the ratio of the second diagonal to the first?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) √(1² + 1²) = √2 m. (b) No: √2 is irrational (about 1.414 m). (c) √(2² + 2²) = √8 = 2√2 m. (d) 2√2 : √2 = 2 : 1, a rational ratio even though both lengths are irrational.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

13. **Assertion (A):** The product of two irrational numbers is always irrational. **Reason (R):** √2 × √2 = 2.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false and R is true: R is a counter-example to A. √2 × √2 = 2 is rational.</details>

14. **Assertion (A):** 1/7 has a non-terminating recurring decimal. **Reason (R):** 7 has a prime factor other than 2 and 5.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true, and R explains A: a fraction in lowest terms terminates only when the denominator has no prime factors except 2 and 5.</details>

15. **Assertion (A):** 8^(2/3) = 4. **Reason (R):** a^(m/n) = (a^(1/n))^m.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true and R explains A: 8^(1/3) = 2 and 2² = 4.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Simplify before classifying; roots of perfect squares are rational.
- Fractions terminate when the lowest-terms denominator has only 2 and 5 as prime factors; otherwise they recur.
- A recurring decimal becomes p/q by multiplying by a power of 10 and subtracting.
- Rationalise by multiplying by the conjugate; (a + b)(a − b) = a² − b².
- a^(m/n) = (n-th root of a)^m, and the usual laws of exponents hold for rational powers.

</div>
