**Practice · Class 9 Maths · Chapter 12.** Read the lesson, [Quadrilaterals](/learn/courses/ncert-class-9-maths/quadrilaterals/), first. Try each question on paper before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Draw the shape, mark what is given, and ask: which two triangles can I use? Almost every quadrilateral question is a diagonal, two triangles and a property.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Draw a neat figure and mark equal sides, equal angles and parallel lines.
2. Write “Given” and “To prove” for proofs.
3. Give a reason for every step, such as “opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal” or “mid-point theorem”.
4. For numbers, write the property used, then substitute, then give units.

## 1-mark questions

1. How many degrees do the four angles of a quadrilateral add up to?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>360°.</details>

2. Name the quadrilateral whose diagonals are equal and bisect each other, but are not perpendicular.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A rectangle (that is not a square).</details>

3. The neighbouring angles of a parallelogram are x° and 2x°. Find x.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>x + 2x = 180, so x = 60.</details>

4. In triangle PQR, A and B are the mid-points of PQ and PR, and QR = 14 cm. Find AB.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>AB = ½ × 14 = 7 cm.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Three angles of a quadrilateral are 80°, 95° and 105°. Find the fourth. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>360° − (80° + 95° + 105°) = 360° − 280° = 80°.</details>

6. One angle of a parallelogram is 40° more than another angle next to it. Find all four angles. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Neighbouring angles add up to 180°: x + (x + 40) = 180, so x = 70. The angles are 70°, 110°, 70°, 110°.

</details>

7. The diagonals of a rectangle are 26 cm and one side is 10 cm. Find its area. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The other side = √(26² − 10²) = √576 = 24 cm. Area = 10 × 24 = 240 cm².

</details>

8. Can a quadrilateral with angles 100°, 80°, 100° and 80° (in order) be a parallelogram? Give a reason. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Yes. Both pairs of opposite angles are equal, which is a test for a parallelogram.</details>

9. Show that a quadrilateral whose diagonals bisect each other at right angles is a rhombus. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Diagonals that bisect each other make it a parallelogram. At O the diagonals meet at 90° with OA = OC and OB = OD, so triangles AOB and AOD have OA common, OB = OD and the right angle between them: they are copies, so AB = AD. A parallelogram with two neighbouring sides equal has all four sides equal: a rhombus.

</details>

Fresh numbers every time. Try until you get three right in a row.

## 5-mark questions

10. Prove that the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Let the diagonals of parallelogram ABCD meet at O. In triangles AOB and COD: AB = CD (opposite sides of a parallelogram), ∠OAB = ∠OCD (alternate angles, AB ∥ DC, transversal AC), and ∠OBA = ∠ODC (alternate angles, transversal BD). So the triangles are copies by ASA. Hence OA = OC and OB = OD, so the diagonals bisect each other.

</details>

11. In triangle ABC, D, E and F are the mid-points of AB, BC and CA. Prove that the triangle DEF has perimeter half that of ABC. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

By the mid-point theorem, DE = ½ AC (D and E are mid-points of AB and BC), EF = ½ AB (E, F are mid-points of BC and CA), and FD = ½ BC. Adding: DE + EF + FD = ½ (AC + AB + BC), which is half the perimeter of ABC.

</details>

## Case-based question

A school is laying out a flower bed in the shape of a quadrilateral PQRS, with the gardener’s string pulled tight along the two diagonals. The diagonals PR and QS cross at O. The gardener finds that PO = OR = 6 m and QO = OS = 8 m, and that the diagonals cross at right angles. Mud paths are to be dug along the four sides.

12. (a) What kind of quadrilateral is PQRS, and why? (b) Find the length of one side. (c) What is the total length of the four paths? (d) Find the area of the bed.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) The diagonals bisect each other, so it is a parallelogram. They also cross at right angles, so it is a rhombus. (b) A side is the hypotenuse of a triangle with sides 6 and 8: √(36 + 64) = 10 m. (c) 4 × 10 = 40 m. (d) Area = ½ × 12 × 16 = 96 m² (half the product of the diagonals).

</details>

## Assertion and reason

13. **Assertion (A):** Every rectangle is a parallelogram. **Reason (R):** In a rectangle both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both are true and R explains A.</details>

14. **Assertion (A):** A quadrilateral with a pair of equal opposite sides is always a parallelogram. **Reason (R):** In a parallelogram, opposite sides are equal.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false (an isosceles trapezium has a pair of equal opposite sides but is not a parallelogram). R is true. So A is false and R is true.</details>

15. **Assertion (A):** The line joining the mid-points of two sides of a triangle is half the third side. **Reason (R):** It is parallel to the third side.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both are true, but R does not explain A. The length is half and the line is parallel: these are two separate parts of the mid-point theorem, and being parallel does not by itself give half the length.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Angle sum of a quadrilateral: 360°.
- Parallelogram: opposite sides and angles equal, neighbouring angles add to 180°, diagonals bisect each other.
- Tests: opposite sides equal, opposite angles equal, diagonals bisect each other, or one pair of sides equal and parallel.
- Rectangle: equal diagonals. Rhombus: perpendicular diagonals. Square: both.
- Mid-point theorem: parallel to the third side, half as long.

</div>
