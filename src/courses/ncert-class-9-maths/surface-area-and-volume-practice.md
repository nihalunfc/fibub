**Practice · Class 9 Maths · Chapter 14.** Read the lesson, [Math of Space: Surface Area and Volume](/learn/courses/ncert-class-9-maths/surface-area-and-volume/), first. Try each question on paper before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Two questions come first: do I need the skin (surface area, square units) or the space (volume, cubic units)? And which solid is it? Write the formula, put in the radius (not the diameter), and finish with the right unit.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Draw the solid and mark r, h, l or the edges.
2. Write the formula before you put in numbers.
3. Use π = 22⁄7 when the radius is a multiple of 7, and π = 3.14 otherwise, unless the question says what to use.
4. Give square units for surface area and cubic units for volume.

## 1-mark questions

1. Write the formula for the volume of a cone.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>V = ⅓πr²h.</details>

2. Find the total surface area of a cube of edge 4 cm.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>6 × 4 × 4 = 96 cm².</details>

3. By what factor does the volume of a sphere grow if its radius is tripled?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>27 times (3³).</details>

4. How many litres are in 2 m³?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>2000 litres.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. A cuboid is 8 cm × 5 cm × 3 cm. Find its volume and its lateral surface area. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Volume = 8 × 5 × 3 = 120 cm³. Lateral surface area = 2 × 3 × (8 + 5) = 78 cm².</details>

6. A cylinder has radius 7 cm and height 20 cm. Find its curved surface area (π = 22⁄7). *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>2πrh = 2 × 22⁄7 × 7 × 20 = 880 cm².</details>

7. A conical heap of grain has base radius 6 m and height 8 m. Find the slant height and the area of canvas needed to cover the curved surface (π = 3.14). *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

l = √(36 + 64) = 10 m. Curved surface = πrl = 3.14 × 6 × 10 = 188.4 m².

</details>

8. Find the volume of a sphere of radius 21 cm (π = 22⁄7). *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

V = 4⁄3 × 22⁄7 × 21³ = 4⁄3 × 22⁄7 × 9261 = 38,808 cm³.

</details>

9. The radius of a cylinder is halved and its height is doubled. What happens to its volume? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

New volume = π (r⁄2)² (2h) = π r² h × (1⁄4 × 2) = half of the old volume. It is halved.

</details>

Fresh numbers every time. Try until you get three right in a row.

## 5-mark questions

10. A cylindrical tank with radius 1.4 m and height 3 m is full of water. (a) Find the volume in litres. (b) How long will it take a pump delivering 440 litres a minute to empty it? (π = 22⁄7) *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) V = 22⁄7 × 1.4 × 1.4 × 3 = 22 × 0.2 × 1.4 × 3 = 18.48 m³ = 18,480 litres. (b) Time = 18,480 ÷ 440 = 42 minutes.

</details>

11. A solid metal sphere of radius 6 cm is melted and recast into a cone of height 24 cm. Find the radius of the cone’s base. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Volume stays the same: 4⁄3 π × 6³ = ⅓ π r² × 24. The left side is 288π and the right side is 8πr². So r² = 36 and r = 6 cm.

</details>

## Case-based question

A sweet shop in Jaipur sells laddoos shaped like balls and kulfi in cone-shaped moulds. A laddoo has diameter 6 cm. A kulfi mould is a cone of radius 3 cm and height 12 cm. The shopkeeper also packs 36 laddoos into a cuboid box measuring 24 cm × 18 cm × 15 cm.

12. (a) Find the volume of one laddoo (π = 3.14). (b) Find the volume of the kulfi mould. (c) Find the volume of the box. (d) How much space in the box is not filled by laddoos?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) r = 3 cm: V = 4⁄3 × 3.14 × 27 = 113.04 cm³. (b) ⅓ × 3.14 × 9 × 12 = 113.04 cm³ (the same volume as a laddoo!). (c) 24 × 18 × 15 = 6480 cm³. (d) 36 laddoos = 36 × 113.04 = 4069.44 cm³. Empty space = 6480 − 4069.44 = 2410.56 cm³ (gaps between round balls are always left).

</details>

## Assertion and reason

13. **Assertion (A):** If the radius of a sphere is doubled, its surface area becomes 4 times. **Reason (R):** The surface area of a sphere is 4πr².
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both are true and R explains A: 4π(2r)² = 16πr² = 4 × 4πr².</details>

14. **Assertion (A):** A cone and a cylinder with the same base and height have equal volumes. **Reason (R):** Three cones of water fill the cylinder.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false: the cone has only one-third of the cylinder’s volume. R is true.</details>

15. **Assertion (A):** The total surface area of a capsule (a cylinder with a hemisphere at each end) is the sum of the total surface areas of its three parts. **Reason (R):** Each part is a separate solid.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false: the circles where the parts are joined are inside, and are not on the surface of the capsule. R is true but does not make A true.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Cuboid: V = lbh; TSA = 2(lb + bh + hl). Cube: V = a³; TSA = 6a².
- Cylinder: V = πr²h; curved 2πrh; total 2πr(r + h).
- Cone: l² = r² + h²; V = ⅓πr²h; curved πrl; total πr(r + l).
- Sphere: V = 4⁄3 πr³; S = 4πr². Hemisphere: V = ⅔πr³; curved 2πr²; total 3πr².
- Joined solids: add volumes; add only outside surfaces.
- Melting and recasting keeps the volume the same.

</div>
