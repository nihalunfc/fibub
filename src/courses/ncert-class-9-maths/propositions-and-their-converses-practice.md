**Practice · Class 9 Maths · Chapter 9.** Read the lesson, [Propositions and their Converses](/learn/courses/ncert-class-9-maths/propositions-and-their-converses/), first. Try each question on paper before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Split every statement into “If P, then Q”. For the converse, swap P and Q, then try to break it with an example. For a proof, give a reason for every step.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Write the statement as “If P, then Q” and name P and Q.
2. For a converse, swap P and Q. For a claim about every case, look for a counterexample.
3. For a proof, write numbered steps and next to each one the reason (“angles on a straight line”, “given”).
4. Finish by saying what you have proved.

## 1-mark questions

1. Is “Is 15 divisible by 3?” a statement? Why?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>No. It is a question, so it is neither true nor false.</details>

2. Write the converse of “If a number ends in 0, then it is divisible by 10.”
<details><summary>Answer</summary>If a number is divisible by 10, then it ends in 0. (This one is true too.)</details>

3. Give a counterexample to “the sum of two prime numbers is even”.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>2 + 3 = 5, which is odd.</details>

4. Two lines cross and one angle is 48°. Write the angle vertically opposite to it.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>48°, because vertically opposite angles are equal.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Is “x + 4 = 10” a statement? What would make it one? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>On its own it is not: it is true for x = 6 and false for other x. It becomes a statement when x is given, for example “If x = 6, then x + 4 = 10” or “x + 4 = 10 for x = 7” (false).</details>

6. Write the hypothesis and the conclusion: “If a triangle is equilateral, then all its angles are 60°.” *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Hypothesis: a triangle is equilateral. Conclusion: all its angles are 60°.</details>

7. Write the converse of “If a quadrilateral is a rhombus, then its four sides are equal.” Is it true? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Converse: if a quadrilateral has four equal sides, then it is a rhombus. It is true: a quadrilateral with four equal sides is, by definition, a rhombus.</details>

8. Show that the converse of “If a number is a multiple of 4, then it is even” is false. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The converse is “If a number is even, then it is a multiple of 4”. Counterexample: 6 is even but 6 ÷ 4 leaves a remainder 2.</details>

9. Two lines cross. One angle is (3x + 10)° and the vertically opposite angle is (5x − 20)°. Find x and the angles. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Vertically opposite angles are equal: 3x + 10 = 5x − 20, so 30 = 2x and x = 15. Each angle is 3(15) + 10 = 55°. The other two angles are 180° − 55° = 125°.

</details>

10. In triangle PQR, PQ = PR and angle P = 80°. Find angles Q and R, giving reasons. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

PQ = PR, so the angles opposite them are equal: angle Q = angle R. The angles add to 180°, so Q + R = 100° and each is 50°.

</details>

## 5-mark questions

11. Prove that vertically opposite angles are equal. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Let two lines AB and CD cross at O. Name the angles: AOC = p, COB = q, BOD = r. (1) p + q = 180°, since AOB is a straight line. (2) q + r = 180°, since COD is a straight line. From (1) and (2), p + q = q + r, so p = r. In the same way, q equals the angle vertically opposite to it. Hence vertically opposite angles are equal.

</details>

12. Prove, by drawing a line through one vertex parallel to the opposite side, that the angles of a triangle add up to 180°. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In triangle ABC, draw a line PQ through A parallel to BC, with P on the side of B. Then angle PAB = angle ABC (alternate angles, PQ parallel to BC with AB the transversal), and angle QAC = angle ACB (alternate angles with AC the transversal). The angles PAB, BAC and CAQ together make the straight angle PAQ = 180°. So angle B + angle A + angle C = 180°.

</details>

13. Prove by contradiction: if n² is even, then n is even. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Suppose n² is even but n is odd. Then n = 2k + 1 for some whole number k, and n² = 4k² + 4k + 1 = 2(2k² + 2k) + 1, which is odd. This contradicts “n² is even”. So the assumption was wrong, and n is even.

</details>

## Case-based question

Ravi says: “I checked n² + n + 41 for n = 1, 2, 3, ..., 10 and got prime numbers every time, so it is always prime.” His sister Tara says that is not proof.

14. (a) Is Ravi’s conclusion proved? (b) Show that Tara is right by finding a value of n that fails. (c) What would a proof of the claim need to do? (d) Write one sentence on what a single counterexample shows.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) No. Ten examples do not cover all whole numbers. (b) n = 40: 40² + 40 + 41 = 1681 = 41 × 41, which is not prime. (c) A proof must show the claim is true for every n using an argument, not a list of cases. (d) One counterexample proves that the claim is false.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

15. **Assertion (A):** The converse of a true statement is always true. **Reason (R):** The converse of “If P then Q” is “If Q then P”.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false (for example “a square is a rectangle” is true but “a rectangle is a square” is false). R is true, as a definition. So A is false and R is true.</details>

16. **Assertion (A):** In a triangle, angles opposite equal sides are equal. **Reason (R):** If two sides are equal, drawing the line from the apex to the midpoint of the base makes two triangles that match exactly.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true and R is the reason for A: the two matching triangles have equal corresponding angles.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- A statement is true or false; questions, commands and opinions are not statements.
- “If P then Q” is false only when P is true and Q is false; the converse swaps P and Q and may be false.
- One counterexample disproves a claim; examples alone never prove it.
- Proofs give a reason for every step; proof by contradiction assumes the opposite and reaches an impossibility.
- Vertically opposite angles are equal; the angles of a triangle add to 180°; base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.

</div>
