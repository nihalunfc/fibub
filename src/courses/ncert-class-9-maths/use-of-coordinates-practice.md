**Practice · Class 9 Maths · Chapter 1.** Read the lesson, [Orienting Yourself: The Use of Coordinates](/learn/courses/ncert-class-9-maths/use-of-coordinates/), first. Try each question on paper before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Three habits win marks here: write the pair as (x, y) in that order, check the signs before naming a quadrant, and in the distance formula square the differences so the signs disappear.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Write the two points with names, A(x₁, y₁) and B(x₂, y₂).
2. Write the formula, then substitute with brackets round negative numbers.
3. State the unit if there is one (units, blocks, metres).
4. Where you can, sketch the right triangle: it catches slips.

## 1-mark questions

1. In which quadrant is (−7, 3)?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Quadrant II (x negative, y positive).</details>

2. Write the coordinates of the origin.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(0, 0).</details>

3. What is the y-coordinate of every point on the x-axis?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>0.</details>

4. Find the distance between (2, 9) and (2, 3).
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The x-coordinates match, so d = |9 − 3| = 6.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Plot the points A(3, 2), B(−3, 2) and C(−3, −2). Find the fourth corner D that makes ABCD a rectangle. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A and B share y = 2, B and C share x = −3, so D must have x = 3 and y = −2: D(3, −2).</details>

6. Find the distance between (−5, 4) and (3, 4). *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The y-coordinates are equal, so d = |3 − (−5)| = 8 units.</details>

7. Find the distance between P(1, −2) and Q(5, 1). *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Across: 5 − 1 = 4. Up: 1 − (−2) = 3. d = √(4² + 3²) = √25 = 5 units.

</details>

8. Show that the points A(0, 0), B(4, 0) and C(2, 2√3) are the corners of an equilateral triangle. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

AB = 4. AC = √(2² + (2√3)²) = √(4 + 12) = 4. BC = √((2 − 4)² + (2√3)²) = √(4 + 12) = 4. All three sides are equal, so the triangle is equilateral.

</details>

Fresh numbers every time. Try until you get three right in a row.

## 5-mark questions

9. The points A(1, 1), B(7, 1) and C(7, 9) are three corners of a rectangle ABCD. Find D, the lengths of the sides and the diagonals, and the perimeter. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

AB is horizontal (y = 1), BC is vertical (x = 7), so D = (1, 9). AB = 6 and BC = 8. The diagonal AC = √(6² + 8²) = 10, and BD = √((1 − 7)² + (9 − 1)²) = 10: equal, as in any rectangle. Perimeter = 2(6 + 8) = 28 units.

</details>

10. Three vertices of a parallelogram ABCD, in order, are A(−2, −1), B(3, −1) and C(5, 3). Find D, check that the diagonals share a mid-point, and find the length of the diagonal AC. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Mid-point of AC = ((−2 + 5)/2, (−1 + 3)/2) = (1.5, 1). If D = (p, q), the mid-point of BD is ((3 + p)/2, (−1 + q)/2). Equate: 3 + p = 3 gives p = 0, and −1 + q = 2 gives q = 3. So D = (0, 3). Check: BD mid-point = (1.5, 1), the same. AC = √(7² + 4²) = √65 ≈ 8.06 units.

</details>

## Case-based question

Meera lives in a town laid out in a grid of streets, one block being 100 m. Her school is at S(2, 1), the library at L(2, 7), the playground at P(8, 7) and her home at H(−1, 1), all in blocks from the bus stand at the origin.

11. (a) Which two places are on the same horizontal street? (b) How far is the library from the school? (c) How many blocks does Meera walk from home to the playground along the streets? (d) How far in a straight line is the school from the playground?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Library and playground share y = 7. (b) The x-coordinates are equal: |7 − 1| = 6 blocks = 600 m. (c) Across |8 − (−1)| = 9 and up |7 − 1| = 6, so 15 blocks. (d) SP = √((8 − 2)² + (7 − 1)²) = √72 ≈ 8.49 blocks, about 849 m.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

12. **Assertion (A):** The point (−3, 0) lies in the second quadrant. **Reason (R):** The x-coordinate of a point in the second quadrant is negative.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false: (−3, 0) lies on the x-axis, which belongs to no quadrant. R is true. So A is false and R is true.</details>

13. **Assertion (A):** The distance between (a, b) and (c, d) is the same as between (c, d) and (a, b). **Reason (R):** In the distance formula the differences are squared.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both are true and R explains A: swapping the points changes the sign of each difference, but squaring removes the sign.</details>

14. **Assertion (A):** The points (2, 3) and (3, 2) are the same point. **Reason (R):** Both pairs use the same two numbers.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false: the points are different, because order matters (the first number is x). R is true as a statement, but it does not make the points the same. So A is false and R is true.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Quadrants: I (+, +), II (−, +), III (−, −), IV (+, −); points on an axis are in no quadrant.
- Parallel to an axis: subtract the changing coordinate and take the positive value.
- Any two points: d = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²).
- Mid-point: the average of the x-values and the average of the y-values.
- Mirror in the x-axis flips y, in the y-axis flips x, in the origin flips both.

</div>
