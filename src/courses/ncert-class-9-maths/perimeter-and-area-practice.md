**Practice · Class 9 Maths · Chapter 6.** Read the lesson, [Measuring Space: Perimeter and Area](/learn/courses/ncert-class-9-maths/perimeter-and-area/), first. Try each question on paper before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Before you start, ask: is this about the edge (perimeter, a length) or the surface (area, square units)? Then write the formula, substitute with units, and check the answer is sensible.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Draw the shape and label every given length.
2. Write the formula, for example area = ½ × base × height, or s = (a + b + c) ÷ 2.
3. Substitute step by step. For Heron’s formula, find s, then s − a, s − b, s − c.
4. Give the unit: m for perimeter, m² for area, and ₹ for cost.

## 1-mark questions

1. Write the formula for the area of a triangle with base b and height h.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Area = ½ × b × h.</details>

2. Find the perimeter of a square of side 12 m.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>4 × 12 = 48 m.</details>

3. What does s stand for in Heron’s formula?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Half the perimeter: s = (a + b + c) ÷ 2.</details>

4. In which unit is area measured: m or m²?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>m² (square metres).</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Find the area of a triangle with base 18 cm and height 7 cm. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>½ × 18 × 7 = 63 cm².</details>

6. The diagonals of a rhombus are 14 cm and 48 cm. Find its area and its side. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Area = ½ × 14 × 48 = 336 cm². The half-diagonals are 7 and 24 and they meet at a right angle, so the side = √(7² + 24²) = √625 = 25 cm.

</details>

7. Can a triangle have sides 5 cm, 7 cm and 13 cm? Give a reason. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>No. 5 + 7 = 12, which is less than 13, so the two short sides cannot meet across the long side.</details>

8. Find the area of a triangle with sides 9 m, 10 m and 17 m. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

s = (9 + 10 + 17) ÷ 2 = 18. Area = √(18 × 9 × 8 × 1) = √1296 = 36 m².

</details>

9. A rectangular plot has area 150 m² and breadth 6 m. How much fencing is needed to go all round it? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Length = 150 ÷ 6 = 25 m. Perimeter = 2 × (25 + 6) = 62 m of fencing.

</details>

Fresh numbers every time. Try until you get three right in a row.

## 5-mark questions

10. A triangular field has sides 20 m, 21 m and 29 m. (a) Show that it is a right triangle. (b) Find its area in two ways. (c) Find the height on the longest side. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) 20² + 21² = 400 + 441 = 841 = 29², so it is a right triangle. (b) Right-triangle way: ½ × 20 × 21 = 210 m². Heron: s = 35, area = √(35 × 15 × 14 × 6) = √44100 = 210 m². (c) ½ × 29 × h = 210, so h = 420 ÷ 29 ≈ 14.5 m.

</details>

11. A four-sided field ABCD has AB = 9 m, BC = 12 m, CD = 8 m and DA = 17 m. A rope along the diagonal AC measures 15 m. Find the area of the field and the cost of levelling it at ₹ 30 per m². *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Triangle ABC has sides 9, 12, 15: s = 18, area = √(18 × 9 × 6 × 3) = √2916 = 54 m² (it is a right triangle: ½ × 9 × 12 = 54). Triangle ACD has sides 15, 8, 17: s = 20, area = √(20 × 5 × 12 × 3) = √3600 = 60 m². Field = 54 + 60 = 114 m². Cost = 114 × 30 = ₹ 3,420.

</details>

## Case-based question

Ravi’s family has a plot in a village near Thrissur. The plot is a rectangle 30 m by 16 m. They want to put a fence round it with 3 rows of wire, and then cover the ground with grass turf. Wire costs ₹ 12 per metre. Turf costs ₹ 25 per square metre. A neighbour says a square plot with the same fence would be a better use of the wire.

12. (a) Find the perimeter and the area of the plot. (b) What does the wire cost for 3 rows? (c) What does the turf cost? (d) What side would a square plot with the same perimeter have, and what would its area be?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Perimeter = 2 × (30 + 16) = 92 m. Area = 30 × 16 = 480 m². (b) 3 rows: 3 × 92 = 276 m of wire, cost = 276 × 12 = ₹ 3,312. (c) Turf = 480 × 25 = ₹ 12,000. (d) A square with perimeter 92 m has side 92 ÷ 4 = 23 m and area 23 × 23 = 529 m², which is 49 m² more than the rectangle. So the neighbour is right.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

13. **Assertion (A):** A triangle with sides 4 m, 5 m and 11 m has an area given by Heron’s formula. **Reason (R):** Heron’s formula works for any three lengths.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both statements are false: 4 + 5 = 9 is less than 11, so there is no triangle, and the number under the square root would be negative (s = 10, s − c = −1).</details>

14. **Assertion (A):** Two rectangles with the same perimeter always have the same area. **Reason (R):** Area = length × breadth.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false: take 8 m by 4 m (perimeter 24 m, area 32 m²) and 6 m by 6 m (perimeter 24 m, area 36 m²). The areas differ. R is true but does not make the perimeters decide the area. So A is false and R is true.</details>

15. **Assertion (A):** A parallelogram and a rectangle on the same base and between the same parallel lines have equal areas. **Reason (R):** Both have area = base × height with the same height.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both are true and R explains A.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Triangle `$\tfrac{1}{2}bh$`; parallelogram `$bh$`; rhombus `$\tfrac{1}{2}d_1d_2$`; trapezium `$\tfrac{1}{2}(a+b)h$`.
- Heron: `$s = \tfrac{a+b+c}{2}$`, area `$= \sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}$`; the sum of any two sides must exceed the third.
- Odd field: split into triangles by a diagonal, add the areas.
- Fencing is perimeter; levelling or turf is area.
- Same perimeter, different area: the square wins among rectangles.

</div>
