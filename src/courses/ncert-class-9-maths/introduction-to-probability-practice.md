**Practice · Class 9 Maths · Chapter 7.** Read the lesson, [The Mathematics of Maybe: Introduction to Probability](/learn/courses/ncert-class-9-maths/introduction-to-probability/), first. Try each question on paper before you open the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Two habits win marks: list or count all the equally likely outcomes before you divide, and check that your answer lies between 0 and 1. For experimental probability, divide by the number of trials, not by the number of favourable cases.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Name the experiment and write its sample space (or the total number of trials).
2. State the event and its favourable outcomes.
3. Write P = favourable ÷ total, and simplify.
4. Check that 0 ≤ P ≤ 1.

## 1-mark questions

1. What is the probability of an impossible event?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>0.</details>

2. A fair coin is tossed once. Write the probability of a tail.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>½.</details>

3. A die is rolled once. What is P(5)?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>1/6.</details>

4. If P(rain) = 0.4, what is P(no rain)?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>1 − 0.4 = 0.6.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. A bag has 7 red and 3 blue marbles. One is drawn at random. Find P(red) and P(blue). *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Total 10. P(red) = 7/10 = 0.7 and P(blue) = 3/10 = 0.3. They add up to 1.</details>

6. A die is rolled. Find the probability of getting (a) a prime number, (b) a number divisible by 3. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Primes are 2, 3, 5: 3/6 = 1/2. (b) Multiples of 3 are 3 and 6: 2/6 = 1/3.</details>

7. A coin was tossed 100 times and gave 46 heads. Find the experimental probability of (a) a head, (b) a tail. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) 46/100 = 0.46. (b) 54/100 = 0.54.</details>

8. Two coins are tossed together. List the sample space and find P(at least one head). *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The outcomes are HH, HT, TH, TT. The ones with at least one head are HH, HT, TH: 3 of 4, so P = 3/4.</details>

9. In a survey of 60 families in a town, 21 owned a two-wheeler only, 18 owned a car, and the rest owned neither. Find the probability that a family chosen at random owns neither. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Neither = 60 − 21 − 18 = 21 families (assuming no family is counted twice). P = 21/60 = 0.35.</details>

## 5-mark questions

10. Two dice are rolled together. (a) How many outcomes are there? Find the probability of (b) a sum of 9, (c) a double, (d) a sum less than 4. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) 6 × 6 = 36. (b) The pairs with sum 9 are (3,6), (4,5), (5,4), (6,3): 4/36 = 1/9. (c) The doubles are (1,1) to (6,6): 6/36 = 1/6. (d) A sum of 2 is (1,1); a sum of 3 is (1,2) and (2,1): 3 pairs, so 3/36 = 1/12.

</details>

11. A pack of 52 cards is well shuffled and one card is drawn. Find the probability that it is (a) a queen, (b) a black card, (c) a red face card, (d) a card that is not a heart, (e) the king of hearts or the ace of spades. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) 4/52 = 1/13. (b) 26/52 = 1/2. (c) Red face cards: 3 in each of hearts and diamonds = 6, so 6/52 = 3/26. (d) Not a heart: 52 − 13 = 39, so 39/52 = 3/4. (e) 2 cards: 2/52 = 1/26.

</details>

## Case-based question

A school in Pune surveyed the weather over 30 June days for a science project. It was rainy on 18 days, cloudy but dry on 7 days, and sunny on 5 days. The class monitor says, “So the probability of a rainy day is 18/30, so tomorrow will certainly be rainy.”

12. (a) Find the experimental probability of each kind of day. (b) Check that they add up to 1. (c) What is the probability that a day is not rainy? (d) Is the monitor right to say tomorrow will certainly be rainy? Explain.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Rainy 18/30 = 0.6, cloudy 7/30 ≈ 0.233, sunny 5/30 ≈ 0.167. (b) 18 + 7 + 5 = 30, so the three add up to 30/30 = 1. (c) 1 − 0.6 = 0.4. (d) No. A probability of 0.6 means rain is more likely than not, but 0.6 is not 1. There is still a 0.4 chance of no rain, so tomorrow is not certain to be rainy.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

13. **Assertion (A):** If you toss a fair coin 10 times and get 7 heads, the coin must be unfair. **Reason (R):** The probability of heads for a fair coin is ½.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false: with only 10 tosses, getting 7 heads is quite possible for a fair coin because short runs are unreliable. R is true. So A is false and R is true.</details>

14. **Assertion (A):** The probability of getting a number less than 7 when a fair die is rolled is 1. **Reason (R):** Every outcome of the die is less than 7.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both are true and R explains A: all 6 outcomes are favourable, so the event is certain and P = 6/6 = 1.</details>

15. **Assertion (A):** When two coins are tossed, P(one head and one tail) = ½. **Reason (R):** The outcomes HT and TH are different.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both are true and R explains A: the favourable outcomes are HT and TH, so 2 out of 4 = ½.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- P(E) = favourable ÷ all outcomes (equally likely). It always lies from 0 to 1.
- P(not E) = 1 − P(E).
- Experimental probability = number of times the event occurred ÷ number of trials.
- Short runs are unreliable; in the long run experimental probability approaches the theoretical one.
- Two coins: 4 outcomes. Two dice: 36 outcomes. List them in order.

</div>
