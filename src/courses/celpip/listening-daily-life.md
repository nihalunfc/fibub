**Module 2 · about 16 minutes.** By the end you will know what Part 2 asks, how to follow offers and choices in an everyday conversation, and how to read feelings from tone. You will practise on five original conversations with 25 questions.

<div class="bigidea">Part 2 is a short everyday exchange where someone has a need, the other person makes offers, and a choice is made. Note the need, every offer, and the one that is accepted.</div>

## 1. What Part 2 looks like

Part 2, **Listening to a Daily Life Conversation**, gives you **one conversation** of about one to two minutes between two people in an everyday setting: a pharmacy, a rental desk, a school, a neighbour’s garden, a gym. Unlike Part 1, it is **not split into sections**: you hear the whole conversation, then answer **5 questions**.

- Heard **once**; questions appear **after** the audio.
- Four options per question; no penalty for wrong answers.
- The screen shows a short situation line, sometimes with a picture.

## 2. What it really tests

Part 2 tests everyday listening: the kind you need at a service counter or with a neighbour. Typical questions ask:

- **Why** is the person there, or what is the problem?
- **What** does the other person offer or suggest?
- **How** does someone feel (worried, relieved, unsure)?
- **What** will happen next, or what did they choose?
- **What is the relationship** or tone between the speakers?

**Everyday analogy.** Think of ordering at a busy café. The barista says, "We’re out of oat milk. I can do almond or soy, or regular milk at no charge." You do not remember every word, but you remember the problem (no oat milk), the offers (almond, soy, regular) and what you chose. Part 2 is that kind of listening.

## 3. The method for Part 2

<div class="steps">

<div class="step">

**Predict the roles.** From the situation line, decide who needs something and who can help. "A conversation at a car rental desk": customer needs a car, agent offers solutions.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Note the need and the obstacle.** The first few turns give the reason for the visit and the problem: `need: prescription. Obstacle: 40 min (short staff)`.

</div>
<div class="step">

**List every offer.** Offers usually come in twos or threes: `parking 2h free / delivery free <5 km`. Each one is a likely answer or a likely trap.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Circle the choice and its reason.** Listen for *I’ll take, perfect, that works, fine, please*. Write the reason too: `delivery ✓ (dose before dinner)`.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Catch the feeling and the last detail.** Part 2 often ends with a small fact (a fee, a date, a form) and often asks about feelings. Note words like *great, oh no, honestly, I’m worried*.

</div>
</div>

## 4. Worked example

Situation line: *Listen to a conversation at a bike shop.*

> **Customer:** Hi. My bike’s back brake is making a horrible squeal. Can you fix it today?
> **Mechanic:** We’re booked today, I’m afraid. I could look at it Thursday morning. Or, if it’s just the brake pads, I can sell you new ones, and it takes about ten minutes to change them yourself.
> **Customer:** I’m not very handy. And I ride to work every day.
> **Mechanic:** Then leave it with us Thursday, and you can borrow one of our rental bikes until it’s ready. No charge.
> **Customer:** Oh, that’s really kind. Let’s do that.

Notes: `need: brake squeal fix. Obstacle: booked today`. `Offers: Thu / pads DIY 10 min / Thu + free rental bike`. `Choice: Thu + loan ✓ (not handy, rides to work)`. `Feeling: grateful ("really kind")`.

*What will the customer do?* A. Buy brake pads and change them herself. B. Leave the bike on Thursday and use a loan bike. C. Get the bike fixed today. D. Stop riding to work.
**Answer: B.** A is a real offer, rejected with "I’m not very handy". C contradicts "we’re booked today".

*How does the customer feel at the end?* A. Annoyed B. Grateful C. Confused D. Worried about the cost
**Answer: B**, from "Oh, that’s really kind." D is a trap: the loan is "no charge", so there is nothing to worry about.

## 5. Question types and traps

| Question | Where the answer usually is | Typical trap |
|---|---|---|
| Why is she here / what is the problem? | First two turns | A detail mentioned later |
| What does he offer? | Middle | An offer the first speaker invents |
| What does she choose? | Near the end, after *perfect, okay, I’ll* | The offer she rejected |
| How does he feel? | Tone words, *but*, *honestly* | A positive word followed by *but* |
| What is true about the fee / time? | Last lines | The normal price, when a special one applies |

**Tone matters.** In Part 2, feeling questions are common. "Oh, great" can be happy or sarcastic depending on the situation: "Oh, great. I have a meeting in two hours" (after hearing there are no cars) is frustration, not joy. Always check the words against the situation.

<div class="strategy">

**The "normal versus this time" rule.** Service staff often say what normally happens and then what happens this time: "Normally it’s seventy-five dollars, but our Moncton office is a partner branch, so there’s no fee there." Questions almost always ask about *this time*. When you hear *normally, usually* or *the regular price*, wait for the exception.

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Small talk at the start is not important."** In Part 2 the first lines usually hold the purpose of the conversation ("I wanted to talk about Noah’s reading"), and at least one question asks about it. Do not use the first lines to settle in; start noting from the very first word.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- **Making offers:** *I can give you, I could, would you like, there’s also, another option is*
- **Accepting:** *perfect, that works, I’ll take it, sounds good, let’s do that, even better*
- **Rejecting politely:** *that’s a bit, I’m not sure, the only thing is, I’d rather not*
- **Showing feelings:** *oh no, oh great (sarcastic), that’s a relief, I’m worried, really kind*
- **Exceptions:** *normally, usually, but this time, in your case, unless*

</div>

## 6. Practice: five everyday conversations

Each conversation has five questions, as in the real Part 2. Listen once with notes, answer all five, then check. Replay afterwards to hear the key words you missed.

<div class="keypoints">

- Part 2: **one everyday conversation, 5 questions after it**, heard once.
- Note the **need, the obstacle, every offer, and the choice** with its reason.
- Rejected offers are the most common trap: you heard them, but they were not chosen.
- Listen for "normally... but this time": the exception is usually asked.
- Check feelings against the situation; "great" can mean the opposite.

</div>
