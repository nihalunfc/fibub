**Module 2 · about 16 minutes.** By the end you will know how news reports are built, how to note one with a simple frame, and how to separate the main event from the details. You will practise on five original local news items with 25 questions.

<div class="bigidea">Every news report answers the same few questions in roughly the same order: what happened, who is involved, why, what the effects are, and what happens next. Listen with that frame and you will not get lost.</div>

## 1. What Part 4 looks like

Part 4, **Listening to a News Item**, plays a short news report of about one and a half to two minutes, read by one speaker, as you might hear on a local radio station. Then you answer **5 questions**, each with four options.

- One speaker, no conversation.
- Heard **once**; questions after the audio.
- Topics are local and everyday: a bridge closing, a community project, a new service at the city, a local hero, an event.

## 2. What it really tests

Part 4 tests whether you can follow **connected information from one speaker**, with more formal language than the conversations in Parts 1 to 3. You need to:

- Identify the **main event** (not a side detail).
- Link **people and organisations** to what they did or said.
- Follow **causes and effects** ("because of", "as a result").
- Catch **opinions** reported in the news (a café owner is worried, a fire chief praises someone).
- Know **what happens next** (a vote, a reopening date, a plan).

**Everyday analogy.** A news report is like a well-packed suitcase. The most important thing goes on top (the main event), then the things you need soon (who, why, effects), and at the bottom the things for later (what happens next). Once you know how it is packed, you know where to reach for each item.

## 3. The 5-line news frame

<div class="steps">

<div class="step">

**Before the audio: write five labels.** `What:`, `Who:`, `Why:`, `Effects / reactions:`, `Next:`. Leave space after each.

</div>
<div class="step">

**First sentence: fill "What".** Reporters put the main event first. Write it in a few words: `Main St bridge closed 3 months`.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Middle: fill "Who" and "Why".** Note names with their role, not just names: `Beth Morrow = café owner`, `engineers found cracks (June)`.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Next part: effects and reactions.** Note who is helped or hurt and how: `drivers +10 min`, `footbridge Mon`, `café worried, half customers across river`.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Last sentences: fill "Next".** The final line is very often about the future: `reopens early Nov`, `council votes next month`, `bake sale Fri → greenhouse`.

</div>
</div>

## 4. Worked example

Situation line: *Listen to a news report.*

> The town of Elkford will open its first outdoor skating trail this winter. The one kilometre loop through Riverside Park was paid for by a local business group, which raised ninety thousand dollars over two years. Recreation manager Dina Pearce says the trail will be flooded as soon as night temperatures stay below minus ten for a week. Skating will be free, and skates can be rented at the park building for five dollars. Not everyone is pleased. Some dog walkers say the trail takes over their favourite path. The town says a new walking trail will be cleared next to the ice by the end of December.

Frame:
`What: first outdoor skating trail, Riverside Park, 1 km, this winter`
`Who: business group paid ($90k, 2 yrs); Dina Pearce = rec manager`
`Why/when: opens after a week of nights < −10`
`Effects: free skating; rentals $5; dog walkers unhappy (lose path)`
`Next: new walking trail by end of Dec`

*Who paid for the trail?* A. The town B. A local business group C. Dina Pearce D. Dog walkers. **B.** A is the trap: the town runs the project, but the business group raised the money.

*When will the trail open?* A. In December B. After a week of very cold nights C. Next year D. When ninety thousand dollars is raised. **B.** A is when the *walking* trail is ready. D already happened.

*What is the town’s response to the dog walkers?* **A new walking trail next to the ice.**

## 5. Question types and traps

| Question | Where to look | Common trap |
|---|---|---|
| What is the report mainly about? | First sentence | A detail that takes up a lot of time |
| Who did X? | Middle, names with roles | The organisation that approved, not the one that paid |
| Why did X happen? | After *because, after, due to* | A later effect given as a cause |
| How does a person feel? | Quotes and reported speech | The reporter’s fact, not the person’s view |
| What will happen next? | Last sentences | Something that already happened |

<div class="strategy">

**Roles beat names.** News items mention several named people, and names are easy to forget or mishear. The questions usually use roles anyway: "What does the café owner say?" So note the role first: `café owner: worried`. If you catch the name too, good, but the role is what you need.

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"News English is too formal for me, so I will just guess."** Local news uses a fixed set of phrases that you can learn quickly: *officials say, residents are being asked to, is expected to, will vote on, approved, a trial*. Learn the phrases below, and most news items become very predictable. Listening to a few minutes of free Canadian local radio or news podcasts each day also helps a lot.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- **Main event:** *will open, has closed, is considering, approved, is being called a hero*
- **People and roles:** *councillor, chair, director, resident, spokesperson, organizers*
- **Reasons:** *after, because of, due to, following, in response to*
- **Effects:** *as a result, this means, residents will have to, adds about*
- **Reported opinions:** *says, argues, points out, is worried that, is not convinced*
- **The future:** *is expected to, will vote, plans to, starting next month, if it works*

</div>

## 6. Practice: five local news items

Write the five frame labels before each item. Listen once, fill the frame, then answer all five questions.

<div class="keypoints">

- Part 4: **one local news report, one speaker, 5 questions after it**, heard once.
- Use the frame: **What, Who, Why, Effects or reactions, Next**.
- The first sentence holds the main event; the last sentences hold what happens next.
- Note people by **role** and link each action to the right person or organisation.
- Learn the common news phrases; they make reports predictable.

</div>
