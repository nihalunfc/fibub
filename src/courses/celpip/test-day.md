**Module 1 · about 11 minutes.** By the end you will know what happens from the moment you arrive until you leave, and you will have a checklist that removes every avoidable risk.

<div class="bigidea">Test day should hold no surprises. If you know the steps, the screen and the timing in advance, all your energy goes into English instead of worry.</div>

## 1. The day, step by step

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">

**Arrive early.** Aim to be there at least 30 minutes before your start time. Late arrivals may lose their seat and their fee.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Check-in.** Staff check your ID against your booking (the names must match exactly), take your photo and may ask you to sign a form. Bring the ID you booked with, usually a valid passport.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Lockers.** Phone, watch, bag, food and notes all go in a locker. You take only your ID into the room.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Your seat.** A computer, a headset with a microphone and note-taking material. You do a short sound check and a microphone check; tell staff at once if anything sounds wrong.

</div>
<div class="step">

**The test.** Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking, in that order, all on the screen. A clock at the top shows the time left for each part.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Speaking happens in the same room.** Others may be speaking at different times. Start on the beep, speak steadily into the microphone and ignore everyone else.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Finish and leave.** Notes are collected. Your results come online after the raters mark your writing and speaking, usually within a few business days (celpip.ca gives the current times, and offers faster results for a fee).

</div>
</div>

## 2. The screen and the clock

Each part opens with short instructions and a sample. The timer counts down in a corner of the screen. When time runs out, the test moves on by itself; you cannot go back to an earlier part. In Listening, the recording plays once and the questions appear afterwards. In Reading, you can move between questions within a part until its time ends.

<div class="strategy">

**Use the instructions time to breathe, not to read.** You will know every instruction from this course, so glance to confirm the task and save your calm for the questions.

</div>

## 3. Notes: what helps and what does not

You cannot write down everything, and trying makes you miss the next sentence. Good notes are **short keywords, numbers, names and arrows**: "Fri → Wed", "Sam: estimate Tue", "✗ fee". Module 2 trains this skill part by part.

## 4. The week before: a checklist

| When | Do this |
|---|---|
| A week before | Check the booking email: date, time, address, the ID needed. Take one full timed mock (Module 6). |
| Three days before | Light practice only: one drill per skill and two speaking tasks with the timer. |
| The night before | Put your ID by the door. Plan the route and the parking. Sleep. |
| The morning | Eat something. Speak English for 10 minutes (read a news article aloud) to warm up your voice. |
| At the centre | Ask staff to fix any headset or microphone problem before the test starts. |

## 5. Check yourself

<figure class="fig" data-fig="drill" data-set="testday" data-n="5"><figcaption>What would you do? Original situations.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="keypoints">

- Arrive 30 minutes early with the exact ID you booked with; the names must match.
- Everything except your ID goes in a locker; note-taking material is provided.
- The sections run in a fixed order with a countdown; you cannot go back to a finished part.
- Speaking is recorded in the same room as others: start on the beep and speak steadily.
- The week before: one full mock, then light practice and sleep.

</div>
