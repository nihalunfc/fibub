**Module 5 · about 22 minutes.** By the end you will know how Task 7 is built, how to plan an opinion in 30 seconds and fill 90 seconds with a clear argument, and you will have practised three timed tasks and 30 drill items.

<div class="bigidea">Task 7 is a short, spoken opinion column: pick a side fast, give two reasons that each have a real example, nod to the other side, and finish where you started.</div>

## 1. What Task 7 looks like

Task 7, **Expressing Opinions**, shows a question about an everyday social issue, often in the form *"Some people think X. Others think Y. Which do you agree with?"* or *"Should your city...?"*

- You usually get **30 seconds to prepare** and **90 seconds to speak**. The test screen shows the exact time.
- There is no picture and no other person to persuade. You speak to the rater, like giving your view at a community meeting.
- Topics are general: city services, work, technology, family, schools, health. You need no special knowledge.

Your answer is rated on **Content/Coherence**, **Vocabulary**, **Listenability** (rhythm, pronunciation and intonation; pauses and self-correction; grammar and sentence structure; variety of sentence types) and **Task Fulfillment** (relevance, completeness, tone and length).

## 2. What it really tests

Task 7 is the most "academic" speaking task. It checks whether you can:

1. **Take a clear position** and keep it from beginning to end.
2. **Support it** with reasons and specific examples, not general statements.
3. **Organise** a long turn so a listener can follow it easily.
4. **Keep talking for 90 seconds** with natural rhythm, without long silences.

**Everyday analogy.** Think of a friend asking, "Should I buy an electric car?" A helpful answer is not "Yes, they’re good." It is: "Yes, I think so. First, you’ll save a lot on gas; my neighbour pays about thirty dollars a month to charge hers. Second, ... Sure, they cost more upfront, but... So yes, go for it." That shape is Task 7.

## 3. Thirty seconds of planning

Thirty seconds is very short, so use a fixed routine:

1. **Seconds 0 to 5: choose a side.** Pick the side that gives you the **easiest examples**, not the "best" side. There is no right answer.
2. **Seconds 5 to 25: write two reasons and one example each.** Three or four words per line: `R1 save $ → neighbour $30/mo`, `R2 cleaner air → kids asthma`.
3. **Seconds 25 to 30: note the other side.** One word: `cost`. You will use it near the end.

Do not write your introduction. You will say it naturally from the question.

## 4. The five-part template

<div class="steps">

<div class="step">

**1. Opinion (about 10 seconds).** Rephrase the question and state your side: *"In my opinion, cities should definitely offer free public transit for seniors."*

</div>
<div class="step">

**2. Reason one + example (about 25 seconds).** *"The main reason is independence. My grandfather stopped driving last year, and the bus is now the only way he can visit his friends or see his doctor. Paying four dollars each way adds up fast on a pension."*

</div>
<div class="step">

**3. Reason two + example (about 25 seconds).** A different type of reason: *"Another reason is that it helps everyone else. When more seniors take the bus, there are fewer older drivers on busy roads at night, which makes the streets safer."*

</div>
<div class="step">

**4. The other side, answered (about 15 seconds).** *"Some people say the city can’t afford it. That’s a fair point, but seniors mostly ride outside rush hour, when buses are half empty anyway."*

</div>
<div class="step">

**5. Conclusion (about 10 seconds).** Restate your opinion in new words: *"So overall, I strongly believe free transit for seniors is a small cost for a big benefit."*

</div>
</div>

**If you finish early,** add a second example to reason two, or explain a consequence: *"And if that happens, ..."*. **If time is running out,** jump to your conclusion; one sentence is enough.

## 6. Model answers for practice task 1

Original transcripts written for this course. Ninety seconds is about 180 to 220 words at a natural pace.

<div class="model">

**Level: around CLB 7.**

I agree that cities should ban cars from downtown on weekends. I have two reasons. First, it is better for people. When there are no cars, people can walk safely, and families with children can enjoy the street. For example, in my city there is a street festival in summer, and the street is closed. Many people come, and it is very nice. Second, it is good for the environment. Cars make a lot of pollution, and if we close the street, the air is cleaner. Also, it is quieter, so people can relax and talk. Some people say that businesses will lose customers. I understand this, but I think more people will come to walk and shop, so the stores will sell more. Also, people can park outside downtown and walk or take the bus. So, I think closing downtown streets on weekends is a good idea for people, for the environment and for the businesses too.

</div>

**Why it scores around CLB 7.** *Content/Coherence:* a clear opinion, two reasons and a counter-argument, in a logical order. *Vocabulary:* everyday words used correctly, with some repetition (*people* appears many times). *Listenability:* simple sentences with few errors, but the structures repeat (*it is..., it is...*) and there is little variety. *Task Fulfillment:* on topic and long enough. The example in reason one is general ("it is very nice") rather than specific.

<div class="model">

**Level: around CLB 10.**

Personally, I’m all for keeping cars out of downtown on weekends, and I think most cities would be surprised by how well it works. The biggest reason is that it brings people back to the centre. Last summer, our city closed Main Street for four Saturdays as a trial, and the difference was striking: cafés put tables in the road, kids were drawing with chalk, and the sidewalks were packed until late. Several shop owners told the local paper that sales actually went up, which leads me to my second point. Businesses tend to assume their customers arrive by car, but downtown shoppers mostly come on foot or by transit, and people who are relaxed tend to stay longer and spend more. Now, I do understand the concern about drivers, especially people with mobility issues or deliveries to make. That’s why I’d keep a few side streets open for accessible parking and early-morning deliveries. With a bit of planning, those problems are easy to solve. So, all things considered, I’m convinced that car-free weekends make downtown livelier, safer and better for business, and I’d love to see my city make it permanent.

</div>

**Why it scores around CLB 10.** *Content/Coherence:* each reason has a specific, vivid example (the four-Saturday trial, the shop owners), and the reasons connect smoothly (*which leads me to my second point*). *Vocabulary:* precise and natural (*striking, mobility issues, livelier, all things considered*). *Listenability:* a wide mix of sentence types, including a colon-style list and a conditional (*With a bit of planning...*). *Task Fulfillment:* a strong, consistent position, the other side handled with a practical solution, and a confident close in time.

## 7. Traps in Task 7

| Trap | What it sounds like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Sitting on the fence | "Both sides have good points... it depends." | Choose one side in your first sentence and keep it |
| Changing sides | Starting "I agree", ending "but maybe it’s not a good idea" | Restate the same opinion in the conclusion |
| Reasons without examples | "It is good for the economy. It is good for health." | One specific example per reason: a person, a place, a number |
| Too many reasons | Five reasons, each one sentence | Two or three reasons, each developed |
| Long silences | Stopping to think for five seconds | Use thinking phrases: *What I mean is..., Let me give you an example...* |
| Ending with no conclusion | Time runs out mid-example | Watch the timer; at about 75 seconds, move to your conclusion |

<div class="strategy">

**Self-check after each practice task.**

- [ ] My opinion was clear in the first sentence.
- [ ] I gave two (or three) different reasons.
- [ ] Each reason had a specific example (a person, a place, a number, an event).
- [ ] I mentioned the other side and answered it.
- [ ] My conclusion repeated my opinion in different words.
- [ ] I spoke for close to 90 seconds without long silences.
- [ ] I used linking phrases (*the main reason, another reason, for example, on the other hand, overall*).

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"I must give my real opinion, and it has to be the clever one."** Raters do not judge what you believe; they judge how clearly you argue it. If your honest view is complicated, choose the simpler side, the one you can explain with quick examples. A clear, well-supported "yes" beats a confused "maybe" every time.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- **Giving your opinion:** *In my opinion..., Personally, I’m all for..., I strongly believe..., I’m firmly against..., From my point of view...*
- **Reasons:** *The main reason is..., Another reason is..., On top of that..., Most importantly...*
- **Examples:** *For example..., For instance..., Take my neighbour, who..., Last year in my city..., A good example of this is...*
- **The other side:** *Some people argue that..., I understand the concern about..., That’s a fair point, but..., Admittedly..., however...*
- **Buying thinking time:** *Let me put it this way..., What I mean is..., The way I see it...*
- **Concluding:** *So overall..., All things considered..., That’s why I believe..., For all these reasons...*

</div>

## 8. Drills

<div class="keypoints">

- Task 7: a social question, usually **30 seconds to prepare and 90 to speak**, no picture and no listener to persuade.
- In planning, choose the side with the easiest examples, then write two reasons with one example each and one word for the other side.
- Template: **opinion → reason 1 + example → reason 2 + example → other side answered → conclusion**.
- Specific examples (a person, a place, a number) are what make a reason convincing.
- Keep the same opinion from start to finish, and move to your conclusion at about 75 seconds.

</div>
