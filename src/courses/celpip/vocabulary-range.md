**Module 4 · about 18 minutes.** By the end you will know what raters mean by range and precision, how to replace vague words with exact ones, which word partners sound natural, and how to show variety without risky words.

<div class="bigidea">Vocabulary marks come from saying exactly what you mean with words you are sure of. Precise beats fancy, and natural beats rare.</div>

## 1. What the Vocabulary area includes

CELPIP rates **Vocabulary** on *word choice, suitable use, range, precision and accuracy*. In plain English:

- **Word choice and precision:** is it the exact word? *Filthy* is more exact than *very dirty*.
- **Suitable use:** does it fit the reader and situation? *Reimburse* fits a company; *pay me back* fits a friend.
- **Range:** do you use different words, or the same few again and again?
- **Accuracy:** is each word used with the right meaning and form? *Sensitive*, not *sensible*, to noise.

## 2. What it really rewards

Raters are not counting long words. They are asking: **could I picture exactly what this writer means?** Compare:

- *The apartment had many problems.*
- *The apartment had a leaking dishwasher, a broken window latch and mould in the bathroom.*

The second sentence uses ordinary words, but each one is exact. That is precision.

**Everyday analogy.** Vocabulary is like a toolbox. With only a hammer, everything becomes a nail: *good, bad, nice, thing, problem*. A good writer carries a screwdriver, a wrench and pliers too, and, just as important, knows which tool fits which job. A fancy tool you do not know how to use can break the job.

## 3. The method: spot, upgrade, check

<div class="steps">

<div class="step">

**Spot the vague words.** After writing, look for *good, bad, nice, very, a lot, thing, stuff, problem, get, big*. These are signals that a more exact word may fit.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Ask "how exactly?"** Very good how? *Reliable? Convenient? Affordable?* A problem how? *Leaking? Delayed? Overcharged?* The answer is usually your better word.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Use natural word partners.** Learn verbs and nouns in pairs: *make a decision, take action, pay attention, play a role, express concern*.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Vary, but only with words you know.** If *important* appears four times, swap one or two for *essential, a priority, key*. Never use a word you have only seen in a thesaurus.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Match the reader.** Formal email: *assist, inform, resolve*. Friendly: *help, let you know, sort out*.

</div>
</div>

### Upgrade table

| Vague | More precise options (choose by meaning) |
|---|---|
| very good | excellent, ideal, reliable, convenient, outstanding |
| bad | faulty (product), rude (person), poor (service), unsafe (place) |
| very big / small | spacious / cramped (room), substantial / minor (amount, change) |
| a lot of | many (countable), a great deal of (uncountable), numerous |
| get | receive (a letter), obtain (a permit), become (tired), arrive (somewhere) |
| problem | issue, fault, delay, leak, mistake, shortage |
| happy / sad | pleased, delighted, relieved / disappointed, frustrated, upset |
| cheap | affordable, reasonably priced, inexpensive |
| give back | return (an item), refund (money) |
| fix | repair, resolve (an issue), replace |

## 4. Worked example: before and after

Task point: *Explain why you are unhappy with the moving company.*

<div class="model">

**Before (around CLB 6-7)**

The movers were very bad. They came very late and they broke a lot of things. The workers were not nice to my wife. It was a big problem for us and we were very sad. The price was also very high.

</div>

Notes: the meaning is clear, but *very* appears five times and *bad, nice, big, sad, high* are vague. The reader cannot picture what happened. Vocabulary range and precision are low.

<div class="model">

**After (around CLB 9-10)**

Unfortunately, the movers arrived three hours late, which meant we had to return the keys to our old apartment after the deadline. Two of my dining chairs were cracked and a lamp was shattered. The crew was also impatient and dismissive when my wife asked them to be careful with the boxes. On top of that, the final bill was $240 higher than the written quote.

</div>

Notes: almost the same ideas, now precise. *Arrived three hours late, cracked, shattered, impatient and dismissive, $240 higher than the written quote* each paint a clear picture. Words are everyday ones used exactly, with no thesaurus risks.

## 5. Vocabulary traps

| Trap | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Look-alike words | *sensible* / *sensitive*, *economic* / *economical*, *affect* / *effect* | Learn them as pairs with an example each |
| Wrong partner | *do a decision*, *make a favour* | *make a decision*, *do a favour* |
| Thesaurus word with wrong shade | *The meal was very obese.* | Only use words you have seen used in context |
| Too informal for the reader | *The service was super sketchy.* | *The service was unreliable.* |
| Copying the task words | Repeating every phrase from the question | Paraphrase with your own words |
| lend / borrow | *Can you borrow me your car?* | *Can you lend me your car?* |

<div class="strategy">

**Vocabulary self-check.**

- [ ] No word (except small words like the, and) appears more than twice.
- [ ] I replaced at least three vague words (good, bad, very, thing, problem) with exact ones.
- [ ] Every verb-noun pair sounds natural (make a complaint, take action).
- [ ] I am sure of the meaning of every word I used.
- [ ] My word choices fit the reader (formal, semi-formal, friendly).

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"I should learn lists of advanced words and put them in every answer."** Rare words dropped into an answer often have the wrong meaning or tone, which lowers accuracy. Learn words in context, in partners, and use the precise ones you can use correctly. Raters reward precision and accuracy as much as range.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- **Describing problems:** *faulty, damaged, delayed, overcharged, out of order, leaking*
- **Describing benefits:** *convenient, affordable, accessible, reliable, beneficial to*
- **Feelings (formal):** *disappointed, concerned, frustrated, grateful, relieved*
- **Word partners:** *make a complaint, make a decision, take action, pay attention to, play a role in, raise a concern*
- **Amounts and change:** *rose sharply, dropped slightly, a significant increase, a minor delay*

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Vocabulary is rated on **word choice, suitable use, range, precision and accuracy**.
- Precise everyday words beat vague words and beat rare words used wrongly.
- Hunt for *good, bad, very, a lot, thing, problem, get* and ask "how exactly?"
- Learn words with their partners: **make a decision, take action, pay attention**.
- Vary repeated words, but only with words you are sure of, and match the reader.
