**Module 4 · about 20 minutes.** By the end you will know how to pick the right tone for any CELPIP email, which words and grammar make writing more or less formal, how to complain politely, and you will have written two emails in different tones.

<div class="bigidea">Tone is how your email sounds to the reader. Decide it from one question, "Who am I writing to, and how well do I know them?", then keep it the same from greeting to sign-off.</div>

## 1. Where tone shows up in Task 1

Every Task 1 tells you who you are writing to. That reader sets the tone, and **tone is part of Task Fulfillment**, together with relevance, completeness and word count. A complete email in the wrong tone loses marks.

Three tones cover almost every task:

| Tone | Typical readers | Greeting | Sign-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Formal** | A company, a city office, a hiring manager, anyone you do not know | Dear Sir or Madam, / Dear Mr. Singh, | Sincerely, / Yours truly, |
| **Semi-formal** | Your manager, a coworker, your child’s teacher, a neighbour, a coach | Dear Ms. Roy, / Hi Mr. Chen, | Best regards, / Thanks again, |
| **Friendly** | Family, close friends | Hi Sam, / Hey Lina, | Take care, / Talk soon, / Love, |

## 2. What tone really is

Tone is made of small choices:

- **Words:** *assist* (formal), *help* (neutral), *give me a hand* (friendly).
- **Contractions:** *I am, I would* (formal); *I’m, I’d* (semi-formal and friendly).
- **Requests:** *I would appreciate it if you could* (formal); *Could you please* (semi-formal); *Can you* (friendly).
- **Feeling:** formal emails report feelings calmly (*I was disappointed*); friendly ones can show them (*I was so upset!*).

**Everyday analogy.** Tone is like clothes. You wear a suit to a job interview, neat casual clothes to the office, and jeans to a friend’s barbecue. None of them is "better"; the mistake is wearing a suit to the barbecue or jeans to the interview, or changing halfway through the day.

## 3. The method: choose, set, keep

<div class="steps">

<div class="step">

**Name the reader and the relationship.** Stranger or organization: formal. Known person in a role: semi-formal. Family or friend: friendly. Write the tone in your plan.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Set the tone in the greeting.** The greeting is the first signal to the rater. Use the table above; match title and family name for formal and semi-formal.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Soften requests and complaints.** Use polite frames even when you are unhappy: *I am afraid that..., Unfortunately..., Would it be possible to...?*

</div>
<div class="step">

**Keep it steady in the body.** Do not jump from *I would appreciate* to *lol* or from *Hey!* to *I hereby request*. Read each paragraph and ask: does this still sound like the same person to the same reader?

</div>
<div class="step">

**Close in the same tone.** The closing line and sign-off should match the greeting.

</div>
</div>

## 4. Worked example: one message, three tones

The message: *you need to change the date of something*. Watch how the same facts change.

**To a dental clinic (formal):** *Dear Sir or Madam, I am writing to ask whether it would be possible to move my appointment on May 14 to a later date. Unfortunately, I have been asked to attend a training course that day. I would be grateful if you could suggest another time. Sincerely, Ana Ruiz*

**To your manager (semi-formal):** *Hi Mr. Dubois, Would it be possible to move our review meeting from Tuesday to Thursday? My son’s school has called a parent meeting on Tuesday afternoon. I’m happy to stay later on Thursday if that helps. Thanks, Ana*

**To your friend (friendly):** *Hey Mel! I’m so sorry, but can we push our movie night to next week? Work just dumped a training course on me that day. I’ll bring the popcorn to make up for it! Ana*

Now a full model in two levels. Task (original): *Your upstairs neighbour often plays loud music late at night. Write an email to your neighbour. In your email: introduce yourself; describe the problem; suggest a solution.*

<div class="model">

**Answer A (around CLB 7): tone slips**

Hello neighbour,

My name is Tomas and I live in apartment 2B, under you. I am writing because of a problem.

Your music is too loud every night. I can hear it until 1 a.m. and I can’t sleep. I have to get up at 6 for work so this is really annoying and honestly kind of rude. You need to stop it.

Maybe you can turn it down after 11. Or use headphones. If not, I will complain to the manager.

Tomas

</div>

Notes on Answer A. **Task Fulfillment:** all three points appear, but the tone slips: "kind of rude", "You need to stop it" and the threat sound aggressive to a neighbour. Under 100 words. **Vocabulary:** limited (loud, too, really). **Readability:** clear paragraphs, simple correct sentences. **Content/Coherence:** the problem is clear, but the solution is thin.

<div class="model">

**Answer B (around CLB 10): steady, polite tone**

Hi there,

I hope you don’t mind me writing. My name is Tomas Novak, and I live in apartment 2B, directly below yours. We have passed each other in the lobby a few times, but I don’t think we have been properly introduced.

I wanted to mention something that has been happening most weeknights. Music from your apartment is quite loud until around 1 a.m., and the bass in particular comes through my bedroom ceiling. I start work at a bakery at 6 a.m., so I have been finding it hard to get enough sleep.

I completely understand that everyone enjoys music, and I am not asking you to stop. Would it be possible to turn the volume down after 11 p.m. on weeknights? Another idea might be to put a rug under the speakers, which often helps with the bass. I would be happy to talk about it in person if that is easier.

Thanks for understanding,
Tomas

</div>

Notes on Answer B. **Task Fulfillment:** semi-formal and friendly, steady all the way through; all points covered; about 160 words. **Content/Coherence:** specific details (2B, the bass, 6 a.m. bakery shift) and two practical solutions. **Vocabulary:** precise and polite (properly introduced, comes through, in particular). **Readability:** smooth connectors and varied sentences.

## 5. Tone traps

| Trap | Example | Better |
|---|---|---|
| Too casual for an organization | *Hey guys, my order is messed up lol* | *I am writing about a problem with my order.* |
| Too stiff for a friend | *I hereby accept your kind invitation.* | *I’d love to come!* |
| Demanding | *Fix it now.* | *Could you please arrange a repair this week?* |
| Threatening | *or I will call a lawyer* | *If this is not possible, I would like to know what other options I have.* |
| Mixed tone | Dear Sir or Madam ... Cheers, mate | Match the sign-off to the greeting |

<div class="strategy">

**Tone self-check.**

- [ ] I named the reader and chose formal, semi-formal or friendly before writing.
- [ ] My greeting and sign-off match that tone.
- [ ] Requests use a polite frame (Could you, Would it be possible, I would appreciate).
- [ ] No slang to organizations; no stiff legal words to friends.
- [ ] Complaints are firm and polite: facts, effect, request.

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Formal is always safest."** Formal is safest for strangers and organizations. For a friend or relative, a very formal email sounds cold or even sarcastic, and it does not fit the task. Match the reader.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- **Softening bad news:** *I am afraid that..., Unfortunately..., I’m sorry to say that...*
- **Polite requests (formal):** *I would be grateful if..., I would appreciate it if you could...*
- **Polite requests (semi-formal):** *Would you mind..., Would it be possible to..., Could you please...*
- **Friendly warmth:** *Thanks a million!, You’re a lifesaver., Can’t wait to see you!*
- **Firm but polite:** *I would like this resolved by..., I trust you will understand that...*

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Tone is part of **Task Fulfillment**: decide it from who the reader is and how well you know them.
- **Formal** for organizations and strangers, **semi-formal** for people you know in a role, **friendly** for family and friends.
- Tone lives in words, contractions, request frames and how feelings are shown.
- Complain **firmly and politely**: facts, effect, request. Never threaten or insult.
- Keep one tone from greeting to sign-off.

</div>
