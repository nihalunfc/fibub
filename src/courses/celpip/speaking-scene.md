**Module 5 · about 19 minutes.** By the end you will have a route for describing any picture in 60 seconds, the place language and grammar that make a description easy to follow, two model answers and three practice scenes. You will also answer 28 practice questions.

<div class="bigidea">In Task 3 you are the listener's eyes. Give the whole scene in one sentence, then walk them around it in a clear route, saying where each thing is, who is there and what they are doing.</div>

## 1. What Task 3 looks like

Task 3, **Describing a Scene**, shows a **picture** on the screen: usually an everyday place full of people doing different things, such as a park, a street, a store, a kitchen or an office. You describe it to someone who cannot see it.

- You usually get about **30 seconds to prepare** and **60 seconds to speak**. The test screen shows the exact time.
- The picture stays on the screen while you speak.
- **Task 4 uses the same picture**, so details you notice now (clouds, a sign, someone about to trip) become your predictions in the next task.

On this page the scenes are described in words, because the skill is the same: notice the setting, the people and the actions, and say them in a clear order.

**Everyday analogy.** Imagine you are on the phone with a friend who is meeting you at a busy festival. "I am by the main stage. On my left there is a food truck with a long line, and in front of me some kids are dancing." You give the big picture, then landmarks and directions. Task 3 is exactly that phone call.

## 2. What it really tests

- **Content/Coherence:** an overview, then a clear route (front to back, left to right) so the listener can build the picture in their head.
- **Vocabulary:** precise nouns and verbs (stall, puddle, clipboard; balancing, pointing, lining up) and adjectives (crowded, cluttered, exhausted).
- **Listenability:** present continuous used correctly (is carrying, are waiting), place phrases, a mix of short and longer sentences.
- **Task Fulfillment:** describe the scene, not your opinion of it; cover the main parts of the picture; use most of the 60 seconds.

## 3. The method: overview, route, people, close

<div class="steps">

<div class="step">

**Prep (30 s): choose the overview and the route.** Say to yourself in one line what the picture is. Then decide your route: foreground to background, or left to right. Note 4 or 5 key words in that order: `market sunny / FG apples scale / L boy balloon ice cream / R guitar dog / M girl eggs phone / BG clock clouds`.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Overview (about 8 s).** "This picture shows a busy farmers' market in a small-town square on a sunny morning."

</div>
<div class="step">

**Route (about 40 s).** Move through 4 or 5 areas. For each: **place phrase + person or thing + action + one extra detail**. "In the foreground, a woman in a red apron is weighing apples for an older man, who is holding a cloth bag."

</div>
<div class="step">

**Notice small details (as you go).** Signs, weather, expressions, something about to happen. They raise your Vocabulary and set up Task 4.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Close (about 8 s).** One sentence on the overall feeling: "Overall, it looks like a lively, cheerful morning, although the weather might be about to change."

</div>
</div>

## 4. Worked example: the farmers' market

**The picture** (described in words): a busy farmers' market in a small-town square on a sunny morning. A clock tower in the background shows 10:15, and dark clouds are just appearing behind it. In the foreground, a woman in a red apron is weighing apples on a scale for an older man who is holding a cloth bag. On the left, a little boy with a yellow balloon is pulling his father by the hand toward an ice cream stand, where four people are lining up. On the right, a man is playing guitar with his case open in front of him, and a dog is sleeping beside him. In the middle, a teenage girl is carrying a tall stack of egg cartons while looking at her phone, and she is walking toward a small step.

Thinking in prep: overview = market, sunny morning. Route = foreground first (the apples), then left (boy), right (guitar), middle (girl), background (clock and clouds). The girl with the phone and the clouds are details worth mentioning: they make the description vivid and help me in Task 4.

<div class="model">

**Model answer, CLB 7 level (about 100 words)**

This is a picture of a farmers' market. It is a sunny day. In the front, there is a woman with a red apron. She is selling apples to an old man. On the left, there is a boy with a yellow balloon. He is pulling his father to the ice cream. Some people are waiting in line. On the right, a man is playing the guitar and there is a dog. In the middle, there is a girl with many eggs. She is looking at her phone. At the back, there is a clock tower and some clouds. Everyone looks happy.

</div>

**Why it scores around this level.** *Content/Coherence:* overview first and a clear route with place words. *Vocabulary:* correct but basic (the front, the back, many eggs). *Listenability:* accurate present continuous, but "there is" starts sentence after sentence. *Task Fulfillment:* covers all main areas, on task; finishes a little early at a natural pace.

<div class="model">

**Model answer, CLB 10 level (about 145 words)**

This picture shows a busy farmers' market in the square of a small town, on what looks like a sunny weekend morning.

In the foreground, a stallholder in a bright red apron is weighing a bag of apples for an older gentleman, who is waiting with a cloth shopping bag over his arm. Over on the left, a little boy clutching a yellow balloon is tugging his dad toward an ice cream stand, where a short line has already formed. On the right, a street musician is playing guitar with his case open for tips, while his dog is fast asleep at his feet.

Right in the middle, a teenage girl is balancing a tall stack of egg cartons, but she is staring at her phone, so she has not noticed a step just ahead of her. In the background, the clock tower reads a quarter past ten, and some dark clouds are starting to roll in.

</div>

**Why it scores higher.** *Content/Coherence:* the same route, but each area has an extra detail that helps the listener see it. *Vocabulary:* precise and varied (stallholder, clutching, tugging, street musician, fast asleep, roll in). *Listenability:* relative clauses (who is waiting), contrast (but she is staring), "while" and "so" sentences; no "there is" chain. *Task Fulfillment:* complete, on task, about 60 seconds.

## 5. Traps in Task 3

| Trap | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No overview | Starting with "There is a dog." | One-sentence overview first |
| Random jumping | Dog, clock, apples, dog again | Fix a route in prep and follow it |
| "There is" chain | "There is... there is... there is..." | Start with the place, then the person and the action |
| Wrong tense | "A man played the guitar." | Present continuous: is playing |
| Inventing a story | "The girl is sad because her boyfriend left." | Describe what you see; soft guesses only (it looks like) |
| One area only | 60 seconds on the musician | 4 or 5 areas, about 10 seconds each |
| Predicting now | "The girl will fall." | Save it for Task 4; here say "she has not noticed the step" |

<div class="strategy">

**Self-check after every Task 3 practice:**

- [ ] My first sentence gave an overview: what, where, when.
- [ ] I followed a clear route (front to back or left to right).
- [ ] I used place phrases: in the foreground, on the left, in the middle, in the background, next to, behind.
- [ ] I used the present continuous for actions (is carrying, are waiting).
- [ ] I added at least one extra detail for each area (colour, expression, what they are holding).
- [ ] I did not start every sentence with "there is".
- [ ] I described 4 or 5 areas and used most of the 60 seconds.

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"I have to describe every single thing in the picture."** You cannot, and you do not need to. Raters want a **clear, organized** description of the main parts with good language. Four or five areas described well, each with an action and a detail, beat a fast list of fifteen objects that the listener cannot put together.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- **Overview:** *This picture shows... ; The scene takes place in... ; It looks like a... morning / afternoon.*
- **Place:** *In the foreground... ; In the background... ; On the left / right... ; In the middle... ; Next to... ; Behind... ; At the far end... ; Just in front of...*
- **People and actions:** *A man in a grey jacket is... ; A group of teenagers are... ; Someone is holding / carrying / pointing at / lining up for...*
- **Soft guesses:** *It looks like... ; She seems to be... ; They might be... ; Judging by..., it is probably...*
- **Linking:** *Meanwhile... ; while... ; At the same time... ; Not far from them...*
- **Closing:** *Overall, it looks like a lively / stressful / peaceful scene.*

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Task 3: usually **30 s to prepare, 60 s to speak**, describing a picture; Task 4 uses the **same picture**.
- Start with a **one-sentence overview**, then follow a **route** (front to back or left to right).
- For each area: **place phrase + person + action (present continuous) + one detail**.
- Avoid a chain of "there is"; use precise verbs and adjectives instead.
- Describe, do not predict; notice clues such as weather and signs for Task 4.

</div>
