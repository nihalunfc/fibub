**Module 5 · about 22 minutes.** By the end you will know how Task 8 is built, have a four-part template and a toolkit of describing language, and you will have practised three timed tasks and 30 drill items.

<div class="bigidea">Task 8 is a phone call about something odd: your listener has no picture, so you build it for them, from the big shape down to the strange details, and then tell them why it matters.</div>

## 1. What Task 8 looks like

Task 8, **Describing an Unusual Situation**, shows a picture of something strange: an odd object for sale, a building with a surprising shape, a vehicle that is half car and half boat, a very unusual piece of furniture. The screen also gives a short situation, such as:

- *You are in a store and see this unusual chair. Call your friend, who is looking for a chair, and describe it.*
- *You see this strange vehicle in your neighbour’s driveway. Phone your brother and describe it.*

The key point: **the person you are speaking to cannot see the picture.** You usually get **30 seconds to prepare** and **60 seconds to speak**; the test screen shows the exact time.

Your answer is rated on **Content/Coherence**, **Vocabulary**, **Listenability** (rhythm, pronunciation and intonation; pauses and self-correction; grammar and sentence structure; variety of sentence types) and **Task Fulfillment** (relevance, completeness, tone and length).

**In this course, the pictures are described in words** inside each practice task. Picture them in your mind first, then describe them as if you were looking at them.

## 2. What it really tests

Task 8 checks whether your words alone can let someone **see** something they have never seen. That needs:

1. **Order.** Big picture first (what it is, where), then details.
2. **Precise describing language:** size, shape, colour, material, position (*on top of, underneath, next to, attached to*).
3. **Comparisons with familiar things:** *about as big as a shopping cart, shaped like a giant egg*.
4. **Purpose.** The situation always has a reason for the call (your friend wants a chair, your brother loves boats). Connect the description to it.

**Everyday analogy.** Imagine describing a lost suitcase to an airline agent on the phone. "It’s black" is useless; half the suitcases are black. "It’s a medium black hard case, about as tall as my knee, with a red ribbon on the handle and a big scratch across the front" lets them find it. Task 8 needs that same useful detail.

## 3. Thirty seconds of planning

1. **Seconds 0 to 10: find the ONE strange thing.** What makes the object unusual? Write it first: `chair = giant hand`.
2. **Seconds 10 to 25: list three or four details, top to bottom or left to right.** `fingers = back, palm = seat`, `bright yellow plastic`, `wheels under thumb`, `price $250`.
3. **Seconds 25 to 30: note the purpose.** `Maya new apt → fun?`

## 4. The four-part template

<div class="steps">

<div class="step">

**1. Set the scene (about 10 seconds).** Greet the listener and say why you are calling: *"Hey Maya, I’m at the furniture store on King Street, and you won’t believe the chair I’m looking at."*

</div>
<div class="step">

**2. Give the big picture (about 10 seconds).** What is it, roughly? *"Basically, it’s an armchair shaped like a giant open hand."*

</div>
<div class="step">

**3. Describe the details in order (about 30 seconds).** Move in one direction (top to bottom, back to front): *"The four fingers and the thumb stand up and make the back and the arms. You sit right in the palm. The whole thing is shiny yellow plastic, about the colour of a school bus, and it’s on little black wheels, so you can roll it around."*

</div>
<div class="step">

**4. Connect it to the purpose (about 10 seconds).** *"You said you wanted something fun for your new place, and it’s only two hundred and fifty dollars. Want me to take a picture and send it?"*

</div>
</div>

**Describing toolkit.** Size: *tiny, about the size of..., as tall as..., twice as wide as*. Shape: *round, square, oval, shaped like..., curved, pointed*. Material: *made of wood, metal, glass, plastic, fabric*. Position: *on top, underneath, in the middle, on the left side, attached to, sticking out of*. Colour: *bright, dark, pale, striped, spotted*.

## 6. Model answers for practice task 1

Original transcripts written for this course. Sixty seconds is about 130 to 150 words.

<div class="model">

**Level: around CLB 7.**

Hi Daniel, I am at a garage sale and I see a very strange bicycle. It is for three people. But the people do not sit one behind the other. They sit next to each other. So there are three seats and three pedals. It has only two wheels, one in the front and one in the back. The wheels are big. There is one handlebar in the middle, and it is very long. The bicycle is green and white. There is also a basket on the back for a picnic. It costs one hundred eighty dollars. I think you will like it because you like cycling and you want something different. Do you want me to buy it?

</div>

**Why it scores around CLB 7.** *Content/Coherence:* the scene, the main strange feature, details and purpose are all there, in a sensible order. *Vocabulary:* basic but correct (*next to each other, handlebar*), with few precise describing words (*green and white* instead of *green and white stripes*). *Listenability:* short, clear sentences, but similar patterns (*It is..., There is...*). *Task Fulfillment:* polite, on task and on time; the details give a rough picture, but the listener may not imagine the shape clearly.

<div class="model">

**Level: around CLB 10.**

Daniel, it’s me. I’m at a garage sale on Birch Street, and I’ve just found the weirdest bike you’ll ever hear about. Picture a regular bike, but stretched sideways: it’s built for three people riding side by side, not in a line. So there’s one extra-wide frame with three seats and three sets of pedals all in a row. Strangely, it only has two big wheels, one at the front and one at the back, and the whole thing is steered with a single handlebar in the middle that’s about as long as a broom handle. It’s painted in green and white stripes, a bit like a candy cane, and there’s a wicker picnic basket strapped to the back. They’re asking a hundred and eighty. You’ve been saying you want something nobody else has, and this is definitely it. Should I grab it before someone else does?

</div>

**Why it scores around CLB 10.** *Content/Coherence:* the main idea comes first (*a regular bike, but stretched sideways*), then the details move logically from the frame to the wheels to the colour. *Vocabulary:* precise and vivid (*stretched sideways, steered, strapped to, about as long as a broom handle, like a candy cane*). *Listenability:* varied sentences, including an imperative (*Picture...*) and a closing question. *Task Fulfillment:* the description is linked to Daniel’s wish, the tone fits a friend, and it ends on time.

## 7. Traps in Task 8

| Trap | What it sounds like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Forgetting the listener cannot see | "As you can see, it’s like this one here." | Say everything: shape, size, colour, position |
| Vague words | "It’s a big weird thing with stuff on it." | Use comparisons: *as tall as a door, shaped like a boot* |
| Jumping around | Colour, then the top, then the price, then the bottom | Start with the big picture, then move in one direction |
| Only listing details | No greeting, no reason for the call | Set the scene and connect to the purpose |
| Describing the ordinary parts | Thirty seconds on the normal wheels | Spend most of your time on what is unusual |

<div class="strategy">

**Self-check after each practice task.**

- [ ] I greeted the listener and said why I was calling.
- [ ] I gave the big picture in one sentence before the details.
- [ ] I described the unusual feature clearly first.
- [ ] I used at least two comparisons (*as big as, shaped like, a bit like*).
- [ ] I used position words (*on top of, underneath, in the middle, attached to*).
- [ ] I connected the description to the listener’s need.
- [ ] I finished with a question or next step before time ran out.

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"I need to know the exact name of every part."** You will often not know the word for a part of a strange object; nobody does. Describe it instead: *"a long metal bar that sticks out of the side, a bit like an arm."* Explaining around a missing word is a real skill and sounds natural. Stopping silently to search for the word is what costs marks.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- **Setting the scene:** *I’m at..., and you won’t believe what I’m looking at. I just saw the strangest...*
- **Big picture:** *Basically, it’s a... Picture a regular..., but... Imagine a... that...*
- **Size and shape:** *about the size of..., as tall as..., twice as wide as..., shaped like..., round, oval, curved, pointed*
- **Position:** *on top of, underneath, in the middle, on the left-hand side, attached to, sticking out of, all the way around*
- **Material and colour:** *made of wood, metal or plastic; bright, pale, dark; striped, spotted, shiny*
- **When you don’t know the word:** *a kind of..., a thing that you use to..., something like a...*
- **Purpose and close:** *You said you wanted..., and this is perfect. Should I buy it? Want me to send you a photo?*

</div>

## 8. Drills

<div class="keypoints">

- Task 8: a strange picture, a listener who **cannot see it**, usually 30 seconds to prepare and 60 to speak.
- In planning, find the one unusual thing first, then three or four details in one direction, then the purpose.
- Template: **set the scene → big picture → details in order → link to the purpose and close**.
- Comparisons with familiar things (*as tall as a fridge, shaped like an egg*) and position words let the listener see it.
- If you do not know a word, describe the thing instead of stopping.
