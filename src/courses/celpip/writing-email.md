**Module 4 · about 22 minutes.** By the end you will know what Writing Task 1 shows on the screen, how to plan an email that covers every point in about three minutes, how to open, develop and close it, and you will have written three timed emails.

<div class="bigidea">The CELPIP email is a short, practical letter with a job to do. Cover every point in the task, in order, with real details and the right tone, and you have most of the score before you worry about fancy words.</div>

## 1. What Task 1 looks like

The screen shows a short situation and a list of points, usually **three**, that your email must cover. For example: *You recently moved into a new apartment. Write an email to your building manager. In your email: describe the problem; explain how it affects you; say what you want the manager to do.*

- **Time:** 27 minutes, with a timer on the screen.
- **Length:** 150 to 200 words. The screen counts them for you.
- **Typing:** you type into a box. There is **no spell check**.
- **Reader:** the task tells you who you are writing to: a company, a manager, a neighbour, a friend. That reader decides your tone (the next lesson covers tone in detail).

## 2. What it really tests

Task 1 tests whether you can use written English to **get something done** in everyday Canadian life: complain, request, thank, apologize, invite, explain. Raters use the four areas from the last lesson:

- **Content/Coherence:** ideas for each point, with details, in a logical order.
- **Vocabulary:** precise, suitable words.
- **Readability:** email format, paragraphs, connectors, grammar, spelling.
- **Task Fulfillment:** every point, the right tone, 150 to 200 words.

**Everyday analogy.** The task is a shopping list. If the list says milk, eggs and bread, coming home with the best milk in town and no bread is still a failed trip. Buy every item first; then choose good quality.

## 3. The method: plan, open, develop, close

<div class="steps">

<div class="step">

**Read and mark (1 minute).** Read the situation twice. Write the reader and the tone (`manager: formal`) and number the points 1, 2, 3.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Plan in notes (2 minutes).** For each point, write one idea and one detail: `1 tap drips, since Mon` `2 can’t sleep, bill up` `3 plumber by Fri`. Invent realistic details: names, dates, prices. You do not need to tell the truth; you need to sound real.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Open (1 minute).** A greeting that fits the reader, then one sentence that says why you are writing: *I am writing about the kitchen tap in apartment 304.*

</div>
<div class="step">

**Develop each point (about 15 minutes).** One short paragraph per point, in task order. Each paragraph: the idea, then one or two details or an example, joined with connectors.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Close and check (about 6 minutes).** A closing sentence that restates the request or thanks the reader, a sign-off and your name. Then check points, word count, verbs and spelling.

</div>
</div>

### Openings and closings that work

| Reader | Greeting | Purpose sentence | Closing line | Sign-off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Company, no name | Dear Sir or Madam, | I am writing to report a problem with... | I look forward to your reply. | Sincerely, / Yours truly, |
| Manager, landlord, teacher | Dear Ms. Patel, | I am writing to ask whether... | Thank you for considering my request. | Best regards, |
| Friend or relative | Hi Jordan, | I just wanted to let you know... | Can’t wait to hear from you! | Take care, / Talk soon, |

## 4. Worked example: from task to email

Task (original): *Your neighbour has helped you a lot since you moved in. Write an email to your neighbour. In your email: thank your neighbour; describe how the help made a difference; invite your neighbour to something.*

**Read and mark:** reader = neighbour I know a little; tone = semi-formal to friendly. Points: 1 thanks, 2 difference, 3 invite.

**Plan:** `1 thanks, shovelled driveway + lent drill` `2 night shift, no time, could sleep` `3 BBQ Sat 6 pm, bring nothing`.

Now two answers to the same task.

<div class="model">

**Answer A (around CLB 7)**

Hi Mr. Lavoie,

I want to say thank you for your help. You shovelled my driveway many times this winter and you also lent me your drill when I put up shelves.

Your help was very important for me. I work night shifts at the hospital, so I am very tired in the morning. When the driveway was clean I could go to sleep quickly. The drill helped me finish my shelves in one hour.

I want to invite you to a barbecue at my house on Saturday at 6 p.m. My wife will make chicken and salads. You don’t need to bring anything. Please tell me if you can come.

Thanks again,
Ravi

</div>

Notes on Answer A. **Content/Coherence:** all points covered with real details (night shifts, shelves, Saturday 6 p.m.); clear order. **Vocabulary:** correct but simple; "very" twice and "help" five times. **Readability:** clear paragraphs, mostly correct grammar, few connectors beyond "so" and "when". **Task Fulfillment:** complete, friendly tone fits, about 115 words, which is **under 150**. This answer would likely score higher with one more detail per point.

<div class="model">

**Answer B (around CLB 10)**

Hi Mr. Lavoie,

I hope you are enjoying the milder weather. I have been meaning to write for a while to thank you properly for everything you have done since we moved in.

You cleared our driveway after nearly every snowfall this winter, often before I was even home, and when I was struggling to put up shelves in the basement, you lent me your drill and showed me how to find the studs.

Honestly, it made a huge difference. I work night shifts at the hospital, so on snowy mornings I used to dread coming home to a buried driveway. Thanks to you, I could park and go straight to bed. The shelves, by the way, are still standing.

To say thank you, we would love to have you over for a barbecue this Saturday at around 6 p.m. Priya is making her famous tandoori chicken, so please just bring your appetite. Let me know if that works for you.

All the best,
Ravi

</div>

Notes on Answer B. **Content/Coherence:** each point is developed with vivid, specific details and a little personality (the shelves are still standing). **Vocabulary:** precise and natural (milder, dread, buried driveway, find the studs, bring your appetite). **Readability:** smooth connectors (and when, so, Thanks to you, To say thank you) and varied sentences. **Task Fulfillment:** warm, neighbourly tone; about 165 words.

## 5. Traps in Task 1

| Trap | What happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A forgotten point | The third bullet gets one rushed line or nothing | Plan all three points before writing |
| Copying the task | "I am writing to describe the problem with my order" | Show the problem in your own words |
| Vague content | "It was bad and I was not happy" | Add who, what, when, how much |
| Wrong tone | Slang to a company, stiff words to a friend | Decide the tone in your first minute |
| Too long | 260 words, no time to check | Aim for 170 to 190 words |

<div class="strategy">

**Self-check after every email.**

- [ ] My greeting and sign-off match the reader.
- [ ] My first sentence says why I am writing.
- [ ] Each task point has its own paragraph, in order, with at least one specific detail.
- [ ] My closing restates the request or thanks the reader.
- [ ] 150 to 200 words; verbs and spelling checked.

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"I must write about my real life."** No rater checks whether your neighbour exists or your coffee maker really broke. Invent clear, believable details if your real life does not fit the task. Inventing a detail takes five seconds; searching your memory can take five minutes.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- **Purpose:** *I am writing to (ask about, report, apply for, thank you for)...*
- **Explaining:** *What happened was..., The reason is that..., As a result,...*
- **Requesting:** *Could you please..., I would appreciate it if..., Would it be possible to...?*
- **Suggesting:** *Perhaps we could..., One option might be to..., How about...?*
- **Closing:** *Thank you for your help., I look forward to hearing from you., Please let me know if you need anything else.*

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Task 1: **an email in 27 minutes, 150 to 200 words**, usually covering **three points**, typed with no spell check.
- Plan first: reader, tone, and one idea plus one detail for each point.
- Structure: **greeting, purpose sentence, one paragraph per point, closing line, sign-off**.
- Specific invented details (dates, prices, names) beat vague true ones.
- Aim for 170 to 190 words and save time for checking.

</div>
