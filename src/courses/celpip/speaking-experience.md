**Module 5 · about 19 minutes.** By the end you will have a story shape that fits 60 seconds, the past tenses that make a story clear, two model answers to compare and three timed practice tasks. You will also answer 28 practice questions.

<div class="bigidea">Task 2 is a mini movie: set the scene, let something happen, show how it ended, and say what it meant to you. One specific event told well beats a general talk about your life.</div>

## 1. What Task 2 looks like

Task 2, **Talking about a Personal Experience**, asks you to describe something that happened to you. Typical topics: a time you helped someone, a time something went wrong, a memorable trip or celebration, a time you learned a new skill, a time you changed your mind.

- You usually get about **30 seconds to prepare** and **60 seconds to speak**. The test screen shows the exact time.
- The question often has **two or three parts**: what happened, how you felt, why it was important, what you learned.
- It is about **your** experience, so you speak in the first person (I, we) and mostly in the **past tense**.

**Everyday analogy.** Imagine a coworker asks on Monday, "How was your weekend?" and something really happened. You would not list every hour. You would say where you were, what went wrong or right, how it ended and how you felt. Sixty seconds is about the length of that story at the coffee machine.

## 2. What it really tests

- **Content/Coherence:** one clear event, told in order, with enough detail that the listener can picture it, and every part of the question answered.
- **Vocabulary:** precise words for actions and feelings (relieved, embarrassed, panicked, grateful) instead of "good" and "bad".
- **Listenability:** past tenses used correctly, clear time words (at first, then, suddenly, in the end), steady rhythm.
- **Task Fulfillment:** the story matches the question (a time **you** helped, not a time someone helped you), and covers feelings or importance when asked.

## 3. The structure: W-E-E-F

**W**hen and where, **E**vent, **E**nd, **F**eelings (or what you learned).

<div class="steps">

<div class="step">

**Prep (30 s): pick one event and check the parts.** Choose the first story that is specific and has a clear end. Note four key words: `Sudbury, Feb, -30 / car died on highway / trucker stopped, jump start / grateful → now carry kit`.

</div>
<div class="step">

**When and where (about 10 s).** One sentence that sets the scene and hooks the listener: "I will never forget the night my car broke down on a highway outside Sudbury, two winters ago."

</div>
<div class="step">

**Event (about 25 s).** What happened, in order, with two or three real details: the temperature, what you did first, what went wrong next. Use past continuous for the background and past simple for actions.

</div>
<div class="step">

**End (about 10 s).** How it was solved or how it finished: "In the end, a truck driver stopped and gave me a jump start."

</div>
<div class="step">

**Feelings and meaning (about 15 s).** How you felt, then and now, and what you learned: "I was so relieved I almost cried. Since then, I always keep an emergency kit in my trunk."

</div>
</div>

## 4. Worked example: choosing the right story

Task: *Talk about a time when you had to learn something new quickly. What was it, and how did you manage?*

In 30 seconds, three stories come to mind: learning to drive (it took a year, so not "quickly"), my first job at a café where I learned the till in one morning (quick, clear, with an end), and learning English in general (too big). The café story fits best.

Notes: `first job, café, Kamloops, age 19 / manager sick, alone at till / lunch rush, line to door / watched video, asked regular / felt proud`.

Thinking: the question has two parts: **what** I learned and **how I managed**. "How" means my steps: a video, a customer who helped. I will save the last 15 seconds for how I felt.

<div class="model">

**Model answer, CLB 7 level (about 110 words)**

I want to talk about my first job. It was in a café in Kamloops when I was nineteen. On my second day, the manager was sick, so I was alone. I did not know how to use the cash register. Then it was lunchtime and many customers came. There was a long line. I was very nervous. I watched a short video on my phone about the cash register. Also, one customer worked in a store and he helped me. After thirty minutes I could do it. In the end, the day was okay. I felt happy and proud, because I learned it very fast.

</div>

**Why it scores around this level.** *Content/Coherence:* one clear event in order, both parts of the question answered. *Vocabulary:* simple and correct, with some repetition (very, many). *Listenability:* correct past tenses, but almost every sentence is short and has the same shape. *Task Fulfillment:* on task and complete, though at a natural pace it ends a little early.

<div class="model">

**Model answer, CLB 10 level (about 150 words)**

The fastest I have ever had to learn something was on my second day at a café in Kamloops, when I was nineteen. I had barely seen the till, and that morning the manager called in sick, so suddenly I was running the front counter on my own.

By noon the lunch rush had started, and the line was almost out the door. I was panicking, honestly, but I did two things. First, while the coffee machine was running, I watched a two-minute video on my phone that explained how to process card payments. Then a regular customer, who turned out to work at a grocery store, noticed I was struggling and talked me through refunds.

By the end of the rush, I was doing it without thinking. I remember feeling exhausted but really proud. Looking back, that day taught me that asking for help is not a weakness; it is often the quickest way to learn.

</div>

**Why it scores higher.** *Content/Coherence:* a hook, a clear sequence (by noon, first, then, by the end), real details (two-minute video, refunds) and a reflection that answers "how did you manage" fully. *Vocabulary:* precise and natural (barely, called in sick, lunch rush, talked me through, struggling). *Listenability:* past perfect (had started), past continuous (was panicking), relative clauses (who turned out to...) and a mix of sentence lengths. *Task Fulfillment:* every part covered in about 60 seconds.

## 5. Traps in Task 2

| Trap | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Too general | "I often help people. Helping is important." | One specific day and place |
| Too big | The story of your whole immigration | One moment from it: the first day, the first problem |
| The wrong person | Task: a time you helped. Answer: a time you were helped | Check who does the action in the question |
| No ending | Time runs out mid-story | Plan the end and the feeling first; skip middle details if needed |
| Present tense story | "Then I go to the store and I see..." | Past simple for the main events |
| Missing part | No feelings or no "why it was important" | Keep the last 15 seconds for it |

<div class="strategy">

**Self-check after every Task 2 practice:**

- [ ] I told one specific event, with when and where in the first sentence.
- [ ] The story matched the question exactly (who did what).
- [ ] I used past tenses correctly (past simple for events, past continuous for background, past perfect for earlier actions).
- [ ] I used time words: at first, then, suddenly, luckily, in the end.
- [ ] I gave two or three details that help the listener picture it.
- [ ] I answered every part, including feelings or what I learned.
- [ ] I finished my story before the time ran out.

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"My story must be true and impressive."** Raters are judging your English and how well you do the task, not checking your life story or wanting a dramatic adventure. A small, ordinary event (a missed bus, a borrowed ladder, a first day at work) told clearly and completely scores better than an exciting story you cannot finish in 60 seconds.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- **Hooks:** *I will never forget the time... ; One experience that really stands out is... ; A few years ago, something happened that...*
- **Setting the scene:** *It was a freezing night in February. ; I was on my way to work when...*
- **Moving the story:** *At first... ; Then... ; Suddenly... ; Luckily... ; Unfortunately... ; By the time... ; In the end...*
- **Feelings:** *I was relieved / embarrassed / grateful / proud / frustrated / nervous. ; I could not believe it.*
- **Meaning:** *Looking back... ; Since then, I always... ; It taught me that... ; That was the moment I realized...*

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Task 2: usually **30 s to prepare, 60 s to speak**, about something that happened to you.
- Use **W-E-E-F**: When and where, Event, End, Feelings or meaning.
- Pick **one specific event** that matches the question; small and clear beats big and dramatic.
- Past simple for events, past continuous for background, past perfect for something earlier.
- Keep the last 15 seconds for feelings or what you learned, and finish the story before time runs out.
