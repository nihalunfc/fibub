**Module 6 · about 16 minutes.** By the end you will be able to run a full, timed CELPIP mock at home with the practice in this course, take a shorter mini mock on this page, and turn your results into a plan for the weeks you have left.

<div class="bigidea">A mock test is a dress rehearsal: same order, same clock, no pausing and no second chances. If you rehearse honestly, test day feels like the second time, not the first.</div>

## 1. What a full mock looks like

The real CELPIP-General has four sections in a fixed order, in one sitting of under three hours: **Listening** (46 to 55 minutes), **Reading** (43 to 56 minutes), **Writing** (53 minutes) and **Speaking** (about 15 minutes). The [test format lesson](/learn/courses/celpip/test-format/) has the details of every part. In a home mock you copy that order and those times as closely as you can.

Think of it like a fire drill. Nobody learns anything new during a fire drill, but everyone finds out where the slow stairs and the stuck doors are. A mock shows you the slow parts of your test: the part where you run out of time, the task where your mind goes blank, the moment your focus drops.

**Set up your space first:**

- A quiet room, a computer with a keyboard, and headphones (the drills read listening aloud with your browser's voice).
- Paper and a pen for notes, like the note-taking material at the test centre.
- Phone in another room. A kitchen timer or the timers on this page, nothing else.
- Spell check off. The writing pad on this site has none, just like the test.

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">

**Before you start.** Write the start time on your paper. Close every other tab. Decide now that you will not pause, rewind or look anything up.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Listening.** Play each clip once. Take short notes. Answer, then move on even if you are unsure. Mark unsure answers with a small "?" on your paper for the review later.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Reading.** Set a timer for each part. When it rings, guess any blank answers and move to the next part. In the real test you cannot go back to a finished part.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Writing.** Use the writing pad timers: 27 minutes for the email, then 26 for the survey. Keep the last three minutes of each for checking.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Speaking.** Do the eight tasks back to back with the speaking timers, recording yourself. Do not restart a task you did badly; the real test will not let you.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Stop and record.** Write down your listening and reading scores, save your two writing answers and keep the recordings for the next lesson.

</div>
</div>

<div class="strategy">

**Take the break that the test does not give you, but only once.** Between Reading and Writing, allow yourself two minutes to stand and drink water, then go straight on. The real test runs in one sitting, so train your body to keep going.

</div>

## 3. A worked example: Priya's first mock

Priya is aiming for CLB 9. She takes her first full mock on a Saturday morning. Here is what her notes say afterwards:

- Listening: 31 out of 38. Lost 4 in Part 5 (Discussion), all after minute 6 of the clip.
- Reading: 30 out of 38. Ran out of time in Part 4 and guessed the last 3.
- Writing: email 172 words, survey only 128 words because she spent too long planning.
- Speaking: Task 5 went blank after 30 seconds; the others felt fine.

**The thinking.** Priya does not decide "I need more practice everywhere". She looks for patterns. Part 5 losses all came late in a long clip, so the problem is focus over time, not vocabulary. Reading Part 4 is a timing problem. The short survey is a planning problem. Task 5 is a structure problem.

**Her plan for next week:** two long listening clips a day with notes ([note skills](/learn/courses/celpip/listening-strategies/)), Reading Part 4 with a strict 13-minute timer, a 4-minute planning limit for the survey from [the timing lesson](/learn/courses/celpip/writing-timing/), and three Task 5 timers using the comparing structure. One full mock again in ten days.

## 4. Mistakes to avoid in a home mock

<div class="myth">

**"A mock only counts if I get a high score."** A mock is a measuring tool, not a performance. A low score that shows you exactly where you lose points is worth more than a high score on items you have already seen. Use fresh items, keep the rules strict, and be glad when it finds a weak spot.

</div>

- **Pausing the clock.** It feels harmless, but it hides your real speed.
- **Using items you have done many times.** You will remember answers, not understand them. Use the first round of each drill and save repeats for later practice.
- **Skipping Speaking because you feel silly.** Speaking alone into a computer is exactly what the test asks. Record it.
- **Doing a mock every day.** A full mock takes nearly three hours and gives the most value when you spend time on the review. One a week in the last month is plenty; see the [study plan](/learn/courses/celpip/study-plan/).

## 6. Review: where the real learning happens

Spend at least as long reviewing as you spent on the mock. For each wrong or unsure answer, write one line: the part, what you chose, and **why** it was wrong. Use short reason labels so patterns jump out:

<div class="phrases">

- **Listening reasons:** *changed plan, missed negative, wrong speaker, number trap, lost focus late, did not predict*
- **Reading reasons:** *ran out of time, matched a word not the meaning, true but not asked, missed "except", guessed vocabulary*
- **Writing reasons:** *missed a point, too short, tone off, no paragraphs, repeated words, verb errors*
- **Speaking reasons:** *long pause, no example, off task, ran out of ideas, too fast, finished early*

</div>

Then count. If one label appears three times or more, go back to the lesson that trains it. Rate your writing and speaking with the checklist in the [next lesson](/learn/courses/celpip/score-yourself/).

<div class="keypoints">

- A mock copies the real test: same order, same clock, one play of each clip, no pausing.
- Use the schedule table: lp1 to lp6, rp1 to rp4, two writing pads, eight speaking timers.
- Take one full mock about a week before the test, and one or two earlier, not every day.
- Review for at least as long as the mock took, and label each mistake with its reason.
- Three or more mistakes with the same reason means: go back to that lesson.

</div>
