**Module 5 · about 22 minutes.** By the end you will know how Task 5 is built, you will have a six-part template that fits into 60 seconds, and you will have practised three timed tasks and 30 drill items.

<div class="bigidea">Task 5 is a friendly sales pitch: you show that you heard the other person’s choice, then you compare the two options point by point until your choice clearly wins for them.</div>

## 1. What Task 5 looks like

Task 5, **Comparing and Persuading**, comes in two screens.

- **Screen 1: choose.** You see two options, each with a short description and a few details (price, distance, features). You pick one. You do **not** speak on this screen. The usual time is about 60 seconds; the test screen shows the exact time.
- **Screen 2: persuade.** Now you see your choice next to the option that another person (a family member, friend or coworker) prefers. You usually get **60 seconds to prepare** and **60 seconds to speak**. Your job is to persuade that person that your choice is better.

Like every speaking task, your answer is rated on four areas: **Content/Coherence**, **Vocabulary**, **Listenability** (rhythm, pronunciation and intonation; pauses and self-correction; grammar and sentence structure; variety of sentence types) and **Task Fulfillment** (relevance, completeness, tone and length).

## 2. What it really tests

Task 5 checks three things at once:

1. **Comparing.** Can you use *cheaper than, closer to, more reliable, not as expensive as, the main difference is*? A list of facts about one option is description, not comparison.
2. **Persuading.** Can you link each fact to a benefit for the listener? "It has free parking" is a fact. "It has free parking, so you won’t spend twenty dollars a day like last time" persuades.
3. **Tone.** You are talking to someone you know who disagrees with you. You need to be warm and respectful, not bossy.

**Everyday analogy.** Think of choosing a restaurant with a friend who wants pizza when you want sushi. You would not say "Pizza is bad." You would say "Pizza sounds good, but the sushi place is closer and they have that lunch deal you liked." That small speech is exactly Task 5.

## 3. Choosing on Screen 1

Pick the option that gives you **more to say**, not the one you personally like. Look for the option with clear numbers and features you can turn into benefits. If both look equal, choose the first one and move on; the time you save is better spent planning on Screen 2. Remember that the second screen shows a different option from the other person, so you will compare your choice with that one.

## 4. The six-part template

This is the plan for your 60 seconds of speaking. Each part is one or two sentences.

<div class="steps">

<div class="step">

**1. Greet and acknowledge (about 5 seconds).** Use the person’s name or role and say something fair about their option: *"Hi Dad, I can see why you like the resort. The spa looks amazing."*

</div>
<div class="step">

**2. State your choice (about 5 seconds).** One clear sentence: *"But I really think the lake cabin is the better choice for us this year."*

</div>
<div class="step">

**3. Reason one, compared (about 15 seconds).** Use a comparative and a concrete detail from the screen, then the benefit: *"First, the cabin is four hundred dollars cheaper, which means we could use that money for Mom’s birthday trip."*

</div>
<div class="step">

**4. Reason two, compared (about 15 seconds).** A different kind of reason (time, comfort, health, family): *"Second, it’s only a two-hour drive, compared to a five-hour flight with two connections."*

</div>
<div class="step">

**5. Answer their concern (about 10 seconds).** Take the best feature of their option and show your choice covers it: *"I know you want to relax, and honestly, sitting on the dock with a coffee is just as relaxing as a spa."*

</div>
<div class="step">

**6. Close with a friendly push (about 5 seconds).** *"So what do you think? Shall I book the cabin tonight before it’s gone?"*

</div>
</div>

**Planning in 60 seconds.** Do not write sentences. Write six short lines: `ack: spa`, `me: cabin`, `R1: $400 cheaper → Mom bday`, `R2: 2 h drive vs 5 h flight`, `concern: relax → dock`, `close: book tonight`.

## 6. Model answers for practice task 1

These are original transcripts written for this course. Read them aloud with the timer to feel how much fits into 60 seconds (about 120 to 150 words).

<div class="model">

**Level: around CLB 7.**

Hi Sam. I know you like the resort in Mexico because it has a pool and a spa. But I think the cabin near Huntsville is better. First, it is cheaper. The cabin is eleven hundred dollars and the resort is almost four thousand dollars. So we save a lot of money. Second, the cabin is only two hours by car. The flight to Mexico is five hours and we have one stop, so it is very tiring for the kids. Also, at the cabin we have canoes and bikes, so we can be active together. There is no Wi-Fi, but I think this is good because we can talk to each other. So, I think the cabin is the best choice. What do you think?

</div>

**Why it scores around CLB 7.** *Content/Coherence:* clear choice and two reasons in order (*first, second, also*). *Vocabulary:* simple but accurate (*cheaper, tiring, active*). *Listenability:* short, mostly correct sentences, but many start the same way ("The cabin is..."), and there is little variety. *Task Fulfillment:* polite, on task and the right length, but the brother’s point (the spa) is mentioned and then dropped, and the money benefit is vague ("a lot of money").

<div class="model">

**Level: around CLB 10.**

Hey Sam, I totally get why the resort caught your eye. A pool and a spa after a long year sounds great. But hear me out, because I think the cabin near Huntsville is the smarter choice. For one thing, it’s about twenty-seven hundred dollars cheaper. That’s not a small difference; it’s basically the cost of the new laptop you’ve been saving for. On top of that, it’s a two-hour drive instead of a five-hour flight with a stopover, so we’d actually start relaxing on the first afternoon rather than sitting in an airport. And I know you’re worried there’s nothing to do without Wi-Fi, but with canoes and bikes included, the kids will be outside all day. Honestly, a swim in the lake is every bit as relaxing as a spa. So, what do you say? Should I book it tonight before someone else does?

</div>

**Why it scores around CLB 10.** *Content/Coherence:* every reason is a comparison with a personal benefit (*the laptop you’ve been saving for*), and the brother’s concern is answered, not ignored. *Vocabulary:* natural, precise phrases (*caught your eye, hear me out, stopover, every bit as*). *Listenability:* a mix of short and long sentences, a question at the end, and contractions that keep the rhythm natural. *Task Fulfillment:* friendly tone, all parts of the task covered, finishes on time with a clear call to action.

## 7. Traps in Task 5

| Trap | What it sounds like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Describing, not comparing | "The cabin has bikes. The cabin has canoes." | Put both options in one sentence: *X, while Y* |
| Ignoring the other option | Never mentioning what the listener prefers | Acknowledge it in part 1 and answer it in part 5 |
| Insulting their choice | "The resort is a stupid idea." | Admit one good point first: *I see why... but* |
| Reading the screen aloud | Listing every number in order | Pick two details and turn each into a benefit |
| Running out of time | Still on reason one at 45 seconds | Keep each part to one or two sentences; practise with the timer |

<div class="strategy">

**Self-check after each practice task.** Tick each line honestly, or listen to your recording.

- [ ] I greeted the person and said something fair about their option.
- [ ] I stated my choice clearly in one sentence.
- [ ] I used at least two comparatives (*cheaper than, closer, not as... as*).
- [ ] Each reason had a detail from the screen and a benefit for the listener.
- [ ] I answered their main concern.
- [ ] I finished with a friendly question or suggestion before time ran out.
- [ ] My tone stayed warm; I never called their idea bad.

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"The more facts I list, the higher my score."** A long list of features sounds like reading an advertisement, and it uses up your time without persuading anyone. Two well-developed comparisons, each with a clear benefit for the listener, are far stronger than six bare facts.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- **Acknowledging:** *I can see why you like..., I totally get the appeal of..., You’re right that... is great, but...*
- **Stating your choice:** *I really think... is the better option, Hear me out, I’d go with...*
- **Comparing:** *cheaper than, a lot closer to, not as expensive as, much more reliable, whereas, while, compared to, instead of, the main difference is...*
- **Adding reasons:** *For one thing, On top of that, What’s more, Another big plus is...*
- **Answering a concern:** *I know you’re worried about..., but; Even if..., we can still...; Actually, ... is just as... as...*
- **Closing:** *So what do you say? Shall we go with...? Can I book it tonight? Let’s give it a try.*

</div>

## 8. Drills

These drills train the building blocks: comparatives, tone, openings and closings, strong reasons and putting a persuasive sentence in order.

<div class="keypoints">

- Task 5 has two screens: **choose** (no speaking), then **persuade** someone who prefers a different option, usually 60 seconds to prepare and 60 to speak.
- Choose the option that gives you more to say, then plan six short lines, not full sentences.
- Template: **acknowledge → your choice → reason 1 compared → reason 2 compared → answer their concern → friendly close**.
- Turn every detail into a benefit for the listener, and use comparatives (*cheaper than, not as... as, whereas*).
- Stay warm and respectful: admit one good point about their option before you argue.

</div>
