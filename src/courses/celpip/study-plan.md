**Module 1 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will have a personal week-by-week plan, a quick idea of your level, and a set of daily habits that steadily raise it.

<div class="bigidea">A plan beats motivation. Decide your test date, your weekly hours and your weakest skill, and let the plan tell you what to do each week.</div>

## 1. Three numbers decide your plan

1. **Weeks until the test.** Most people need 4 to 12 weeks of steady work to move up one or two levels.
2. **Hours a week.** Five to eight hours, spread over most days, beats one long weekend session.
3. **Your weakest skill.** It gets about twice the time of the others, because every skill must reach your goal on its own.

## 2. Find your weakest skill

Answer this short mixed check honestly, without a dictionary. Then try the warm-up speaking and writing tasks below. Whichever felt hardest is probably your weakest skill.

## 4. What a good study week looks like

| Day | 45-60 minutes |
|---|---|
| Mon | One lesson in your focus skill + its drill |
| Tue | Focus skill drills + 15 minutes of Canadian news listening |
| Wed | One lesson in another skill + its drill |
| Thu | Focus skill: timed practice (speaking timer or writing pad) |
| Fri | Vocabulary: 15 new words from this week, used in sentences |
| Sat | Timed mixed practice; review every mistake |
| Sun | Rest, or light reading in English |

## 5. Daily habits that move your level

- **Listen actively** for 15 minutes a day to Canadian radio, news or podcasts. After each piece, say out loud who, what, where, when and why.
- **Read real Canadian texts**: city notices, workplace emails, news and opinion pieces. Note words you would like to use.
- **Speak every day**, even alone. Describe what you see out of the window for one minute, with the timer.
- **Write one short email a day** in your head or on paper: to a landlord, a manager, a friend.
- **Keep a mistake list.** Every wrong drill answer goes on it with the reason. Read it every Sunday.

<div class="myth">

**"I just need to do lots of practice tests."** Practice tests show your level but do not raise it on their own. Learn the skill first (the lessons), practise it in pieces (the drills), then test it under time (the mocks).

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Your plan comes from three numbers: weeks left, hours a week and your weakest skill.
- Give your weakest skill about twice the time; keep the others warm with one drill each.
- Study most days in short sessions; take a full mock near the start and the end.
- Daily habits: active listening, real Canadian reading, one minute of timed speaking, a mistake list.
