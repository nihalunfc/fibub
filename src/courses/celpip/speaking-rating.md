**Module 5 · about 16 minutes.** By the end you will know how the Speaking test runs, what raters listen for in each of the four rating areas, and a simple way to use your preparation time on every task. You will also try one timed task and answer 29 practice questions.

<div class="bigidea">Raters cannot see you; they only hear a recording. Every point you score comes from what reaches their ears: clear ideas, good words, easy-to-follow speech and a complete answer to the task you were given.</div>

## 1. What the Speaking test looks like

The Speaking test is the last part of CELPIP-General. It has **8 tasks** and takes **about 15 minutes**. There is no examiner in the room: you read (and sometimes see) each task on the screen, a preparation timer counts down, then a speaking timer starts and the computer **records** you. When the speaking time ends, the recording stops and the next task begins.

| Task | Name | Usual prep / speaking time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Giving Advice | 30 s / 90 s |
| 2 | Talking about a Personal Experience | 30 s / 60 s |
| 3 | Describing a Scene (a picture) | 30 s / 60 s |
| 4 | Making Predictions (same picture, what happens next) | 30 s / 60 s |
| 5 | Comparing and Persuading | see lesson 5.6 |
| 6 | Dealing with a Difficult Situation | see lesson 5.7 |
| 7 | Expressing Opinions | see lesson 5.8 |
| 8 | Describing an Unusual Situation | see lesson 5.9 |

These are the usual times; the test screen shows the exact time for every task. You can make short notes during the preparation time.

**Everyday analogy.** The Speaking test is like leaving eight voice messages in a row. The person who listens later cannot ask "sorry, what?" If your message is clear, complete and easy to follow, they understand it. If it is full of long pauses or wanders off topic, they give up. Raters are that listener.

## 2. The four rating areas

Raters listen to your recordings and judge four areas. Learn what each one means, because every tip in this module is aimed at one of them.

**Content/Coherence: your ideas and their order.** Do you have enough ideas? Are they explained with reasons, details and examples? Do they come in a clear order, with linking words so the listener sees how they connect? *Analogy:* a good tour guide. They do not just point at things; they explain why each thing matters and move from stop to stop in a clear route.

**Vocabulary: your words.** Are your words precise ("cluttered", not "not nice"), varied (not "good" ten times) and used correctly ("make a decision", not "do a decision")? *Analogy:* a cook's spice rack. More spices help only if you use the right one in the right dish.

**Listenability: how easy you are to follow.** This covers four things:
- **rhythm, pronunciation and intonation** (word stress, clear sounds, a voice that rises and falls);
- **pauses, interjections and self-correction** (long silences, lots of "um", restarting sentences);
- **grammar and sentence structure**;
- **variety of sentence types** (short and long, simple and complex, questions, "if" sentences).

*Analogy:* a smooth road. Small bumps are fine, but potholes every few metres make the drive exhausting.

**Task Fulfillment: doing the job you were given.** Is your answer **relevant** (on the topic), **complete** (every part of the task covered), in the right **tone** (friendly to a friend, polite to a manager) and the right **length** (using most of the time)? *Analogy:* a delivery driver. A beautiful drive is worthless if the parcel goes to the wrong house or half of it is missing.

## 3. The method: the same 30 seconds every time

For Tasks 1 to 4 you get about 30 seconds to prepare. That is enough for a plan, not a script. Use the same routine every time so you never freeze.

<div class="steps">

<div class="step">

**Seconds 0 to 5: read the task twice.** Find the **listener** (who am I talking to?) and every **part** of the question (advice about what? what happened and how did you feel?). Circle them in your mind.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Seconds 5 to 10: choose your main answer.** One clear choice: the advice, the story, the overview of the picture. Do not wait for a perfect idea. A simple idea explained well beats a clever idea explained badly.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Seconds 10 to 25: note two or three points with one detail each.** Key words only: `bike: cheap $200 / exercise / parking`. Writing full sentences wastes time and makes you read instead of speak.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Seconds 25 to 30: say your first sentence silently.** A ready first sentence stops the classic three-second silence at the start of the recording.

</div>
<div class="step">

**While speaking: follow your notes and watch the clock.** Move to the next point when you finish one. When about 10 seconds are left, wrap up with a closing sentence.

</div>
</div>

## 4. Worked example: two answers, four areas

Mini task (Task 1 style): *Your friend Omar has just moved to a new city and feels lonely. Give him advice.* Here are the openings of two answers, about 20 seconds each.

> **Answer A:** Um... Omar... uh, you should, um, go out. Go out is good. Because, um, lonely is bad. Yes. You can go out and, um... meet people. Meet people is good.

> **Answer B:** Omar, I know moving somewhere new can feel lonely, so here are two things I would do. First, join a club built around something you already enjoy, like the soccer league at the community centre. You would see the same people every week, which makes friendships much easier.

How would raters hear them?

- **Content/Coherence.** A has one vague idea ("go out") with no reason that explains anything. B gives a clear preview ("two things"), a specific idea and a reason with a result.
- **Vocabulary.** A repeats "good" and "go out". B uses "built around", "community centre", "friendships".
- **Listenability.** A has a filler in almost every phrase and broken grammar ("lonely is bad"). B flows, with a long sentence and a short one.
- **Task Fulfillment.** Both speak to Omar and stay on topic, but A will run out of ideas long before 90 seconds; B is set up to fill the time.

Notice that B uses no rare words. It scores better because it is **organized, specific and smooth**.

## 5. Common ways to lose points

| Problem | Area it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Silent first three seconds | Listenability | Prepare your first sentence in the last 5 seconds of prep |
| Talking to the rater ("In this task I will...") | Task Fulfillment (tone) | Speak to the person in the task, by name if given |
| Answering only half the question | Task Fulfillment (completeness) | Note every part of the task as a key word |
| One idea repeated in new words | Content/Coherence | Two or three different points, each with a detail |
| "Good, nice, bad" for everything | Vocabulary | Learn 2 or 3 precise words for each common topic |
| Restarting sentences to fix tiny errors | Listenability | Fix once, quickly, or just keep going |
| Stopping at 40 seconds of 90 | Task Fulfillment (length) | Plan one extra detail or example for each point |

<div class="strategy">

**Practise with a recorder, then listen as a rater.** Record one task a day with the timer below. Play it back and ask four questions, one per area: Were my ideas clear and in order? Did I repeat words? Where did I pause or say "um"? Did I answer every part, to the right person, for most of the time? Fix one thing in the next recording.

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"I need a Canadian accent to score high."** You do not. Pronunciation is part of Listenability, but the goal is **clear and easy to follow**, not sounding like you grew up in Toronto. Clear word stress, steady rhythm and few long pauses matter far more than your accent.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- **Buying thinking time (instead of "um"):** *Let me think... ; That is a good question. ; The main thing is...*
- **Previewing:** *I have two suggestions. ; Here is what I would do. ; Let me tell you about...*
- **Moving on:** *First... ; Another thing is... ; On top of that... ; Finally...*
- **Fixing a slip quickly:** *I mean... ; or rather...*
- **Closing:** *So, to sum up... ; All in all... ; Anyway, I hope that helps.*

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Speaking: **8 tasks, about 15 minutes, recorded on computer**; raters listen to the recordings later.
- Four rating areas: **Content/Coherence** (ideas and order), **Vocabulary** (words), **Listenability** (rhythm, pronunciation, pauses, grammar, sentence variety), **Task Fulfillment** (relevance, completeness, tone, length).
- Use prep time for a plan: listener and parts, main answer, two or three key-word points, first sentence ready.
- Simple ideas explained clearly beat clever ideas explained badly.
- Speak to the person in the task, cover every part and use most of the time.

</div>
