**Module 1 · about 15 minutes.** By the end you will know every part of the CELPIP-General test: what it asks, how many questions it has and how long you get. Keep this page as your map; every later lesson zooms into one part.

<div class="bigidea">CELPIP has four sections in a fixed order: Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking. Each one is split into short parts, and every part tests a different everyday skill.</div>

## 1. The whole test at a glance

<figure class="fig" data-fig="testmap" data-mode="celpip"><figcaption>The four sections, sized by time. Listening and Reading times vary a little because some unscored trial questions may be added.</figcaption></figure>

The official times are **Listening 46 to 55 minutes**, **Reading 43 to 56 minutes**, **Writing 53 minutes** and **Speaking 15 minutes**, all in one sitting of under three hours. Some test versions include a few **unscored** questions that are being tried out for future tests. You cannot tell which ones they are, so give every question your full effort.

## 2. Listening: 6 parts, 38 scored questions

| Part | Name | Questions | What you hear | What it tests |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Problem Solving | 8 | A conversation in which two people solve a problem, in sections | Following details and the decision people reach |
| 2 | Daily Life Conversation | 5 | A conversation about an everyday matter | Understanding the situation, plans and feelings |
| 3 | Listening for Information | 6 | Someone explaining or asking for information | Picking out facts, reasons and instructions |
| 4 | News Item | 5 | A short local news report | Main event, people involved, causes and results |
| 5 | Discussion | 8 | Three people discussing a topic, often with a video of the scene | Who thinks what, and how the discussion develops |
| 6 | Viewpoints | 6 | A longer talk with different opinions | Arguments, opinions and the speaker's purpose |

All questions are multiple choice. You hear the recording first, then see the questions, so you cannot read the questions in advance. Notes help a lot.

## 3. Reading: 4 parts, 38 scored questions

| Part | Name | Questions | What you read |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reading Correspondence | 11 | A personal or work letter or email, then a reply with gaps to fill |
| 2 | Reading to Apply a Diagram | 8 | A diagram (schedule, menu, flyer, map) and an email that refers to it |
| 3 | Reading for Information | 9 | A factual text in paragraphs; you match statements to the paragraph that says them |
| 4 | Reading for Viewpoints | 10 | An article with arguments, then a reader's comment with gaps to fill |

## 4. Writing: 2 tasks

| Task | Name | Time | Length | What you do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Writing an Email | 27 minutes | 150-200 words | Write an email about an everyday situation, usually covering three points |
| 2 | Responding to Survey Questions | 26 minutes | 150-200 words | Choose one of two options in a survey and explain why, with reasons |

You type your answers. There is **no spell check**, so the drills in this course turn it off too.

## 5. Speaking: 8 tasks

Each task gives you a short time to prepare, then a beep, then time to speak. A countdown shows on the screen. The timings below are the usual ones; the test screen always shows the exact time for each task.

| Task | Name | Prepare | Speak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Practice | A warm-up question (not scored) | 30 s | 60 s |
| 1 | Giving Advice | 30 s | 90 s |
| 2 | Talking about a Personal Experience | 30 s | 60 s |
| 3 | Describing a Scene | 30 s | 60 s |
| 4 | Making Predictions | 30 s | 60 s |
| 5 | Comparing and Persuading | 60 s | 60 s |
| 6 | Dealing with a Difficult Situation | 60 s | 60 s |
| 7 | Expressing Opinions | 30 s | 90 s |
| 8 | Describing an Unusual Situation | 30 s | 60 s |

Try the timer now with a warm-up question. Do not worry about quality yet; just feel how short 30 seconds of preparation is.

## 6. How each section is marked

Listening and Reading are marked by the computer: each correct answer adds to your score, and **wrong answers cost nothing**, so never leave a question blank. Writing and Speaking are rated by trained people on four areas, which Module 4 and Module 5 explain in detail:

- **Content and coherence**: good ideas, well organised, with examples.
- **Vocabulary**: a range of words, used precisely and correctly.
- **Readability** (writing) or **Listenability** (speaking): grammar, sentence variety, linking words, pronunciation and flow.
- **Task fulfilment**: answering every part of the task, with the right tone and length.

<div class="keypoints">

- Order: Listening (6 parts, 38 questions), Reading (4 parts, 38 questions), Writing (2 tasks), Speaking (8 tasks).
- Recordings play **once**; questions come after the recording.
- Writing: an email in 27 minutes and a survey response in 26 minutes, each **150-200 words**, typed without spell check.
- Speaking: 8 recorded tasks with 30-60 s to prepare and 60-90 s to speak.
- Wrong answers in Listening and Reading cost nothing: always answer.

</div>
