**Module 6 · about 14 minutes.** By the end you will have a day-by-day plan for the last seven days, a sleep routine that works, and a 15-minute warm-up for the morning of the test.

<div class="bigidea">The final week is for sharpening, not building. You keep your skills warm with short timed practice, fix only small things, and arrive rested.</div>

## 1. What the last week can and cannot do

By the last week, your level is mostly set. Seven days is not long enough to add a new CLB level, but it is long enough to lose a few points through tiredness, panic or careless timing, and long enough to win a few back by being sharp. Think of an athlete before a race: the hard training is done weeks earlier. The final days are light runs, good food and sleep, so the legs are fresh on the day.

What the final week **can** do:

- Make the timing automatic, so you never run out of time on the email or the survey.
- Fix one or two small, repeated mistakes from your mistake list.
- Get your listening ear and speaking voice used to working in English every day.
- Remove every practical worry (ID, route, time) so your mind is free.

What it **cannot** do: teach you a new grammar system, give you 500 new words, or make up for months without practice. Do not try. It only adds stress.

## 2. The plan, day by day

Count back from your test day (Day 0). The [test-day lesson](/learn/courses/celpip/test-day/) covers what happens at the centre; this plan covers the days before it.

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">

**Day 7: last full mock.** Take a complete, timed mock using the [mock test lesson](/learn/courses/celpip/mock-tests/). Do it at the same time of day as your real test if you can, so your brain gets used to working then.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Day 6: review the mock.** Label each mistake with its reason. Rate your writing and speaking with the [self-rating checklist](/learn/courses/celpip/score-yourself/). Choose your two "final fixes", for example "follow changed plans" and "add a time to every request".

</div>
<div class="step">

**Days 5 and 4: targeted practice.** About 60 to 90 minutes a day. One drill round from your weakest listening part and one from your weakest reading part. One timed email or survey. Three speaking tasks with the timer, recorded. Work on your two final fixes only.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Day 3: mixed and lighter.** About 45 minutes: one listening round, one reading round, two speaking tasks. Check your booking email: date, start time, address, ID.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Day 2: light touch.** About 30 minutes: read your mistake list and your phrase banks, do two speaking tasks and one listening round. Plan your route and travel time. Lay out your clothes.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Day 1, the day before: rest.** Twenty minutes at most: read your phrase banks aloud and do one easy speaking task. Then stop. Walk, cook, watch something in English for fun. ID by the door. Normal bedtime.

</div>
</div>

<div class="strategy">

**Practise at test pace, not study pace.** In the final week, every drill should be timed. You are no longer learning how to answer; you are training to answer well inside the clock.

</div>

## 3. A worked example: Mehdi's final week

Mehdi's test is on a Saturday at 9 a.m. His last mock (Day 7, the Saturday before, at 9 a.m.) shows: listening strong, reading Part 3 weak (6 of 9), survey answers a little short, Speaking Task 6 awkward.

**His two final fixes:** in Reading Part 3, read the statement first and find the matching paragraph's meaning, not its words; in the survey, plan for 4 minutes only.

**His week:** Sunday he reviews. Monday and Tuesday: one round of rp3 each day, one survey with a 4-minute planning limit, and three Task 6 timers. Wednesday: a mixed hour and he checks his booking. Thursday: half an hour and he tests the bus route at 8 a.m. to see the traffic. Friday: twenty minutes of phrase banks, a short walk, bed at his usual 11 p.m.

Notice what he does **not** do: he does not start a new vocabulary book, take a second mock on Wednesday, or stay up late on Friday "just to check one more thing".

## 4. Sleep, food and nerves

**Sleep.** Memory for what you practised settles while you sleep, and tiredness slows listening more than almost anything else. Keep the same bedtime and wake-up time for the whole week, close to the hours you will need on test day. If your test is early, start waking early from Day 5, not just on the last morning.

**Screens and caffeine.** Stop studying an hour before bed. If coffee or tea keeps you awake, have it only in the morning in the final week. On the test day, drink what you normally drink: this is not the day for an extra-large coffee.

**Food.** Eat a normal breakfast you know agrees with you. A test of nearly three hours runs past many people's snack time, and you cannot eat in the room, so eat well before you leave.

**Nerves.** Some nerves are normal and even useful: they keep you alert. If your heart is racing, try slow breathing: breathe in for 4 counts, out for 6, five times. Remind yourself that you have rehearsed this test already in your mocks.

<div class="myth">

**"If I study all night before the test, I will remember more."** A night of cramming trades a little extra knowledge for a tired brain that listens slowly, reads slowly and forgets words when speaking. Sleep helps your brain keep what you practised. The last evening is for rest.

</div>

## 5. The test-day morning warm-up (15 minutes)

Do this at home or on the way, at least an hour before your start time. It is like stretching before a run: easy, short, and only to get things moving.

| Minutes | Do this |
|---|---|
| 0 to 5 | Read a short news article aloud, slowly and clearly. Feel your mouth make the word endings. |
| 5 to 8 | Listen to one short English clip (a drill round or a podcast) and say the main idea in one sentence. |
| 8 to 12 | One speaking task with the timer below, out loud, at normal volume. |
| 12 to 15 | Read your two final fixes and five favourite phrases from your phrase banks. Then stop. |

- **Calm-down sentences for yourself:** *I have done this before in my mocks. One question at a time. Guess and move on.*
- **Speaking starters to have ready:** *If I were you, I would... ; I clearly remember a time when... ; In this picture, I can see... ; I would strongly recommend... ; In my opinion...*
- **Writing openers to have ready:** *I am writing to... ; I am writing regarding... ; In my view, Option A is the better choice because...*

## 6. Check your final-week judgement

<figure class="fig" data-fig="drill" data-set="m6-week" data-n="8"><figcaption>What would you do in the last week? Original situations.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="keypoints">

- The final week sharpens; it does not build. No new books, no big word lists.
- Day 7 full mock, Day 6 review, Days 5 to 2 shorter timed practice, Day 1 rest.
- Work on two "final fixes" from your mistake list, nothing more.
- Keep the same bedtime all week, close to the hours of your test day.
- On the morning, a 15-minute warm-up: read aloud, one clip, one speaking timer, your fixes.

</div>
