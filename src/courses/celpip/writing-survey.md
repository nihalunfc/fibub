**Module 4 · about 22 minutes.** By the end you will know what Writing Task 2 looks like, how to choose an option in under a minute, how to build two strong reasons with examples, and you will have written three timed survey answers.

<div class="bigidea">Task 2 is a short opinion with proof. Pick a side quickly, give two clear reasons, prove each one with a concrete example, and say your choice again at the end.</div>

## 1. What Task 2 looks like

The screen describes a situation, often a community, workplace or school decision, and then a survey question with **two options**. For example: *Your city has money for one project. Option A: build a new public library branch. Option B: build an outdoor skating rink.* You choose one and explain why.

- **Time:** 26 minutes.
- **Length:** 150 to 200 words.
- **Format:** **not an email.** No greeting, no sign-off. Just your answer in paragraphs.
- **Typing:** no spell check; the screen shows a word count.

## 2. What it really tests

Task 2 checks whether you can **take a position and support it** in clear written English. The same four rating areas apply:

- **Content/Coherence:** two or three good reasons, each explained and supported with examples or details, in a clear order.
- **Vocabulary:** precise words for benefits, costs and effects.
- **Readability:** paragraphs, connectors (Firstly, Another reason, For example, On the other hand), correct grammar and spelling.
- **Task Fulfillment:** a clear choice, everything relevant to the question, a suitable tone, 150 to 200 words.

**Everyday analogy.** Task 2 is like recommending a restaurant to a friend. You do not list every restaurant in town. You say "Go to Rosa’s", give two reasons ("the portions are huge and it is open late"), tell a quick story ("I took my parents there last month and we still talk about the lasagna"), and say it again: "Seriously, try Rosa’s."

Step through a body paragraph and watch each move (Point, Explain, Example, Link) add one sentence.

<div class="steps" data-fig="@lang-peel-steps">
<div class="step"><p><strong>Point.</strong> Open with one sentence that states the idea of the paragraph.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><strong>Explain.</strong> Say why it is true or how it works, in one or two sentences.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><strong>Example.</strong> Give one specific example that proves the point.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><strong>Link.</strong> Close by tying the paragraph back to the question.</p></div>
</div>

Notice that the Point and the Link say nearly the same thing; the Explain and Example in between do the proving.

## 3. The method: choose, reason, prove, wrap up

<div class="steps">

<div class="step">

**Choose in under a minute.** Read both options. Ask: for which one can I think of two concrete reasons fastest? Choose that one, even if you would vote differently in real life.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Plan two reasons and two examples (2 minutes).** Write: `A library. R1 free for all, seniors/families. Ex: my mother, ESL books. R2 all-year, rink only winter. Ex: summer reading club.`

</div>
<div class="step">

**Open with your choice (1 to 2 sentences).** Paraphrase the question, state your choice and preview your reasons: *If I had to choose, I would support a new library branch, mainly because it would serve everyone, all year.*

</div>
<div class="step">

**Write one paragraph per reason.** Topic sentence (the reason), explanation (why it matters, who benefits), example (a short real-sounding story or fact). Mention the other option briefly in one paragraph to show why yours is stronger.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Wrap up and check.** One or two sentences that restate the choice: *For these reasons, ...* Then check word count, verbs and spelling.

</div>
</div>

## 4. Worked example: two levels

Task (original): *Your workplace wants to improve staff well-being and can afford one of these. Option A: a free on-site gym. Option B: one extra paid day off every month. Choose the option you prefer and explain why.*

**Choose:** B is easiest: rest, family time, everyone benefits. **Plan:** `B. R1 everyone benefits (not all like gym). Ex: Lena, bad knee. R2 real rest, fewer sick days. Ex: me, appointments. Other side: gym nice but only some use.`

<div class="model">

**Answer A (around CLB 7)**

I think Option B is better. An extra day off every month is very good for workers.

First, everyone can use a day off. Not all people like the gym. For example, my coworker Lena has a bad knee and she can’t do exercise. With a day off she can rest or see her family. So Option B is good for all workers, not only some workers.

Second, people need rest. When I am tired I make mistakes at work. If I have one more day off, I can go to the doctor or do my shopping, and I don’t need to take a sick day. This is good for the company too.

The gym is a nice idea but many people will not use it. So I choose Option B.

</div>

Notes on Answer A. **Content/Coherence:** a clear choice, two reasons, an example for each, and a mention of the other side: good structure. **Vocabulary:** simple and repeated ("good" four times, "workers"). **Readability:** clear paragraphs and basic connectors (First, Second, For example, So); a few short, choppy sentences. **Task Fulfillment:** relevant, no email format, about 130 words, so **under 150**. One more detail in each reason would fix it.

<div class="model">

**Answer B (around CLB 10)**

If I had to choose, I would strongly recommend Option B, an extra paid day off each month. In my view, it would improve the well-being of far more employees than a gym.

The main reason is that a day off benefits everyone, whatever their age or health. A gym only helps people who already enjoy working out. My colleague Lena, for instance, has a knee injury and cannot use most equipment, but she would gladly spend an extra day hiking gently with her kids or simply resting.

Another important reason is that regular rest reduces stress and absences. Many of us use sick days for dentist appointments or car repairs because there is no other time. With one guaranteed day each month, staff could handle these tasks without feeling guilty, and they would return to work more focused.

Admittedly, an on-site gym would be convenient for some people. However, its benefits would be limited to a small group, while a day off would reach the entire team. For these reasons, Option B is clearly the better investment.

</div>

Notes on Answer B. **Content/Coherence:** a clear position, two well-developed reasons with specific examples, and a fair answer to the other side. **Vocabulary:** precise (well-being, absences, guaranteed, convenient, investment). **Readability:** strong connectors (The main reason, for instance, Another important reason, Admittedly, However, For these reasons) and varied sentence length. **Task Fulfillment:** fully relevant, suitable tone, about 180 words.

## 5. Traps in Task 2

| Trap | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Sitting on the fence | "Both options are good." | Choose one in the first sentence |
| Email format | "Dear Survey Team, ... Sincerely" | Start straight with your choice |
| Too many thin reasons | Six reasons, one line each | Two or three reasons, each explained with an example |
| Only personal taste | "I just like gyms." | Explain who benefits and how |
| Changing your mind | Ending with "but maybe A is better" | Restate your choice clearly |
| Spending ages choosing | Five minutes deciding | Pick the side you can support fastest |

<div class="strategy">

**Survey self-check.**

- [ ] My first sentence states one option clearly (no greeting).
- [ ] Two or three reasons, each in its own paragraph with an explanation and an example.
- [ ] I mention the other option briefly and show why mine is better.
- [ ] My last sentence restates my choice.
- [ ] 150 to 200 words; connectors, verbs and spelling checked.

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"One option is the correct answer, so I must guess what the rater wants."** Both options are designed to be reasonable. You are scored on how clearly you choose and how well you support your choice, not on which side you pick.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- **Stating your choice:** *If I had to choose, I would..., In my view, Option A is..., I would strongly recommend...*
- **Adding reasons:** *The main reason is that..., Another important reason is..., In addition,...*
- **Giving examples:** *For instance,..., For example,..., Take my neighbour, who...*
- **Answering the other side:** *Admittedly,..., Although Option B has its merits,..., While some people may prefer...*
- **Concluding:** *For these reasons,..., Overall,..., That is why I believe...*

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Task 2: **26 minutes, 150 to 200 words**, **choose one of two options and give reasons**, **no email format**.
- Choose fast: pick the side you can support with two concrete reasons.
- Structure: **choice + preview, reason 1 + example, reason 2 + example, short conclusion**.
- Mention the other option once to show why yours is stronger.
- There is no right option; support is what scores.

</div>
