**Module 4 · about 18 minutes.** By the end you will have a minute-by-minute plan for both writing tasks, a typing speed target, a fast checking routine for a test with no spell check, and you will have done two full timed tasks back to back.

<div class="bigidea">Good writers lose marks when the clock wins. A fixed plan (read, plan, write, check) with a halfway check on the clock makes sure every point is covered and every answer is checked.</div>

## 2. What timing really affects

Running out of time usually hurts **Task Fulfillment** (a missing point, a missing conclusion, too few words) and **Readability** (no time to catch errors). Both are avoidable with a plan.

**Everyday analogy.** Think of cooking dinner for guests arriving at 7. If you spend an hour on the starter, the main course is raw. Good cooks plan backward: the main course goes in at 6, the salad at 6:45, the table is set by 6:55. A writing task is the same: you plan the time for each part before you begin.

## 3. The minute-by-minute plan

<div class="steps">

<div class="step">

**Minutes 0 to 3: read and plan.** Read the task twice. Note the reader and tone (email) or your choice (survey). Write one idea and one detail per point or reason in short notes.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Minutes 3 to 12: opening and the first point or reason.** Write steadily. Do not stop for small typos; mark a doubtful word in your head and keep going.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Minute 13: halfway check.** Look at the clock and the word count. You should have the opening and first body paragraph done (about 70 to 90 words). If you are behind, keep the next paragraphs shorter.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Minutes 13 to 21: remaining points and the closing.** Finish every point, then a closing line (email) or conclusion (survey). Aim for about 170 to 190 words.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Minutes 21 to 26: the final check.** Points and word count, then verbs, then sentence boundaries, then spelling. Use the last minute as a spare. For the 26-minute survey, take the spare minute out of the writing time.

</div>
</div>

### Typing speed

To write 180 words in about 18 minutes you need **10 words a minute** of thinking and typing. Most people think more slowly than they type, so aim for comfortable copying speed of 30 words a minute or more. If you type slower, practise 10 minutes a day: copying a paragraph, then writing a short timed answer.

## 4. Worked example: a timed email, minute by minute

Task (original): *Your company is moving to a new office. Write an email to your manager. In your email: say what you like about the new office; describe one concern; suggest a solution.*

**Minute 1 to 3 (notes):** `manager: semi-formal. 1 near subway, bright kitchen. 2 no bike parking, I cycle. 3 rack in garage / city rack form.`

**Minute 12 (halfway):** the greeting, purpose sentence and paragraph on point 1 are done; 85 words. On track.

**Minute 21:** all three points and the closing are done; 163 words.

**Minute 21 to 26 (check):** points 1, 2, 3 all present; count 163. Verbs: *the office have* → *has*. Boundaries: *I cycle every day, there is no rack* → *..., but there is no rack*. Spelling: *recieve* → *receive*, *untill* → *until*. Finished with a minute to spare.

<div class="model">

**The finished email (around CLB 9)**

Hi Ms. Fraser,

Thank you for sharing the plans for our new office on Granville Street. I wanted to give you some quick feedback, as you asked.

First, I really like the location. It is only two minutes from the subway station, which will cut my commute by about twenty minutes. The open kitchen with large windows also looks bright and much more comfortable than our current lunch room.

However, I have one concern. I cycle to work every day, but there is no secure bike parking in the new building. Several other colleagues cycle as well, and leaving bikes on the street overnight is risky.

One solution might be to install a small bike rack in the underground garage. Alternatively, the city has a program that provides free racks for businesses, and I would be happy to fill in the application form.

Thanks for considering this. I am looking forward to the move.

Best regards,
Daniel

</div>

Notes: **Task Fulfillment:** all three points, semi-formal tone, about 160 words. **Content/Coherence:** clear details for each point. **Vocabulary:** precise (secure bike parking, commute, alternatively). **Readability:** clear paragraphs and connectors; errors caught in the check.

## 5. Timing traps

| Trap | What happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No plan | A point is forgotten or the order is messy | Always spend 2 to 3 minutes on notes |
| Perfect first sentence | Five minutes on the opening | Use a ready-made purpose sentence |
| Fixing typos while writing | Writing flow stops again and again | Keep going; fix in the final check |
| No halfway check | Discover at minute 24 that a point is missing | Look at clock and count at minute 13 |
| Writing to the last second | No check at all | Stop writing by minute 21 or 22 |

<div class="strategy">

**The final check (5 minutes), in this order.**

- [ ] Every task point covered (email) or a clear choice with reasons (survey); a closing or conclusion.
- [ ] Word count between 150 and 200.
- [ ] Verbs: agreement and tense.
- [ ] Sentence boundaries: no fragments, no comma splices.
- [ ] Spelling: my personal list of tricky words and typical typos.

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"If I write faster I will score higher because I will write more."** Above 200 words you are outside the range, and the extra time comes out of your check. A complete, checked answer of 170 to 190 words is the goal, not the longest answer possible.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- **Ready-made purpose sentences:** *I am writing to..., I wanted to let you know..., If I had to choose, I would...*
- **Ready-made closings:** *Thank you for considering my request., For these reasons, I believe...*
- **Fast connectors to keep the flow:** *First, In addition, However, For example, As a result*

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Email **27 minutes**, survey **26 minutes**, each **150 to 200 words**, typed with **no spell check**.
- Plan: **3 minutes notes, about 18 minutes writing, 5 minutes checking**, one minute spare.
- Check the clock and word count **at halfway** (about minute 13).
- Final check order: **points and count, verbs, sentence boundaries, spelling**.
- Build typing speed with 10 minutes of practice a day before the test.
