**Module 5 · about 20 minutes.** By the end you will have a structure that fills 90 seconds every time, the grammar of giving advice, two model answers to compare, and three timed practice tasks. You will also answer 29 practice questions.

<div class="bigidea">Task 1 is a friendly phone call with a plan: say your advice, give two or three reasons that each come with a real detail, and end warmly. Structure, not clever words, fills the 90 seconds.</div>

## 1. What Task 1 looks like

Task 1, **Giving Advice**, is the first speaking task. The screen describes a situation: someone you know (a friend, a family member, a coworker, a neighbour) has a problem or a decision to make, and you give them advice.

- You usually get about **30 seconds to prepare** and **90 seconds to speak**. The test screen shows the exact time.
- It is the **longest** of the first four tasks, so running out of things to say is the biggest risk.
- Sometimes the task offers a choice or names two things to cover ("where to live and how to get to work"). Cover every part.

**Everyday analogy.** Think of a friend calling you because they are stuck. You would not read them a list. You would say "Okay, here is what I would do", explain why, maybe tell them what happened when your cousin did the same thing, and say "let me know how it goes". That is the shape of a strong Task 1 answer.

## 2. What it really tests

Task 1 checks that you can **advise someone clearly and kindly for a full minute and a half**. In the four rating areas:

- **Content/Coherence:** clear advice, real reasons, details or examples, and a logical order (first, also, finally).
- **Vocabulary:** precise words for the topic (budget, commute, flexible schedule), not "good" and "nice" over and over.
- **Listenability:** advice grammar (should, could, if I were you), smooth linking, few long pauses.
- **Task Fulfillment:** you speak **to the person** with a friendly tone, cover every part and use most of the 90 seconds.

## 3. The structure: A-R-R-W

Use the same frame every time: **A**dvice, **R**eason 1, **R**eason 2, **W**rap-up.

<div class="steps">

<div class="step">

**Prep (30 s): listener, parts, advice.** Who are you talking to, and what exactly do they need advice about? Pick one clear piece of advice (or one choice) and two reasons. Notes in key words: `Omar / join club / meet same people weekly / soccer at comm. centre / volunteer`.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Opening (about 10 s): greet, show you understand, give the advice.** "Omar, I know moving to a new city can feel lonely, so here are two things I would do."

</div>
<div class="step">

**Reason 1 (about 30 s): point, why, detail.** "First, join a club... That way you see the same people every week... For example, the soccer league at the community centre plays every Tuesday."

</div>
<div class="step">

**Reason 2 (about 30 s): point, why, detail.** "Another thing is volunteering... It gives you something useful to do on weekends, and you meet people who care about the same things."

</div>
<div class="step">

**Wrap-up (about 10 s): repeat the advice in a few words, end warmly.** "So, sign up for something this week and say yes to invitations. Call me and tell me how it goes!"

</div>
</div>

If you finish early, add a **warning** ("Just do not sign up for too much at once") or a **quick personal example** ("When I moved to Regina, the running club changed everything for me").

## 4. Worked example: from notes to answer

Task: *Your sister Priya has been offered a better-paid job, but it is a ninety-minute commute each way. She asks for your advice.*

**Prep notes (30 s):** `Priya / take job? → YES if can work from home 2 days / ask HR / train: read, rest / try 3 months`.

Thinking: the task asks for advice on one decision. I will choose "take it, but with conditions". Reason 1: ask for work-from-home days. Reason 2: use the train time well. Wrap-up: try it for three months. Each reason gets a detail (two days a week, reading, a fixed review date). That is enough for 90 seconds.

Now compare two full answers.

<div class="model">

**Model answer, CLB 7 level (about 130 words)**

Priya, congratulations on the job offer! I think you should take it, but first you should ask some questions. First, ask the company if you can work from home some days. If you work from home two days a week, you only travel three days. That is much better. Also, the commute is long, but you can use the time. You can read on the train or listen to podcasts. Many people do this and they are happy. And the salary is higher, so you can save money for your house. But you should try it and see. If it is too tiring after a few months, you can look for another job. So my advice is, take the job, ask about working from home, and try it. Good luck!

</div>

**Why it scores around this level.** *Content/Coherence:* clear advice and two reasons in order (first, also), with a third added at the end. *Vocabulary:* clear but basic (good, happy, tiring). *Listenability:* mostly short, simple sentences in the same pattern ("you can... you can..."). *Task Fulfillment:* speaks to Priya, friendly, on task, but at a natural pace it fills only about 60 to 70 of the 90 seconds.

<div class="model">

**Model answer, CLB 10 level (about 200 words)**

Priya, first of all, congratulations: a raise like that is a big deal. My honest advice is to accept the offer, but only after you have negotiated a couple of things, because a ninety-minute commute each way could wear you down fast.

The most important thing is to ask whether you can work from home part of the week. Plenty of companies allow it now, and if you only made the trip three days a week instead of five, you would get back about six hours every week. That alone would make the difference between exhausted and manageable.

On top of that, think about how you would use the travel time. If you take the train rather than drive, those ninety minutes stop being wasted time. You could finally get through those books you keep buying, or answer emails so your workday is shorter.

Finally, give yourself a deadline. Try it for three months, and then sit down and ask yourself honestly whether the extra money is worth the time. If it is not, you will have more experience on your résumé and you can look for something closer.

So, negotiate first, then give it a fair try. Call me after you talk to them!

</div>

**Why it scores higher.** *Content/Coherence:* three developed points, each with a reason and a concrete result (six hours a week, books, a three-month review), and clear signposts (the most important thing, on top of that, finally). *Vocabulary:* precise and natural (negotiated, wear you down, manageable, a fair try). *Listenability:* a mix of long and short sentences, conditionals ("if you only made the trip three days a week, you would...") and no wasted words. *Task Fulfillment:* warm tone for a sister, every part covered, about 90 seconds at a relaxed pace.

## 5. Traps in Task 1

| Trap | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Talking to the rater | "In this task I will give advice to my sister." | Talk to Priya: "Priya, congratulations..." |
| Sitting on the fence | "There are good and bad points. It depends." | Choose. You can add a condition: "take it, but..." |
| A list with no support | "It is cheap, easy, fast, fun, healthy." | Fewer points, each with why and an example |
| Wrong tone | "Dude, just do it" to an older neighbour | Friendly for friends, polite and patient for people you know less |
| Half the task | Talking about housing when the task said housing and transport | Note every part in prep and tick it off |
| A long story about yourself | 60 seconds about your own move | A personal example is support, one or two sentences |

<div class="strategy">

**Self-check after every Task 1 practice:**

- [ ] I spoke to the person, by name, in a friendly or polite tone.
- [ ] My advice was clear in the first 10 seconds.
- [ ] I gave two or three reasons, each with a detail, example or result.
- [ ] I used linking words (first, another thing, that way, otherwise, finally).
- [ ] I covered every part of the task.
- [ ] I used advice grammar correctly (should + verb, if I were you I would, why don't you).
- [ ] I spoke for at least 75 of the 90 seconds and ended with a wrap-up, not silence.

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"More advice is better, so I should list as many ideas as possible."** Raters reward ideas that are **explained**, not counted. Seven one-word reasons sound like a shopping list and use almost no grammar. Two or three reasons, each with "because" and "for example", show much more of your English and fill the time naturally.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- **Opening:** *I know this is a big decision, so here is what I would do. ; Congratulations! My honest advice is... ; I am sorry to hear that. Let me share a few ideas.*
- **Giving advice (strong):** *You should... ; The most important thing is to... ; You had better... (for warnings)*
- **Giving advice (soft):** *If I were you, I would... ; Why don't you...? ; It might be a good idea to... ; Have you thought about...?*
- **Reasons and results:** *That way... ; This means... ; so you would... ; Otherwise...*
- **Examples:** *For example... ; When my cousin did this... ; Just imagine...*
- **Wrap-up:** *So, to sum up... ; Whatever you decide... ; Let me know how it goes. Good luck!*

</div>

## 7. Practice: the language of advice

<figure class="fig" data-fig="drill" data-set="s-advice" data-n="8"><figcaption>29 questions: tone, openings and closings, linking words, advice grammar, support, what is missing, word order and word stress.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="keypoints">

- Task 1: usually **30 s to prepare, 90 s to speak**, advising someone you know.
- Use **A-R-R-W**: Advice, Reason 1, Reason 2, Wrap-up; each reason gets a why and a detail.
- Give your advice in the first 10 seconds, speaking **to the person**.
- Advice grammar: **should + verb**, **if I were you, I would**, **why don't you**, **suggest + -ing**.
- If you finish early, add a warning or a short personal example; end with a warm wrap-up, never silence.

</div>
