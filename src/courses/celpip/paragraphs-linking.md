**Module 4 · about 18 minutes.** By the end you will be able to build a clear paragraph, choose the right linking word for each job, avoid the common connector mistakes, and turn a block of text into an answer that is easy to follow.

<div class="bigidea">Paragraphs show the reader the shape of your answer; linking words show how each idea connects to the next. Together they turn a list of sentences into a clear message.</div>

## 1. Where paragraphs and connectors are rated

Both CELPIP writing tasks are rated on **Readability**, which includes *format and paragraphing* and *connectors*, together with grammar, sentence structure, spelling and punctuation. Organization also matters under **Content/Coherence**. So clear paragraphs and accurate linking words help you in two areas at once.

On the test, you type into a plain box. Press Enter twice to leave a **blank line** between paragraphs. That is the clearest signal of structure.

## 2. What a good paragraph looks like

A good CELPIP paragraph is short (2 to 5 sentences) and does one job:

1. **Topic sentence:** the main idea. *The main advantage of the garden is that it would bring neighbours together.*
2. **Explanation:** why or how. *Residents would meet while working on their plots and share tools and tips.*
3. **Example or detail:** proof. *On my street, a small shared flower bed has already led to two block parties.*
4. **Link (optional):** a sentence that ties back or leads on. *This kind of connection is exactly what our area needs.*

**Everyday analogy.** A paragraph is a box when you move house. Each box holds one room’s things and has a label on top (the topic sentence). Connectors are the arrows on the boxes: *this way up, kitchen, fragile*. Without labels and arrows, everything arrives but nobody knows where it goes.

## 3. The method: build, link, check

<div class="steps">

<div class="step">

**Plan one paragraph per job.** Email: opening, one per task point, closing. Survey: choice, one per reason, conclusion. That is usually 4 or 5 paragraphs.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Start each body paragraph with a topic sentence.** Tell the reader what the paragraph is about before giving details.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Choose the connector for the real link.** Ask: is the next idea an addition, a contrast, a reason, a result, an example or a sequence? Pick from that row of the table below.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Use reference words too.** *This, these, it, such, the problem* point back to earlier ideas and make the text flow without repeating nouns.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Check for overuse.** If one connector appears more than twice, swap one or remove it. Not every sentence needs a connector.

</div>
</div>

### Linking words by job

| Job | Between sentences | Inside one sentence |
|---|---|---|
| Add | In addition, Furthermore, Moreover, Also | and, as well as, not only ... but also |
| Contrast | However, On the other hand, In contrast, Nevertheless | but, although, while, whereas, despite + noun |
| Reason | The reason is that, This is because | because, since, as, due to + noun |
| Result | As a result, Therefore, Consequently, That is why | so, which means that |
| Example | For example, For instance | such as, like, including |
| Sequence | First, Then, After that, Finally | before, after, when, once |
| Condition | Otherwise, In that case | if, unless, as long as |
| Conclusion | For these reasons, Overall, In short | |

Punctuation note: connectors that start a sentence (*However, As a result*) are followed by a comma. Words like *because, although, if* join two clauses into one sentence; they cannot stand alone with a full stop after a single clause.

## 4. Worked example: before and after

**Before** (one block, few connectors):

*I think the city should build bike lanes. Bike lanes are safe. Many students cycle. Cars drive fast on King Street. My son was nearly hit last year. Bike lanes are cheap. Parking is expensive to build. A parking garage costs millions. Bike lanes cost less. Bike lanes are better.*

Every sentence is correct, but the reader must work out how the ideas connect. There are no paragraphs and "bike lanes" repeats seven times.

**After:**

<div class="model">

If I had to choose, I would support new bike lanes rather than more downtown parking.

The main reason is safety. Many students cycle to the college along King Street, where cars often drive fast. In fact, my son was nearly hit there last year. Protected lanes would separate cyclists from traffic, so accidents like this would be far less likely.

In addition, bike lanes are much cheaper. A new parking garage can cost millions of dollars, whereas painted and protected lanes cost a fraction of that. As a result, the city could spend the savings on other services.

For these reasons, I believe bike lanes are the smarter choice for our city.

</div>

What changed: four paragraphs with a blank line between them; topic sentences (*The main reason is safety*, *In addition, bike lanes are much cheaper*); connectors that match the real link (*where, In fact, so, whereas, As a result, For these reasons*); and reference words (*there, this, that*) replacing repetition. Readability and Content/Coherence both rise, with almost the same ideas.

## 5. Connector traps

| Trap | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong meaning | *I love the area. Therefore, it is noisy.* | *However, it is noisy.* |
| Fragment | *Because the bus was late.* | *I was late because the bus was late.* |
| Comma splice | *The rent is high, I cannot afford it.* | *The rent is high, so I cannot afford it.* |
| Despite + clause | *Despite it was raining, we went.* | *Despite the rain, we went.* / *Although it was raining, we went.* |
| Same connector everywhere | *Moreover ... Moreover ... Moreover* | Vary, or let some sentences flow without one |
| On the contrary for contrast | *Option A is cheap. On the contrary, B is fast.* | *On the other hand, B is fast.* |

<div class="strategy">

**Paragraph and linking self-check.**

- [ ] 4 or 5 paragraphs, each with a blank line before it.
- [ ] Every body paragraph starts with a topic sentence.
- [ ] Each connector matches the real link (add, contrast, reason, result, example).
- [ ] No connector used more than twice.
- [ ] No fragments starting with because or although; no comma splices.

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"More connectors means a higher score."** Raters look for connectors used accurately and naturally. A connector in every sentence, or one with the wrong meaning, makes writing harder to read. Use them where the link needs to be shown.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- **Topic sentences:** *The main advantage is..., The biggest problem is..., Another reason is...*
- **Pointing back:** *This means that..., Because of this,..., Such a change would...*
- **Showing emphasis:** *In fact,..., Most importantly,..., Above all,...*
- **Conceding:** *Admittedly,..., It is true that..., but...*

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Paragraphs and connectors are part of **Readability**; organization also counts in **Content/Coherence**.
- One paragraph per job, a **blank line** between paragraphs, a **topic sentence** first.
- Pick connectors by their job: add, contrast, reason, result, example, sequence, condition, conclusion.
- Avoid fragments, comma splices, wrong-meaning connectors and overuse.
- Reference words (this, these, such) link ideas without repeating nouns.
