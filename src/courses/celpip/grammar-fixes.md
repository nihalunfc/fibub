**Module 4 · about 20 minutes.** By the end you will know the eight grammar mistakes that appear most often in CELPIP practice answers, how to fix each one, which spelling errors to watch for with no spell check, and a quick checking routine.

<div class="bigidea">You do not need perfect grammar, but you do need to stop the few mistakes that appear in almost every sentence. Fix verbs, agreement and sentence boundaries first; they are frequent, easy to see and quick to repair.</div>

## 1. Where grammar is rated

Grammar and sentence structure, and spelling and punctuation, are part of **Readability**, together with paragraphs and connectors. Errors that make a sentence hard to understand can also weaken **Content/Coherence**, because the rater cannot follow your idea.

Because you type with **no spell check**, spelling mistakes stay on the screen unless you catch them.

## 2. What really costs marks

Not all errors are equal. Raters notice errors that are **frequent** (the same mistake in many sentences) and errors that **block meaning**. One missing article is small. A wrong verb tense in every sentence, or sentences that never end, makes the whole answer feel weaker.

**Everyday analogy.** Grammar errors are like potholes on a road. One small pothole, and you drive on. A pothole every few metres, and the whole trip feels bumpy, even if the scenery (your ideas) is beautiful. Fill the most common holes first.

## 3. The eight costly mistakes

| # | Mistake | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Subject-verb agreement | *My neighbour have two dogs.* | *My neighbour **has** two dogs.* |
| 2 | Past simple for finished time | *Yesterday I go to the clinic.* | *Yesterday I **went** to the clinic.* |
| 3 | Present perfect with since/for | *I live here since 2020.* | *I **have lived** here since 2020.* |
| 4 | Present perfect with finished time | *I have received it yesterday.* | *I **received** it yesterday.* |
| 5 | Uncountable nouns | *an information, furnitures, advices* | ***some** information, furniture, advice* |
| 6 | Comma splice | *The rent is high, I can’t afford it.* | *The rent is high, **so** I can’t afford it.* |
| 7 | Fragment | *Because the bus was late.* | ***I was late** because the bus was late.* |
| 8 | Verb patterns | *look forward to hear, suggest you to call, I am agree* | *look forward to **hearing**, suggest **that you call**, I **agree*** |

Also watch: *If I will have time* → *If I have time*; *more better* → *better*; *Every students* → *Every student*; indirect questions: *Could you tell me when **the office opens**?* (not *when does the office open*).

### Spelling: the words people miss most

No spell check means you need your own list. Common traps in CELPIP-type emails: *receive, believe, necessary, until, appreciate, opportunity, schedule, separate, definitely, accommodation, recommend, which*. Canadian spelling is a good habit for this test: *neighbour, colour, centre, cheque, travelled, cancelled* (and either spelling is fine as long as you are consistent).

## 4. The method: a three-pass check

<div class="steps">

<div class="step">

**Pass 1: verbs.** Read only the verbs. For each one ask: who is the subject (agreement)? When did it happen (tense)? Fix *has/have*, *-s*, past forms.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Pass 2: sentence boundaries.** Look at every full stop and comma. Does each sentence have a subject and a verb? Are two sentences glued with just a comma? Add *so, but, because* or a full stop.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Pass 3: nouns, articles and spelling.** Check plurals and uncountable nouns, *a/an/the*, and your personal list of spelling traps.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Keep a personal error list.** After each practice task, write down your top three repeated errors. On test day, search for those first.

</div>
</div>

## 5. Worked example: fixing a paragraph

<div class="model">

**Before (around CLB 6)**

I am writing to complain about the noises from the gym under my apartment. I live here since two years. Every morning at 5 a.m. they plays loud music, I cannot sleep. Because I work until midnight. Yesterday I have called the manager but he don’t answer. I look forward to hear from you.

</div>

Errors: *the noises* (noise is usually uncountable here); *I live here since two years* (tense and preposition); *they plays* (agreement); comma splice (*music, I cannot*); fragment (*Because I work until midnight.*); *have called* with *yesterday*; *he don’t* (agreement); *look forward to hear*.

<div class="model">

**After (around CLB 9)**

I am writing to complain about the noise from the gym under my apartment. I have lived here for two years, and the problem started only last month. Every morning at 5 a.m., the gym plays loud music, so I cannot sleep. This is especially difficult because I work until midnight. Yesterday I called the manager, but he did not answer. I look forward to hearing from you.

</div>

The ideas are the same. Fixing eight errors, and adding one detail (*started only last month*), makes the paragraph easy to read and lifts Readability.

## 6. Traps when fixing grammar

| Trap | Why it hurts | Better |
|---|---|---|
| Rewriting whole sentences in the last minute | New errors appear | Make small, sure fixes only |
| Avoiding all complex sentences | Range of sentence structure drops | Use some *because, although, which* sentences, then check them |
| Changing correct Canadian spelling to US, or mixing both | Looks inconsistent | Pick one spelling system and keep it |
| Fixing only the first paragraph | Errors in the closing remain | Check every paragraph, even quickly |

<div class="strategy">

**Grammar self-check.**

- [ ] Every verb agrees with its subject (he has, they have, the gym plays).
- [ ] Finished past times (yesterday, last week) use the past simple.
- [ ] Since/for with situations that continue use the present perfect.
- [ ] No sentence starts with because/although and stops after one clause.
- [ ] No two full sentences joined only by a comma.
- [ ] Uncountable nouns (information, advice, furniture, equipment) have no -s and no a/an.
- [ ] My personal top three errors checked; my spelling list checked.

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Simple sentences are safest, so I will only write short ones."** Short sentences avoid some errors, but Readability includes sentence structure, and an answer made only of short sentences reads as choppy. Mix simple and longer sentences, and check the longer ones carefully.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- **Safe, correct frames:** *I have been waiting since..., I would appreciate it if you could..., I look forward to hearing from you.*
- **Joining clauses correctly:** *..., so ..., ..., but ..., ... because ..., Although ..., ...*
- **Uncountable with a counter:** *a piece of advice, some information, an item of furniture*
- **Indirect questions:** *Could you tell me when..., I would like to know whether...*

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Grammar, sentence structure, spelling and punctuation are part of **Readability**.
- Fix the frequent errors first: **agreement, tenses, comma splices, fragments, uncountable nouns, verb patterns**.
- No spell check: keep a **personal spelling list** and check it every time.
- Use a **three-pass check**: verbs, sentence boundaries, then nouns and spelling.
- In the last minute, make small, sure fixes; never start big rewrites.
