**Module 4 · about 18 minutes.** By the end you will know the four areas CELPIP raters look at in your writing, what each one rewards, and how to use them as a simple checklist on every practice answer.

<div class="bigidea">Raters do not give one mark for "good English". They look at four separate things, so a strong answer is strong in all four: good ideas, the right words, easy to read, and exactly what the task asked.</div>

## 1. What the Writing test looks like

The Writing test has **two tasks** and takes about 53 minutes in total.

| | Task 1: Writing an Email | Task 2: Responding to Survey Questions |
|---|---|---|
| Time | 27 minutes | 26 minutes |
| Length | 150 to 200 words | 150 to 200 words |
| What you do | Write an email that covers the points in the task (usually three) | Choose one of two options and give reasons |
| Format | Email: greeting, paragraphs, closing | No email format: just your opinion and reasons |

You **type** your answers. There is **no spell check**, and the screen shows a word count. The rest of this module has one lesson for each task and one lesson for each rating area, so this lesson is your map.

## 2. The four rating areas

Each answer is rated on four areas. Learn what each one includes, because every tip in this module fits into one of them.

**Content/Coherence: your ideas.** The number of ideas, their quality, how well they are organized, and the examples and details that support them. A sentence like "The service was bad" is an idea with no support. "We waited fifty minutes and the soup arrived cold" is an idea with detail.

**Vocabulary: your words.** Word choice, suitable use, range, precision and accuracy. Do you say exactly what you mean ("the tap drips all night" instead of "the tap has a problem")? Do you avoid repeating the same word? Is every word used with the right meaning?

**Readability: how easy it is to read.** Format and paragraphing, connectors (linking words), grammar and sentence structure, spelling and punctuation. This is the "surface" of your writing: a reader should never have to stop and guess.

**Task Fulfillment: did you do the job?** Relevance, completeness, tone and word count. Every point covered, nothing off topic, the right politeness for the reader, and 150 to 200 words.

**Everyday analogy.** Think of a restaurant review with four stars to give. One star for the food (content), one for the menu choices (vocabulary), one for the clean, comfortable room (readability) and one for getting exactly the meal you ordered (task fulfillment). A beautiful room cannot save the wrong dish, and a great dish in a dirty room still loses a star.

## 3. Turning the four areas into a method

<div class="steps">

<div class="step">

**Task Fulfillment first: read the task twice.** Underline every point you must cover and note who the reader is. This decides your tone and your paragraphs before you write a word.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Content/Coherence: one idea plus one detail per point.** For each point, jot one idea and one specific detail (a time, a cost, a name, a result). Put them in a logical order.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Readability while writing: paragraphs and connectors.** One paragraph per point, a blank line between paragraphs, and a linking word to show how ideas connect (because, however, as a result).

</div>
<div class="step">

**Vocabulary while writing: the precise word you are sure of.** Choose exact everyday words over vague ones, and do not repeat the same adjective. Skip words you are not sure about.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Check all four in the last minutes.** Points covered and word count, then verbs and agreement, then spelling and punctuation. The lesson on timing gives you a full routine.

</div>
</div>

## 4. Worked example: two answers to the same task

Task (original): *You bought a coffee maker online and it stopped working after a week. Write an email to the company. In your email: describe what you bought; explain the problem; say what you want the company to do.*

<div class="model">

**Answer A (around CLB 6)**

Hello,

I buy a coffee maker from your website. It was good but now it is not good. It stop working. I am very angry because I need coffee every day and it is not working.

I want you to give me my money back. Please do it fast. This is not good service.

Thank you
Kamal

</div>

Notes on Answer A. **Content/Coherence:** the ideas are thin; there is no date, model or price, and "not good" says nothing specific. **Vocabulary:** "good" and "not good" repeat; the range is narrow. **Readability:** verb errors (I buy, It stop) and a short, choppy rhythm. **Task Fulfillment:** only about 60 words, far under 150; the tone ("Please do it fast", "This is not good service") is a little rude for a company.

<div class="model">

**Answer B (around CLB 10)**

Dear Customer Service Team,

I am writing about the Brewline 12-cup coffee maker I ordered from your website on October 2 (order number 48817). It arrived quickly and worked well for the first few days.

Unfortunately, on October 9 the machine stopped heating. The power light still comes on, but the water stays cold, and nothing comes through the filter. I have tried a different outlet and cleaned the machine with vinegar as the manual suggests, but the problem has not changed. As I drink coffee before every early shift, this has been quite inconvenient.

Since the coffee maker is still well within your 30-day return period, I would like a replacement unit sent to my address. If that is not possible, I would accept a full refund of $79.99. I can return the faulty machine with the prepaid label you provide.

I would appreciate a reply by the end of next week. Thank you for your help.

Sincerely,
Kamal Haddad

</div>

Notes on Answer B. **Content/Coherence:** each point has specific detail (model, date, order number, what exactly fails, what was tried) in a logical order. **Vocabulary:** precise words (faulty, prepaid label, return period, stays cold). **Readability:** clear paragraphs, connectors (Unfortunately, Since, If that is not possible), correct grammar and spelling. **Task Fulfillment:** all three points covered, firm but polite tone, about 165 words.

Notice that Answer B does not use rare or impressive words. It wins because it is **specific, complete and polite**.

## 5. Common ways to lose marks in each area

| Area | Costly habit | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Content/Coherence | General statements with no details | Add a time, number, place or result to each point |
| Vocabulary | The same word again and again (good, nice, problem) | Pick a precise word for each use |
| Readability | One big block of text; run-on sentences | Paragraph per point; one idea per sentence |
| Task Fulfillment | Skipping a point, wrong tone, under 150 words | Tick each point in your plan; check the count |

<div class="strategy">

**Your four-area checklist.** Copy this and use it after every practice answer in this course.

- [ ] Content/Coherence: every point has an idea and a specific detail, in a sensible order.
- [ ] Vocabulary: no word repeated more than twice; every word used with a meaning I am sure of.
- [ ] Readability: paragraphs with blank lines; connectors; verbs checked; spelling checked.
- [ ] Task Fulfillment: all points answered, tone fits the reader, 150 to 200 words.

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Long, difficult words are what get high scores."** Raters look at precision and accuracy, not length. A rare word used wrongly lowers your Vocabulary score and can make a sentence hard to read. Use the most exact word you are sure of.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- **Adding detail:** *on (date), at about (time), which cost, for example, specifically*
- **Showing order:** *first, then, after that, finally*
- **Showing reasons and results:** *because, since, as a result, so*
- **Polite requests:** *I would appreciate it if, could you please, I would like*

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Writing has two tasks: an **email (27 min)** and a **survey response (26 min)**, each **150 to 200 words**, typed with **no spell check**.
- **Content/Coherence:** number and quality of ideas, organization, examples and details.
- **Vocabulary:** word choice, suitable use, range, precision and accuracy.
- **Readability:** format and paragraphs, connectors, grammar and sentence structure, spelling and punctuation.
- **Task Fulfillment:** relevance, completeness, tone and word count. A missing point hurts even perfect English.
