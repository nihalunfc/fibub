**Module 6 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will know how to find and read your results, what to do if one score is short, when re-evaluation might make sense, and how to use your scores with Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC).

<div class="bigidea">Your results are a map, not a verdict. If you reached your target, put them to work quickly; if one skill is short, the scores show you exactly where to aim the next few weeks.</div>

## 1. Getting your results

After the test, the Listening and Reading answers are marked by computer, and trained raters mark your Writing and Speaking. Your results appear online in your CELPIP account, usually within a few business days. CELPIP also offers faster results for an extra fee. The exact times and options change, so check **celpip.ca** for what applies to your booking.

Your results show a **level for each skill**, from M (minimal) and 1 up to 12, and the test date. The [scores lesson](/learn/courses/celpip/scores-and-clb/) explains how CELPIP levels line up with the Canadian Language Benchmarks (CLB): for levels 4 to 10, the CELPIP level and the CLB level are the same number, and CELPIP 11 and 12 both count as CLB 10 in IRCC's tables.

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">

**Log in to your CELPIP account** with the details you used to book. Results appear there; you may also get an email when they are ready.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Download and save a copy** of your official results. Keep it with your immigration papers, and note your test date.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Compare each skill with your target.** Write the four levels next to the four levels you need, such as CLB 7 in each skill or CLB 9 for more points.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Decide your next step:** use the scores now, consider re-evaluation, or plan a retake.

</div>
</div>

## 2. Using your scores with IRCC

**Express Entry.** IRCC accepts CELPIP-General for Express Entry. Results are valid for **two years from the test date**, and they must still be valid on the day you submit your application for permanent residence, not only when you create your profile. You enter your results yourself in your online Express Entry profile: the test type, the test date, the certificate or registration details asked for, and your level in each skill, exactly as they appear on your results. Later, you upload a copy of the results with your application.

Think of it like a bus ticket with an expiry date: it does not matter how good the seat is if the ticket runs out before you board. If your profile may stay in the pool for a long time, keep an eye on the test date.

**Citizenship.** For citizenship, IRCC accepts CELPIP-General or CELPIP-General LS (Listening and Speaking) as one way to prove your language skills, at CLB 4 or higher in listening and speaking. Read IRCC's citizenship language page for the full list of accepted proof and current rules.

**Other programmes.** Provinces, employers, professional bodies and some colleges have their own rules and may ask for different levels or recent results. Always check the organisation's own page.

<div class="strategy">

**Copy carefully.** Typing a level wrongly in your profile can cause big problems later, because your documents must match what you claimed. Enter your scores slowly, check them against your results twice, and keep the copy you downloaded.

</div>

## 3. If a score is short

First, breathe. Being one level short in one skill is very common, and it is usually the easiest gap to close. Then look closely.

**Which skill, and by how much?** Write it down plainly: "Speaking 7, need 9" or "Reading 8, need 9". One level in one skill is a focused job; several levels in several skills needs a longer plan.

**Does it match your mocks?** If your mocks were clearly higher in that skill, nerves, a noisy room or bad timing may have played a part. If your mocks were close to the result, the result is probably fair, and you need more practice, not just another try.

### Re-evaluation

CELPIP offers re-evaluation, where your answers are rated again. It is not free, and there is a deadline after you receive your results. Check **celpip.ca** for the current options, fees and deadlines. It makes most sense when one Writing or Speaking score is clearly lower than your other skills and your mocks, especially when one level would reach your target. Remember that a new rating can stay the same, and it can also go up or down.

### Retaking the test

You can book another test; check **celpip.ca** for any rules about waiting times between tests and for fees. A new sitting gives a fresh score in **every** skill, so a retake is a full test, not one section.

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">

**Wait for your feelings to settle,** a day or two, then plan calmly.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Choose a date with enough time:** often three to six weeks for one level in one skill, longer for bigger gaps. Booking for next week rarely changes a result.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Spend about 60% of practice on the short skill**, and the rest keeping the others warm, since every skill is scored again.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Take a full mock** a week before, then follow the [final week plan](/learn/courses/celpip/final-week/).

</div>
</div>

## 4. A worked example: Lin's results

Lin needs CLB 9 in every skill for the points she wants. Her results: Listening 10, Reading 9, Writing 9, Speaking 8.

**The thinking.** Three skills meet the target; Speaking is one level short. Her last two mocks rated her speaking about the same, using the [self-rating checklist](/learn/courses/celpip/score-yourself/), and her recordings showed long pauses and answers that ended early. So the result looks fair, and re-evaluation is unlikely to help. A retake with focused speaking work makes more sense.

**Her plan:** a test in five weeks. Every day: three speaking tasks with the timer, recorded, rating Listenability and Content each time; the [fluency lesson](/learn/courses/celpip/fluency-pronunciation/) twice a week. Twice a week, one listening round, one reading round and one timed writing task, so the other skills stay where they are.

**Another case: Arjun.** Arjun needs CLB 7. He gets Listening 9, Reading 8, Speaking 8 but Writing 6. His mocks always rated his writing 8. On the day, he wrote his survey for the wrong option by misreading the question. Here, a retake soon makes sense, with careful reading of the task first. Re-evaluation could be worth a look too, but a misread task usually stays a misread task.

<div class="myth">

**"If I retake straight away, I will do better because I know the test now."** Knowing the test helps a little, but your English is the same as last week. Without focused practice on the short skill, the most likely result is the same score again, for another fee. Give yourself enough time to change something.

</div>

## 5. If you reached your target

Congratulations. Save your results, note the expiry date (two years from the test date for Express Entry) and update your profile carefully. Keep using English every day: reading the news, talking to neighbours, writing emails. Your skills keep growing, and that helps at work, in your community and on any later test.

<div class="keypoints">

- Results appear in your CELPIP account, usually within a few business days; download and keep a copy.
- For Express Entry, results are valid for two years from the test date and must be valid when you apply; enter them carefully in your profile.
- Re-evaluation exists, with a fee and a deadline; check celpip.ca. It suits a Writing or Speaking score that is clearly out of line.
- A retake scores every skill again: give most time to the short skill and keep the others warm.
