**Module 2 · about 18 minutes.** By the end you will be able to name the eight traps that cause most wrong answers in CELPIP Listening, spot each one in a short clip, and use a two-second check before you click. You will practise on 24 original clips, one trap each.

<div class="bigidea">Wrong options are not random. They are built from things you really heard, twisted in one of a few predictable ways. Learn the eight twists and you can see the trap before you fall in.</div>

## 1. Why traps work

Every CELPIP listening question has four options, and three are wrong. Test writers make wrong options **attractive** on purpose, otherwise the test could not tell strong listeners from weaker ones. The easiest way to make a wrong option attractive is to use words that were really in the recording. That is why you so often feel "I heard that!" about a wrong answer.

This lesson brings together everything from lessons 2.1 to 2.7. The traps are the same across all six parts; only the recordings change.

**Everyday analogy.** A good magic trick works because your eyes follow the hand that moves, not the one that does the trick. A listening trap works the same way: your ear grabs the word you recognise, while the real answer is said in other words.


Play a clip and watch the sentence appear, then choose the answer you would write.

## 2. The eight traps

| # | Trap | What you hear | What the trap option does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Changed plan** | "Let’s meet at the library... actually, the café" | Offers the first plan |
| 2 | **Repeated word** | "The meeting room is booked, so it’s online" | Repeats "meeting room" |
| 3 | **True but not asked** | Three reasons, then "but mostly because..." | Offers a true detail that is not the main reason |
| 4 | **Negative** | "I don’t mind X. What I can’t stand is Y." | Offers X |
| 5 | **Numbers** | Several prices, times or a calculation | Offers the wrong number or the old one |
| 6 | **Speaker mix-up** | Two or three people with different ideas | Gives one person’s idea to another |
| 7 | **Tone** | "Oh, wonderful. The bus is late again." | Takes positive words literally |
| 8 | **Condition** | "I’ll come if my shift ends on time." | Treats a possible plan as certain |

## 3. The two-second check

Before you click any answer, run this check. With practice it takes about two seconds.

<div class="steps">

<div class="step">

**Is it final?** Does this option match the **last** plan, decision or number in my notes, or an earlier one that changed? (Traps 1 and 5.)

</div>
<div class="step">

**Is it the same meaning, or just the same words?** If the option copies the recording’s exact words, check the meaning twice. (Trap 2.)

</div>
<div class="step">

**Does it answer this question?** It may be true, but is it what the question asks: the *main* reason, *this* person, *this* time? (Traps 3 and 6.)

</div>
<div class="step">

**Did I flip it?** Was there a *not, don’t mind, never, unless* or a sarcastic tone that reverses the words? (Traps 4 and 7.)

</div>
<div class="step">

**Is it certain or only possible?** Was it said with *if, as long as, maybe*? Only choose it if the question asks about possibility. (Trap 8.)

</div>
</div>

## 4. Worked example: one clip, four traps

Situation line: *Listen to two coworkers.*

> **Maya:** Are you driving to the conference in Regina on Thursday?
> **Cole:** I was going to, but the forecast is snow. I don’t mind driving in snow, honestly, but the highway was closed last time. So I’m taking the train at seven fifteen. It arrives two and a half hours later.
> **Maya:** I’ll join you, as long as my manager signs my travel form today.

Question: *How will Cole travel, and when will he arrive?*
A. By car, arriving at 7:15
B. By train, arriving at 9:45
C. By train, arriving at 7:15
D. By car, if the highway is open

- A combines a **changed plan** (driving) and the **wrong number** (the departure time).
- B is correct: train, and 7:15 plus 2 hours 30 minutes is 9:45.
- C is the **numbers** trap: right transport, departure time instead of arrival.
- D is a **condition** trap built from a real detail (the highway), but Cole never says he will drive if it is open.

Question: *Why did Cole decide not to drive?* A trap option would be "He does not like driving in snow". That is the **negative** trap: he said "I don’t mind driving in snow". The real reason is the highway closure last time.

Question: *What is certain about Maya?* Nothing is certain: she will join him "as long as" her manager signs. A trap would be "She will take the train with Cole", which is only possible.

## 5. How traps look in each part

| Part | Traps you will see most |
|---|---|
| 1 Problem Solving | Changed plan, true but about another person, total versus share |
| 2 Daily Life | Rejected offer, "normally" versus "this time", tone |
| 3 Information | Numbers, missed exceptions, combining two facts |
| 4 News | Wrong organisation, effect given as cause, past given as future |
| 5 Discussion | Speaker mix-up, condition, task belongs to someone else |
| 6 Viewpoints | Argument from the wrong side, concession taken as main view, extreme words |

<div class="strategy">

**Keep a trap diary.** After every practice set, write one line for each mistake: the question number and the trap number from the table (1 to 8). After a few sets, you will see that two or three traps cause most of your errors. Then you know exactly what to listen for. Most learners find they have a "favourite" trap, often the changed plan or the repeated word.

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"If two options both seem right, the longer one is correct."** There is no reliable rule about length or position in CELPIP. Options are written to be similar in length. Use meaning, not shape: run the two-second check on both options, and choose the one that matches the final, certain meaning in your notes.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- **Changed plan:** *actually, on second thought, I was going to, instead, how about, let’s not*
- **Main reason:** *mostly, mainly, the real reason, above all, what matters most*
- **Negatives that flip:** *I don’t mind, not that, it’s not that... it’s just, not tomorrow, can’t stand*
- **Tone markers:** *oh great, just what I needed, I suppose, polite enough, well, I’m impressed*
- **Conditions:** *if, as long as, unless, only if, if not*

</div>

## 6. Practice: 24 trap clips

Each clip is very short and built around one trap, named in the clip title so you can learn to recognise it. Listen once, run the two-second check, then answer. Read the "trap" note after each answer even when you are right.

**Next step.** When you can score 7 or 8 out of 8 on each round, go back to the full-length sets in lessons 2.2 to 2.7 and redo the ones you found hardest. Notice how many of your old mistakes were one of these eight traps.

<div class="keypoints">

- Wrong options are built from words you **really heard**, twisted in one of eight ways.
- The eight traps: **changed plan, repeated word, true but not asked, negative, numbers, speaker mix-up, tone, condition**.
- Before clicking, run the two-second check: final? same meaning? answers this question? flipped? certain?
- Different parts favour different traps; know which ones to expect where.
- Keep a trap diary and train against your most common trap.

</div>
