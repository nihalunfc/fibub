**Module 5 · about 22 minutes.** By the end you will know how Task 6 is built, how to choose the person to talk to, how to deliver bad news kindly in 60 seconds, and you will have practised three timed tasks and 30 drill items.

<div class="bigidea">Task 6 is a polite phone call you would rather not make: you choose who to speak to, tell them the hard news honestly, show you care about their side, and offer a way forward.</div>

## 1. What Task 6 looks like

Task 6, **Dealing with a Difficult Situation**, shows a short paragraph about a problem. You are stuck between two people or two duties, and you cannot please both. The screen then gives you **two options**, usually two people you could talk to, for example:

- *Talk to your friend and explain why you cannot help him move on Saturday*, or
- *Talk to your boss and explain why you cannot work the extra shift on Saturday*.

You choose one option and speak **directly to that person**, as if they were in front of you or on the phone. You usually get **60 seconds to prepare** and **60 seconds to speak**; the test screen shows the exact time.

Your answer is rated on **Content/Coherence**, **Vocabulary**, **Listenability** (rhythm, pronunciation and intonation; pauses and self-correction; grammar and sentence structure; variety of sentence types) and **Task Fulfillment** (relevance, completeness, tone and length). In this task, **tone** matters a lot.

## 2. What it really tests

Task 6 checks whether you can handle an awkward conversation like an adult in a Canadian workplace or friendship:

1. **Say the hard thing clearly.** The listener must understand what you can or cannot do.
2. **Be polite and kind.** Use softeners (*I’m afraid, unfortunately, I was wondering if*) and empathy (*I know this puts you in a tough spot*).
3. **Give real reasons.** Short, believable reasons, not a long story.
4. **Offer a solution.** Another time, another person, a partial help or something to make up for it.

**Everyday analogy.** Think of a good restaurant server telling you the dish you ordered is sold out. They apologise, explain briefly, and immediately suggest something similar, maybe with a free dessert. You leave happy even though the news was bad. Your Task 6 answer should leave the listener feeling the same way.

## 3. Choosing the person

Choose the option that gives you **the clearest reasons and the best solution to offer**. Usually one option is easier to explain. If you cannot decide in five seconds, pick the first one. Do not try to talk to both people; the task asks you to speak to **one**. You may mention the other person as part of your reason (*my boss needs me that day*), but speak only to the person you chose.

## 4. The six-part template

<div class="steps">

<div class="step">

**1. Greet and prepare them (about 5 seconds).** *"Hi Tom, have you got a minute? I need to talk to you about Saturday."* This warns the listener that something is coming.

</div>
<div class="step">

**2. Deliver the news, softened (about 10 seconds).** *"I’m really sorry, but I won’t be able to help you move after all."* One clear sentence, no hiding it.

</div>
<div class="step">

**3. Explain the reason (about 15 seconds).** One or two short reasons: *"My manager just told me two people are off sick, and if I don’t come in, the store can’t open."*

</div>
<div class="step">

**4. Show empathy (about 5 to 10 seconds).** *"I know you were counting on me, and I feel terrible about leaving you short."*

</div>
<div class="step">

**5. Offer a solution (about 15 seconds).** Be specific: *"I’ve already asked my cousin Ravi, and he can come with his truck. And I’ll come by on Sunday to help you unpack and put the furniture together."*

</div>
<div class="step">

**6. Close warmly (about 5 seconds).** *"Again, I’m so sorry. Pizza on Sunday is on me, okay?"*

</div>
</div>

**Planning in 60 seconds.** Six short lines: `hi Tom`, `news: can’t move Sat`, `why: 2 sick, store`, `feel: counting on me`, `fix: Ravi + truck; me Sun unpack`, `close: pizza on me`.

**Formal or friendly?** Match the person. To a friend or family member, use first names and contractions (*I’m, can’t*). To a boss, landlord or teacher, add more softeners (*I was hoping we could..., Would it be possible to...*) and avoid slang. Polite does not mean cold; warmth is welcome with everyone.

## 6. Model answers for practice task 1 (talking to Tom)

Original transcripts written for this course. Each is about 60 seconds when read at a natural pace.

<div class="model">

**Level: around CLB 7.**

Hi Tom. I have bad news. I am sorry, but I cannot help you move on Saturday. My manager called me yesterday. Two people at work are sick, so he needs me to work on Saturday. I need the money because my rent is going up next month. I know you need help and I feel bad about it. Maybe I can help you on Sunday? I can come in the morning and help you carry the boxes and clean the old apartment. Also, my brother has a car, so I can ask him to help you on Saturday. Again, I am very sorry, Tom. Please tell me if Sunday is okay for you.

</div>

**Why it scores around CLB 7.** *Content/Coherence:* the news, the reason, empathy and a solution all appear in a sensible order. *Vocabulary:* simple and clear, with few phrases beyond everyday words. *Listenability:* mostly correct, but the sentences are short and similar, and there are no contractions, which makes the rhythm sound stiff. *Task Fulfillment:* polite and complete, with two offers. The offer about the brother is a little vague ("I can ask him").

<div class="model">

**Level: around CLB 10.**

Hey Tom, do you have a sec? It’s about Saturday, and I’m afraid it’s not great news. My manager called last night; two people are off sick, and if I don’t go in, there’s literally nobody to open the store. I tried to get out of it, honestly, but with my rent going up, I really can’t afford to turn down the shift. I know this leaves you in a tough spot, and I hate letting you down after I promised. So here’s what I’ve done: I already talked to my brother Sunil, and he’s happy to come over at nine with his van, which is probably more useful than me anyway. And I’ll be at your place first thing Sunday to help you unpack and put the beds together. Dinner’s on me that night, too. I’m really sorry, man. Does that work for you?

</div>

**Why it scores around CLB 10.** *Content/Coherence:* the listener is prepared ("not great news"), and each part flows into the next with clear links (*so here’s what I’ve done, and*). *Vocabulary:* natural, idiomatic phrases (*turn down the shift, a tough spot, first thing Sunday*). *Listenability:* varied sentences, contractions and a closing question give a natural rhythm. *Task Fulfillment:* friendly tone that fits a friend, honest reason, and a specific, already-arranged solution.

## 7. Traps in Task 6

| Trap | What it sounds like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Talking about the person, not to them | "I would tell Tom that I can’t help him." | Speak directly: *"Tom, I’m really sorry, but..."* |
| Hiding the news | Thirty seconds of small talk before the problem | Deliver the news in your first two sentences |
| Too many reasons | A long story about every detail of the week | One or two short, believable reasons |
| No solution | Ending on "Sorry, that’s all." | Offer another day, another helper or a way to make up for it |
| Wrong tone | Slang with a landlord, or a cold, formal speech to a close friend | Match the person: friendly with friends, polite and softened with a boss |

<div class="strategy">

**Self-check after each practice task.**

- [ ] I spoke directly to one person, using *you*, not *he* or *she*.
- [ ] I prepared them, then gave the news clearly in the first 15 seconds.
- [ ] I gave one or two short reasons.
- [ ] I showed empathy for how they feel.
- [ ] I offered a specific solution (who, when, what).
- [ ] My tone fit the person (friend, boss, landlord, teacher).
- [ ] I ended warmly before time ran out.

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Being polite means apologising again and again."** Saying "sorry" five times uses up your time and sounds anxious, not polite. One sincere apology at the start, real empathy in the middle and one more "sorry" at the end is enough. What really shows respect is the solution you offer.

</div>

<div class="phrases">

- **Preparing the listener:** *Have you got a minute? There’s something I need to tell you. I’m afraid it’s not great news.*
- **Softening bad news:** *I’m really sorry, but...; Unfortunately, I won’t be able to...; I’m afraid I can’t... after all.*
- **Giving reasons:** *The thing is...; Something came up at work; I just found out that...*
- **Empathy:** *I know you were counting on me; I understand this is frustrating; I feel terrible about this.*
- **Offering a solution:** *What I can do is...; How about if I...; Would it help if...; I’ve already asked...*
- **Asking a favour (formal):** *I was wondering if it would be possible to...; Would you consider...; I’d really appreciate it if...*
- **Closing:** *Again, I’m so sorry. Let me know if that works. I owe you one.*

</div>

## 8. Drills

<div class="keypoints">

- Task 6: a difficult situation, **two options** (usually two people), you choose one and speak **directly to them**, usually 60 seconds to prepare and 60 to speak.
- Template: **prepare them → soft but clear news → short reason → empathy → specific solution → warm close**.
- Give the news early; one or two reasons are enough.
- A specific offer (who, when, what) is the strongest part of the answer.
- Match your tone to the person: friendly with friends and family, softened and respectful with a boss or landlord.

</div>
