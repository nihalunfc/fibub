**Module 1 · about 12 minutes.** By the end you will know what CELPIP tests, which of its two versions you need, and how to use this course to reach your target level on your own.

<div class="bigidea">CELPIP is a Canadian English test you take on a computer in one sitting. It measures how well you use English in everyday Canadian life, and the Government of Canada accepts it for permanent residence and citizenship.</div>

## 1. What the letters mean

CELPIP stands for the **Canadian English Language Proficiency Index Program**. It is owned by Paragon Testing Enterprises, a company based in Vancouver. You take it at a test centre, on a computer, wearing a headset with a microphone.

Unlike many English tests, CELPIP is not about university lectures or academic essays. It is about **real life in Canada**: a neighbour asking for help, a manager explaining a new rule, an email to a landlord, a community meeting about a new park. If you can handle those situations in English, you can do well.

## 2. The two versions

<div class="steps" data-fig="testmap" data-mode="celpip">
<div class="step">

**CELPIP-General** tests all four skills: Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking. It takes under three hours in one sitting. This is the version for **Express Entry** and most other permanent residence programmes. Press Next to see each section.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Listening** comes first: six parts, from a short problem between two people to a group discussion and a news report. Each recording plays **once**.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Reading**: four parts. An email or letter, a diagram such as a timetable or flyer, an information text, and a text with different people's opinions.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Writing**: two tasks. An email (27 minutes) and a response to a survey question where you choose between two options (26 minutes). Both ask for 150 to 200 words.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Speaking**: eight short tasks, each with a little time to prepare and then up to a minute and a half to speak. Your answers are recorded. The whole section takes about 15 minutes.

</div>
</div>

**CELPIP-General LS** tests only **Listening and Speaking**. It takes about 1 hour 10 minutes and costs less. It is meant for **Canadian citizenship** and some professional bodies.

| | CELPIP-General | CELPIP-General LS |
|---|---|---|
| Skills | Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking | Listening, Speaking |
| Time | under 3 hours | about 1 hour 10 minutes |
| Used for | Express Entry, provincial nominee and other PR programmes, some work and study needs | Citizenship, some professional designations |

<div class="myth">

**"I need the full test for citizenship."** Citizenship only asks for listening and speaking at CLB 4 or higher, so General LS is enough. But if you already have a valid CELPIP-General result, it also counts.

</div>

## 3. Who takes CELPIP

- People applying for **permanent residence** through Express Entry (Canadian Experience Class, Federal Skilled Worker, Federal Skilled Trades) and many provincial programmes.
- People applying for **Canadian citizenship** who need to prove their English.
- Some professionals whose licensing body asks for a language score.

Results are usually valid for **two years** for immigration, so plan your test date around your application. Lesson 1.3 explains how your levels turn into Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) levels and Express Entry points.

## 4. CELPIP or another test?

IRCC also accepts other English tests for immigration. Many people choose CELPIP because:

- everything is on the computer in **one sitting** (no separate speaking appointment);
- the accents and situations are **Canadian**, which feels familiar if you already live here;
- you **type** your writing, which is faster than handwriting for many people.

If you are applying to universities rather than immigrating, a test such as TOEFL is more common; fibub has a free [TOEFL preparation course](/learn/courses/toefl/) too.

## 6. How to use this course

1. **Go in order.** Module 1 shows you the test and builds your plan. Modules 2 to 5 teach each skill part by part. Module 6 is mock tests and the final week.
2. **Do every drill and quiz.** Reading about a skill is not the same as practising it. Each lesson has practice with instant marking and the reason for every answer.
3. **Use the timers.** The writing pad and speaking timer copy the real timing, so you get used to the clock.
4. **Record yourself.** In the speaking lessons you can record your answer and listen back with a checklist. It stays on your device.
5. **Keep your progress.** Ticks for finished lessons are kept in this browser. Tick "Remember my progress" on the course page so they are still there next time.

<div class="keypoints">

- CELPIP is a Canadian, computer-based English test about everyday life, owned by Paragon Testing Enterprises.
- **CELPIP-General** (all four skills, under 3 hours) is for permanent residence, including Express Entry.
- **CELPIP-General LS** (listening and speaking, about 1 hour 10 minutes) is for citizenship.
- Speaking is recorded on the computer; there is no face-to-face interview.
- Work through this course in order, doing every drill, timer and quiz.
