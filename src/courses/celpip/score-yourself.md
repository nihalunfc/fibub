**Module 6 · about 17 minutes.** By the end you will be able to rate your own writing and speaking against the four CELPIP areas, spot your weakest area in each answer, and read your mock listening and reading scores sensibly.

<div class="bigidea">Without a teacher, you can still be your own rater: compare each answer to a fixed checklist, one area at a time, and always find the one thing that would lift it most.</div>

## 1. Why rate yourself, and how honest to be

CELPIP raters look at four areas in Writing and Speaking: **Content/Coherence**, **Vocabulary**, **Readability** (writing) or **Listenability** (speaking), and **Task Fulfillment**. The lessons on [how writing is rated](/learn/courses/celpip/writing-rating/) and [how speaking is rated](/learn/courses/celpip/speaking-rating/) explain each one. This lesson turns them into a checklist you can use after every practice answer and every mock.

Rating yourself is like checking your own car before a long drive. You are not a mechanic, but if you go round with a list (tires, oil, lights, wipers), you will find most problems before they find you. The checklist does the same for your English.

Two warnings. First, the checklist below is **our own**, written in plain words to help you practise. It is not the official CELPIP scale, and it cannot give you an official level. Second, most learners are either too kind or too harsh to themselves. The fix is to rate one area at a time and only tick a line when you can point to the proof in your answer.

## 2. The self-rating checklist

Give each area a mark from 1 to 4 using these plain descriptions: **4** = strong, nothing obvious to fix; **3** = good, one clear weakness; **2** = uneven, several weaknesses; **1** = hard to follow or mostly missing.

<div class="strategy">

**Content/Coherence: ideas and order**

- [ ] My main idea or purpose is clear in the first two sentences.
- [ ] Every point has a specific detail or example (a name, a time, a number, a small story).
- [ ] Ideas come in a sensible order, with a clear link between them.
- [ ] Nothing goes off topic.

**Vocabulary: the right words**

- [ ] I used precise words, not only *good, bad, nice, thing, very*.
- [ ] I did not repeat the same key word more than two or three times.
- [ ] Phrases sound natural (*make a decision*, not *do a decision*).

**Readability (writing) or Listenability (speaking): easy to follow**

- [ ] Writing: paragraphs, correct spelling and punctuation, sentences of mixed length.
- [ ] Speaking: steady pace, few long pauses, clear word endings, natural stress.
- [ ] Verb tenses and endings are mostly correct (*he goes, she has moved*).
- [ ] Linking words guide the reader or listener (*first, however, as a result*).

**Task Fulfillment: did I do what was asked?**

- [ ] I answered every part of the task.
- [ ] Tone fits the person (formal for a manager, friendly for a friend).
- [ ] Writing: 150 to 200 words. Speaking: I used most of the time.
- [ ] I chose one clear side or option when the task asked me to.

</div>

## 3. Rating method, step by step

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">

**Wait, then reread.** Leave the answer for at least an hour, or until the next day. Fresh eyes catch more. For speaking, play the recording once without stopping.

</div>
<div class="step">

**One area at a time.** Read or listen once for Task Fulfillment only, then once for Content/Coherence, and so on. Mixing areas makes you tick everything.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Find the proof.** Tick a line only when you can point to the exact sentence that proves it. No proof, no tick.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Give a mark from 1 to 4** for each area, using the descriptions above.

</div>
<div class="step">

**Choose one fix.** Pick the lowest area and write one action for next time, such as "add a time to every request" or "pause only at full stops".

</div>
</div>

## 4. Worked example 1: rating an email

**Task:** Your neighbour's dog barks every night until late. Write to your neighbour, Mr. Fraser. Explain the problem, say how it affects you, and suggest a solution. (Shortened here; the real task asks for 150 to 200 words.)

<div class="model">

Hi Mr. Fraser,

I am your neighbour from number 14. I want to tell you about your dog. Your dog is very loud every night and it is very bad. I can't sleep and I am very tired in my work. Also my son has exam next week and he can't study.

I hope you understand. Thank you.

Ravi

</div>

**Rating, one area at a time:**

- **Task Fulfillment: 2.** The problem and its effect are there, but there is **no solution**, which was one of the three points. "Hi" is a little casual for a neighbour he does not know well, though not wrong.
- **Content/Coherence: 2.** The purpose is clear early. But "every night" with no time, and no example of when, keeps it vague. A detail like "until about midnight, since early October" would help.
- **Vocabulary: 2.** "Very" appears three times, plus "very bad" and "loud". Better: *barks constantly, keeps us awake, exhausted at work*.
- **Readability: 3.** Short, clear sentences and paragraphs. Small errors: "has exam" (needs *an exam*), "tired in my work" (*at work*).

**One fix:** before writing, number the three task points on paper and tick each one when the email covers it. Missing a point costs more than any grammar slip.

## 5. Worked example 2: rating a spoken answer

**Task 7 (opinion):** Should cities build more bike lanes, even if it means fewer parking spaces? Here is a transcript of a learner's 90-second answer, with pauses shown as (…).

<div class="model">

I agree that cities should build more bike lanes. (…) The main reason is safety. In my town, Kamloops, many people want to cycle to work, but they are afraid because they must ride next to fast trucks. With a separate lane, (…) more people would feel safe enough to try it. Second, bike lanes help the environment and also, um, health, because cycling is good exercise. Of course, some drivers will lose parking. But in my opinion, if more people cycle, there will be fewer cars, so (…) parking will be easier anyway. So overall, I think bike lanes are a good investment for cities.

</div>

**Rating:**

- **Task Fulfillment: 4.** A clear side from the first sentence, reasons, and a reply to the other side. Close to 90 seconds.
- **Content/Coherence: 3.** The first reason has a real example (Kamloops, trucks). The second reason ("environment and health") is only named, not explained. One example there would make it a 4.
- **Vocabulary: 3.** Good phrases (*feel safe enough, a good investment*), but "good" appears twice. Could say *cycling builds fitness*.
- **Listenability: 3.** Three short pauses and one "um" are fine. Listen for whether word endings like *lanes* and *trucks* are clear.

**One fix:** give every reason one example, not only the first. The [opinions lesson](/learn/courses/celpip/speaking-opinions/) has a reason-plus-example frame.

<div class="myth">

**"If my grammar is perfect, I will get a high score."** Grammar is only part of one area. An answer with perfect grammar that misses a task point, uses plain words and has no examples can still score lower than one with a few small errors that does everything the task asked, with rich detail. Rate all four areas.

</div>

## 6. Listening and reading: a rough level from your mocks

Listening and Reading are marked right or wrong, so your mock gives you a count. Turn it into a percentage: correct answers ÷ questions × 100. Then use this **rough guide**. It is our own estimate for practice, **not an official conversion**: the real test is scored by Paragon in its own way, some questions may be unscored, and our original items are not the same difficulty as the real ones.

| Accuracy on fresh items | What it probably means |
|---|---|
| 90% or more | You are likely in the high range. Keep practising under time and protect your speed. |
| 75% to 89% | A solid, upper-middle range. Look at which parts lose points and target them. |
| 60% to 74% | A middle range. Work through the part lessons again and use the traps lesson. |
| Below 60% | Build basic listening or reading every day before more mocks. |

**Read the trend, not one score.** One mock can be lucky or unlucky. Three mocks over three weeks tell a true story. If your target is CLB 9 and you sit at the top of the 75 to 89% band on fresh items, plus good writing and speaking ratings, you are probably close; a [full mock](/learn/courses/celpip/mock-tests/) and the [scores lesson](/learn/courses/celpip/scores-and-clb/) help you plan the rest.

<div class="phrases">

- **Proof words for Content:** *for example, last month, at about 7 p.m., my coworker Ana, because*
- **Vocabulary upgrades:** *very tired → exhausted; very loud → noisy; good → useful, reliable, worthwhile; bad → serious, harmful, disappointing*
- **Fix notes to yourself:** *add a time; answer point 3; one example per reason; pause at full stops; vary "very"*

</div>
