**Module 1 · about 20 minutes.** By the end you will have a week-by-week plan for 26 weeks that covers every NET unit in a sensible order, a weekly routine to follow, and a way to adapt it to the time you actually have.

<div class="bigidea">Core first, then the Part C units, then mocks. Every week ends with mixed practice from the question bank, and every mock ends with a review. The plan works because each unit builds on the one before.</div>

## 1. How the plan is built

- **Core physics first** (weeks 1 to 16): mathematical methods, classical mechanics, electromagnetism, quantum mechanics, statistical physics and electronics. This is the whole of Part B and most of Part C.
- **Part C units next** (weeks 17 to 22): atomic and molecular, condensed matter and nuclear and particle physics, plus the NET-only extras of the core units.
- **Mocks and revision last** (weeks 23 to 26), with earlier mocks at weeks 8, 16 and 22.
- **Part A all along**: 20 minutes, three times a week, from [lesson 1.3](/learn/courses/csir-net-physics/part-a-aptitude/).

For each unit, start with its roadmap lesson in this course (lessons [2.1](/learn/courses/csir-net-physics/maths-roadmap/) to [10.1](/learn/courses/csir-net-physics/nuclear-roadmap/)). It lists what NET asks, the order to study, and the lessons to read. The GATE Physics lessons teach most of the core at MSc level; the JAM lessons are the refreshers when a basic idea is shaky.

## 3. A weekly routine

| Day | Plan |
|---|---|
| Mon to Thu | One or two lessons a day: read, work the live problems, then 10 bank questions on that topic. |
| Mon, Wed, Fri | 20 minutes of Part A (one bank set). |
| Fri | Catch up on anything unfinished. |
| Sat | Mixed set of 25 bank questions across all units so far, timed at 3 minutes each. |
| Sun | Review: redo every wrong question without looking, and write the trap in your notebook. Half a day off. |

**Worked example.** You score 9 out of 25 on Saturday's mixed set in week 10. Sorting the 16 misses: 7 are from the week-4 to week-5 mechanics topics, 5 are this week's quantum, 4 are slips. The fix for next week: one evening redoing mechanics practice sets, and a habit of checking units before choosing an option. The plan keeps moving; the review decides where the spare hours go.

## 4. If you have less (or more) time

- **3 months**: take two plan weeks per calendar week. Keep mocks 1 to 3 and do one a week in the last month. Read the NET-only extras (weeks 12, 15, 22) for their key points and worked examples only.
- **4 months**: drop weeks 23 and 24 into one, and merge weeks 12 and 15 into the Part C weeks.
- **A year**: run the plan twice. The first pass at half speed with every practice set, the second pass as written with all the mocks. Add a GATE PH attempt in between: much of the syllabus is shared.
- **Working full time**: keep the order, aim for 12 to 15 hours a week, and let each plan week take two calendar weeks.

<div class="myth">

**"I must finish every book before I start practising."** Practice is where you learn what you do not know. Ten questions straight after a lesson tell you more than another hour of reading.

**"Mocks are for the last month."** One early mock (week 8) shows the time pressure and the style of choices while there is still time to change how you study.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- 26 weeks: core units (weeks 1 to 16), Part C units and NET extras (17 to 22), mocks and revision (23 to 26).
- Start every unit with its roadmap; study from the GATE lessons, refresh basics from the JAM lessons.
- Daily lesson plus 10 questions; Saturday mixed set; Sunday review of every wrong answer.
- Mocks at weeks 8, 16 and 22, then weekly; review each one.
- Short of time: double up weeks and trim the NET-only extras first.

</div>
