**Practice · Class 12 Chemistry · Chapter 7.** Read the chapter lesson, [Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers](/learn/courses/ncert-class-12-chemistry/alcohols-phenols-and-ethers/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer. Marks go for the correct reagent, a balanced equation with conditions, and a one-line reason.

<div class="bigidea">Four questions open most answers in this chapter: Is there an O–H for hydrogen bonding? Is the carbon with OH primary, secondary or tertiary? Can the negative charge on the oxygen be spread out (acidity)? Is the halide primary (SN2) or tertiary (elimination)?</div>

1. Write the equation with reagents and conditions above the arrow (temperature matters here: 413 K ether, 443 K alkene).

3. For comparison questions, write the order with “>” signs and the reason in one line.

## 1-mark questions

1. Give the IUPAC name of CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₂OH.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>2-methylpropan-1-ol.</details>

2. Which reagent converts a carboxylic acid directly to a primary alcohol?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>LiAlH₄ (followed by water). NaBH₄ is too mild.</details>

3. Name the two products of the cumene process.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Phenol and propanone (acetone).</details>

4. Why is ethanol completely soluble in water?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Its OH forms hydrogen bonds with water molecules.</details>

5. Which test gives a white precipitate with phenol?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Bromine water: 2,4,6-tribromophenol.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

6. (2) Why is the boiling point of an alcohol much higher than that of an ether of the same molar mass?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The alcohol has an O–H bond, so its molecules are held together by hydrogen bonds, which need much more energy to break. The ether has no O–H, so only weak dipole forces act.</details>

7. (2) Arrange in increasing acid strength with a reason: ethanol, phenol, 4-nitrophenol.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Ethanol < phenol < 4-nitrophenol. The phenoxide ion is stabilised by resonance; the –NO₂ group withdraws electrons and spreads the charge further. Ethanol has no resonance stabilisation of its anion.</details>

8. (3) Write the product and conditions when propan-2-ol is (a) heated with concentrated H₂SO₄ at 443 K, (b) oxidised with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇, (c) treated with sodium.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Propene, by dehydration. (b) Propanone (a ketone; it cannot be oxidised further without breaking a C–C bond). (c) Sodium propan-2-oxide and hydrogen gas.</details>

9. (3) How can you distinguish propan-1-ol, propan-2-ol and 2-methylpropan-2-ol with one reagent? Describe what you would see.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Lucas reagent (conc. HCl + anhydrous ZnCl₂) at room temperature. The tertiary alcohol turns the mixture cloudy at once; the secondary alcohol takes about five minutes; the primary alcohol gives no cloudiness at room temperature. The speed follows the stability of the carbocation: 3° > 2° > 1°.</details>

10. (3) Why is tert-butyl methyl ether made from sodium tert-butoxide and iodomethane rather than from sodium methoxide and tert-butyl bromide?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Williamson synthesis is an SN2 reaction and needs an unhindered carbon to attack. Iodomethane is a methyl halide, so it works. tert-Butyl bromide would not undergo back-side attack; the methoxide would act as a base and give 2-methylpropene by elimination.</details>

11. (3) Write the reactions of phenol with (a) dilute HNO₃, (b) bromine water, (c) CO₂ with NaOH, then acid.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) A mixture of 2-nitrophenol and 4-nitrophenol. (b) A white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol. (c) Salicylic acid (2-hydroxybenzoic acid), the Kolbe reaction.</details>

12. (3) The pKa of phenol is 10. Calculate the percentage ionised at pH 10 and at pH 11.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

At pH = pKa the ratio [A⁻]/[HA] = 1, so 50 % is ionised. At pH 11 the ratio is 10^(11 − 10) = 10, so the fraction is 10/11 = 91 %.

</details>

## 5-mark questions

13. (5) (a) Describe the mechanism of the dehydration of ethanol to ethene in three steps. (b) Explain why 3° alcohols dehydrate more easily than 1° alcohols. (c) Give the product when butan-2-ol is dehydrated, and the rule that decides it.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Step 1: the OH oxygen is protonated by H₂SO₄ to give R–OH₂⁺. Step 2: water leaves (slow) to give a carbocation. Step 3: a base removes H⁺ from the carbon next to C⁺ and the C=C bond forms. (For ethanol, a primary alcohol, the carbocation is stabilised by the solvent and the concerted loss is more likely, but the three steps are the board answer.)

(b) Dehydration goes through a carbocation; the tertiary carbocation is stabilised by three alkyl groups (inductive effect and hyperconjugation), so it forms fastest.

(c) But-2-ene (the major product) because Saytzeff's rule says that the more substituted alkene is formed.

</details>

14. (5) (a) Explain why phenol is more acidic than ethanol with resonance structures. (b) Give two reactions that show the ring of phenol is activated. (c) How is phenol converted into aspirin (name the intermediate)?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) The phenoxide ion has its charge delocalised onto the ortho and para carbons: three extra resonance structures; ethoxide has only one structure and its alkyl group pushes electrons onto O.

(b) Bromine water gives 2,4,6-tribromophenol without a catalyst; dilute nitric acid gives nitrophenols at low temperature.

(c) Kolbe reaction gives salicylic acid (phenol, NaOH, CO₂, acid); salicylic acid with ethanoic anhydride gives aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid).

</details>

## Case-based question

15. **Case.** A school lab technician has three unlabelled bottles. They hold (i) ethoxyethane, (ii) butan-1-ol and (iii) phenol solution. Their boiling points are 35 °C, 118 °C and 182 °C (for pure phenol), and the technician has Na metal, neutral FeCl₃ solution and bromine water.

(a) Which bottle will have the lowest boiling point, and why? (1)
(b) Which bottle gives no gas with sodium? (1)
(c) Which test gives a violet colour and with which bottle? (1)
(d) Write one reaction that distinguishes butan-1-ol from phenol with bromine water. (1)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Ethoxyethane: no O–H bond, so no hydrogen bonding. (b) Ethoxyethane (no acidic hydrogen). (c) Neutral FeCl₃ with the phenol solution. (d) Phenol with bromine water gives a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol; butan-1-ol only decolourises it very slowly or not at all.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

16. **Assertion (A):** Phenol does not give a Lucas test.
**Reason (R):** The C–O bond in phenol has partial double-bond character.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true and R explains A. Resonance shortens and strengthens the C–O bond, so the OH cannot be replaced by Cl through a carbocation. Answer: option 1 (both true, R explains A).</details>

17. **Assertion (A):** Ethers are cleaved by HI but not by NaOH.
**Reason (R):** The RO⁻ ion is a good leaving group.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is true and R is false. RO⁻ is a poor leaving group, which is why a strong acid is needed to protonate the oxygen first and turn it into ROH, a good leaving group.</details>

18. **Assertion (A):** Methanol is more acidic than tert-butanol.
**Reason (R):** Alkyl groups push electrons towards oxygen and destabilise the alkoxide ion.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true and R explains A. More alkyl groups means a less stable alkoxide and so a weaker acid.</details>
