**Practice · Class 12 Chemistry · Chapter 10.** Read the chapter lesson, [Biomolecules](/learn/courses/ncert-class-12-chemistry/biomolecules/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer. Board answers earn marks for the correct name, a one-line reason and, where asked, a neat structure or equation.

<div class="bigidea">Every polymer here is built by a condensation that loses water: glycosidic link (sugars), peptide bond (amino acids), phosphodiester link (nucleotides). Questions ask how the units are joined, what the shape does, and why.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For sugars, say whether the anomeric carbon is free (reducing) or used (non-reducing).
2. For proteins, name the level of structure and the bond or attraction that holds it.
3. For DNA questions, use Chargaff's rules: A = T, G = C, and the hydrogen bonds 2 and 3.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the monosaccharides formed on hydrolysis of lactose.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>D-galactose and D-glucose.</details>

2. What is the name of the bond between two amino acids in a protein?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Peptide bond (–CO–NH–).</details>

3. Which vitamin deficiency causes night blindness?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Vitamin A.</details>

4. Name the two purine bases in DNA.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Adenine and guanine.</details>

5. What is a polysaccharide that forms the cell wall of plants?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Cellulose.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

6. (2) What are anomers? Give an example.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Anomers are two cyclic sugars that differ only in the configuration at the new chiral carbon formed on ring closure (the anomeric carbon, C1). α-D-glucose and β-D-glucose are anomers.</details>

7. (2) Why is sucrose a non-reducing sugar although it contains glucose?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The glycosidic link joins C1 of glucose to C2 of fructose, so both anomeric carbons are used. Neither ring can open to give a free CHO or C=O group.</details>

8. (2) What is the difference between a nucleoside and a nucleotide?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A nucleoside is a base plus a sugar. A nucleotide is a nucleoside with a phosphate group attached.</details>

9. (3) Write the zwitterion structure of glycine and explain why amino acids have high melting points.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>⁺H₃N–CH₂–COO⁻. Amino acids are salt-like (ionic) crystals, held together by strong electrostatic attraction between the charges, so they melt at high temperatures and dissolve in water.</details>

10. (3) What happens when a protein is denatured? Give two ways to denature a protein and one example from daily life.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The secondary and tertiary structure are lost (the shape unfolds), and with it the biological activity; the primary structure (peptide bonds) stays. Heat and acid or alkali do it. Boiling an egg makes the white set; adding lemon to milk curdles it.</details>

11. (3) Name three differences between DNA and RNA.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>DNA has deoxyribose, RNA has ribose; DNA has thymine, RNA has uracil; DNA is double-stranded, RNA is usually single-stranded.</details>

12. (3) 6.84 g of sucrose (342 g/mol) is hydrolysed completely. Find the mass of glucose formed.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Moles of sucrose = 6.84/342 = 0.020 mol. One mole gives one mole of glucose, so mass = 0.020 × 180 = 3.6 g.

</details>

## 5-mark questions

13. (5) (a) Give three reactions that show glucose has an aldehyde group and five OH groups. (b) Why does glucose not give the Schiff test? (c) What is mutarotation?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Bromine water gives gluconic acid (aldehyde); HCN gives a cyanohydrin and hydroxylamine an oxime (carbonyl); acetic anhydride gives a pentaacetate (five OH).

(b) Glucose exists mainly as a cyclic hemiacetal, so there is only a very small amount of free CHO (less than 0.02 %).

(c) Mutarotation is the slow change of the optical rotation of a fresh solution of α- or β-glucose (+111° or +19.2°) to the equilibrium value of +52.5°, because the ring opens and closes, and the α and β forms interconvert.

</details>

14. (5) (a) Name the four levels of protein structure. (b) Which bond or force stabilises each? (c) How does a peptide chain of 50 amino acids differ in bonds from one of 10?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a, b) Primary: sequence, peptide bonds. Secondary: α-helix and β-sheet, hydrogen bonds between backbone C=O and N–H. Tertiary: folding of the whole chain, by hydrogen bonds, ionic attraction, hydrophobic packing and disulphide bridges. Quaternary: assembly of several chains, held by the same weak attractions.

(c) The 50-residue chain has 49 peptide bonds; the 10-residue chain has 9.

</details>

## Case-based question

15. **Case.** A student studies a short DNA fragment with 40 base pairs. Chemical analysis shows it has 15 adenine bases on one strand and 30 % of all the bases on both strands together are G or C.

(a) How many A–T base pairs are there? (1)
(b) How many hydrogen bonds hold the fragment together? (1)
(c) Which type of base pair makes DNA more stable to heat, and why? (1)
(d) Name the enzyme-driven process by which the fragment is copied before a cell divides. (1)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) G–C pairs = 30 % of 40 = 12, so A–T pairs = 28. (b) 28 × 2 + 12 × 3 = 56 + 36 = 92 hydrogen bonds. (c) G–C, which has three hydrogen bonds against two for A–T. (d) Replication, carried out by DNA polymerase.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

16. **Assertion (A):** Cellulose cannot be digested by humans.
**Reason (R):** Humans lack the enzyme that breaks the β-1,4 glycosidic link.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true and R explains A. Option 1.</details>

17. **Assertion (A):** Amino acids have high melting points and are soluble in water.
**Reason (R):** They exist as zwitterions.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true and R explains A: the ionic character gives strong attractions between molecules and favourable interaction with water. Option 1.</details>

18. **Assertion (A):** A competitive inhibitor can be overcome by adding more substrate.
**Reason (R):** The inhibitor permanently destroys the enzyme.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is true and R is false. A competitive inhibitor binds reversibly to the active site; extra substrate competes it away. Option 3.</details>
