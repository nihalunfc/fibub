**Practice · Class 12 Chemistry · Chapter 2.** Read the chapter lesson, [Electrochemistry](/learn/courses/ncert-class-12-chemistry/electrochemistry/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer. Marks go for the half-reactions, the formula, the substitution with units, and the final answer.

<div class="bigidea">Four tools solve almost every question: E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode, the Nernst equation for non-standard concentrations, Faraday’s law m = ItM/(nF) for electrolysis, and Λm = 1000κ/c for conductance.</div>

## How to set out an answer

1. Write the two half-reactions and say which is oxidation (anode) and which is reduction (cathode).
2. Use reduction potentials from the table; do not multiply E° by coefficients.
3. For concentrations other than 1 M, write Q for the cell reaction first, then use Nernst.
4. For electrolysis: Q = It (seconds!), then moles of electrons = Q/F, then moles of metal = moles of e⁻ ÷ n.

## 1-mark questions

1. In a galvanic cell, which electrode is the anode and what happens there?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The negative electrode; oxidation occurs there (the metal loses electrons).</details>

2. What is the use of a salt bridge?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It completes the electrical circuit and keeps both half-cell solutions electrically neutral by letting ions move.</details>

3. State Kohlrausch’s law in one line.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>At infinite dilution the molar conductivity of an electrolyte is the sum of the molar conductivities of its cation and anion, each contributing independently.</details>

4. Why is it safer to store zinc-plated iron sheets outdoors than tin-plated sheets that may be scratched?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Zinc is more reactive than iron, so it corrodes first and protects the iron even where the coating is scratched. Tin is less reactive than iron, so a scratched tin coat makes iron corrode faster.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

5. (2) Write the cell reaction and cell notation for the combination of the electrodes Ag⁺/Ag (+0.80 V) and Zn²⁺/Zn (−0.76 V).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Zn is the anode, Ag the cathode. Notation: Zn(s) | Zn²⁺(aq) || Ag⁺(aq) | Ag(s). Cell reaction: `$\text{Zn} + 2\text{Ag}^+ \to \text{Zn}^{2+} + 2\text{Ag}$`. E°cell = 0.80 − (−0.76) = 1.56 V.

</details>

6. (2) What happens to the emf of a Daniell cell as it discharges? Explain with the Nernst equation.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

It falls. As the cell works, [Zn²⁺] rises and [Cu²⁺] falls, so Q = [Zn²⁺]/[Cu²⁺] increases, and `$E = E^\circ - \frac{0.0592}{2}\log Q$` decreases until E = 0 when Q = K (the cell is dead).

</details>

7. (3) A cell has E° = 0.46 V for a reaction with n = 2. Find ΔG° and log K at 298 K (F = 96500 C/mol).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

`$\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ = -2\times96500\times0.46 = -88780$` J/mol = −88.8 kJ/mol.

`$\log K = nE^\circ/0.0592 = 2\times0.46/0.0592 = 15.5$`, so K ≈ 3 × 10¹⁵.

</details>

8. (3) The resistance of a 0.05 mol/L electrolyte solution is 250 Ω in a cell of cell constant 1.25 cm⁻¹. Find κ and Λm.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

`$\kappa = 1.25/250 = 5.0\times10^{-3}$` S/cm. `$\Lambda_m = 1000\kappa/c = 1000\times5.0\times10^{-3}/0.05 = 100$` S cm² mol⁻¹.

</details>

9. (3) How long must a current of 4 A flow to deposit 2.7 g of aluminium from Al³⁺? (Al = 27 g/mol, F = 96500 C/mol)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Moles Al = 2.7/27 = 0.10 mol; electrons = 0.30 mol; Q = 0.30 × 96500 = 28 950 C. `$t = Q/I = 28950/4 = 7237$` s ≈ 2 h 0 min 37 s.

</details>

10. (3) Why does the molar conductivity of a weak electrolyte rise so sharply on dilution, unlike a strong electrolyte?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A weak electrolyte ionises only slightly in concentrated solution; on dilution its degree of ionisation α increases, so many more ions are produced per mole. A strong electrolyte is already fully ionised, and on dilution only the crowding of ions falls, so its Λm rises only slightly.</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

11. (5) Derive the Nernst equation for the cell reaction `$aA + bB \to cC + dD$` and use it to show that log K = nE°/0.0592 at 298 K.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

For the reaction, `$\Delta G = \Delta G^\circ + RT\ln Q$` with `$Q=\dfrac{[C]^c[D]^d}{[A]^a[B]^b}$`.

The electrical work from the cell gives `$\Delta G = -nFE$` and `$\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ$`. Substituting, `$-nFE = -nFE^\circ + RT\ln Q$`, so

`$E = E^\circ - \dfrac{RT}{nF}\ln Q$`. At 298 K with `$\ln = 2.303\log$`, `$\dfrac{2.303RT}{F}=0.0592$` V, so `$E = E^\circ - \dfrac{0.0592}{n}\log Q$`.

At equilibrium E = 0 and Q = K: `$0 = E^\circ - \dfrac{0.0592}{n}\log K$`, so `$\log K = \dfrac{nE^\circ}{0.0592}$`.

</details>

12. (5) Explain, with the electrode reactions, the working of the lead storage battery during discharge and charging.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Discharge. Anode (Pb): `$\text{Pb} + \text{SO}_4^{2-} \to \text{PbSO}_4 + 2e^-$`. Cathode (PbO₂): `$\text{PbO}_2 + 4\text{H}^+ + \text{SO}_4^{2-} + 2e^- \to \text{PbSO}_4 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$`. Overall: `$\text{Pb} + \text{PbO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to 2\text{PbSO}_4 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$`. Both plates are coated with PbSO₄, sulphuric acid is used up and its density falls (from about 1.28 to 1.10 g/mL). Each cell gives about 2 V.

Charging. An external source larger than 2 V reverses the reaction: PbSO₄ is converted back to Pb at one plate and to PbO₂ at the other, and H₂SO₄ is regenerated, so the density of the acid rises again. The battery is a secondary cell.

</details>

13. (5) Using the data: Λ°m(HCl) = 426, Λ°m(NaCl) = 126, Λ°m(CH₃COONa) = 91 S cm² mol⁻¹, (a) find Λ°m(CH₃COOH); (b) a 0.01 mol/L solution of ethanoic acid has Λm = 12.5 S cm² mol⁻¹, find α and K<sub>a</sub>.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) By Kohlrausch’s law, `$\Lambda_m^\circ(\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}) = 426 + 91 - 126 = 391$` S cm² mol⁻¹.

(b) `$\alpha = \Lambda_m/\Lambda_m^\circ = 12.5/391 = 0.032$`. `$K_a = \dfrac{c\alpha^2}{1-\alpha} = \dfrac{0.01\times(0.032)^2}{0.968} = 1.06\times10^{-5}$`.

</details>

## Case-based question

14. **Why the iron gate rusts and the scooter battery dies.** A school’s iron gate by the sea rusts quickly, though the gate in the school’s hill campus rusts slowly. A groundskeeper paints one gate and fits a block of magnesium to a buried iron water pipe. Take E° values: Fe²⁺/Fe −0.44 V, Mg²⁺/Mg −2.37 V, O₂, H⁺/H₂O +1.23 V.

(a) (1) Write the anode reaction of the rusting process.

(b) (1) Why does the seaside gate rust faster?

(c) (1) How does the magnesium block protect the pipe?

(d) (1) Calculate E° of the cell formed by iron (anode) and oxygen (cathode).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) `$\text{Fe} \to \text{Fe}^{2+} + 2e^-$`.

(b) Sea spray provides salt, which makes the water in contact with the iron a much better electrolyte, so the rusting cell delivers current faster.

(c) Magnesium has a much lower reduction potential (−2.37 V) than iron (−0.44 V), so magnesium is oxidised in preference and the pipe becomes the cathode (sacrificial protection).

(d) E°cell = E°(cathode) − E°(anode) = 1.23 − (−0.44) = 1.67 V, large and positive, so rusting is spontaneous.

</details>

## Assertion-reason questions

Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

15. **A:** The cell potential of a galvanic cell becomes zero after a long time. **R:** The reaction quotient becomes equal to the equilibrium constant.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A: at Q = K, E = 0 and ΔG = 0, so no more work can be obtained.</details>

16. **A:** Electrolysis of aqueous NaCl solution gives hydrogen at the cathode, not sodium. **R:** Water is reduced more easily than Na⁺ ions.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A: the reduction potential of water to H₂ is much higher (less negative) than that of Na⁺ to Na (−2.71 V), so H₂ forms.</details>

17. **A:** Molar conductivity of a strong electrolyte decreases when the concentration increases. **R:** The ions get closer, and their attraction slows them.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A) Both true and R explains A: in a stronger solution the interionic attraction is greater and the ions move more slowly, so each mole conducts less.</details>
