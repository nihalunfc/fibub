**Practice · Class 12 Chemistry · Chapter 4.** Read the chapter lesson, [The d- and f-Block Elements](/learn/courses/ncert-class-12-chemistry/the-d-and-f-block-elements/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer. Board marks go for the correct configuration, the reason in one clear sentence and the final answer with units.

<div class="bigidea">Almost every question in this chapter is answered from three ideas: the 3d and 4s electrons are close in energy, a partly filled d subshell gives colour and magnetism, and a half-full or full subshell is extra stable.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For configurations, write the atom first, then remove 4s electrons before 3d electrons for ions.
2. For magnetic moment, count the unpaired electrons n and write μ = √(n(n + 2)) BM.
3. For an “explain” question give the cause in one sentence (energy, size or stability) and then the effect.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the element of the 3d series that shows the maximum number of oxidation states.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Manganese, +2 to +7.</details>

2. Why is Zn not considered a transition element?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Its atom (3d¹⁰ 4s²) and its common ion Zn²⁺ (3d¹⁰) both have a completely filled d subshell.</details>

3. Which transition metal has a positive E°(M²⁺/M) value?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Copper (+0.34 V).</details>

4. Give the formula used for the spin-only magnetic moment.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>μ = √(n(n + 2)) BM, where n is the number of unpaired electrons.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

5. (2) Why do transition metals have high melting points?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both the (n−1)d and the ns electrons take part in metallic bonding, and the more unpaired d electrons there are the stronger the bond, so a lot of energy is needed to break the lattice.</details>

6. (2) Write the electron configurations of Cr and Cu and give the reason for the unusual ones.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Cr is [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹ and Cu is [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹. A half-filled or a completely filled d subshell has extra stability (exchange energy, symmetry), and the 3d and 4s energies are close, so one 4s electron moves into 3d.</details>

7. (2) Why is Sc³⁺ colourless but Ti³⁺ purple?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Sc³⁺ is 3d⁰: there is no d electron to jump between the split d levels, so no visible light is absorbed. Ti³⁺ is 3d¹: the single d electron can absorb a photon and jump, so a colour is transmitted.</details>

8. (3) Explain why Mn²⁺ is more stable than Mn³⁺, but Fe³⁺ is more stable than Fe²⁺.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Mn²⁺ is 3d⁵ (half-filled, extra stable) so a third electron is hard to remove: Mn³⁺ is a strong oxidant. Fe²⁺ is 3d⁶; losing one electron gives Fe³⁺ with the stable 3d⁵, so Fe²⁺ is easily oxidised to Fe³⁺.</details>

9. (3) What is meant by interstitial compounds? Give two properties.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Compounds formed when small atoms (H, C, N) occupy the gaps between the metal atoms in the lattice, for example TiC. They are very hard and have high melting points (higher than the metal), they are chemically inert and keep metallic conduction. They are non-stoichiometric.</details>

10. (3) Calculate the spin-only magnetic moment of Ni²⁺ and say whether it is paramagnetic.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Ni²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁸, so n = 2 (two unpaired). `$\mu = \sqrt{2\times4} = 2.83$` BM. It is paramagnetic.

</details>

11. (3) What is the lanthanoid contraction? Give two consequences.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The slow decrease in size from La to Lu, because the 4f electrons shield the nucleus poorly. Consequences: Zr and Hf (4d and 5d) have almost the same radius and similar chemistry; and the lanthanoids are hard to separate from one another. Also the basic strength of Ln(OH)₃ falls from La to Lu.</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

12. (5) Explain the following with reasons: (a) transition metals form coloured ions, (b) they show variable oxidation states, (c) they are good catalysts. Give one example for (c).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Colour: the d orbitals split in a ligand environment. A d electron absorbs a photon of energy equal to the gap and jumps; the colour seen is complementary to the light absorbed. With d⁰ or d¹⁰ no jump is possible, so those ions are colourless.

(b) Oxidation states: the 3d and 4s electrons are close in energy, so any number of them can be used in bonding. The states differ by one unit, for example Mn +2 to +7.

(c) Catalysis: they change oxidation state easily and pass the reactants atoms or electrons, and the surface adsorbs reactant molecules. Example: V₂O₅ in the contact process, SO₂ + V₂O₅ → SO₃ + V₂O₄, then V₂O₄ + ½O₂ → V₂O₅.

</details>

13. (5) Compare the lanthanoids and the actinoids under four headings: subshell being filled, oxidation states, radioactivity and basic character.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

| Property | Lanthanoids | Actinoids |
|---|---|---|
| Subshell | 4f | 5f |
| Oxidation states | mostly +3 (some +2, +4) | +3 with +4 to +7 common |
| Radioactivity | only promethium | all |
| Basic character | less basic hydroxides | more basic hydroxides |

The 5f orbitals extend further and are close to 6d and 7s in energy, so the actinoids use more electrons and show more states; they also have a larger contraction.

</details>

## Case-based question

A student measures the effect of a magnet on four solutions of nitrate salts of equal concentration. The balance shows a very small drop for solution P, and gains of 12 mg, 24 mg and 60 mg for solutions Q, R and S. The ions are Zn²⁺, Cu²⁺, Mn²⁺ and Ni²⁺ in some order.

14. (a) Which ion is in P? (b) Which is in S? (c) Calculate the magnetic moment of the ion in R. (d) Why does the balance reading of P drop?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) P is Zn²⁺ (3d¹⁰), the only diamagnetic ion: it is pushed out of the field, so the reading falls slightly.

(b) S gained the most, so it has the most unpaired electrons: Mn²⁺ (3d⁵, n = 5). The gains go in the ratio 1 : 2 : 5 for Q, R, S, which fits Cu²⁺ (n = 1), Ni²⁺ (n = 2) and Mn²⁺ (n = 5).

(c) R is Ni²⁺, 3d⁸, n = 2: `$\mu = \sqrt{2\times4} = 2.83$` BM.

(d) All the electrons of Zn²⁺ are paired, so the ion has no magnet of its own; the field induces a very weak opposing magnetism and the sample is pushed out.

</details>

## Assertion-reason questions

15. **Assertion (A):** Cr has the configuration 3d⁵ 4s¹, not 3d⁴ 4s². **Reason (R):** A half-filled d subshell is extra stable.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R explains A.</details>

16. **Assertion (A):** Zr and Hf are very difficult to separate. **Reason (R):** They have the same number of valence electrons and nearly the same radius because of the lanthanoid contraction.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true, and R explains A.</details>

17. **Assertion (A):** Copper dissolves in dilute hydrochloric acid. **Reason (R):** E°(Cu²⁺/Cu) is positive.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false (Cu does not displace hydrogen from dilute acid), R is true: that is why Cu does not dissolve.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Configurations: write the atom, remove 4s electrons before 3d. Cr and Cu are the exceptions.
- μ = √(n(n + 2)) BM; paramagnetic if n > 0.
- Colour needs a d–d jump (partly filled d subshell); d⁰ and d¹⁰ are colourless.
- Variable oxidation states, catalysis and complexes come from close 3d and 4s energies and empty d orbitals.
- Lanthanoid contraction: poor 4f shielding; Zr and Hf alike; lanthanoids hard to separate.
- Actinoids: 5f, radioactive, many oxidation states, larger contraction.

</div>
