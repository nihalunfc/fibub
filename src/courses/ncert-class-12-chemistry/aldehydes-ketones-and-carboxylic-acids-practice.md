**Practice · Class 12 Chemistry · Chapter 8.** Read the chapter lesson, [Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids](/learn/courses/ncert-class-12-chemistry/aldehydes-ketones-and-carboxylic-acids/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer. Marks go for the right reagent, the product with its name, and a one-line reason.

<div class="bigidea">Ask three questions of every problem: Is it an aldehyde or a ketone (can it be oxidised)? Does it have an α hydrogen (aldol) or none (Cannizzaro)? In an acid, which groups pull electrons (stronger) and which push (weaker)?</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Name the functional group and say which reaction type applies (addition, oxidation, condensation).
2. Write the equation with reagents above the arrow and name the organic product.
3. For “explain”, give the cause (electron effect, resonance, hydrogen bonding) and then the effect.

## 1-mark questions

1. Give the IUPAC name of CH₃CH(CH₃)CHO.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>2-methylpropanal.</details>

2. Which test gives a yellow precipitate with propanone?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Iodoform test (I₂ with NaOH): yellow CHI₃.</details>

3. What is the product of ethanal with HCN?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Ethanal cyanohydrin, 2-hydroxypropanenitrile, CH₃CH(OH)CN.</details>

4. Name the reagent that reduces C=O to CH₂ in acidic medium.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Zinc amalgam with concentrated hydrochloric acid (Clemmensen reduction).</details>

5. Write the formula of the compound formed when benzene reacts with CH₃COCl and AlCl₃.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>C₆H₅COCH₃ (acetophenone).</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

6. (2) Why do carboxylic acids have higher boiling points than aldehydes and ketones of the same molar mass?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Carboxylic acid molecules form hydrogen-bonded dimers (two O–H···O bonds per pair). Aldehydes and ketones have no O–H and are held only by dipole–dipole forces.</details>

7. (2) Why is benzaldehyde less reactive than ethanal in nucleophilic addition?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The benzene ring donates electrons by resonance to the carbonyl carbon, reducing its positive charge. In ethanal the CH₃ group has only a weak electron-pushing effect.</details>

8. (2) How would you distinguish pentan-2-one from pentan-3-one?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Iodoform test. Pentan-2-one has a CH₃CO– group and gives yellow iodoform; pentan-3-one does not.</details>

9. (3) Write the products of: (a) propanal + Tollens’ reagent, (b) propanone + NaBH₄ then water, (c) benzaldehyde + concentrated NaOH.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Propanoate and a silver mirror (propanoic acid after acidification). (b) Propan-2-ol. (c) Sodium benzoate and benzyl alcohol (Cannizzaro reaction).</details>

10. (3) Arrange in increasing order of acid strength and give reasons: ClCH₂COOH, CH₃COOH, HCOOH, FCH₂COOH.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>CH₃COOH < HCOOH < ClCH₂COOH < FCH₂COOH (pKa 4.76, 3.75, 2.86, 2.59). Alkyl groups push electrons and weaken the acid; H has no such effect; Cl and F withdraw electrons (−I) and stabilise the anion, with F stronger because it is more electronegative.</details>

11. (3) Explain with the mechanism why heating ethanal with dilute NaOH gives but-2-enal.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Dilute base removes an α hydrogen from ethanal to give an enolate ion. The enolate carbon attacks the C=O of a second ethanal, giving the alkoxide, which is protonated to the aldol, 3-hydroxybutanal. On heating the –OH and an α hydrogen are lost as water, giving CH₃CH=CHCHO.

</details>

12. (3) 3.0 g of a monobasic acid needs 50 mL of 1 mol/L NaOH for complete neutralisation. Find its molar mass and name a likely acid.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Moles of NaOH = 1 × 0.050 = 0.050 mol = moles of acid. Molar mass = 3.0/0.050 = 60 g/mol, so the acid is ethanoic acid (CH₃COOH).

</details>

## 5-mark questions

13. (5) (a) Show with curved steps how HCN adds to propanone. (b) Why is the reaction faster with a trace of base? (c) Give the reactivity order for HCHO, CH₃CHO, CH₃COCH₃ with reasons.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) CN⁻ attacks the δ⁺ carbonyl carbon, the π electrons move to oxygen to give an alkoxide, and the oxygen picks up H⁺ from HCN to give the cyanohydrin (CH₃)₂C(OH)CN.

(b) HCN is a weak acid; base produces CN⁻, which is a much better nucleophile than HCN.

(c) HCHO > CH₃CHO > CH₃COCH₃: each alkyl group pushes electrons to the carbonyl carbon (less δ⁺) and adds steric hindrance.

</details>

14. (5) (a) Why is the pKa of ethanoic acid (4.76) lower than that of phenol (10)? (b) How do you distinguish a carboxylic acid from a phenol in the lab? (c) Write the equation for decarboxylation of sodium ethanoate.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) The carboxylate ion shares its charge between two electronegative oxygen atoms (two equal resonance structures); in phenoxide the charge is shared with ring carbons, which are less electronegative, so it is less stable.

(b) The acid gives brisk CO₂ with NaHCO₃; phenol does not (it is too weak an acid). Phenol also gives a violet colour with neutral FeCl₃.

(c) CH₃COONa + NaOH → CH₄ + Na₂CO₃ (heated with soda lime, CaO).

</details>

## Case-based question

15. **Case.** A student has three unlabelled liquids in test tubes. They are propanal, propanone and propanoic acid. She tests each with (i) Tollens’ reagent and (ii) sodium hydrogencarbonate.

(a) Which liquid gives CO₂? (1)
(b) Which liquid gives a silver mirror? (1)
(c) Which liquid gives neither result, and what test would show it has a C=O group? (1)
(d) Write the equation for the reaction in (a). (1)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Propanoic acid. (b) Propanal. (c) Propanone; the 2,4-DNP test gives an orange-yellow precipitate (and so does propanal). (d) CH₃CH₂COOH + NaHCO₃ → CH₃CH₂COONa + H₂O + CO₂.

</details>

## Assertion and reason

16. **Assertion (A):** Benzaldehyde does not give a red precipitate with Fehling’s solution.
**Reason (R):** Fehling’s solution oxidises only aliphatic aldehydes.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true and R explains A. Option 1.</details>

17. **Assertion (A):** Methanal gives the Cannizzaro reaction but ethanal gives the aldol reaction in dilute alkali.
**Reason (R):** Methanal has no α hydrogen.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true and R explains A: ethanal has α hydrogens and so forms the enolate; methanal cannot. Option 1.</details>

18. **Assertion (A):** Ethanoic acid is a stronger acid than ethanol.
**Reason (R):** In ethanoate, the negative charge is shared by two oxygen atoms.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true and R explains A. Option 1.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Aldehydes are oxidised (Tollens, Fehling); ketones are not; methyl ketones give iodoform; 2,4-DNP tests any C=O.
- Nucleophilic addition: HCHO > RCHO > R₂CO (electronic and steric reasons).
- α hydrogen: aldol (dilute base); none: Cannizzaro (concentrated base).
- Acid strength: –I groups increase it, alkyl groups decrease it; carboxylates are stabilised by resonance.

</div>
