**Practice · Class 12 Chemistry · Chapter 5.** Read the chapter lesson, [Coordination Compounds](/learn/courses/ncert-class-12-chemistry/coordination-compounds/), first. Try each question on paper before opening the answer. Marks are given for the formula, the rule used, and the final name, number or structure.

<div class="bigidea">Four habits solve almost everything here: count donor atoms for the coordination number, add charges for the oxidation state, name ligands alphabetically, and for CFT decide between weak field (spread out) and strong field (pair up in t₂g).</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Write the formula with brackets: complex inside, counter ions outside.
2. For oxidation state: charge of complex = metal state + sum of ligand charges.
3. For naming: ligands in alphabetical order, then metal with its Roman numeral; -ate if the complex is an anion.
4. For magnetism: write t₂g and e_g occupation, count unpaired electrons, then μ = √(n(n + 2)).

## 1-mark questions

1. Define a ligand.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>An ion or molecule with at least one lone pair that donates it to a central metal atom or ion to form a coordinate bond.</details>

2. Write the formula of tetraamminedichloridocobalt(III) chloride.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>[Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]Cl.</details>

3. What is meant by the term “chelate”?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A complex in which a polydentate ligand binds the metal through two or more donor atoms and so closes a ring, such as [Co(en)₃]³⁺.</details>

4. Which shape do four-coordinate complexes of Pt(II) usually have?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Square planar.</details>

## 2- and 3-mark questions

5. (2) Give the IUPAC name of K₃[Fe(CN)₆].

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Potassium hexacyanidoferrate(III). Three K⁺ and six CN⁻ give 3 − 6 = −3 for the anion, so Fe is +3, and an anionic complex takes the -ate ending (ferrate).</details>

6. (2) Write the formulae of an ionisation isomer pair for [Co(NH₃)₅Br]SO₄.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>[Co(NH₃)₅Br]SO₄ and [Co(NH₃)₅SO₄]Br. The first gives a white precipitate with BaCl₂, the second a pale yellow one with AgNO₃.</details>

7. (2) Why is [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ diamagnetic but [NiCl₄]²⁻ paramagnetic?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>CN⁻ is a strong-field ligand: the two unpaired 3d electrons of Ni²⁺ are pushed into pairs, leaving one empty 3d orbital, so dsp² hybridisation gives a square planar, diamagnetic complex. Cl⁻ is weak, so the unpaired electrons stay and sp³ hybridisation gives a tetrahedral complex with 2 unpaired electrons.</details>

8. (3) Draw or describe the geometrical isomers of [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺ and state which is polar.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>In the **cis** isomer the two Cl are 90° apart, so their dipoles do not cancel: polar (violet). In the **trans** isomer the two Cl are opposite each other and their dipoles cancel: non-polar (green).</details>

9. (3) Explain why [Co(en)₃]³⁺ has optical isomers but [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ does not.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The three bidentate en rings make [Co(en)₃]³⁺ a propeller whose mirror image cannot be superimposed on it, so it is chiral and exists as a pair of enantiomers. [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ is perfectly symmetric with several mirror planes, so it is achiral.</details>

10. (3) Calculate the CFSE (in Δₒ) of a low-spin octahedral d⁶ ion and a high-spin octahedral d⁶ ion.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Low spin: t₂g⁶ e_g⁰: `$\text{CFSE} = -0.4\times 6 = -2.4\,\Delta_o$`.

High spin: t₂g⁴ e_g²: `$\text{CFSE} = -0.4\times 4 + 0.6\times 2 = -0.4\,\Delta_o$`.

</details>

11. (3) Arrange NH₃, CN⁻ and Cl⁻ in order of increasing Δₒ and say which gives a low-spin Fe²⁺ complex.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Cl⁻ < NH₃ < CN⁻ (spectrochemical series). CN⁻ gives the large Δₒ that makes [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ low spin (t₂g⁶, diamagnetic); Cl⁻ gives a high-spin complex.</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

12. (5) Explain Werner's theory using the compound CoCl₃·5NH₃. Why does it give 2 moles of AgCl with excess AgNO₃?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Cobalt shows a primary valence (oxidation state +3, satisfied by negative ions that may be free) and a secondary valence (coordination number 6, satisfied by groups bonded directly in an octahedron).

In CoCl₃·5NH₃ the six places in the secondary sphere hold 5 NH₃ and 1 Cl⁻, so the formula is [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂. The two Cl⁻ outside the bracket are free and are precipitated by Ag⁺: 2 mol AgCl. The Cl inside is covalently bonded to cobalt and gives no precipitate.

</details>

13. (5) Using valence bond theory, give the hybridisation, shape and magnetic behaviour of [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ and [FeF₆]³⁻.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**[Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻:** Fe²⁺ is 3d⁶. Strong-field CN⁻ pairs the electrons into three 3d boxes, freeing two 3d orbitals. Two 3d + 4s + three 4p give d²sp³, octahedral, inner orbital. No unpaired electron: diamagnetic, low spin.

**[FeF₆]³⁻:** Fe³⁺ is 3d⁵. Weak-field F⁻ does not pair the electrons. The 4s, three 4p and two 4d orbitals give sp³d², octahedral, outer orbital. Five unpaired electrons: `$\mu = \sqrt{5\times7} = 5.92$` BM, paramagnetic, high spin.

</details>

## Case-based question

A chemistry club prepares three solutions from the same sample of chromium(III) chloride hexahydrate, CrCl₃·6H₂O: A is violet, B is blue-green and C is dark green. When 0.01 mole of each is treated with excess AgNO₃, A gives 4.3 g, B gives 2.9 g and C gives 1.4 g of white precipitate. (M of AgCl = 143.5 g/mol.)

14. (a) Calculate the moles of AgCl in each case. (b) Write the formula of each solution's complex. (c) What type of isomerism is this? (d) Can the cation of the violet solution show cis–trans isomerism? Why?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) A: 4.3/143.5 = 0.030 mol; B: 2.9/143.5 = 0.020 mol; C: 1.4/143.5 = 0.010 mol. That is 3, 2 and 1 free chloride per formula unit.

(b) A: [Cr(H₂O)₆]Cl₃; B: [Cr(H₂O)₅Cl]Cl₂·H₂O; C: [Cr(H₂O)₄Cl₂]Cl·2H₂O.

(c) Hydrate isomerism (a kind of structural isomerism): the amount of water inside the coordination sphere differs.

(d) No. In [Cr(H₂O)₆]³⁺ all six ligands are the same, so every arrangement of them round the metal is identical; cis and trans need at least two different kinds of ligand.

</details>

## Assertion-reason questions

15. **Assertion (A):** [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ is diamagnetic. **Reason (R):** Co³⁺ in a strong field has the configuration t₂g⁶ e_g⁰.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true, and R explains A.</details>

16. **Assertion (A):** Tetrahedral complexes are generally high spin. **Reason (R):** Δₜ is smaller than the pairing energy because Δₜ = 4/9 Δₒ.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true, and R explains A.</details>

17. **Assertion (A):** Complexes of Zn²⁺ are colourless. **Reason (R):** Zn²⁺ has a d¹⁰ configuration, so no d–d transition is possible.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true, and R explains A.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Coordination number = donor atoms; oxidation state from charges; name ligands alphabetically.
- AgNO₃ precipitates only free (outside-the-bracket) chloride.
- Cis and trans are geometrical isomers; chirality (no mirror plane) gives optical isomers.
- Strong field: low spin, t₂g first (d²sp³); weak field: high spin (sp³d²). Tetrahedral: always high spin.
- CFSE octahedral = (−0.4 n(t₂g) + 0.6 n(e_g))Δₒ; μ = √(n(n + 2)).

</div>
