Run the history of the universe back far enough and the temperature and density climb without limit. Long before the formulas give infinity, they stop being trustworthy. By the end you will be able to work out where that happens, explain why, and say honestly what we do and do not know about it.

<div class="bigidea">Three constants of nature, G for gravity, ħ for quantum physics and c for light, combine into one length, one time and one energy. At those scales gravity and quantum effects are equally strong, and physics as we know it stops. The first 10⁻⁴³ seconds belong there.</div>

## 1. Units made by nature

In 1899 Max Planck noticed that G, ħ (he used h) and c can be combined into a length, a time, a mass and a temperature that do not depend on any human choice, such as the size of the Earth or the length of a day:

```math
\ell_\text{P} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar G}{c^3}}, \qquad t_\text{P} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar G}{c^5}}, \qquad E_\text{P} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar c^5}{G}}
```

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>ℓ<sub>P</sub></dt><dd>the Planck length, 1.616 × 10<sup>−35</sup> m: about 10<sup>20</sup> times smaller than a proton</dd>
<dt>t<sub>P</sub></dt><dd>the Planck time, 5.39 × 10<sup>−44</sup> s: the time light takes to cross one Planck length</dd>
<dt>E<sub>P</sub></dt><dd>the Planck energy, 1.22 × 10<sup>19</sup> GeV (about 2 × 10<sup>9</sup> J); as a mass, 2.18 × 10<sup>−8</sup> kg, about 22 micrograms; as a temperature, 1.42 × 10<sup>32</sup> K</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>The scales where gravity (G), quantum physics (ħ) and relativity (c) all matter at once. They are not a "smallest possible size", but the place where our current theories can no longer be trusted.</dd>
</dl>

## 2. Why gravity and quantum physics collide there

To see something of size L you need a wave shorter than L, and in quantum physics a shorter wave carries more energy: about ħc/L. Squeeze that energy into a region of size L and, by E = mc² and general relativity, it has a Schwarzschild radius 2GE/c⁴ ([Lesson 3.7](/learn/courses/cosmology/black-holes/)). Make L small enough and the probe itself collapses into a black hole as big as the thing you wanted to see. Setting the two equal gives L of about the Planck length. Below it, the question "what is here?" has no answer in today's physics.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="planck-era-2"><figcaption>Mass against size. Below the black line, things are black holes; below the blue line, they are blurred by quantum fuzz. Everything we know sits in the clear region. The lines meet at the Planck scale, where we need a theory of quantum gravity.</figcaption></figure>

For an electron the quantum blur is about 10<sup>44</sup> times bigger than its Schwarzschild radius; for the Sun it is the other way round. Only near the crossing point do both matter: in the very early universe, and at the centres of black holes.

## 3. How far away is that?

<figure class="fig" data-fig="planck-era"><figcaption>A ladder of sizes, in powers of ten. The Planck length to the observable universe spans about 62 of them. The half-way point is a grain about a tenth of a millimetre wide. The bottom 16 powers of ten, below what the LHC can resolve, have never been explored.</figcaption></figure>

A grain of sand scaled up to the size of the observable universe would make a Planck length look about the size of that grain. The LHC collides protons at 1.36 × 10<sup>4</sup> GeV, about 10<sup>15</sup> times below the Planck energy; a collider built the same way would need to be light-years around.

## 4. The Planck era

Running the hot Big Bang backwards, the temperature reaches the Planck temperature at around the Planck time, 10<sup>−43</sup> s. Before that, we have no trusted description at all. This is the **Planck era**.

Two careful points:

- **Inflation changes the picture.** If inflation happened, the hot Big Bang began at reheating, probably at temperatures well below the Planck temperature. "10<sup>−43</sup> s after the Big Bang" then means 10<sup>−43</sup> s after a moment that our equations extrapolate to, not a moment we know happened.
- **The singularity is a warning sign, not a result.** Hawking and Penrose proved (1970) that, in general relativity with ordinary matter, the past must end in a singularity. In 2003 Arvind Borde, Alan Guth and Alexander Vilenkin showed that even an inflating universe cannot be extended infinitely into the past under general conditions. Both results assume general relativity holds all the way, which it surely does not at the Planck scale. Whether time had a beginning is still open.

## 5. Candidates for quantum gravity

- **String theory.** Particles are tiny vibrating strings, about the Planck length in size; one vibration is the graviton, the quantum of gravity. It needs extra space dimensions curled up very small. It has had mathematical successes, such as counting the quantum states of certain black holes (1996). It has made no prediction yet confirmed by experiment.
- **Loop quantum gravity.** Space itself is quantised: areas and volumes come in tiny chunks of about the Planck size. Applied to cosmology, models suggest the Big Bang may be replaced by a "bounce" from an earlier contracting universe (2006). This too is untested.
- **Others:** asymptotic safety, causal sets, causal dynamical triangulations and more. Each has supporters; none is established.

Tests are hard but not impossible. In 2009 the Fermi telescope timed gamma rays of very different energies from a burst 7 billion light-years away arriving within about a second of the burst's start, ruling out some simple guesses that space is "grainy" at the Planck length. Primordial gravitational waves (Lesson 11.4) would be a direct signal from near this frontier.

<div class="myth">

**"The Planck length is the pixel size of the universe."** Nobody has shown that space comes in pixels. The Planck length is where general relativity and quantum physics, as we know them, both stop working, so something new must happen there. Some theories (like loop quantum gravity) do give a smallest area; others do not. It is a signpost to the unknown, not a measured grain size.

<div class="keypoints">

- From G, ħ and c: Planck length 1.616 × 10<sup>−35</sup> m, Planck time 5.39 × 10<sup>−44</sup> s, Planck energy 1.22 × 10<sup>19</sup> GeV, Planck temperature 1.42 × 10<sup>32</sup> K.
- There, probing a distance needs so much energy that the probe would become a black hole: quantum physics and gravity both matter, and we have no tested theory.
- The LHC is about 10<sup>15</sup> times below the Planck energy; 16 of the 62 powers of ten between the Planck length and the observable universe are unexplored.
- The Planck era (before about 10<sup>−43</sup> s) is the limit of our knowledge, not a known event; inflation may mean the hot Big Bang never reached it.
- String theory and loop quantum gravity are leading candidates for quantum gravity; neither has been confirmed by experiment.
