**Module 5 · about 14 minutes.** Read first: [5.3 Critical density and Omega](/learn/courses/cosmology/critical-density/). The Friedmann equation needs to know the density at every moment. Each ingredient thins out at its own rate as the universe grows, and this lesson shows why. By the end you will be able to say which ingredient ruled the universe at any size.

<div class="bigidea">Grow the universe and matter spreads out, light spreads out and loses energy, and dark energy stays exactly as it is. So light ruled the young universe, matter the middle years, and dark energy rules the future.</div>

## 1. Three ways to thin out

<ul class="timeline">
<li><b>Matter (atoms and dark matter):</b> the same particles in a bigger box. Double the size and the volume is 8 times bigger, so the density is 8 times smaller: <b>ρ ∝ 1/a³</b>.</li>
<li><b>Radiation (light and fast neutrinos):</b> photons spread out just like matter (1/a³), and each one is stretched to a longer wavelength and lower energy (another 1/a): <b>ρ ∝ 1/a⁴</b>.</li>
<li><b>Dark energy (a cosmological constant):</b> an energy of empty space itself. More space, same energy in each cubic metre: <b>ρ = constant</b>.</li>
</ul>

```math
\rho_r \propto a^{-4}, \qquad \rho_m \propto a^{-3}, \qquad \rho_\Lambda = \text{constant}
```

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Power of a</dt><dd>how fast each ingredient fades as the universe grows: the higher the power, the faster it fades</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Run time backwards and radiation grows fastest, so it must have dominated early on. Run forwards and only dark energy is left. A universe's history is a hand-over between ingredients.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

## 2. Three eras

<ul class="timeline">
<li><b>Radiation era</b> (first ~50,000 years): light and neutrinos outweighed matter. The universe grew as a ∝ t<sup>1/2</sup>.</li>
<li><b>Matter era</b> (50,000 years to about 10 billion years): matter ruled; a ∝ t<sup>2/3</sup>. This is when galaxies formed (Module 10).</li>
<li><b>Dark-energy era</b> (the last ~4 billion years and the future): dark energy outweighs matter, and the expansion grows exponentially.</li>
</ul>

The moment matter overtook radiation is called **matter-radiation equality**. It happened at redshift about 3,400, around 50,000 years after the Big Bang (Lesson 6.8). Because radiation is 1/a⁴ and matter 1/a³, they were equal when a = Ω<sub>r</sub> / Ω<sub>m</sub>.

<div class="problem" data-problem="equality"></div>

**Worked example.** Matter and dark energy were equal when Ω<sub>m</sub>/a³ = Ω<sub>Λ</sub>, so a = (0.31 / 0.69)<sup>1/3</sup> = 0.77, a redshift of about 0.3, around 3.5 billion years ago. The Sun and Earth formed in the matter era; we live early in the dark-energy era.

<div class="history">

**Why we live at a special time.** Dark energy and matter happen to be about equal right now, within a factor of two or three, although one is constant and the other has changed by huge factors. Physicists call this the "why now?" or coincidence problem. It may be chance, or a clue about dark energy (Module 9).

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"If dark energy has constant density and the universe grows, energy is being created from nothing."** In an expanding universe, energy is not conserved in the everyday way: the light losing energy as it stretches is the other side of the same fact. General relativity has no single total energy for the whole universe.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Matter thins as 1/a³ (spreading), radiation as 1/a⁴ (spreading plus stretching), dark energy not at all.
- So radiation ruled first, matter next (from about 50,000 years, z ≈ 3,400), and dark energy now (from about 3.5 billion years ago).
- Radiation era a ∝ t<sup>1/2</sup>; matter era a ∝ t<sup>2/3</sup>; dark-energy era: exponential growth.
- Matter and dark energy are close to equal today: the "why now?" puzzle.

</div>
