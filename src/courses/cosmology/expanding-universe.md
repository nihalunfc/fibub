**Module 4 · about 13 minutes.** Read first: [4.2 Hubble's law](/learn/courses/cosmology/hubble-law/). Hubble's law says speed grows with distance. This lesson shows the simple picture that explains it. By the end you will be able to explain expansion with no centre, and say what does and does not expand.

<div class="bigidea">The universe expanding means the distances between galaxies are all growing by the same factor at the same time. Nothing is flying away from a centre: the space between galaxies is stretching.</div>

## 1. Raisins in rising dough

Put raisins in bread dough and let it rise. Every distance in the dough doubles in, say, an hour. Sit on any raisin and watch:

- A raisin 1 cm away ends up 2 cm away: it moved 1 cm in the hour.
- A raisin 3 cm away ends up 6 cm away: it moved 3 cm in the hour, three times as fast.

**Speed in proportion to distance.** That is exactly Hubble's law, and it comes from nothing more than even stretching. The raisins do not crawl through the dough. The dough between them grows. Galaxies are the raisins; space is the dough.

## 2. One number for the whole universe

If every distance grows by the same factor, then one number tracks the size of everything. Cosmologists call it the **scale factor**, written a. Today a = 1. When a was 0.5, every distance between far-apart galaxies was half what it is now.

```math
d(t) = a(t)\, d_{\text{now}} \quad\Rightarrow\quad v = \frac{\dot a}{a}\, d = H\, d
```

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>a(t)</dt><dd>the scale factor: how big the universe is at time t, compared with today</dd>
<dt>ȧ</dt><dd>how fast a is growing (the dot means "rate of change")</dd>
<dt>H = ȧ/a</dt><dd>the Hubble rate: the fraction by which distances grow per second. Today it is H<sub>0</sub></dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Hubble's law is not an extra rule. It follows automatically from all distances stretching together. Module 5 builds the whole history of the universe from a.</dd>
</dl>

## 3. Everyone is at the centre, so no one is

An astronomer in a galaxy a billion light-years away sees the same thing we do: galaxies moving away in every direction, faster the farther. No galaxy has a special place. This is a big clue that the universe is the same everywhere on large scales, an idea we test in [Lesson 4.8](/learn/courses/cosmology/cosmological-principle/).

<div class="myth">

**"The universe is expanding into something."** As far as we can tell, the universe is not a ball growing inside a bigger empty room. Distances within it grow. There need not be any "outside": the loaf in the picture has a crust, but the universe, as far as we know, has no edge.

</div>

## 4. What does not expand

Your body, the Earth, the Solar System and the Milky Way are not getting bigger. They are held together by forces much stronger than the gentle stretching: atoms by electric forces, the Solar System and galaxies by gravity. Even the **Local Group**, with Andromeda, is bound. The stretching only wins across the vast gaps between groups and clusters of galaxies.

How gentle is it? At 70 km/s per megaparsec, a distance grows by about 0.007% every million years. Across the 100,000 light-years of our galaxy that would be a tiny push, easily held by gravity.

<div class="history">

**Faster than light?** A galaxy far enough away, beyond about 14 billion light-years today, recedes faster than light. This breaks no law. Relativity forbids anything from passing something else, near by, faster than light. Here nothing passes anything: space between is growing. Davis and Lineweaver's 2004 paper "Expanding confusion" untangles this and other puzzles.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Expansion means all distances between far-apart galaxies grow by the same factor at the same time.
- Even stretching gives Hubble's law automatically: twice the distance, twice the speed.
- The scale factor a tracks the size of the universe (a = 1 today); the Hubble rate is H = ȧ/a.
- Every galaxy sees itself at the centre, so there is no centre.
- Bound things (atoms, planets, galaxies, the Local Group) do not expand.

</div>
