## 1. Ideas that carry Indian names

<figure class="fig" data-fig="india-in-cosmology"><figcaption>India in cosmology and astrophysics: selected landmarks. Tap a year or use the slider to read each story.</figcaption></figure>

- **Meghnad Saha** (1893-1956). His 1920 ionisation equation says how a hot gas divides itself between atoms and ions. It turned star spectra into thermometers, and the same balance, played out across the whole universe, tells when hydrogen became neutral and the CMB went free ([Lesson 6.9](/learn/courses/cosmology/recombination/)). He also founded the Saha Institute of Nuclear Physics in Kolkata.
- **Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar** (1910-1995). Aged 19, he found that a white dwarf heavier than about 1.4 Suns cannot hold itself up. That limit makes Type Ia supernovae nearly standard ([Lesson 2.4](/learn/courses/cosmology/type-ia-supernovae/)), and so led, 68 years later, to the discovery of dark energy. Nobel Prize in Physics, 1983; NASA's Chandra X-ray Observatory is named after him.
- **Amal Kumar Raychaudhuri** (1923-2005). His 1955 equation, worked out in Kolkata, shows how gravity focuses streams of matter. It is the heart of the Penrose-Hawking theorems: in general relativity a beginning like the Big Bang is hard to avoid ([Lesson 11.6](/learn/courses/cosmology/planck-era/)).
- **Jayant Narlikar** (1938-2025). With Fred Hoyle he built an alternative theory of gravity (1964), and he argued for a steady-state-like universe for decades ([Lesson 6.2](/learn/courses/cosmology/big-bang-vs-steady-state/)). Science needs good critics, and he was one. Founding director of IUCAA in Pune and a much-loved science writer in Marathi and English. He died in May 2025.
- **C. V. Vishveshwara** (1938-2017). In 1970 he showed that a disturbed black hole rings with its own set of notes, its quasi-normal modes. LIGO hears that ring-down at the end of every merger ([Lesson 12.6](/learn/courses/cosmology/standard-sirens/)).
- **Thanu Padmanabhan** (1957-2021), at IUCAA, wrote textbooks on structure formation and gravity used around the world and worked on the meaning of the cosmological constant.

## 2. Instruments and teams today

- **GMRT**, near Pune: 30 dishes, each 45 m across, spread over 25 km, conceived by Govind Swarup and run by NCRA-TIFR. It studies hydrogen in galaxies, pulsars and the radio sky at metre wavelengths, and was upgraded (uGMRT) by 2019.
- **Pulsar timing.** Pulsars are cosmic clocks. The **Indian Pulsar Timing Array (InPTA)** times them with the uGMRT at low frequencies, which helps correct for the effects of gas along the way. In June 2023, InPTA and the European array, with teams in the USA, Australia and China, reported evidence for a background of gravitational waves with periods of years, probably from pairs of giant black holes ([Lesson 3.9](/learn/courses/cosmology/gravitational-waves/)).
- **AstroSat**, India's first space observatory, launched on 28 September 2015, observing in ultraviolet and X-rays at once.
- **SARAS**, from the Raman Research Institute, tested the EDGES cosmic-dawn claim and did not find it ([Lesson 12.7](/learn/courses/cosmology/dark-ages-21cm/)).
- **Optical telescopes**: the 3.6 m Devasthal telescope (ARIES, Nainital, 2016) and the International Liquid Mirror Telescope (2022), and the 2 m Himalayan Chandra Telescope (IIA, Hanle, Ladakh).
- **Big collaborations.** Indian scientists were authors of the first gravitational-wave detection (2016), Indian groups analyse data from Planck and the big galaxy surveys and run their own simulations, and India takes part in building the **Square Kilometre Array** ([Lesson 12.7](/learn/courses/cosmology/dark-ages-21cm/)).
- **LIGO-India**, approved by the government in April 2023, is being built near Aundha in Hingoli district, Maharashtra, with US partners, and is planned to join the network in the 2030s. A detector far from the others makes the sky position of each event much sharper:

## 3. How to become a cosmologist

There are three kinds of cosmologist, and the field needs all of them: **theorists** (new models, like early dark energy or inflation), **observers and instrument builders** (telescopes, detectors, surveys like Euclid and SKA), and **computational cosmologists** (simulations and data analysis; the line between them is blurring). All three start the same way.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="india-in-cosmology-2"><figcaption>The usual steps, in India or abroad, with typical ages. Every route has exceptions: people join from engineering, mathematics and computer science at every stage.</figcaption></figure>

**At school (Classes 9 to 12).** Physics and mathematics are the core. Learn calculus early if you can. Read widely: this course, popular books, and the news of new discoveries ([fibub's discoveries blog](/blog/discoveries/jades-gs-z14-1-faint-galaxy-redshift-14/) explains real papers simply). Join an astronomy club; look through a telescope.

**Undergraduate.** A BSc or BS in physics, with as much mathematics as you can take (linear algebra, differential equations, probability, complex numbers). Integrated BS-MS programmes at the IISERs and NISER, and the BS at IISc, give research experience early. Learn to code in **Python**: almost all cosmology is now done with code, from fitting data to running simulations. The astronomy course's lessons on [reading research papers](/learn/courses/astronomy/papers-arxiv/) and [working with real data in Python](/learn/courses/astronomy/fits-python/) are a good start.

**Summer research.** Apply for summer projects from the second year: the IAS-INSA-NASI Summer Research Fellowship, TIFR's Visiting Students' Research Programme (VSRP), and the summer schools and student programmes of IUCAA, RRI and other institutes. A project with a real researcher teaches more than any exam, and their letter of recommendation matters for every later step.

**Master's and PhD in India.** IIT JAM is the main entrance to MSc programmes at the IITs and IISc. For a PhD (or integrated PhD after a BSc), JEST is used by many research institutes; CSIR NET and GATE qualify you for PhD fellowships; some institutes, such as TIFR, run their own tests. fibub has free preparation courses for [IIT JAM physics](/learn/courses/iit-jam-physics/), [GATE physics](/learn/courses/gate-physics/) and [CSIR NET physics](/learn/courses/csir-net-physics/). A PhD takes about five years, with a monthly fellowship.

**Abroad.** PhD programmes in the USA often take students straight after a BSc (five to six years, usually paid); in Europe and the UK a PhD is shorter (three to four years) and usually follows an MSc. Applications rest on grades, a statement of purpose, research experience and letters. Many Indian cosmologists trained abroad and came back to build groups here.

**After the PhD**, most people do one to three postdoctoral positions, often in different countries, before a permanent post as faculty or a scientist. It is a long road, and the skills (mathematics, statistics, coding, clear writing) are valued everywhere, from data science to climate research, if you change direction.

<div class="myth">

**"To become a cosmologist you need to be a genius, or go to an IIT, or study abroad."** What you need is steady work at mathematics and physics, curiosity, coding, and a few people who have seen you do research. Cosmologists come from state universities, IISERs, IITs and colleges across India; the exams (JAM, JEST, NET, GATE) are open to all graduates. Start where you are.

<div class="keypoints">

- Saha's ionisation equation (1920), Chandrasekhar's limit (1930), Raychaudhuri's equation (1955), Hoyle-Narlikar gravity (1964) and Vishveshwara's black hole ringing (1970) are woven into modern cosmology.
- Indian instruments and teams today: GMRT/uGMRT, InPTA (2023 nanohertz evidence), AstroSat (2015), SARAS 3 (2022), the SKA, and LIGO-India (approved 2023, planned for the 2030s).
- Research centres include IUCAA, NCRA, TIFR, ICTS, RRI, IIA, IISc, HRI, PRL and ARIES, plus IISERs, IITs and universities.
- Route: physics and mathematics at school; BSc/BS physics; summer projects; MSc via IIT JAM; PhD via JEST, CSIR NET, GATE or institute tests (or abroad); postdocs; a research post.
- Learn Python and read papers early. The open questions of this module are waiting.
