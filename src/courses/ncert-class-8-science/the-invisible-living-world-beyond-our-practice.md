**Practice · Class 8 Science · Chapter 2.** Read the chapter lesson, [The Invisible Living World: Beyond Our Naked Eye](/learn/courses/ncert-class-8-science/the-invisible-living-world-beyond-our/), first. Try each question before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Small things, big effects: microbes make curd and medicines, clean up waste and fix nitrogen, but also spoil food and cause disease. Total magnification is eyepiece × objective.</div>

## How to answer

1. For lists, name the microbe and the job (for example "yeast: makes bread rise").
2. For magnification, write the formula, the numbers and the unit (×).
3. Use the words "because" or "so" in longer answers, to link cause and effect.

## 1-mark questions

1. Who first used the word "cell" for the boxes seen in cork?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Robert Hooke (1665).</details>

2. Name a bacterium that turns milk into curd.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Lactobacillus.</details>

3. Which disease is caused by a protozoan carried by a mosquito?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Malaria (the protozoan is Plasmodium).</details>

4. Name an antibiotic obtained from a mould.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Penicillin (from Penicillium).</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. A microscope has a 10× eyepiece and a 40× objective. Find its magnification. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Total = 10 × 40 = 400×.</details>

6. Why are viruses called "on the border" between living and non-living things? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>They are not cells and cannot grow, feed or reproduce on their own, so they seem non-living. Inside a host cell they multiply rapidly, like living things.</details>

7. Why does a curd starter need warm, not boiling, milk? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Boiling hot milk would kill the Lactobacillus bacteria. Warm milk lets them grow and divide.</details>

8. Describe two ways in which bacteria help farmers. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Rhizobium bacteria in the root nodules of pulses turn nitrogen from the air into a form that plants can use, making the soil more fertile. Decomposer bacteria rot dead plants and animals and return nutrients to the soil.</details>

9. Name three methods of preserving food and say how each one works. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Drying removes the water that microbes need. Cooling in a fridge slows the microbes down. Salting or adding sugar draws water out of microbes, so they cannot grow (pickles, jam). Heating and sealing kills microbes and keeps new ones out.</details>

10. A bacterium divides every 20 minutes. How many bacteria are there from one after 2 hours? *(3 marks)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

2 hours = 120 minutes, so 120 ÷ 20 = 6 doublings.

Number = 2⁶ = **64** bacteria.

</details>

## 5-mark questions

11. Name the five main groups of microorganisms. Give one example of each and one place where it is found or one thing it does. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Bacteria: Lactobacillus (curd). Fungi: yeast (bread and idli rising). Protozoa: Amoeba (ponds), or Plasmodium (malaria). Algae: Spirogyra (ponds, makes food by photosynthesis). Viruses: influenza or polio (multiply inside a host cell, cause disease).</details>

12. Explain, with the steps, how milk changes into curd. Why is the curd sour? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Milk is warmed to about body temperature. A spoonful of old curd carrying Lactobacillus bacteria is added. The container is covered and kept warm for some hours. The bacteria feed on milk sugar, divide again and again and make lactic acid. The acid makes the milk proteins clump together, so the milk sets into curd. The curd tastes sour because of the lactic acid.</details>

## Case-based question

13. Kavya leaves a bowl of cooked rice on the kitchen counter overnight in July. In the morning it smells sour and has fuzzy white patches. She puts another bowl of the same rice in the fridge and it is fine.

(a) What caused the smell and the patches? *(1 mark)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Microorganisms (bacteria and moulds) growing and multiplying in the rice.</details>

(b) Why did the rice in the fridge stay fresh? *(1 mark)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Cold slows the growth of microbes.</details>

(c) Name two things microbes need to grow that the rice provided. *(1 mark)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Food (the rice) and moisture; warmth was provided by the room.</details>

(d) Suggest one more way to keep food safe. *(1 mark)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Dry it in the sun, salt it, or heat and seal it.</details>

## Assertion-reason

14. **Assertion (A):** Antibiotics do not work against viral colds. **Reason (R):** Antibiotics kill bacteria, and a cold is caused by a virus.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true, and R explains A.</details>

15. **Assertion (A):** Pickles do not spoil easily. **Reason (R):** Salt and oil in pickles take away the water and air that microbes need.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true, and R explains A.</details>

16. **Assertion (A):** All microorganisms are harmful. **Reason (R):** Microorganisms are found only in dirty places.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both false. Most microbes are harmless or useful (curd, decomposers), and they live everywhere, including clean places.</details>
