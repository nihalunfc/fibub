**Biology · Class 8 · Chapter 3 · about 45 minutes.** By the end you can say what health really means, plan a balanced meal, tell infectious from non-infectious diseases, trace how germs spread and how to stop them, and explain immunity and vaccines.

<div class="bigidea">Health is not just "not being ill". It is a balance of body, mind and community, built every day by what you eat, how you sleep, how you keep clean and how you look after others. Many diseases can be prevented, and vaccines teach the body how to defend itself before the germ arrives.</div>

## 1. What is health?

The World Health Organization says health is "a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being, not merely the absence of disease". So there are three sides.

- **Physical health:** the body works well. You have energy, sleep well and are free of illness.
- **Mental health:** you can handle stress, express your feelings, learn and enjoy life. Feeling worried before an exam is normal; feeling hopeless for weeks is a reason to talk to someone you trust and, if needed, a doctor or counsellor.
- **Social health:** you get on with family, friends and neighbours, and you belong to a community that cares.

The three are linked. A long sleepless week (physical) makes you irritable (mental) and then you quarrel with friends (social). Check how the sides balance:

**Things that affect health:** food and water, sleep, exercise, cleanliness, the air and surroundings (pollution, crowded homes), money and access to doctors, family and friends, and habits such as tobacco or too much screen time.

<div class="myth">

**"If I am not sick, I am healthy."** Health also means energy, a calm mind and good relationships. Someone can have no disease and still be unwell in mind or lonely.

</div>

## 2. A balanced diet

Food gives **energy**, material for **growth and repair**, and substances that **protect** the body. A **balanced diet** has all the nutrients in the right amounts.

| Nutrient | Job in the body | Foods | If missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrates | energy | rice, roti, millets, potato | tired, weak |
| Proteins | growth and repair | dal, eggs, fish, milk, soya | stunted growth, swelling (kwashiorkor) |
| Fats | stored energy, keep warm, absorb vitamins | oil, ghee, nuts | too little energy; too much causes obesity |
| Vitamin A | eyesight, skin | carrot, papaya, spinach, liver | night blindness |
| Vitamin C | healthy gums and skin, heals wounds | amla, orange, guava, lemon | scurvy (bleeding gums) |
| Vitamin D | strong bones | sunlight, milk, eggs | rickets |
| Iron | makes haemoglobin in red blood cells | spinach, jaggery, dates, meat | anaemia |
| Calcium | bones and teeth | milk, ragi, curd | weak bones |
| Iodine | thyroid gland | iodised salt, fish | goitre (swollen neck) |
| Fibre | keeps the gut moving | whole grains, fruits, vegetables | constipation |
| Water | carries nutrients, controls temperature | water, buttermilk, coconut water | dehydration |

Notice that no single food has everything. A typical Indian thali is balanced on purpose: roti or rice (energy), dal (protein), vegetables (vitamins, minerals, fibre), curd (calcium) and a little oil.

**Undernutrition** (too little food or too few nutrients) and **overnutrition** (too much, especially sugar and fat) both harm health. Eat slowly, eat a rainbow of vegetables, limit packaged snacks and sugary drinks, drink clean water and be active every day for about an hour.

## 3. Diseases: infectious and non-infectious

A **disease** is a condition in which the body or mind does not work properly.

- **Infectious (communicable) diseases** are caused by germs (microbes, Chapter 2) and can spread from one person to another: cold, flu, measles, cholera, typhoid, malaria, TB, dengue.
- **Non-infectious (non-communicable) diseases** are not caused by germs and do not spread: diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease, most cancers, allergies and deficiency diseases such as anaemia.

Also, a disease can be **short-lived (acute)**, like a cold, or **long-lasting (chronic)**, like diabetes or TB.

### How germs spread

The germs must travel from a source to a new person. The common routes are:

- **Air:** droplets from coughs and sneezes (cold, flu, TB, measles).
- **Water and food:** drinking or eating something contaminated (cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A, diarrhoea).
- **Touch:** direct contact or shared towels (ringworm, scabies, pink eye).
- **Vectors:** an animal that carries germs, such as mosquitoes (malaria, dengue, chikungunya) or houseflies.
- **Blood and body fluids:** from mother to baby, or through unsterile needles (hepatitis B, HIV). Hugging, sharing food or sitting next to someone does not spread HIV.

### Stopping the spread

Prevention is cheaper and kinder than cure.

1. **Wash your hands** with soap for at least 20 seconds: before eating or cooking, after the toilet, after coughing or touching animals.
2. **Drink safe water**, keep food covered, wash fruit and vegetables.
3. **Clean surroundings:** put rubbish in bins, never leave water standing in pots, tyres or coolers (mosquitoes breed in it), use toilets, not open defecation.
4. **Cover your mouth** when coughing; stay home when ill.
5. **Get vaccinated** on time.

Handwashing is one of the cheapest and most powerful health tools. See how much difference it makes in a crowd. Each dot is a person; the infection passes to neighbours; vaccinated people (yellow) are never infected and also shield others.

## 4. How the body fights back: immunity

Your body has several layers of defence, working like a fort.

<div class="steps" data-fig="c8-health-defence">
<div class="step">Barriers: skin is a tough wall; mucus and tiny hairs in the nose trap germs.</div>
<div class="step">Chemical defences: tears, saliva and strong stomach acid kill many germs.</div>
<div class="step">If germs get inside, white blood cells find and swallow them. Pus is a heap of dead white cells.</div>
<div class="step">Special white cells make antibodies that stick to one kind of germ and mark it for destruction.</div>
<div class="step">Memory: some cells remember the germ, so next time the response is faster. This is **immunity**.</div>
</div>

**Immunity** is the body's ability to fight a disease. You get it after recovering from an infection (you rarely catch measles twice) or by vaccination. A good diet, sleep and exercise keep the immune system strong.

### Vaccines

A **vaccine** contains a dead or weakened germ, or a harmless piece of one. It cannot make you seriously ill, but your white blood cells treat it as an enemy, make antibodies and keep memory cells. When the real germ arrives later, the response is fast and strong.

<div class="steps" data-fig="c8-health-vaccine">
<div class="step">A weakened or harmless piece of the germ is given as a vaccine, often by injection.</div>
<div class="step">White blood cells notice it. You are not ill because it is harmless.</div>
<div class="step">The body makes antibodies and memory cells that remember the germ.</div>
<div class="step">Later the real germ enters. Memory cells recognise it at once.</div>
<div class="step">Antibodies arrive quickly and destroy the germ before it can make you ill.</div>
</div>

<figure class="fig" data-fig="@bio-vaccine-response"><figcaption>Antibody level against time. After the first dose the level rises slowly. After a second dose, it rises faster and much higher, and stays high for longer: that is memory.</figcaption></figure>

India's immunisation programme gives free vaccines to babies and children against diseases such as polio, measles, tuberculosis (BCG), hepatitis B, diphtheria, whooping cough and tetanus. Vaccination has wiped out smallpox from the world, and India was declared polio-free in 2014.

**Herd immunity.** When most people in a community are vaccinated, a germ has trouble finding a new person to infect, so even people who cannot be vaccinated (very young babies, people with weak immunity) are protected.

<div class="myth">

**"Antibiotics cure all infections, so I can take them for a cold."** Antibiotics work only against bacteria. A cold or flu is caused by a virus, so antibiotics do nothing. Taking them without need helps bacteria become resistant. Only use medicines that a doctor prescribes, and finish the course.

</div>

## 5. Looking after your health

- **Sleep** 8 to 10 hours as a school student; the body repairs itself while you sleep.
- **Move** for about an hour a day: play, walk, cycle, yoga.
- **Screens:** take breaks; look at far things every 20 minutes to rest your eyes.
- **Stay away from tobacco, alcohol and drugs**, which harm the body and the mind.
- **Talk** about worries with someone you trust; asking for help is a sign of strength.
- **First aid:** wash a wound with clean water, cover it and see a doctor if deep. Know the number for an ambulance (108 in much of India).
- **Community health:** clean water supply, drains, rubbish collection and health centres protect everyone. When one person stays healthy, the whole community is a little safer.

<div class="keypoints">

- Health has three sides: physical, mental and social. Not being ill is not enough.
- A balanced diet has carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, fibre and water. Missing nutrients cause deficiency diseases (iron: anaemia; vitamin C: scurvy; vitamin D: rickets; iodine: goitre; vitamin A: night blindness).
- Infectious diseases are caused by germs and spread by air, water, food, touch and vectors; non-infectious diseases (diabetes, heart disease) do not spread.
- Handwashing with soap, safe water, clean surroundings, covering coughs and vaccines prevent infection.
- Defences: skin and mucus, chemicals, white blood cells, antibodies and memory cells give immunity.
- A vaccine trains the immune system with a harmless form of the germ; when most people are vaccinated, herd immunity protects everyone.
- Antibiotics kill bacteria, not viruses; use medicines only as a doctor advises.

</div>
