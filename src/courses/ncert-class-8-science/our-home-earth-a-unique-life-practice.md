**Practice · Class 8 Science · Chapter 13.** Read the chapter lesson, [Our Home: Earth, a Unique Life-Sustaining Planet](/learn/courses/ncert-class-8-science/our-home-earth-a-unique-life/), first. Try each question before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Earth supports life because of its distance from the Sun, its liquid water and its atmosphere, all linked by cycles. The greenhouse effect is natural and useful, but extra greenhouse gas warms the planet.</div>

## How to answer

1. Name the sphere or layer first, then say what it contains or does.
2. For the water cycle, give the steps in order and name the energy source (the Sun).
3. In numerical questions, write the rule, then the numbers with units.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the layer of the atmosphere in which weather occurs.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The troposphere.</details>

2. Which gas forms about 78% of the air?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Nitrogen.</details>

3. What is the process in which plants release water vapour from their leaves called?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Transpiration.</details>

4. Which layer of the atmosphere contains the ozone layer?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The stratosphere.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Name the four spheres of the Earth and say what each contains. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Lithosphere: rocks, soil and landforms. Hydrosphere: all water. Atmosphere: the layer of gases around the Earth. Biosphere: all living things.</details>

6. Why do mountaineers carry oxygen cylinders on very high peaks? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Air pressure falls with height, so the air is much thinner at the top of a high mountain. Each breath contains less oxygen than at sea level.</details>

7. Why is the upper troposphere colder than the ground even though it is slightly nearer the Sun? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The air is heated mainly from the ground below, which is warmed by the Sun. The distance to the Sun changes by almost nothing over a few kilometres, and thinner air holds less heat, so it gets colder with height.</details>

8. The temperature on the ground is 32 °C. Estimate the temperature at 8 km, if it falls by 6.5 °C per km. *(3 marks)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Fall in temperature = 6.5 × 8 = 52 °C. Temperature at 8 km = 32 − 52 = **−20 °C**.

</details>

9. List the steps of the water cycle in order. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Evaporation (and transpiration), condensation into clouds, precipitation as rain, snow or hail, then runoff into rivers and the sea and infiltration into the ground, and the cycle repeats.</details>

10. Why is fresh water called a precious resource although the Earth is covered with water? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>About 97% of the water is salty. Of the fresh 3%, most is frozen in glaciers and ice caps, or underground. Only a tiny part (about 1% of the fresh water) is in rivers and lakes that is easy for people to use.</details>

11. What is meant by the habitable zone? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The region around a star where the temperature allows liquid water to exist on a planet's surface: not so near that water boils away, not so far that it freezes.</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

12. Describe the greenhouse effect. Why is it both useful and harmful? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Sunlight warms the ground, which gives out heat (infrared rays). Greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide, methane and water vapour absorb some of this heat and send part of it back down, so the heat escapes more slowly and the surface stays warmer. It is useful because without it the Earth would average about −18 °C and be frozen; with it the average is about 15 °C. It is harmful when human activities such as burning fossil fuels and cutting forests add extra carbon dioxide, which traps more heat and causes global warming: melting glaciers, rising seas, hotter weather and changed rainfall.</details>

13. Explain with the help of a diagram how rain forms in the water cycle. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Sketch the Sun, the sea, a mountain, clouds and a river with arrows. The Sun heats the sea and water evaporates into water vapour (also water is released from plants by transpiration). The warm, moist air rises and cools, so the vapour condenses into tiny droplets that form clouds. The droplets join into larger drops, become too heavy and fall as rain (or snow or hail in cold places). The water flows into rivers and the sea, or soaks into the ground, and the cycle starts again.</details>

## Case-based question

14. Anita reads that a planet called Kepler-X is at 0.5 AU from a star just like our Sun. She is told that Earth is at 1 AU and that sunlight falls as 1 ÷ d², where d is the distance in AU. The habitable zone of this star is from about 0.95 to 1.7 AU. *(4 marks)*

   (a) How many times as much starlight does Kepler-X receive as Earth does?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>1 ÷ 0.5² = 1 ÷ 0.25 = 4 times.</details>

   (b) Is Kepler-X inside the habitable zone?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>No. At 0.5 AU it is closer than 0.95 AU, so it is inside the inner edge of the zone.</details>

   (c) What is likely to happen to liquid water on its surface?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>It would be too hot, so water would evaporate or boil away.</details>

   (d) Name a planet in our own solar system that is too hot and one that is too cold for liquid water on its surface.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>Too hot: Venus (or Mercury). Too cold: Mars today (or Jupiter and beyond).</details>

## Assertion and reason

For each, choose: (A) both true and R explains A, (B) both true but R does not explain A, (C) A true, R false, (D) A false, R true.

15. **Assertion:** Without the greenhouse effect, the Earth would be much colder. **Reason:** Greenhouse gases trap some of the heat that leaves the surface.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). Both are true and the reason explains the assertion.</details>

16. **Assertion:** The air gets colder as you climb through the troposphere. **Reason:** The air is heated mainly from the ground, so the layers higher up are further from the heat source.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). Both are true and the reason explains the assertion. (Temperature falls about 6.5 °C for every km.)</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Four spheres: lithosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere, biosphere.
- Atmosphere layers: troposphere (weather), stratosphere (ozone), mesosphere (meteors), thermosphere.
- Water cycle: evaporation, condensation, precipitation, runoff and infiltration, driven by the Sun.
- Only about 3% of Earth's water is fresh; most of it is ice or groundwater.
- Habitable zone: where liquid water can exist; sunlight falls as 1 ÷ d².
- The greenhouse effect warms Earth from -18 °C to 15 °C; extra carbon dioxide causes global warming.
