**Practice · Class 8 Science · Chapter 10.** Read the chapter lesson, [Light: Mirrors and Lenses](/learn/courses/ncert-class-8-science/light-mirrors-and-lenses/), first. Try each question before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Three rules do most of the work: i = r (law of reflection), images in a plane mirror are as far behind as the object is in front, and the number of images in two mirrors is 360 ÷ angle − 1.</div>

## How to answer

1. In ray diagrams, draw the normal as a dotted line and put arrows on every ray.
2. Measure the angles from the normal, not from the mirror.
3. When you describe an image, say three things: real or virtual, upright or inverted, bigger or smaller.

## 1-mark questions

1. State the law of reflection in one line.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection (and the rays and the normal lie in one plane).</details>

2. What kind of image does a plane mirror form?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Virtual, upright, the same size as the object and laterally inverted.</details>

3. Which lens is thicker at the middle than at the edges?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A convex lens.</details>

4. Name the band of seven colours formed when white light passes through a prism.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A spectrum (VIBGYOR).</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. A ray makes an angle of 25° with a plane mirror. What is the angle of reflection? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The angle of incidence is measured from the normal: 90° − 25° = 65°. By the law of reflection, the angle of reflection is also 65°.</details>

6. A boy stands 2.5 m in front of a plane mirror. How far is he from his image? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The image is 2.5 m behind the mirror, so he is 2.5 + 2.5 = 5 m from it.</details>

7. Why are convex mirrors used as rear-view mirrors in vehicles? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A convex mirror spreads out the reflected rays, so it gives a wide field of view and shows traffic over a large area. The image is always upright, though smaller.</details>

8. Why does a rough surface like a wall not form an image even though it reflects light? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The surface is uneven, so each ray still obeys the law of reflection but the normal points in different directions at different points. The rays scatter in all directions and no image forms.</details>

9. Two mirrors are placed at 72° to each other. How many images of a coin placed between them are formed? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Images = 360 ÷ 72 − 1 = 5 − 1 = 4.</details>

10. Differentiate between a real image and a virtual image. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A real image is formed where light rays actually meet; it can be caught on a screen and is usually inverted. A virtual image is formed where rays only seem to come from; it cannot be caught on a screen and is upright. A plane mirror and a magnifying glass give virtual images.</details>

11. A concave mirror has a radius of curvature of 36 cm. What is its focal length? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>f = R ÷ 2 = 36 ÷ 2 = 18 cm.</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

12. Describe how you would find the image of an object placed beyond the centre of curvature of a concave mirror by drawing two rays. State the nature of the image. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Draw the principal axis, mark P, F and C, and an arrow for the object beyond C. Ray 1: from the top of the object, draw a ray parallel to the axis; after reflection it passes through F. Ray 2: from the top of the object, draw a ray through F; after reflection it goes parallel to the axis. The two reflected rays meet at one point, which is the top of the image. Drop a line to the axis to complete it. The image lies between F and C and is real, inverted and smaller than the object.</details>

13. Explain, with the help of a prism, how white light is dispersed. Why does a rainbow form? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>When a narrow beam of white light enters a prism, it is refracted at both faces. Each colour of white light bends by a different amount, with violet bending the most and red the least. So the colours leave in slightly different directions and fan out into a spectrum of seven colours (red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet). This splitting is dispersion. A rainbow forms when sunlight enters raindrops, which act like tiny prisms: the light is refracted, reflected inside the drop and refracted again, and the colours spread out. You must have the Sun behind you and rain in front.</details>

## Case-based question

14. A shopkeeper puts a large curved mirror high in a corner of his shop. In it he can see the whole shop floor and customers near the door, but everything looks smaller than in a flat mirror. *(4 marks)*

   (a) What kind of mirror is it?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>A convex mirror.</details>

   (b) Why does it show so much of the shop?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>It spreads out the reflected rays, so it covers a wide field of view.</details>

   (c) What is the nature of the image in this mirror?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>Virtual, upright and smaller than the object.</details>

   (d) Name one other use of this kind of mirror.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>Rear-view and side mirrors of vehicles, or mirrors at a blind bend of a road.</details>

## Assertion and reason

For each, choose: (A) both true and R explains A, (B) both true but R does not explain A, (C) A true, R false, (D) A false, R true.

15. **Assertion:** A convex lens can be used as a magnifying glass. **Reason:** When the object is inside the focus, a convex lens forms a virtual, upright and enlarged image.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). Both are true, and the reason explains the assertion.</details>

16. **Assertion:** The image formed by a plane mirror is real. **Reason:** The reflected rays only seem to come from a point behind the mirror.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(D). The assertion is false: the image is virtual, not real. The reason is true, and it is exactly why the image is virtual.</details>
