**Practice · Class 8 Science · Chapter 7.** Read the chapter lesson, [Particulate Nature of Matter](/learn/courses/ncert-class-8-science/particulate-nature-of-matter/), first. Try each question before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">For every question ask three things about the particles: how close are they, how fast do they move and how strongly do they attract?</div>

## How to answer

1. Describe the particles (spacing, movement, attraction) before giving the conclusion.
2. Give one everyday example to support each statement.
3. For changes of state name the change and the temperature where it happens.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the process by which a smell spreads through a room.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Diffusion.</details>

2. In which state of matter are particles arranged in a fixed pattern?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>In a solid.</details>

3. What is the change of a solid directly into a gas called?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Sublimation.</details>

4. State the boiling point of water at normal pressure.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>100 °C.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Why does the water level hardly rise when sugar is dissolved in it? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>There are spaces between the water particles. Sugar particles move into those spaces, so the total volume increases very little.</details>

6. Give two differences between the particles of a solid and those of a liquid. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>In a solid the particles are fixed in a pattern and only vibrate, and the pull between them is strong. In a liquid the particles can slide past each other and the pull is weaker, so a liquid flows.</details>

7. Why does a gas fill a container completely, while a solid does not? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Gas particles are far apart and attract each other very weakly. They move freely in all directions at high speed, so they spread out until they fill the whole container. In a solid the particles are held in place by strong attraction.</details>

8. Why does ink spread faster in hot water than in cold water? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>At a higher temperature the water and ink particles move faster, so they mix more quickly by diffusion.</details>

9. A 100 mL of marbles and 100 mL of sand are mixed and the volume is 168 mL. Find the volume that the sand put into the gaps and explain what it shows. *(3 marks)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Volume lost = `$200 - 168 = 32$` mL. This shows that there are gaps between the marbles, and that the sand particles filled 32 mL of them. In the same way, particles of one substance can occupy the spaces between the particles of another.

</details>

10. Why do wet clothes dry faster on a hot, windy day? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>In heat the water particles move faster, so more leave the surface as vapour. Wind carries the vapour away so that more water can evaporate. Both make evaporation faster.</details>

## 5-mark questions

11. Compare the three states of matter in terms of particle arrangement, movement, shape, volume and compressibility. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Solid: particles very close in a fixed pattern, vibrate about fixed places, fixed shape and volume, cannot be compressed. Liquid: particles close but can slide, fixed volume but take the shape of the container, hardly compressible. Gas: particles far apart and moving freely at high speed, no fixed shape or volume (fills the container), easily compressed.</details>

12. Describe what happens to the particles and to the temperature when ice is heated until it becomes steam. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>As the ice is heated its particles vibrate faster and the temperature rises. At 0 °C the particles begin to break out of their fixed pattern: the ice melts. The temperature stays at 0 °C until all the ice has melted, because the heat is used to loosen the particles. The water then warms up, with particles sliding faster. At 100 °C the water boils: particles escape as steam and the temperature stays at 100 °C until all has boiled. In the steam the particles are far apart and move freely.</details>

## Case-based question

Rohan drops a purple crystal in a glass of cold water and the same crystal in a glass of warm water. Without stirring, after 5 minutes the warm water is evenly coloured and the cold water has colour only near the crystal.

13. Name the process that spreads the colour. *(1 mark)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Diffusion.</details>

14. Why was the colour spread faster in the warm water? *(1 mark)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The particles move faster at the higher temperature.</details>

15. What would happen if the crystal were dropped into the same water and left for a day without stirring? *(1 mark)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The colour would spread through all the water and become even.</details>

16. Would diffusion be faster or slower in a gas? Why? *(1 mark)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Faster, because gas particles are far apart and move quickly, with few things to slow them down.</details>

## Assertion and reason

For each, choose: (A) both true and R explains A, (B) both true but R does not explain A, (C) A true and R false, (D) A false and R true.

17. **Assertion:** Liquids have no fixed shape. **Reason:** The particles in a liquid can slide past one another.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). Sliding particles let the liquid take the shape of its container.</details>

18. **Assertion:** Steam at 100 °C can cause a more serious burn than boiling water at 100 °C. **Reason:** Steam carries hidden heat that it gives out when it condenses.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). The heat absorbed during boiling is released again as the steam condenses on the skin.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Particles are tiny, move all the time, have spaces between them and attract each other.
- Solid, liquid and gas differ in spacing, movement and attraction.
- Diffusion is faster in gases and at higher temperature.
- Temperature stays flat while a substance melts or boils.
