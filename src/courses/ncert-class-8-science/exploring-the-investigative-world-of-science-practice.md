**Practice · Class 8 Science · Chapter 1.** Read the chapter lesson, [Exploring the Investigative World of Science](/learn/courses/ncert-class-8-science/exploring-the-investigative-world-of-science/), first. Try each question before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Four habits: ask a testable question, change one variable at a time, repeat and take the mean, and let the data decide.</div>

## How to answer

1. In a fair-test question, name the variable that is changed, the one that is measured and the ones that stay the same.
2. For readings, show the working: sum, divide, unit.
3. In longer answers use words like "because" and "so" to link the evidence to the conclusion.

## 1-mark questions

1. What is a hypothesis?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A testable guess about what will happen, made before the test.</details>

2. Name the error caused by looking at a scale from the side.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Parallax error.</details>

3. What is a control in an experiment?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A set-up where nothing is changed, kept to compare with the set-up that is changed.</details>

4. Which is an observation: "the ice melted in 12 minutes" or "the ice melted because the room was warm"?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>"The ice melted in 12 minutes" is an observation (timed). The other is an inference.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Why should only one variable be changed in a test? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>If two variables are changed together, any difference in the result could be due to either, so you cannot tell which one caused it. Changing one makes the cause clear.</details>

6. A student tests whether a ball bounces higher on a hard floor than on a carpet. Name the variable changed and the variable measured. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Changed: the type of floor (surface). Measured: the bounce height. The same ball and the same drop height must be kept.</details>

7. Four timings of a toy car are 3.2 s, 3.0 s, 3.4 s and 3.2 s. Find the mean. *(3 marks)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Sum = 3.2 + 3.0 + 3.4 + 3.2 = 12.8 s.

Mean = 12.8 ÷ 4 = **3.2 s**.

</details>

8. Why is it better to time 20 swings of a pendulum and divide by 20 than to time one swing? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A human takes a fraction of a second to start and stop the watch. That error is the same size whether we time one swing or twenty, but divided among twenty swings it is twenty times smaller for each swing, so the time of one swing is much more exact.</details>

9. Distinguish between an observation and an inference with an example of each. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>An observation is what you directly see, hear or measure (the puddle has dried). An inference is a reason you work out from it (the Sun heated the water and it evaporated). Observation is evidence; inference is an explanation that may need testing.</details>

## 5-mark questions

10. Plan a fair test to find out whether the temperature of water affects how fast sugar dissolves. Name the variable changed, the variable measured and at least three variables kept the same, and say how you would record and use the results. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Changed: water temperature (for example 10 °C, 30 °C, 50 °C, 70 °C). Measured: the time for the sugar to dissolve completely, with a stopwatch. Kept the same: the mass of sugar, the volume of water, the same glass and the same stirring. Record the temperatures and times in a table, repeat each three times and take the mean. Plot temperature on the x axis and mean time on the y axis, draw a best-fit line and conclude from the pattern (sugar dissolves faster as the water gets hotter). Mention how sure you are and any error sources.</details>

11. A spring stretches 1.5 cm for each 100 g load. Describe how you would show this on a graph and what the graph tells you. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Hang loads of 100 g, 200 g, 300 g and so on, measure the extension each time and record it in a table. Plot load on the x axis (with its unit, g) and extension on the y axis (cm), with even scales. The points lie close to a straight line through the origin: draw the line of best fit. The line shows that the extension is proportional to the load. Its steepness (slope) is about 1.5 cm per 100 g. A point far from the line would be a doubtful reading to check or repeat.</details>

## Case-based question

12. Meera wants to know whether plants grow faster with more light. She puts a bean seedling on a bright window sill and another bean seedling, in a bigger pot with more water, in a dim cupboard. After a week the sill plant is 8 cm taller.

(a) Why is this not a fair test? *(1 mark)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>She changed three things at once: light, pot size and water.</details>

(b) How would you correct it? *(1 mark)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Use two identical seedlings, pots, soil and the same water; change only the amount of light.</details>

(c) What would be the dependent (measured) variable? *(1 mark)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The height of the seedlings.</details>

(d) How could Meera make her result more reliable? *(1 mark)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Use several seedlings in each condition, repeat the test and take the mean heights.</details>

## Assertion-reason

13. **Assertion (A):** Taking the mean of several readings makes a measurement more reliable. **Reason (R):** Random errors tend to cancel out when readings are averaged.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true, and R explains A.</details>

14. **Assertion (A):** A hypothesis that is shown wrong by a test is useless. **Reason (R):** Only hypotheses that are proved right help science.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both false. A wrong hypothesis rules out an idea and points to a better one, which is useful.</details>

15. **Assertion (A):** Reading a ruler with your eye directly above the mark avoids parallax error. **Reason (R):** From the side, the pointer seems to line up with a different mark.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true, and R explains A.</details>
