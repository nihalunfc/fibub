**Practice · Class 8 Science · Chapter 9.** Read the chapter lesson, [The Amazing World of Solutes, Solvents, and Solutions](/learn/courses/ncert-class-8-science/the-amazing-world-of-solutes-solvents/), first. Try each question before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Two ideas do most of the work: mass per cent = mass of solute ÷ mass of solution × 100, and solubility = the most solute that dissolves in 100 g of water at that temperature.</div>

## How to answer

1. In a numerical, write the formula, then the numbers with units, then the answer with a unit.
2. For "why" questions, give the cause in one clear sentence (particle size, temperature, saturation) and then the result.
3. In definitions, use the key word: solute, solvent, saturated, solubility.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the substance that does the dissolving in a solution.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The solvent.</details>

2. Is the solubility of a gas in water higher in cold water or in hot water?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>In cold water. A gas dissolves less as the water warms.</details>

3. Which mixture shows the Tyndall effect: salt water or milk?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Milk, because its particles are big enough to scatter light.</details>

4. Write one example of a solution in which the solute is a gas.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Soda water (carbon dioxide dissolved in water). Oxygen dissolved in pond water is another.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Why does sugar dissolve faster in hot tea than in cold milk? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The particles in hot tea move faster and collide with the sugar crystals harder and more often, so they pull the sugar particles off more quickly.</details>

6. Differentiate between a saturated and an unsaturated solution. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A saturated solution has dissolved all the solute it can at that temperature, so extra solute stays undissolved. An unsaturated solution can still dissolve more solute.</details>

7. 30 g of salt is dissolved in 120 g of water. Find the mass per cent of the solution. *(3 marks)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Mass of solution = 30 + 120 = 150 g. Mass per cent = 30 ÷ 150 × 100 = **20%**.

</details>

8. A student says, "When sugar dissolves in water it disappears." Is she right? Explain. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>No. The sugar breaks into tiny particles that spread among the water particles. It is still there: the water tastes sweet, and the solution weighs as much as the sugar and water together.</details>

9. Why does a hot saturated solution of potassium nitrate give crystals when it cools? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The solubility of potassium nitrate falls as the temperature falls. The cooler water cannot hold all the solute that the hot water held, so the extra comes out as crystals.</details>

10. Why do we use kerosene and not water to remove a candle wax stain? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Wax is an oily (non-polar) substance. Like dissolves like, so it dissolves in kerosene but not in water.</details>

11. Chalk powder in water, milk and salt solution are three mixtures. Name the type of each and say which one settles on standing. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Chalk powder in water is a suspension (it settles). Milk is a colloid (does not settle, scatters light). Salt solution is a true solution (does not settle, does not scatter light).</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

12. Explain how particle size decides whether a mixture is a solution, a colloid or a suspension. Give one example and one property for each. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>In a solution the particles are smaller than 1 nm, so they do not scatter light or settle (example: salt in water). In a colloid the particles are between 1 and 1000 nm, so they scatter a light beam (Tyndall effect) but still do not settle (example: milk). In a suspension the particles are bigger than 1000 nm, so they scatter light and settle on standing, and can be filtered out (example: mud in water).</details>

13. 200 mL of a 40% juice concentrate is diluted to a 10% drink. How much water must be added? Show your steps. *(5 marks)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The juice does not change when water is added. Juice in the concentrate = 40% of 200 mL = 80 mL.

For the 10% drink, the total volume must be 80 ÷ 0.10 = 800 mL.

Water to add = 800 − 200 = **600 mL**.

</details>

## Case-based question

14. Meena stirs spoons of sugar (20 g each) into 100 g of water at 20 °C. The first 10 spoons (200 g) dissolve, but the 11th will not dissolve fully and sugar lies at the bottom. She then warms the water to 60 °C and the sugar at the bottom dissolves. *(4 marks)*

   (a) What word describes the solution at 20 °C when no more sugar dissolves?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>Saturated.</details>

   (b) Why did the sugar at the bottom dissolve when she warmed the water?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>The solubility of sugar increases with temperature, so hotter water can hold more.</details>

   (c) If she cools the solution back to 20 °C slowly, what will she see?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>Sugar crystals will form, because the cool water cannot hold the extra sugar.</details>

   (d) Would the same method work to dissolve more carbon dioxide in a cold drink? Explain.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>No. A gas dissolves less in warm water, so warming would make the gas escape.</details>

## Assertion and reason

For each, choose: (A) both true and R explains A, (B) both true but R does not explain A, (C) A true, R false, (D) A false, R true.

15. **Assertion:** A torch beam is visible in milk but not in salt solution. **Reason:** Milk particles are larger and scatter light.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). Both are true and the reason explains the assertion (Tyndall effect).</details>

16. **Assertion:** A fizzy drink goes flat sooner in warm weather. **Reason:** The solubility of gases increases with temperature.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(C). The assertion is true but the reason is false: the solubility of gases falls as the temperature rises.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Solute dissolves in solvent; the solution is uniform and keeps the total mass.
- Saturated: no more can dissolve at that temperature. Solubility of solids rises with temperature; of gases it falls.
- Mass per cent = mass of solute ÷ mass of solution × 100. Diluting keeps the solute the same and increases the volume.
- Solution: under 1 nm, clear, does not settle. Colloid: 1 to 1000 nm, scatters light, does not settle. Suspension: over 1000 nm, settles.
- Like dissolves like: oils in kerosene, salt and sugar in water.

</div>
