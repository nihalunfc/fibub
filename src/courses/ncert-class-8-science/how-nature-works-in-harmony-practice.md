**Practice · Class 8 Science · Chapter 12.** Read the chapter lesson, [How Nature Works in Harmony](/learn/courses/ncert-class-8-science/how-nature-works-in-harmony/), first. Try each question before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Energy goes one way (Sun to heat) and shrinks to a tenth at each step; matter goes round and round with the help of decomposers.</div>

## How to answer

1. When you write a food chain, start with the producer and put arrows from food to eater.
2. For energy questions, multiply by 0.1 once for each step up the chain.
3. For "what will happen if" questions, trace the effect step by step: who loses food, who loses a predator.

## 1-mark questions

1. What do we call the non-living parts of an ecosystem, such as sunlight and soil?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Abiotic factors.</details>

2. Name two decomposers.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Fungi and bacteria (earthworms also help).</details>

3. What is the first link in every food chain?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A producer (a green plant or alga).</details>

4. What is a group of interlinked food chains called?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A food web.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Write a food chain with four links from a pond. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Algae → water flea → small fish → big fish (any correct pond chain with arrows from food to eater).</details>

6. Why are food chains rarely longer than five links? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Only about 10% of the energy passes to the next level, so after four or five steps there is too little energy left to support another level of animals.</details>

7. Plants capture 8000 units of energy. How much reaches the secondary consumers? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Primary consumers get 10% of 8000 = 800 units. Secondary consumers get 10% of 800 = 80 units.</details>

8. What happens to the dead leaves on the forest floor and why is this important? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Decomposers (fungi, bacteria, earthworms) break them down into simple nutrients. These return to the soil and are used by plants again, so nutrients are recycled and the soil stays fertile.</details>

9. Differentiate between a food chain and a food web. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A food chain is a single line showing who eats whom. A food web is many food chains linked together, because most animals eat more than one kind of food.</details>

10. Why is leaf litter decomposed faster in a monsoon forest than in a desert? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Decomposers need warmth and moisture. The monsoon forest is warm and damp, so they work quickly. A desert is dry and decomposers hardly work.</details>

11. Why is it a bad idea to remove all the snakes from a farm? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Snakes eat rats and mice. Without snakes the rodents would multiply and eat the crops and stored grain. Removing one species upsets the balance of the food web.</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

12. Explain the 10 per cent rule with an example of a food chain. Why does it limit the number of top carnivores? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Only about 10% of the energy stored in one trophic level is passed on to the next; the rest is used by the organisms for living or lost as heat. Example: grass (10 000 units) → deer (1000) → tiger (100). If another level were added, it would get only 10 units. As the energy gets smaller at each step, a top carnivore needs a very large area and many herbivores to find enough food, so there are few of them.</details>

13. Describe how nutrients cycle through an ecosystem. Name the role of each group of organisms. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Producers (plants) take nutrients from the soil and build their bodies. Herbivores eat plants, and carnivores eat herbivores, so the nutrients pass up the food chain. When organisms die or leave waste, decomposers (fungi, bacteria, earthworms) break them down into simple nutrients that return to the soil. Plants take these up again, completing the cycle. Matter is recycled in this way, but energy flows through in one direction and is lost as heat.</details>

## Case-based question

14. In a village, farmers began to hunt the owls and hawks that came to their fields because they thought the birds were harmful. Within two years the rats in the fields multiplied, ate much of the grain and a disease spread among them. The farmers then stopped hunting and protected the birds, and the rat numbers slowly dropped. *(4 marks)*

   (a) Write a food chain from this story.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>Grain → rat → owl (or hawk).</details>

   (b) Why did the rats increase when the owls were hunted?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>The rats lost their predators, so fewer rats were eaten and the rats multiplied.</details>

   (c) What does this show about the balance in an ecosystem?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>Predators control the numbers of their prey. Removing one species upsets the balance and affects the others.</details>

   (d) Suggest one safe way for farmers to control rats.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>Protect owls and hawks (for example by putting up perches or nest boxes), keep grain stores closed, or use traps instead of poisons.</details>

## Assertion and reason

For each, choose: (A) both true and R explains A, (B) both true but R does not explain A, (C) A true, R false, (D) A false, R true.

15. **Assertion:** There are far fewer tigers than deer in a forest. **Reason:** Energy decreases at each higher trophic level.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). Both are true and the reason explains the assertion.</details>

16. **Assertion:** Energy is recycled in an ecosystem in the same way as nutrients. **Reason:** Energy flows in one direction, from the Sun through the food chain and is lost as heat.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(D). The assertion is false: nutrients are recycled but energy flows one way. The reason is true.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Food chain arrows go from food to eater; webs link many chains.
