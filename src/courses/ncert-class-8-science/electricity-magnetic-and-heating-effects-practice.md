**Practice · Class 8 Science · Chapter 4.** Read the chapter lesson, [Electricity: Magnetic and Heating Effects](/learn/courses/ncert-class-8-science/electricity-magnetic-and-heating-effects/), first. Try each question before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Current makes a magnetic field and heat. Electromagnets use the first; heaters and fuses use the second: H = I²Rt and I = P ÷ V.</div>

## How to answer

1. Write the formula, then the numbers with units, then the answer with a unit.
2. For electromagnet questions give three ways to make it stronger: turns, current, soft iron core.
3. For safety answers, say what melts or switches off, and why.

## 1-mark questions

1. Who discovered that a current produces a magnetic field?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Hans Christian Oersted (1820).</details>

2. Name the material used as the core of an electromagnet.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Soft iron.</details>

3. Which effect of current is used in an electric iron?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The heating effect.</details>

4. What is the unit of electrical energy used in your electricity bill?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The kilowatt-hour (kWh), also called a unit.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. A compass needle placed under a wire turns when a current flows. What does this show? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>It shows that a current-carrying wire makes a magnetic field (the magnetic effect of current), which acts on the compass needle.</details>

6. State two ways to increase the strength of an electromagnet. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Increase the number of turns of wire, or increase the current. Putting a soft iron core inside the coil also makes it much stronger.</details>

7. Why is an electromagnet more useful than a bar magnet in a crane? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>It can be switched on to pick up the load and switched off to release it, and its strength can be changed.</details>

8. Why does the filament of a bulb glow but the connecting wires do not? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The filament is very thin and has a high resistance, so the same current produces much more heat in it (H = I²Rt), and it becomes white-hot. The connecting wires are thick with low resistance, so very little heat is made in them.</details>

9. What is a fuse and how does it protect a circuit? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A fuse is a short thin wire of a low-melting alloy placed in series in the circuit. If the current becomes too large (overload or short circuit) the wire heats up and melts, which breaks the circuit and stops the current before the wiring can overheat.</details>

10. An iron has a heating coil of resistance 40 Ω carrying 5 A. Find the heat produced in 10 s. *(3 marks)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

H = I² R t = 5² × 40 × 10 = 25 × 40 × 10 = **10 000 J**.

</details>

## 5-mark questions

11. Describe how an electric bell works. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>When the switch is pressed, current flows through the coil of an electromagnet. The electromagnet pulls a soft iron strip, and the hammer attached to it strikes the gong. The movement of the strip breaks the contact in the circuit, so the current stops and the coil is no longer a magnet. The spring pulls the strip back, the contact closes again, current flows and the process repeats very quickly, so the bell rings continuously while the switch is pressed.</details>

12. A family uses a 2000 W geyser for 30 minutes, a 100 W fan for 5 hours and four 10 W LED bulbs for 5 hours each day. Find the units used each day. *(5 marks)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Geyser: 2 kW × 0.5 h = 1 unit.

Fan: 0.1 kW × 5 h = 0.5 unit.

Four bulbs: total 40 W = 0.04 kW; 0.04 × 5 = 0.2 unit.

Total = 1 + 0.5 + 0.2 = **1.7 units** per day.

</details>

## Case-based question

13. A house has a socket with a 10 A fuse. Aman plugs a 2000 W heater, a 1000 W iron and a 1500 W microwave into the same extension board on the 230 V mains. After a few seconds the fuse melts.

(a) Find the total current. *(1 mark)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Total power = 2000 + 1000 + 1500 = 4500 W.

I = 4500 ÷ 230 ≈ **19.6 A**.

</details>

(b) Why did the fuse melt? *(1 mark)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The current (about 19.6 A) was above the 10 A rating, so the fuse wire heated up and melted. This was an overload.</details>

(c) Why is it unsafe to replace the fuse with a thick copper wire? *(1 mark)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The copper wire would not melt, so the circuit would not be broken and the house wires could overheat and start a fire.</details>

(d) What should Aman do? *(1 mark)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Use the appliances on separate sockets or one at a time, and fit the correct rated fuse.</details>

## Assertion-reason

14. **Assertion (A):** A soft iron core makes an electromagnet stronger. **Reason (R):** The core also becomes magnetised when the current flows.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true, and R explains A.</details>

15. **Assertion (A):** A fuse is placed in series with the appliance. **Reason (R):** The current to the appliance must pass through the fuse wire.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both true, and R explains A.</details>

16. **Assertion (A):** Doubling the current in a heater coil doubles the heat produced. **Reason (R):** Heat produced is proportional to the current.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both false. Heat is proportional to I², so doubling the current makes four times the heat.</details>
