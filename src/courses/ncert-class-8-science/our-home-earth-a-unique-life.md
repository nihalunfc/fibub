**Science · Class 8 · Chapter 13 · about 50 minutes.** By the end you can name the four spheres of the Earth, describe the layers of the atmosphere, follow the water cycle, say how little fresh water there is, explain why Earth can hold life, and describe the greenhouse effect.

<div class="bigidea">Earth is the only place we know that has life. It sits at the right distance from the Sun, has liquid water, and wears a blanket of air. These things are linked in cycles that keep the planet working, so what we do to one part affects the rest.</div>

## 1. The four spheres

You can think of the Earth as four connected parts, called **spheres**.

- The **lithosphere** is the solid, rocky outer part: mountains, rocks, soil and the ocean floor.
- The **hydrosphere** is all the water: oceans, rivers, lakes, glaciers, groundwater and the water vapour in the air.
- The **atmosphere** is the blanket of gases around the Earth.
- The **biosphere** is all living things, from soil bacteria to blue whales. It exists in a thin shell where the other three meet.

They are not separate. A tree (biosphere) drinks water (hydrosphere) from soil (lithosphere) and releases oxygen into the air (atmosphere). A river carries mud from the mountains to the sea. Pick one sphere at a time to see where it sits:

**“The atmosphere is just empty space around the Earth.”** It is a mixture of gases, mostly nitrogen (78%) and oxygen (21%), with a little argon, carbon dioxide and water vapour. It has weight and presses on you from all sides.

## 2. The atmosphere

Air gets thinner as you go up, because there is less air above pressing down. On Mount Everest (8.8 km) the air pressure is only about a third of that at sea level, which is why climbers need oxygen cylinders.

The atmosphere is divided into layers by how the temperature changes with height.

1. **Troposphere** (0 to about 12 km): most of the air, and all the clouds and weather. Temperature **falls** with height, about 6.5 °C per km. Aeroplanes cruise near its top.
2. **Stratosphere** (12 to 50 km): contains the **ozone layer** (about 20 to 30 km), which absorbs harmful ultraviolet rays from the Sun and warms this layer.
3. **Mesosphere** (50 to 85 km): the coldest layer. Meteors burn up here, which we see as shooting stars.
4. **Thermosphere** (above 85 km): very thin, very hot air. Auroras form here, and the International Space Station orbits at about 400 km.

## 3. The water cycle

The water on Earth is the same water that was here for billions of years. It moves in a great loop called the **water cycle**, powered by the Sun.

1. **Evaporation:** the Sun heats the oceans, lakes and wet ground and water turns into water vapour.
2. **Transpiration:** plants also give out water vapour through their leaves.
3. **Condensation:** rising vapour cools and turns into tiny droplets of cloud.
4. **Precipitation:** droplets join and fall as rain, snow or hail.
5. **Runoff and infiltration:** some water flows over the land into rivers and back to the sea; some soaks into the ground to become groundwater.

## 4. Water for living things

About 71% of the Earth's surface is covered with water, but nearly all of it is salty. Fresh water, the kind we can drink and use for crops, is very rare.

This is why saving water matters: rainwater harvesting, fixing leaking taps, drip irrigation in farms, and not polluting rivers and wells. Groundwater, which millions of Indian farmers depend on, is being used faster than rain refills it in many places.

## 5. Why is Earth able to support life?

Many things must go right for life, and Earth has them all.

- **The right distance from the Sun.** Sunlight gets weaker with distance (it follows 1 ÷ d², where d is the distance in astronomical units, with Earth at 1). Too close and the oceans would boil, as on Venus; too far and they would freeze, as on Mars today. The "just right" band, where liquid water can stay on a planet's surface, is the **habitable zone**, roughly 0.95 to 1.7 AU for our Sun.
- **Liquid water.** All the life we know needs it.
- **A blanket of air** with oxygen to breathe, and an ozone layer to block harmful rays.
- **A steady supply of energy** from the Sun and recycling of materials by the water cycle and decomposers.

## 6. The greenhouse effect

Earth does not stay warm just because of the sunshine. If the Earth had no atmosphere, the average temperature would be about **-18 °C**, cold enough to freeze the oceans. Instead it is about **+15 °C**. The difference is the **greenhouse effect**.

Sunlight passes through the air and warms the ground. The warm ground gives out heat as invisible infrared rays. Some gases in the air, such as **carbon dioxide, methane and water vapour**, absorb part of this heat and send some of it back down. So the heat leaves the Earth more slowly, and the surface stays warmer. A greenhouse used for growing plants works in a similar way (its glass traps warm air), which is where the name comes from.

The natural greenhouse effect is good: it makes Earth liveable. The trouble is that burning coal, oil and gas and cutting forests puts extra carbon dioxide into the air. More greenhouse gas traps more heat and the Earth warms. This is **global warming**, and it brings hotter summers, melting glaciers, rising seas and changed rainfall.

<div class="keypoints">

- The Earth has four spheres: lithosphere (rock), hydrosphere (water), atmosphere (air) and biosphere (life). They overlap and interact.
- The atmosphere is mostly nitrogen (78%) and oxygen (21%). Its layers are troposphere (weather), stratosphere (ozone layer), mesosphere (meteors) and thermosphere.
- Temperature falls about 6.5 °C per km in the troposphere; air pressure falls with height.
- The water cycle: evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation, runoff and infiltration, driven by the Sun.
- About 97% of Earth's water is salty; of the fresh 3%, about 69% is ice and 30% groundwater.
- Life needs liquid water, the right distance from the Sun (the habitable zone), an atmosphere and a steady energy source.
- Sunlight received falls as 1 ÷ d². The greenhouse effect keeps Earth near 15 °C instead of -18 °C; extra carbon dioxide from burning fuels causes global warming.

</div>
