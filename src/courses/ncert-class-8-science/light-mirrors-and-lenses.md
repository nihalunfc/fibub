**Science · Class 8 · Chapter 10 · about 50 minutes.** By the end you can use the law of reflection, say what a plane mirror does to an image, tell concave from convex mirrors and lenses, draw the image formed by a concave mirror, and explain how a prism splits white light.

<div class="bigidea">Light travels in straight lines and changes direction in two ways: it bounces off a surface (reflection) or bends as it goes from one material into another (refraction). Mirrors use the first; lenses and prisms use the second.</div>

## 1. Reflection of light

You see a cricket ball because light from the Sun bounces off it into your eyes. When light bounces off a surface, it is **reflected**. A smooth, shiny surface such as a mirror, still water or polished steel reflects light in a neat way; a rough surface such as paper or a wall sends it in all directions (that is why you can read a page from any side, but you cannot see your face in it).

To describe a reflection, draw a line at right angles to the mirror at the point where the ray lands. It is called the **normal**.

- The **incident ray** is the ray that arrives.
- The **reflected ray** is the ray that leaves.
- The **angle of incidence (i)** is the angle between the incident ray and the normal.
- The **angle of reflection (r)** is the angle between the reflected ray and the normal.

### The law of reflection

1. The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection: `$i = r$`.
2. The incident ray, the normal and the reflected ray all lie in one plane (flat sheet).

## 2. Images in a plane mirror

A **plane mirror** is a flat mirror. Look into one and you see an **image** of yourself. Its features:

- The image is **virtual**: the reflected rays only *seem* to come from behind the mirror, so you cannot catch it on a screen.
- It is **upright** (the right way up) and **the same size** as you.
- It is as far **behind** the mirror as you are in front.
- It is **laterally inverted**: left and right are swapped. Raise your right hand and your image raises its left. That is why **AMBULANCE** is printed back to front on the front of the van: a driver ahead sees it correctly in the rear-view mirror.

### Two mirrors at an angle

Put two mirrors at an angle and an object between them. Each mirror forms an image, and each image is itself reflected in the other mirror. The number of images is

```math
n = \frac{360^\circ}{\text{angle}} - 1
```

At 90° you see 3 images, at 60° you see 5, and the smaller the angle, the more. This is how a **kaleidoscope** makes its patterns, and at 0° (parallel mirrors, as in a barber's shop) you seem to see images going on for ever.

## 3. Spherical mirrors: concave and convex

Cut a shiny steel spoon, or think of the inside and outside of a polished bowl. A **spherical mirror** is part of the surface of a sphere.

- A **concave mirror** curves inwards (the shiny side is the inside of the bowl).
- A **convex mirror** bulges outwards (the shiny side is the outside).

Words you need: the **pole (P)** is the middle of the mirror; the **centre of curvature (C)** is the centre of the sphere the mirror is part of; the **radius of curvature (R)** is the distance PC; the **principal axis** is the line through P and C; and the **principal focus (F)** is the point where parallel rays meet (or seem to come from) after reflection. The distance PF is the **focal length (f)**, and

```math
f = \frac{R}{2}
```

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c8-light-mirrors"><figcaption>Parallel rays meet a plane, concave or convex mirror. Concave: they converge at F. Convex: they spread out, and traced backwards they seem to come from F behind the mirror. Change the curve radius to move F.</figcaption></figure>

### Uses

- **Concave mirrors** gather light into a beam or give a magnified view: torches, car headlights, shaving mirrors, a dentist's mirror, solar cookers.
- **Convex mirrors** always give a smaller, upright, virtual image but show a wide area: vehicle side mirrors, shop security mirrors, mirrors at blind road bends. You often see "objects in mirror are closer than they appear" on them.

## 4. Refraction and lenses

When light goes from air into water or glass it slows down and its path **bends** at the boundary. This bending is **refraction**. Going into a denser medium the ray bends *towards* the normal; coming out it bends *away*. That is why a straw looks bent in a glass of water and a pool looks shallower than it is.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c8-light-refract"><figcaption>Send a ray from air into water or glass and watch it bend towards the normal. Choose Glass to air and push the angle past about 42 degrees: the ray cannot leave and reflects back inside (total internal reflection).</figcaption></figure>

A **lens** is a piece of transparent glass or plastic with curved surfaces that uses refraction to bend light.

- A **convex lens** is thicker in the middle. It brings parallel rays together at its focus F.
- A **concave lens** is thinner in the middle. It spreads parallel rays out so they seem to come from F.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c8-light-lens"><figcaption>Choose Convex or Concave, then switch between Parallel rays and Image of an object. In image mode the focal length is 50: slide the object past 50 and the real, upside-down image becomes a virtual, magnified one.</figcaption></figure>

What each lens does to an image:

| Lens | Image formed | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| Convex, object far away | real, upside down, smaller | camera, eye, projector |
| Convex, object inside the focus | virtual, upright, magnified | magnifying glass |
| Concave (any distance) | virtual, upright, smaller | spectacles for short sight |

Try both lenses in the [Lens ray diagram](/learn/lens-ray-diagram/) tool:

A **magnifying glass** is just a convex lens held close to a page. Your eye also has a convex lens that focuses light on the back of the eye, and **spectacles** use extra lenses to correct it: a concave lens for short sight and a convex lens for long sight.

## 5. Dispersion: splitting white light

Sunlight looks white, but it is a mixture of colours. A glass **prism** makes this visible. White light enters one face and bends. Each colour bends by a slightly different amount, with **violet bending most and red least**, so the beam fans out into a band of colours called a **spectrum**: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet (**VIBGYOR** when read from violet). The splitting is called **dispersion**.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c8-light-prism"><figcaption>A white beam enters a glass prism and spreads into seven colours on the screen. Change the angle of incidence and see the spread change. (The glass here spreads colours a little more than real glass so you can see it.)</figcaption></figure>

A **rainbow** is the same thing done by millions of raindrops, each acting like a tiny prism, when the Sun is behind you and rain is in front. Newton showed that the colours are in the light itself and that a second prism can put them back together to make white. You can make the same effect by spinning a disc painted in the seven colours: the colours blend to a dull white.

Light is also the fastest thing we know: about 300 000 km/s. Sunlight takes about 8 minutes to reach us, and light from the nearest star beyond the Sun takes over 4 years.

<div class="keypoints">

- Light reflected from a smooth surface obeys the law of reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection, both measured from the normal, and all three lines lie in one plane.
- A plane mirror forms a virtual, upright image the same size as the object, as far behind the mirror as the object is in front, with left and right swapped.
- Two mirrors at an angle make 360 ÷ angle − 1 images.
- A concave mirror converges parallel rays at its focus; a convex mirror spreads them out. The focal length is half the radius of curvature, f = R ÷ 2.
- Concave mirrors are used in torches and shaving mirrors; convex mirrors give a wide view (vehicle mirrors).
- A real image can be caught on a screen; a virtual image cannot.
- Refraction is the bending of light as it passes between two materials. A convex lens converges light and a concave lens diverges it.
- A prism disperses white light into seven colours: violet bends the most and red the least.

</div>
