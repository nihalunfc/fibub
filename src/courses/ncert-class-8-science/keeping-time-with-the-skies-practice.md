**Practice · Class 8 Science · Chapter 11.** Read the chapter lesson, [Keeping Time with the Skies](/learn/courses/ncert-class-8-science/keeping-time-with-the-skies/), first. Try each question before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Remember three numbers: the Earth turns 15° an hour, the Moon's phases repeat every 29.5 days, and a year is 365.25 days.</div>

## How to answer

1. Say which motion causes each effect: rotation (day and night), the Moon's orbit (phases), the Earth's orbit (year, seasons).
2. For eclipses, name the order of the three bodies (Sun, Moon, Earth) and the Moon phase.
3. Draw a quick sketch when you can: a circle for the Earth, a dot for the Moon.

## 1-mark questions

1. In which direction does the Earth rotate?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>From west to east.</details>

2. What is the star that appears almost fixed in the northern sky called?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The pole star (Dhruva Tara, Polaris).</details>

3. On which day of the lunar month does a lunar eclipse occur?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>On a full Moon day.</details>

4. How many days does a leap year have?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>366 days.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Why does the Sun appear to rise in the east and set in the west? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The Earth rotates from west to east, so our place turns into the sunlight on the east side of the Sun and turns out of it later. The Sun only appears to move the other way.</details>

6. How long does the shadow of a vertical stick stay shortest, and at what time of the day is it shortest? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>It is shortest at about noon, when the Sun is highest in the sky.</details>

7. The Earth rotates through 360° in 24 hours. Find the difference in local time between two places 20° of longitude apart. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The Earth turns 360 ÷ 24 = 15° per hour, so 1° takes 4 minutes. 20° × 4 = 80 minutes, which is 1 hour 20 minutes. The eastern place is ahead.</details>

8. Why do we always see the same face of the Moon? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The Moon turns once on its axis in the same time that it goes once round the Earth, so the same side always faces us.</details>

9. Why is there no eclipse every month? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The Moon's orbit is tilted about 5° to the path of the Earth round the Sun. Most months the Moon is slightly above or below the Sun-Earth line at new Moon and at full Moon, so its shadow misses the Earth and the Earth's shadow misses the Moon.</details>

10. A lunar calendar has 12 months of 29.5 days. How many days is this short of a 365.25-day solar year? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>12 × 29.5 = 354 days. 365.25 − 354 = 11.25 days short. This is why lunar calendars add extra months from time to time.</details>

11. Why are constellations not real groups of stars? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The stars of a constellation are at very different distances from us. They only look close together from Earth. People joined them with imaginary lines to make patterns that are easy to remember.</details>

## 5-mark questions (model answers)

12. Explain with a sketch why we see different phases of the Moon. Name four phases in order from the new Moon. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The Moon reflects sunlight and half of it is always lit. It goes round the Earth once in about 29.5 days, so we see its lit half from a changing angle. At new Moon the Moon is between the Earth and the Sun, so the lit side faces away and we see nothing. A quarter of the way round (first quarter) we see half of the lit face. At full Moon the Moon is on the far side of the Earth from the Sun and we see all of the lit face. Order: new Moon, waxing crescent, first quarter, waxing gibbous, full Moon, then waning phases back to new Moon.</details>

13. Distinguish between a solar eclipse and a lunar eclipse. In each case, name the Moon phase and which body is in the middle. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>In a solar eclipse the Moon is in the middle, between the Sun and the Earth, at new Moon. The Moon's shadow falls on a small part of the Earth, where people see the Sun blocked, wholly (total) or partly (partial). In a lunar eclipse the Earth is in the middle, between the Sun and the Moon, at full Moon. The Earth's shadow falls on the Moon, which grows dark or reddish. A solar eclipse is seen only from a small region; a lunar eclipse is visible from the whole night side of the Earth. Never look at a solar eclipse without proper eye protection.</details>

## Case-based question

14. Rohan photographs the sky for four hours with a camera on a tripod pointing towards the north. In the photo, every star has left a curved trail around one star near the middle, which has hardly moved. *(4 marks)*

   (a) Name the star near the middle of the trails.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>The pole star (Dhruva Tara, Polaris).</details>

   (b) Why have the stars left curved trails?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>The Earth rotates, so the stars appear to turn around the pole star during the exposure.</details>

   (c) Through what angle has each trail turned in 4 hours?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>15° per hour × 4 hours = 60°.</details>

   (d) How can the Saptarishi help him find the pole star?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>The two stars at the end of the bowl (the pointer stars) point towards it. Extend the line from them about five times the gap between them.</details>

## Assertion and reason

For each, choose: (A) both true and R explains A, (B) both true but R does not explain A, (C) A true, R false, (D) A false, R true.

15. **Assertion:** A solar eclipse cannot happen on a full Moon day. **Reason:** In a solar eclipse the Moon is between the Sun and the Earth.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). Both are true. At full Moon the Earth is between the Sun and the Moon, so a solar eclipse is impossible.</details>

16. **Assertion:** A year of 12 lunar months is 354 days long. **Reason:** The Earth takes 354 days to go round the Sun.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(C). The assertion is true (12 × 29.5 = 354). The reason is false: the Earth takes about 365.25 days.</details>
