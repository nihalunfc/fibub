**Practice · Class 8 Science · Chapter 5.** Read the chapter lesson, [Exploring Forces](/learn/courses/ncert-class-8-science/exploring-forces/), first. Try each question before opening the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Three ideas do most of the work: add forces with direction in mind, friction always opposes the motion, and W = m × g.</div>

## How to answer

1. Write the formula, then the numbers with units, then the answer with a unit.
2. For net force, say which way it points.
3. Use the words *mass* for kg and *weight* for newtons.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the unit in which weight is measured.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Newton (N).</details>

2. Is friction a contact or a non-contact force?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A contact force.</details>

3. Give one example of a non-contact force.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Gravity, magnetic force or electrostatic force.</details>

4. What does a spring balance measure?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Force (for example, weight).</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. State two effects a force can have on a moving ball. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>It can change the ball's speed (speed up or slow down) and it can change its direction of motion.</details>

6. A rubbed balloon sticks to a wall. Name the force and say whether it needs contact. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Electrostatic force. The charge on the balloon attracts the wall; the pull acts across a tiny gap, so it is a non-contact force that works even before touching.</details>

7. Two children pull a cart. One pulls with 60 N to the right and the other with 35 N to the left. Find the net force. Is the cart in balance? *(3 marks)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Forces are in opposite directions, so net force = 60 − 35 = **25 N to the right**. It is not zero, so the forces are unbalanced and the cart speeds up toward the right.

</details>

8. Why do tyres have grooves, and why are ball bearings put in wheels? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Grooves increase friction with the road so the vehicle grips and can brake. Ball bearings turn sliding friction into rolling friction, which is much smaller, so the wheel turns easily and wears less.</details>

9. A body has a mass of 30 kg. Find its weight on Earth (g = 10 m/s²) and on the Moon (g = 1.6 m/s²). *(3 marks)*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

On Earth: `$W = 30 \times 10 = 300$` N. On the Moon: `$W = 30 \times 1.6 = 48$` N. The mass stays 30 kg in both places.

</details>

10. Why is it easier to push a loaded trolley with wheels than to drag the same load on the floor? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Wheels roll, and rolling friction is much smaller than sliding friction, so a smaller force is needed.</details>

## 5-mark questions

11. Explain, with an example each, contact and non-contact forces. Describe how the weight of an astronaut changes between the Earth and the Moon and why. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Contact forces act only when objects touch: muscular force when we pull a cart, and friction when a box slides on the floor. Non-contact forces act across a gap: a magnet pulls a nail, a rubbed comb attracts paper, and gravity pulls an apple down. Weight is the pull of gravity, W = m × g. The astronaut's mass stays the same but g on the Moon (1.6 m/s²) is about one sixth of that on Earth (9.8 m/s²), so the astronaut weighs about one sixth as much on the Moon.</details>

12. A box stays at rest when pushed with 10 N, 20 N and 30 N, but moves when pushed with 40 N. What does this tell you about friction? Say how friction can be reduced. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Up to 30 N the friction between the box and floor grows to match the push, so the forces are balanced and the box does not move. This is static friction, and its limit lies between 30 N and 40 N. At 40 N the push is larger than the greatest friction, so the box moves (sliding friction, a bit smaller, takes over). Friction can be reduced by oiling or polishing the surfaces, using powder, making surfaces smoother, or using wheels and ball bearings.</details>

## Case-based question

A class sets up a hanging spring balance. With a 2 N load the spring stretches 4 cm. The teacher then takes the balance, still with a 1 kg bottle, to a table and drags the bottle across the floor, reading 3 N just as it starts to slide.

13. How far will the spring stretch with a 5 N load, if the stretch is proportional to the load? *(1 mark)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Stretch per newton = 4 ÷ 2 = 2 cm/N, so 5 N gives 10 cm.</details>

14. What is the size of the friction just as the bottle begins to slide? *(1 mark)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>About 3 N, equal to the pull (static friction at its limit).</details>

15. The reading drops to 2 N once the bottle moves steadily. Which kind of friction is this? *(1 mark)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Sliding friction, which is smaller than the greatest static friction.</details>

16. Which two forces act on the bottle in the vertical direction, and how do they compare? *(1 mark)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Its weight (down) and the support force of the floor (up). They are equal, so they are balanced.</details>

## Assertion and reason

For each, choose: (A) both true and R explains A, (B) both true but R does not explain A, (C) A true and R false, (D) A false and R true.

17. **Assertion:** A feather and a coin have different weights on Earth. **Reason:** They have different masses.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). Weight = m × g, and g is the same for both, so the larger mass has the larger weight.</details>

18. **Assertion:** Friction can be zero between two surfaces that are pressed together. **Reason:** All surfaces have tiny bumps.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(D). A is false: because of the bumps there is always some friction between touching surfaces. R is true.</details>

- Mass is in kg and constant; weight W = m × g is in newtons.
