**Science · Class 8 · Chapter 8 · about 45 minutes.** By the end you can sort a substance as an element, a compound or a mixture, write the symbols of common elements, and pick the right method to separate a mixture.

<div class="bigidea">Everything around us is either a pure substance (an element or a compound) or a mixture. Ask how many kinds of atom there are and whether they are chemically joined, and you can sort anything.</div>

## 1. Elements

An **element** is a substance made of only one kind of atom. It cannot be broken into simpler substances by ordinary chemical methods. Iron, copper, gold, oxygen, hydrogen, carbon and sulfur are elements. About 118 elements are known.

Every element has a **symbol**: one capital letter (H, O, C, S) or a capital with a small letter (Na, Fe, Cu). Many come from the English name. Some come from old Latin names: sodium **Na** (natrium), iron **Fe** (ferrum), copper **Cu** (cuprum), silver **Ag** (argentum), gold **Au** (aurum).

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c8-matter-symbols"><figcaption>Tap an element. You see its symbol, where the symbol comes from, whether it is a metal and its state at room temperature, with a picture of its atoms.</figcaption></figure>

Elements are grouped as:

- **Metals** (iron, copper, aluminium): shiny, can be hammered into sheets (malleable) and drawn into wires (ductile), and conduct heat and electricity. Mercury is a liquid metal.
- **Non-metals** (carbon, sulfur, oxygen): often dull, brittle if solid, usually poor conductors. Many are gases.

<div class="myth">

**“Oxygen gas (O₂) is a compound because it has two atoms.”** The two atoms are the *same* kind, so it is still an element. A compound needs different kinds of atom.

</div>

## 2. Compounds

A **compound** is a substance made of two or more elements **chemically joined in a fixed ratio**. Water is always hydrogen and oxygen in a mass ratio of about 1 : 8; carbon dioxide is always carbon and oxygen. A compound is written with a **formula**: H₂O means every molecule has 2 hydrogen atoms and 1 oxygen atom.

The properties of a compound are **different** from its elements: hydrogen burns and oxygen helps things burn, but water puts out fires. You can only split a compound by a chemical change, not by simple physical methods.

<div class="steps" data-fig="c8-matter-build">
<div class="step">Two molecules of hydrogen (H₂) and one of oxygen (O₂): both are elements.</div>
<div class="step">The bonds inside the molecules break.</div>
<div class="step">The atoms rearrange.</div>
<div class="step">Two molecules of water, 2 H₂O, form.</div>
<div class="step">Water is a new substance with new properties.</div>

## 3. Mixtures

A **mixture** has two or more substances mixed **without** chemical joining, in any proportion. Each keeps its own properties and they can be separated by simple physical methods. Air (nitrogen, oxygen and more), sea water, soil, milk and steel are mixtures.

- **Homogeneous**: the same all through; you cannot see the parts (sugar solution, air, brass).
- **Heterogeneous**: the parts can be seen (sand and water, a salad, muddy water).

### Mixture or compound? Iron and sulfur

Mix grey iron filings with yellow sulfur powder. A magnet picks out the iron and leaves the sulfur, so the substances are only mixed. Now heat the mixture strongly. A black solid, iron sulfide, forms, and the magnet can no longer pull the iron out because it has formed a new substance.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="c8-matter-ironsulfur"><figcaption>Choose "Just mixed", lower the magnet and the iron lifts out. Choose "After heating" and try again: nothing moves, because a compound has formed.</figcaption></figure>

| | Mixture | Compound |
|---|---|---|
| Made by | just mixing | chemical joining |
| Ratio | any | fixed |
| Properties | those of the parts | new |
| Separation | physical methods | chemical methods only |

## 4. Separating mixtures

Choose a method that matches a **different property** of the parts.

| Method | Uses the property | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Handpicking | size, colour | stones from rice |
| Winnowing | weight | husk from grain |
| Sieving | size | flour from bran |
| Magnet | magnetism | iron from sand |
| Sedimentation and decantation | sinking | muddy water |
| Filtration | insoluble solid in a liquid | sand from water |
| Evaporation | liquid turns to vapour, solid stays | salt from sea water |
| Distillation | different boiling points | pure water from salt water |

<div class="steps" data-fig="c8-matter-separate">
<div class="step">Iron filings, sand and salt are mixed.</div>
<div class="step">A magnet takes out the iron.</div>
<div class="step">Add water and stir: salt dissolves, sand does not.</div>
<div class="step">Filter: the sand stays on the paper and salt water passes through.</div>
<div class="step">Evaporate the water to get the salt back.</div>
</div>

In India people have used these methods for ages: winnowing wheat on the threshing floor, making salt from sea water in shallow pans in Gujarat and Tamil Nadu, and filtering drinking water through cloth.

<div class="keypoints">

- An element has one kind of atom; its symbol is one or two letters (Na, Fe, Cu come from Latin names).
- A compound has different elements chemically joined in a fixed ratio and has new properties.
- A mixture has substances just mixed in any ratio; each keeps its properties; it can be homogeneous or heterogeneous.
- Separate a mixture by a property that differs: magnet, filtration, evaporation, distillation, winnowing, sieving.

</div>
