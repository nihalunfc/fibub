**Module 2 · about 12 minutes.** You'll be able to choose the cheapest test for your idea and run it honestly.

## 1. Minimum, viable, product

An **MVP (minimum viable product)** is not a cheap version of the final product. It is <mark>the smallest experiment that answers your riskiest question</mark>. Ask: what must be true for this business to work? Then test the shakiest assumption first.

<!--fig:mvp-picker-->

> **Key idea:** If you're not a little embarrassed by your first version, you probably spent too long on it.

## 2. Six kinds of MVP

1. **Landing page.** Describe the product, add a "join the waitlist" or "pre-order" button, send a little traffic. Tests interest.
2. **Pre-sale.** Take deposits or full payment before building. Tests willingness to pay.
3. **Concierge.** Do the service by hand for a few customers. Tests whether you can deliver value.
4. **Wizard of Oz.** Customers see an app; behind it, you do the work. Tests usage without building the hard parts.
5. **Clickable prototype.** A tap-through design. Tests whether people understand it.
6. **Single-feature product.** Real software that does one job. Tests retention.

## 3. Pre-sales: the legal care

Taking money before you can deliver is legal in all three countries if you are honest about it:

- Say clearly that it is a **pre-order**, with an expected delivery date.
- Say what happens if you are late or cannot deliver: **refund in full**.
- Keep the money traceable (a business account, Lesson 6.1), and record it properly for tax.

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 require sellers to show the total price, delivery terms and refund and cancellation policy before purchase. GST may be due on advances for services when received, so talk to your accountant once you register.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

Provincial consumer protection laws (such as Ontario's Consumer Protection Act, 2002 for internet agreements) require clear disclosure of delivery date and give consumers cancellation rights if goods are not delivered within 30 days of the promised date.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

The FTC's Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule says you must ship within the time you state (or 30 days if you state none), tell buyers about delays and offer a refund.

</div>

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Ravi runs a concierge MVP: for 2 pharmacies, he types their stock into a spreadsheet and sends WhatsApp alerts 60 days before expiry. After one month, write-offs drop by half. One owner offers ₹1,500 a month to keep it going. The question "will they pay?" is answered before a single line of code.

## 5. Practice

1. Which MVP tests "will people keep using it every week"?
2. Your landing page gets 500 visitors and 3 sign-ups. What did you learn?
3. List three things a pre-order page should state.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Wizard of Oz or a single-feature product.
2. Interest is weak with that message or audience: change one thing (the headline, the audience, the price) and test again.
3. It is a pre-order, the expected delivery date, and the refund policy if you are late or cannot deliver.
