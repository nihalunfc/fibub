<!--fig:country-->

**Module 10 · about 12 minutes.** You'll agree, in writing, the things that most often break founding teams.

<div class="bigidea">Agree roles, equity, vesting, IP, decisions and what happens when someone leaves in the first weeks, in writing, while everyone still gets along.</div>

## 1. Why write it down

Most startup failures that are not about the market are about the team: a founder who stops working but keeps a third of the company, an argument over who decides, code written on a personal laptop before the company existed. A short agreement made in the first weeks prevents all three.

<!--fig:agreement-builder-->

### Watch it happen

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Week 1:</b> two founders, no paperwork. Code sits on personal laptops; nobody has agreed who decides.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Week 2:</b> they agree roles, a 55/45 split, 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff, and that all work belongs to the company.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Week 3:</b> a lawyer turns it into a shareholders’ agreement (and articles, or stock purchase agreements); US founders file 83(b) elections within 30 days.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Month 18:</b> one founder leaves. The agreement already says what they keep (vested shares) and what returns (the rest): settled in a week, not a lawsuit.</p></div>
</div>


## 2. What to cover

1. **Roles:** who leads product, engineering, sales, money; who is CEO.
2. **Equity:** the split (Lesson 10.2) and an option pool for future staff.
3. **Vesting:** founders' shares vest over time, usually 4 years with a 1-year cliff (Lesson 10.3).
4. **IP assignment:** all work for the business, before and after incorporation, belongs to the company.
5. **Decisions and deadlock:** what needs everyone's agreement; how a tie is broken.
6. **Time and pay:** full-time from when; salary when the company can afford it.
7. **Money in:** loans or cash for shares, on agreed terms.
8. **Leaving:** good and bad leaver terms, buy-back price (Lesson 10.5).
9. **Confidentiality and non-solicitation** after leaving.

## 3. Where it lives

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

Write a **shareholders' agreement (SHA)** and copy the key rights (transfer restrictions, buy-back, board seats) into the **articles of association**, which bind the company. Indian law does not have founder share vesting built in, so founders use "reverse vesting": the SHA lets the company or other founders buy back unvested shares at a set price. Stamp the SHA under your state's stamp act.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

Use a **shareholders' agreement**; if every shareholder signs and it restricts the directors' powers, it is a **unanimous shareholder agreement (USA)** under the CBCA or provincial law. Founders' shares are issued at a nominal price, with vesting written as a buy-back right on unvested shares.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

Each founder signs a **stock purchase agreement** with vesting (a company repurchase right on unvested shares) and a **confidential information and invention assignment agreement (CIIAA)**. Within **30 days** of receiving shares that vest, each founder files an **83(b) election** with the IRS, so tax is on today's tiny value, not on the value when shares vest. Missing it can cost a fortune later and cannot be fixed.

</div>

> **Key idea:** The agreement is for the bad day, not the good one. Write it so a stranger could apply it if the founders stopped speaking.

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founders.* Asha and Ben start a logistics app in Pune. In week two they spend an evening with the builder above: Asha 55 % (she started a year earlier and is full-time), Ben 45 %; 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff for both; Asha is CEO; raising money needs both; deadlock goes to a named mentor. A lawyer turns it into an SHA and amended articles for ₹30,000. Eighteen months later Ben takes a job abroad; the agreement settles it in a week.

<div class="myth">

**"We are friends, so we do not need an agreement."** Friends are exactly who need one: the agreement protects the friendship by settling the hard questions before money, stress or a new job abroad make them personal.

</div>

## 5. Practice

1. Name three things a co-founder agreement should cover.
2. Why should founders' shares vest?
3. What US filing protects founders from tax as their shares vest?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Any three of: roles, equity, vesting, IP assignment, decisions, deadlock, pay, money in, leaving, confidentiality.
2. So a founder who leaves early does not keep a large stake for work they will not do.
3. An 83(b) election, within 30 days of the share grant.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Cover roles, equity, vesting, IP assignment, decisions, deadlock, pay, money in, leaving and confidentiality.
- India: shareholders’ agreement plus matching articles; Canada: shareholders’ (or unanimous shareholder) agreement; USA: stock purchase agreements and invention assignment.
- US founders must file an 83(b) election within 30 days of receiving vesting shares.
- Every founder assigns all work for the business, including pre-incorporation work, to the company.
- Write it for the bad day: clear enough that a stranger could apply it.

</div>
