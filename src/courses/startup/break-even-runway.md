**Module 2 · about 14 minutes · school maths.** You'll be able to work out your break-even point and runway, and plan before cash runs out.

<div class="bigidea">Break-even tells you how many sales cover your fixed costs; runway tells you how many months until the cash runs out. Know both before you spend.</div>

## 1. Break-even

**Fixed costs** stay the same whatever you sell: rent, salaries, software. **Variable costs** grow with each sale: materials, delivery, payment fees. The **contribution** of each sale is price minus variable cost.

```math
\text{break-even sales} = \frac{\text{fixed costs}}{\text{price} - \text{variable cost per sale}}
```

Below break-even you lose money each month; above it, each extra sale adds its full contribution to profit.

## 2. Runway

**Burn** is how much cash you lose each month. **Runway** is how long until the bank is empty:

```math
\text{runway (months)} = \frac{\text{cash in the bank}}{\text{monthly burn}}
```

<!--fig:runway-sim-->

> **Key idea:** Raising money or a loan takes 3 to 6 months. Start when you still have at least 6 to 9 months of runway, not when you have 2.

## 3. Cash is not profit

A business can be profitable on paper and still run out of cash:

- **Late payers.** You invoice in March; the customer pays in June.
- **Stock.** You buy inventory months before selling it.
- **Taxes you hold.** GST, HST or sales tax you collected must be paid to the government on schedule. It sits in your account but is not yours: keep it aside.
- **Tax instalments.** Income tax often has to be paid in advance during the year.

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

Advance tax is due in four instalments (15 June, 15 September, 15 December, 15 March) if your tax for the year will be ₹10,000 or more. GST is usually paid monthly with GSTR-3B (or quarterly under the QRMP scheme for small taxpayers).

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

Corporations may need monthly or quarterly tax instalments; sole proprietors pay quarterly instalments if their net tax owing is over $3,000 (in Quebec $1,800) in the current year and either of the two previous years. GST/HST is filed monthly, quarterly or yearly depending on sales.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

The self-employed pay estimated tax quarterly (Form 1040-ES; due about 15 April, 15 June, 15 September and 15 January) if they expect to owe $1,000 or more. Sales tax is filed monthly, quarterly or yearly depending on the state and your volume.

</div>

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up numbers.* Ravi's fixed costs are ₹1,20,000 a month (himself at a small salary, a developer, tools). Contribution per pharmacy is ₹1,220.

- Break-even: 1,20,000 ÷ 1,220 ≈ **99 pharmacies**.
- He has ₹10,00,000 saved and earns from 10 pharmacies now: burn ≈ 1,20,000 − 12,200 = ₹1,07,800 a month.
- Runway ≈ 10,00,000 ÷ 1,07,800 ≈ **9.3 months**, longer as he adds customers.

To reach 99 pharmacies in 9 months he needs about 10 new ones every month. That is the target his plan must hit, or he must raise money.

<div class="myth">

**"If we are profitable on paper, we cannot run out of cash."** Late payers, stock bought ahead, and tax you collected or owe in instalments can empty the bank while the profit and loss looks fine.

</div>

## 5. Practice

1. Fixed costs C$8,000/month; price C$50; variable cost C$10. Break-even customers?
2. Cash US$90,000, burn US$7,500. Runway?
3. Name two reasons cash can run out while profit looks fine.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. 8,000 ÷ 40 = 200 customers.
2. 12 months.
3. Customers paying late; buying stock ahead; tax bills (sales tax, instalments) falling due.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Break-even sales = fixed costs ÷ (price − variable cost).
- Runway = cash ÷ monthly burn.
- Start raising with 6 to 9 months of runway left; raising takes 3 to 6 months.
- Cash is not profit: late payers, stock and tax payments drain cash.
- Keep collected GST, HST or sales tax aside: it is not yours.

</div>
