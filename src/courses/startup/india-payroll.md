**Module 9 · about 16 minutes · India.** You'll set up a legal salary structure, register for the schemes that apply, and run monthly payroll.

<div class="bigidea">In India, payroll means a salary with wages at least half of pay, PF and ESI once you are big enough, professional tax, monthly TDS, and the labour codes’ letters, leave and gratuity rules.</div>

## 1. The labour codes

On 21 November 2025 India brought four **labour codes** into force, replacing 29 older laws: the Code on Wages, the Industrial Relations Code, the Code on Social Security and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code. State rules under the codes are still being finalised in some states, so check your state's notifications. For a startup the big changes are:

- **One definition of wages:** basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance must be **at least 50 %** of total pay; excess allowances are added back. That raises PF and gratuity for salary structures built on a small basic.
- **Appointment letters** for every employee, and wages paid by the 7th of the next month.
- **Gratuity** after one year for fixed-term employees (five years for permanent staff).
- Social security extended to gig and platform workers through aggregator contributions.

## 2. Registrations

| What | When | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Shops and Establishments registration | Within 30 days of starting, under your state's law | State labour portal |
| TAN, for TDS on salary | Before the first salary with tax | Income Tax / NSDL |
| EPF | 20 or more employees (voluntary earlier) | EPFO portal (Shram Suvidha) |
| ESI | 10 or more employees in a covered area | ESIC portal |
| Professional tax | Most states, once you pay salaries (and for the company itself) | State tax department |
| Labour Welfare Fund | Some states (Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and others) | State board |

## 3. The payslip

<!--fig:payslip-->

- **EPF:** 12 % of wages from the employee and 12 % from you (8.33 % of it to the pension scheme, capped at ₹1,250), plus about 1 % for insurance and admin. Contributions are compulsory on wages up to ₹15,000 a month; above that, you may limit PF to ₹15,000.
- **ESI:** 0.75 % from employees earning up to ₹21,000 a month, 3.25 % from you; covers medical care, sickness and maternity.
- **Professional tax:** up to ₹2,500 a year per employee, by state slab.
- **TDS on salary:** work out each employee's yearly tax (new regime by default, ₹75,000 standard deduction) and deduct it evenly each month; deposit by the 7th, file Form 24Q quarterly, give **Form 16** by 15 June.

## 4. Leave, bonus and gratuity

Leave follows your state's shops act (often 12 to 18 days of earned leave plus casual and sick leave) and the OSH Code. The **bonus** law requires a minimum 8.33 % bonus for employees earning up to ₹21,000 a month once you have 20 staff (after the first five years of a new business, with conditions). **Gratuity** is 15 days' wages for each year of service (15/26 of monthly wages × years), tax-free up to ₹20 lakh; set aside about 4.81 % of wages each month.

## 5. Monthly payroll

<div class="su-steps">

Running payroll each month

1. Collect attendance and leave, and any investment declarations (old regime only).
2. Work out gross pay, PF, ESI, professional tax and TDS for each employee.
3. Pay salaries by bank transfer by the 7th of the next month, with payslips.
4. Deposit PF and ESI by the 15th (ECR on the EPFO portal), TDS by the 7th, professional tax by the state's date.
5. Each quarter file Form 24Q; each year issue Form 16 and file any state returns.

</div>

## 6. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Neha hires a developer in Bengaluru at ₹60,000 a month, with basic at 40 %. Under the 50 % rule wages are ₹30,000. Her company has 22 staff, so EPF applies: she caps PF at ₹15,000 wages, so ₹1,800 from the employee and ₹1,800 plus about ₹150 admin and insurance from her. ESI does not apply above ₹21,000. Karnataka professional tax is ₹200. TDS under the new regime is nil (income ₹7.2 lakh). Take-home ₹58,000; her cost about ₹63,400 with the gratuity provision.

<div class="myth">

**"Keeping basic pay low saves PF and gratuity."** Not any more. Under the labour codes, allowances above half of pay are added back to wages, so a low basic no longer lowers PF or gratuity.

</div>

## 7. Practice

1. A startup with 8 staff: must it register for EPF? ESI?
2. Basic is 30 % of pay. What happens to PF wages under the codes?
3. When is Form 16 due?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. EPF not yet (20+), though it can join voluntarily; ESI not yet (10+).
2. Wages are raised to 50 % of pay for PF and gratuity.
3. By 15 June after the financial year.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Four labour codes in force since 21 November 2025: one wage definition (at least 50 % of pay), appointment letters, timely wages.
- EPF from 20 employees: 12 % + 12 % on wages (you may cap at ₹15,000); ESI from 10 employees for pay up to ₹21,000: 0.75 % + 3.25 %.
- Deduct TDS on salary monthly, deposit by the 7th, file 24Q quarterly, issue Form 16 by 15 June.
- Deposit PF and ESI by the 15th; professional tax by your state’s date.
- Gratuity: 15/26 × monthly wages × years; after 1 year for fixed-term staff, 5 years for permanent.
