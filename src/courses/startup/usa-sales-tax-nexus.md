**Module 7 · about 15 minutes · USA.** You'll know in which states you must register, what to tax and how to stay safe in an audit.

<div class="bigidea">You must collect a state’s sales tax only where you have nexus: a physical presence, or sales into the state above its threshold, usually $100,000 a year.</div>

## 1. Nexus: the link that makes you collect

A state can require you to collect its sales tax only if you have **nexus** there:

- **Physical nexus:** an office, staff (even one remote employee), a warehouse or stock (including goods in a marketplace's fulfilment centre), or regular sales visits.
- **Economic nexus:** since *South Dakota v. Wayfair* (2018), sales into a state above its threshold, even with no presence at all. Every state with a sales tax now has one, usually **$100,000** of sales in the current or previous calendar year.

Your home state is always a nexus state.

<!--fig:nexus-check-->

## 2. What is taxable?

Tangible goods are taxable in every sales tax state unless exempt (groceries and medicine often are). **Services** are mostly not taxed, with many exceptions (repairs, cleaning, telecoms; Hawaii, New Mexico and South Dakota tax most services). **Software as a service** and digital downloads are taxable in roughly half the states and not in others. Look up your product state by state before you register; it changes the answer more than the threshold.

## 3. Registering and collecting

<div class="su-steps">

From nexus to first return

1. Register for a **sales tax permit** (seller's permit) with the state's revenue department; most are free, a few charge a small fee. Register *before* you collect: collecting without a permit is illegal in many states.
2. Set the rate in your checkout or invoicing software. Most states are **destination-based** (the buyer's address decides state plus local rate); a few, such as Texas for in-state sales, use the seller's location.
3. Collect resale or exemption certificates from business buyers who do not pay tax, and keep them on file.
4. File on the schedule the state assigns (monthly, quarterly or yearly, usually by the 20th after the period), with nil returns when nothing was sold.
5. Keep sales records and certificates for at least as long as the state can audit, often 3 to 4 years.

</div>

> **Key idea:** If you should have collected and did not, the tax becomes your own cost, with interest and penalties. If you find you had nexus in the past, most states offer a **voluntary disclosure agreement** that limits how far back they go.

## 4. Marketplaces

Every sales tax state has a **marketplace facilitator** law: Amazon, Etsy, eBay, Walmart and similar platforms collect and remit tax on sales made through them. Your own website sales are still your responsibility, and states differ on whether marketplace sales count toward your own threshold.

## 5. Tools that help

A sales tax engine in your store platform (Shopify, Stripe Tax and others) calculates rates by address. The 24 **Streamlined Sales Tax** member states offer one free registration for all of them, and some certified service providers file for free for remote sellers.

## 6. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Lena runs a candle shop from Denver. Last year: $40,000 in Colorado, $130,000 to California, $115,000 to Florida, $60,000 to Oregon, all from her own website. Colorado: home state, collect. California: under $500,000, not yet. Florida: over $100,000, register. Oregon: no sales tax. She registers in Florida and sets her checkout to charge Florida buyers 6 % plus the county surtax.

<div class="myth">

**"Online sellers only collect tax in their home state."** That changed in 2018. Every state with a sales tax now has economic nexus rules, so a busy online shop may need permits in many states.

</div>

## 7. Practice

1. A New York startup hires one remote employee in Georgia. What happens?
2. Does selling SaaS mean you collect tax everywhere you pass $100,000?
3. What should you do before charging tax in a new state?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Physical nexus in Georgia: register there and collect on taxable sales into Georgia, whatever the sales level. (Also register for Georgia payroll tax.)
2. No: only in states that tax SaaS.
3. Register for a permit there first.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Nexus comes from physical presence (staff, office, stock, including marketplace warehouses) or from sales above a state’s threshold.
- Most thresholds are $100,000 a year; California and Texas $500,000; New York $500,000 and 100 sales.
- What is taxable differs by state: goods usually, services and SaaS only in some.
- Register for a permit before collecting; file on the state’s schedule, including nil returns.
- Marketplaces collect on sales they handle; your own site’s sales remain your job.

</div>
