**Module 9 · about 16 minutes · Canada.** You'll register for payroll, pay your first employee correctly and meet every remittance date.

<div class="bigidea">In Canada you deduct income tax, CPP and EI from each pay, add your own CPP and 1.4 times the EI, and send it all to the CRA by the 15th of the next month.</div>

## 1. Before the first payday

<div class="su-steps">

Hiring your first employee

1. Add a **payroll (RP) account** to your Business Number with the CRA.
2. Get the employee's SIN and a signed federal **TD1** (and provincial TD1, or TP-1015.3 in Quebec) to set their tax credits.
3. Register with your province's **workers' compensation** board (WSIB in Ontario, WorkSafeBC and so on) if your industry is covered.
4. Give a written offer or contract that meets your province's employment standards (Lesson 9.6).
5. Choose payroll software or the CRA's free **PDOC** calculator, and a pay schedule (weekly, every two weeks, twice a month).

</div>

## 2. What comes off each pay, and what you add

<!--fig:payslip-->

| | Employee | Employer |
|---|---|---|
| CPP (2026) | 5.95 % of earnings from $3,500 to $74,600, plus CPP2 4 % up to $85,000 | The same again |
| EI (2026) | 1.63 % up to $68,900 of insurable earnings | 1.4 × the employee's premium |
| Income tax | Federal + provincial, from the CRA tables or PDOC | – |
| Quebec | QPP, a lower EI rate (1.30 %) and QPIP instead | QPP, EI and QPIP shares, plus Quebec payroll contributions |

Owners with more than 40 % of the voting shares are not insurable under EI, so no EI is deducted from their own salary.

## 3. Remitting and year end

- **Remit** the income tax, CPP and EI you deducted plus your shares by the **15th of the following month** (new small employers; larger ones remit more often). Pay online through your bank or My Business Account. Late remittances cost a 3 % to 10 % penalty.
- **T4 slips** and the T4 summary by the **last day of February**.
- A **Record of Employment (ROE)** when an employee stops working or has an interruption in earnings, usually within 5 days.
- **Ontario Employer Health Tax** applies only on payroll over the $1 million exemption; BC, Manitoba, Newfoundland and Labrador and Quebec have their own payroll taxes with their own thresholds.

## 4. Employment standards

Each province sets minimum wage (Ontario $17.60 an hour from October 2025, rising each October), overtime (after 44 hours a week in Ontario, 40 in BC with daily rules), vacation (at least **2 weeks with 4 % vacation pay** in most provinces, 3 weeks and 6 % after 5 years in Ontario), public holidays, and notice or pay when you end employment. Federally regulated businesses (banks, telecoms, airlines) follow the Canada Labour Code instead.

## 5. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Diego hires a Toronto analyst at $60,000 plus 4 % vacation pay. Each month: gross $5,200; CPP $292, EI $85 and income tax about $750 come off, so take-home is about $4,075. He adds his own CPP ($292) and EI ($119): his cost is about **$5,610 a month**. He remits about $1,540 to the CRA by the 15th of the next month.

<div class="myth">

**"Payroll deductions are the employee’s money, so a late remittance only hurts them."** Deductions you hold are trust funds for the government. Late remittances bring 3 % to 10 % penalties, and directors can be personally liable for unremitted source deductions.

</div>

## 6. Practice

1. What two federal forms does a new employee fill in?
2. Your startup's founder owns 60 % of the shares and draws a salary. Is EI deducted?
3. By when are T4 slips due?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. A TD1 (and provincial TD1) plus their SIN for your records; in Quebec also the TP-1015.3.
2. No: owners with more than 40 % of the voting shares are not insurable.
3. The last day of February after the year.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Register a CRA payroll (RP) account; get a SIN and TD1 from each employee.
- 2026: CPP 5.95 % each from $3,500 to $74,600 plus CPP2; EI 1.63 % employee and 2.282 % employer up to $68,900.
- Remit by the 15th of the next month (small employers); T4s by the last day of February; ROE when earnings stop.
- Provinces set minimum wage, overtime, vacation (2 weeks and 4 % in most) and notice.
- Workers’ compensation and provincial payroll taxes (such as Ontario EHT over $1 million) may apply.
