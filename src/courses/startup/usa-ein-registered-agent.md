**Module 4 · about 14 minutes · USA.** You'll get your EIN, choose a registered agent and know when your company must register in another state.

## 1. The EIN

The **Employer Identification Number** is the business's federal tax ID: nine digits like 12-3456789. Despite the name, you need one even with no employees if you are a corporation, a multi-member LLC, or you want a business bank account (banks ask for it).

<div class="su-steps">

Getting an EIN

1. Form the LLC or corporation first; the EIN application asks for the legal name and formation date.
2. Go to irs.gov and open the online EIN application (it runs during set weekday hours). Use only irs.gov: it is free.
3. The "responsible party" (an owner or officer) gives their SSN or ITIN. Founders without one apply by fax or mail with Form SS-4.
4. Answer the questions: entity type, reason (started a new business), start date, expected employees.
5. Download the confirmation letter (CP 575) at the end. Save it: banks and payment processors ask for it.

</div>

## 2. The registered agent

Every LLC and corporation needs a **registered agent** with a physical street address in each state where it is formed or registered, open during business hours to receive **service of process** (lawsuits) and state notices. You can be your own agent in your home state; for Delaware or other states, founders pay a commercial agent (often $50 to $300 a year). Missing mail from the agent can mean missing a lawsuit or losing good standing.

## 3. Doing business in other states

<!--fig:foreign-qualify-->

If the company does business in a state other than the one it was formed in, it **qualifies** there as a "foreign" LLC or corporation: a certificate of authority, a registered agent there, that state's annual report and taxes. Skipping it can bring penalties and stop you from suing in that state's courts until you fix it.

> **Key idea:** Company-law registration, payroll registration and sales tax registration are three different tests. A remote employee usually triggers payroll registration in their state; online sales can trigger sales tax (Lesson 7.4) without any foreign qualification.

## 4. State tax accounts

- **Sales tax permit** where you sell taxable goods or services (Module 7).
- **State payroll withholding** and **state unemployment insurance (SUI)** accounts before the first pay day in each state where employees work (Module 9).
- **State income or franchise tax** accounts, opened by registering with the state's revenue department.

## 5. Worked example

*Made-up founders.* Clinivo Inc. (Delaware, founders in California) gets its EIN online in 15 minutes and saves the CP 575. Its Delaware registered agent costs $125 a year; in California the company is its own agent at its office. When Clinivo hires an engineer in Austin, Texas, it registers with the Texas Workforce Commission for unemployment tax (Texas has no state income tax withholding) and, on its lawyer's advice, qualifies in Texas as a foreign corporation.
