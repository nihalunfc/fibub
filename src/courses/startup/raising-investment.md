**Module 6 · about 18 minutes.** You'll understand how founders sell part of the company for money, the common instruments, and the securities laws that apply.

## 1. Equity in one picture

Investors give money for a share of the company. **Pre-money valuation** is what the company is worth before the money; **post-money** = pre-money + new money. Investor ownership = new money ÷ post-money.

```math
\text{ownership} = \frac{\text{investment}}{\text{pre-money} + \text{investment}}
```

Raise ₹2 crore at ₹8 crore pre-money: the investor owns 2 ÷ 10 = 20 %.

## 2. The instruments

- **Priced round:** investors buy preferred shares at a set price per share, with a term sheet, shareholder agreement and board rights. Standard from Series A; sometimes seed.
- **SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity):** money now for shares later, at the next priced round, at a price set by a **valuation cap** and/or **discount**. Fast and cheap; popular in the US and Canada.
- **Convertible note:** a loan that converts into shares at the next round (with a cap and discount), with interest and a maturity date.

<!--fig:safe-convert-->

> **Key idea:** A SAFE feels like free money but it is dilution you have already sold. Keep a running total of all SAFEs and model them in your cap table (Lesson 6.6).

## 3. Securities law: every share sale is regulated

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

Shares in a private company are offered by **private placement** (Companies Act section 42: an offer letter PAS-4 to identified investors, a special resolution, money through banking channels, allotment within 60 days, PAS-3 return of allotment). SAFEs as such don't fit Indian law; seed investors use **compulsorily convertible preference shares (CCPS)**, **convertible debentures**, or **convertible notes**, which DPIIT-recognised startups may issue for ₹25 lakh or more per tranche, converting within 10 years. Foreign money follows **FEMA**: pricing at or above fair value from a valuation report, sector rules, and reporting (FC-GPR) through your bank within 30 days.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

Every distribution of securities needs a prospectus or a **prospectus exemption** under NI 45-106: accredited investors, family, friends and business associates, and others. Many exempt sales require filing a **report of exempt distribution** (Form 45-106F1) with the provincial securities regulator within 10 days and paying a fee. SAFEs adapted to Canadian law are common at pre-seed.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

Most startups sell under **Regulation D Rule 506(b)** (accredited investors, no general advertising) or **506(c)** (advertising allowed, accredited investors only with verification). File **Form D** with the SEC within 15 days of the first sale, plus state notice filings. The **post-money SAFE** published by Y Combinator is the standard seed instrument. Crowdfunding under Regulation CF lets non-accredited investors take part through registered portals.

</div>

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founders.* Clinivo Inc. (Delaware) raises US$500,000 on post-money SAFEs with a US$5 million cap and 20 % discount, files Form D, and later raises a seed round at US$2.00 per share when the company had 4 million fully diluted shares. Cap price = 5,000,000 ÷ 4,000,000 = US$1.25; discount price = US$1.60. They convert at US$1.25 into 400,000 shares: about 9.1 % after conversion. Try these numbers in the calculator.

## 5. Practice

1. US$1 million at US$4 million pre-money. Investor ownership?
2. Why might an investor want both a cap and a discount?
3. Name the filing a US startup makes after a Regulation D sale.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. 1 ÷ 5 = 20 %.
2. The cap protects them if the company grows a lot before the round; the discount rewards early risk if it doesn't.
3. Form D with the SEC (within 15 days), plus state notice filings.
