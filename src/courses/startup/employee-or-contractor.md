**Module 9 · about 12 minutes.** You'll know who must be on payroll, and how to work with contractors without getting a bill for back taxes later.

<div class="bigidea">Whether someone is an employee depends on how the work really happens (who controls it, whose tools, who carries the risk), not on what the contract calls them.</div>

## 1. Why it matters

An **employee** works in your business; you withhold their income tax, pay employer contributions, and give them minimum wage, holidays and notice. A **contractor** runs their own business and invoices you; they handle their own taxes. Calling an employee a contractor does not make them one, and getting it wrong means back payroll taxes, penalties and claims for holiday pay or severance.

<!--fig:worker-test-->

## 2. The tests

- **Control:** do you decide when, where and how the work is done?
- **Tools and equipment:** whose laptop, software, vehicle?
- **Chance of profit or risk of loss:** fixed-price jobs, their own costs, the ability to hire helpers.
- **Integration:** is the work part of your core business, done alongside your staff, or a separate service?
- **Independence:** other clients, a business name, website, insurance, invoices.

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

Indian courts look mainly at control and integration. A long-term "consultant" who works only for you, on your schedule, can be found an employee for PF, ESI, gratuity and the labour codes. Contractors are paid against invoices with TDS under 194J (10 %) or 194C (1 % or 2 %); employees' tax is deducted under section 192.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

The CRA (and Revenu Québec) apply the four-factor test from the courts; provinces apply their own tests for employment standards. You or the worker can ask the CRA for a ruling (form CPT1). Contractors over $30,000 a year charge you GST/HST.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

The IRS looks at behavioural control, financial control and the relationship; the Department of Labor uses an "economic reality" test for minimum wage and overtime; and states such as California, Massachusetts and New Jersey use the stricter **ABC test**, under which a worker is an employee unless the work is outside your usual business. Contractors give you a W-9 and get a **1099-NEC** if paid $2,000 or more in 2026.

</div>

## 3. Hiring contractors safely

<div class="su-steps">

Working with a contractor

1. Write a contract for a defined project or service, with deliverables, price and an IP assignment (Lesson 11.3).
2. Let them choose how and when to work, use their own equipment and invoice you.
3. Collect their tax details: PAN (India), GST/HST number if registered (Canada), Form W-9 (USA) or W-8BEN for someone abroad.
4. Pay against invoices; deduct TDS where Indian law requires it.
5. Review every 6 months: if they now look like staff, offer employment.

</div>

> **Key idea:** Contractors are ideal for short, specialised or uncertain work. For a full-time role at the core of your business that you direct day to day, hire an employee or use an employer of record.

## 4. Hiring someone in another country

You usually cannot simply put a foreign resident on your payroll. Options: a contractor agreement (if they are genuinely independent), an **employer of record (EOR)** that employs them locally for a fee, or setting up a local entity once you have several people there. Remote employees can also create a tax presence for your company abroad.

## 5. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Ana's Vancouver startup has paid a developer $6,000 a month for a year. He works 9 to 5 on her team's sprints, uses a company laptop and has no other clients. The checker says employee. She puts him on payroll from next month (CPP, EI, income tax and vacation pay) and asks her accountant about the past year.

<div class="myth">

**"If they sign a contractor agreement and send invoices, they are a contractor."** Tax and labour authorities look at reality. A full-time "contractor" on your schedule, using your laptop and working only for you, is usually an employee, and you owe the missed payroll taxes.

</div>

## 6. Practice

1. Name two signs that a person is in business for themselves.
2. Why is the ABC test stricter?
3. What can you use to hire a full-time person in another country without opening an entity there?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Other clients; sets own prices; own tools and insurance; can hire helpers; can make a loss.
2. The worker must also do work outside your usual business, which few full-time workers do.
3. An employer of record.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- The label does not decide; control, tools, chance of profit, integration and independence do.
- Misclassification brings back payroll taxes, penalties and claims for holiday pay or notice.
- India: long-term exclusive "consultants" can be employees for PF, ESI and gratuity; Canada: CRA four-factor test and CPT1 rulings; USA: IRS test plus the ABC test in some states.
- Contractors need a written contract with IP assignment and their tax details (PAN, GST/HST number, W-9 or W-8BEN).
- For full-time people abroad, use an employer of record or a local entity.

</div>
