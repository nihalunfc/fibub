**Module 10 · about 11 minutes.** You'll agree a split the whole team can live with for years, and give later joiners fair options.

<div class="bigidea">Split equity by what each founder will commit and risk from now on, with credit for real work and cash so far; then protect the split with vesting.</div>

## 1. Look forward, not back

The idea and the first months of work are worth little next to the five to ten years it takes to build a company. A split should reward what each person will **commit and risk from now on**, with credit for real work and cash already put in. A founder who feels short-changed in year three is a bigger risk than a split that is a few points off "fair".

<!--fig:equity-split-->

### Watch it happen

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Score:</b> each founder rates everyone 0 to 10 on each line, then the team agrees one score per line.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Weigh:</b> multiply each score by its weight (full-time counts 3, the idea 0.5) and add up each founder’s points.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Divide:</b> each founder’s points ÷ all points = their starting percentage.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Talk:</b> round to numbers everyone accepts; check nobody would feel cheated in year three.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Protect:</b> put every founder on vesting and set aside the option pool.</p></div>
</div>


## 2. What counts

- **Commitment:** full-time now versus part-time or "when we raise".
- **Past work:** a working prototype, first customers.
- **Cash** put in (or record it as a loan or at the same price per share).
- **Skills** the product cannot be built without.
- **Risk:** leaving a well-paid job, giving up salary.
- The **idea** alone counts for little.

## 3. Everyone else

| Who | Typical stake | How |
|---|---|---|
| Co-founders | The rest, often equal or near-equal | Founder shares with vesting |
| Option pool (future staff) | 10 % to 20 %, set before the first priced round | ESOP / stock option plan (Lesson 10.4) |
| First employees | 0.5 % to 2 % each | Options, 4-year vesting |
| Advisers | 0.1 % to 1 % | Shares or options vesting over 1 to 2 years, tied to real work |

> **Key idea:** If one person scores far below the others, they may not be a co-founder at all. Offering them a salary and options can be fairer to everyone.

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founders.* Three founders in Toronto score themselves: Maya started a year earlier and built the prototype; Dev joins full-time from launch; Lin will stay part-time for a year. The weighted split above gives about 44 %, 36 % and 20 %. They agree on 42 %, 38 % and 20 %, with Lin's shares vesting only from the day she goes full-time, and set aside a 12 % pool before their seed round.

<div class="myth">

**"Whoever had the idea deserves the most equity."** Ideas are cheap; execution over years creates the value. The idea earns a little credit, but commitment, skills and risk matter far more.

</div>

## 5. Practice

1. Why is a 90/10 split between two full-time founders usually a warning sign?
2. Name two things that justify a larger share.
3. Where do the first employees' options come from?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. The 10 % founder does similar work for a tiny reward and is likely to leave or resent it; investors notice.
2. Starting earlier with real work, cash invested, being full-time sooner, scarce skills, more risk taken.
3. The option pool.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Most value is created after the split, so weight future commitment over the idea.
- Count full-time commitment, past work, cash, skills and risk.
- Near-equal splits between full-time founders are common; big gaps need a clear reason.
- Option pool 10 % to 20 %; first employees 0.5 % to 2 %; advisers 0.1 % to 1 %.
- Vesting protects whatever split you choose.

</div>
