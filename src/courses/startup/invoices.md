**Module 7 · about 11 minutes.** You'll issue invoices your customers can claim tax on, number them properly and fix mistakes the legal way.

<div class="bigidea">A tax invoice is a legal document: with the right details your customer can claim the tax back and you pass an audit; without them both of you lose.</div>

## 1. Why the details matter

An invoice is a tax document. Your registered customers can claim input credits only from an invoice that shows certain details, and an auditor checks your invoices against your returns. A missing tax number can cost your client their credit, and you the client.

<!--fig:invoice-check-->

### Watch it happen

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>A receipt says $113, HST 13 % included.</b> How much is tax?</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Wrong way:</b> 13 % of $113 = $14.69. Too much, because the 13 % was charged on the price before tax, not on $113.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Right way:</b> price before tax = $113 ÷ 1.13 = $100.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Tax:</b> $113 − $100 = $13. Shortcut: total × 13 ÷ 113.</p></div>
</div>


## 2. What each country needs

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

A **tax invoice** (CGST Rule 46) shows: your name, address and GSTIN; a serial number unique for the financial year (up to 16 characters); date; customer name, address and GSTIN if registered; HSN or SAC code; description, quantity and taxable value; the rate and amount of CGST and SGST, or IGST; place of supply for inter-state sales; whether reverse charge applies; and a signature or digital signature. For an unregistered customer buying ₹50,000 or more, add their address and state.

Issue it **before or at** delivery of goods, and within **30 days** of supplying a service. Composition dealers issue a *bill of supply* with no tax shown. Above ₹5 crore turnover, each B2B invoice must get an **IRN and QR code** from the e-invoice portal.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

The CRA sets three levels for input tax credit claims:

- **Under $100:** your name (or trade name), the date, and the total.
- **$100 to $499.99:** add your **GST/HST registration number** and the tax charged (or a note that tax is included, with the rate).
- **$500 and up:** add the buyer's name, the payment terms and a description of each item.

Quebec sales also show your QST number. Show GST, HST and PST separately where they apply.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

There is no federal invoice format and no tax number on invoices. Good practice and most states' rules: your business name and address, a unique invoice number, date, buyer, description and price, and **sales tax shown separately**. For any sale where you charge no tax because the buyer will resell or is exempt, keep a valid **resale or exemption certificate** on file: without it, an auditor can make you pay the tax.

</div>

## 3. Numbering and corrections

- Number invoices in one **unbroken series** (for example INV-2026-0001, -0002). Use a new series each year if you like, never reuse a number.
- Never edit or delete an invoice you have sent and reported. Correct it with a **credit note** (to reduce) or a **debit note** (to increase), referring to the original.
- Send invoices as PDF and keep copies (and sales records) for the time the law sets: 6 years in Canada, at least 72 months after the annual GST return due date in India, 3 to 4 years or more by state in the USA (Lesson 4.8).

> **Key idea:** Make your invoice template right once, in your accounting software or a generator, and every invoice after that passes automatically.

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Noor's Toronto client asks why it cannot claim the HST on her $1,130 invoice. Her template shows her name, date and total but not her GST/HST number, the buyer's name or the description. She reissues it with the missing details (same number, clearly marked as corrected; it was not yet in a return) and fixes her template.

<div class="myth">

**"Tax inside a $113 HST-included price is $14.69 (13 % of $113)."** The tax is 13 % of the price before tax: $113 × 13 ÷ 113 = $13. Taking the percentage of the total always gives too much.

</div>

## 5. Practice

1. An Indian consultant's invoice shows 18 % GST as one line to a client in another state. What is wrong?
2. Which three details does a $600 Canadian invoice need beyond a $90 one?
3. A US buyer says "we're exempt". What do you ask for?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Inter-state supply should show IGST (and the place of supply), not an unlabelled GST line.
2. The GST/HST number and tax charged, plus the buyer's name, payment terms and item description.
3. A completed exemption (or resale) certificate valid in your state.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- India (Rule 46): GSTIN of both sides, unique serial number, HSN/SAC, taxable value, CGST/SGST or IGST, place of supply; e-invoice IRN above ₹5 crore.
- Canada: name, date and total under $100; add GST/HST number and tax from $100; add buyer, terms and description from $500.
- USA: no federal format; show sales tax separately and keep exemption certificates for untaxed sales.
- Correct sent invoices with credit or debit notes; keep invoices for 6 years (Canada), 72 months after the annual return (India), 3 to 4+ years (US states).
- Tax inside a tax-included price = total × rate ÷ (100 + rate).

</div>
