**Module 4 · about 14 minutes · Canada.** You'll know when you need a Business Number, which CRA accounts to open, and which provincial accounts sit alongside them.

<div class="bigidea">The CRA gives your business one Business Number, with a program account for each tax: RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll, RC for corporate tax and RM for imports and exports.</div>

## 1. What the BN is

The **Business Number (BN)** is a nine-digit number the CRA gives a business. It never changes. Each tax area you deal with is a **program account**: the BN plus two letters and a four-digit reference, for example **123456789 RT0001**.

You need a BN when you **incorporate** (the CRA issues one to federal corporations and most provincial ones automatically) or when you need any program account. Getting it is **free**.

<!--fig:bn-accounts-->

## 2. The program accounts

| Account | For | When |
|---|---|---|
| **RT** | GST/HST | Required once taxable sales pass $30,000 in four consecutive quarters (or in one quarter); voluntary before that |
| **RP** | Payroll deductions | Before the first pay day for any employee, including yourself if your corporation pays you a salary |
| **RC** | Corporate income tax | Every corporation (opened automatically) |
| **RM** | Import-export | Before importing or exporting goods commercially |
| **RZ** | Information returns | For some slips such as T5 (dividends) or T5018 |

## 3. How to register

<div class="su-steps">

Getting a BN and accounts

1. Have ready: legal name, address, owners' SINs (sole proprietors and partners) or the corporation number, start date, main activity, expected sales.
2. Register online with **Business Registration Online (BRO)**, by phone with the CRA, or through your provincial registry if your province offers combined registration.
3. Add program accounts (RT, RP, RM) now or later in **My Business Account**.
4. Set up **My Business Account** access so you can file returns, pay and authorise your accountant (as a representative).
5. Register for provincial accounts separately where needed.

</div>

> **Key idea:** Keep the GST/HST you collect separate from your own money, and note your filing frequency (monthly, quarterly or yearly) as soon as the RT account opens: the CRA will expect returns even for periods with no sales.

## 4. Provincial accounts

- **Quebec:** **Revenu Québec** administers both GST and QST for most Quebec businesses; register with it.
- **BC, Saskatchewan, Manitoba:** register for **PST/RST** with the province if you sell taxable goods or some services there.
- **Workers' compensation:** WSIB (Ontario), WorkSafeBC, WCB Alberta and so on, if your industry must be covered.
- **Employer health tax** in some provinces (Ontario EHT, BC EHT) once payroll passes their exemption.

## 5. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Northlight Robotics Inc. (Lesson 4.4) gets BN 765432109 with RC0001 automatically. Before their first sale they add RT0001 voluntarily (to claim input tax credits on equipment). When they hire their first engineer, they add RP0001 and register with WCB Alberta. Importing motors from Japan, they add RM0001.

<div class="myth">

**"The GST/HST I collect is part of my revenue."** It belongs to the government. You hold it until your return is due, minus the input tax credits on your own business purchases.

</div>

## 6. Practice

1. Which account do you need before paying your first employee?
2. Why might a startup register for GST/HST before it must?
3. Who handles QST registration?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. RP (payroll).
2. To claim input tax credits for GST/HST paid on start-up costs, and because big clients often expect it.
3. Revenu Québec (which also administers GST for most Quebec businesses).
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- One BN per business; corporations get one with RC0001 at incorporation.
- Add RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll) and RM (import-export) when needed.
- Register through Business Registration Online or My Business Account.
- Provinces add their own accounts: Quebec QST, BC and Saskatchewan PST, Manitoba RST, workers’ compensation.
- Keep collected tax separate and note your filing frequency.

</div>
