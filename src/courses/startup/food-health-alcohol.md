**Module 5 · about 15 minutes.** You'll know which food, health and alcohol permissions apply to your business and when to get them.

<div class="bigidea">Anything people eat, drink, take or put on their body needs permission before the first sale: food licences, inspected premises and strict labels, and alcohol needs a licence that depends on location.</div>

## 1. Why these are different

Food, drink, medicine, cosmetics and alcohol can hurt people, so <mark>you need permission before your first sale</mark>, premises are inspected, and labels follow strict rules. Plan 4 to 12 weeks for approvals.

<!--fig:food-licence-->

## 2. Food by country

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

Every food business needs FSSAI registration or a licence through **FoSCoS**: **basic registration** up to ₹12 lakh turnover; **state licence** above that up to ₹20 crore; **central licence** above ₹20 crore and for importers, exporters, e-commerce food businesses and some activities. Display the 14-digit FSSAI number at the premises, on packs and on online listings. Restaurants also need the municipal trade licence, a fire NOC by size, and in some cities a police "eating house" licence.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

Restaurants and food premises are inspected by the **local public health unit** under provincial food premises rules, and need trained food handlers. Making food to sell across provinces, or importing or exporting it, needs a **Safe Food for Canadians licence** from the **CFIA** with a preventive control plan. Pre-packaged food follows the federal labelling rules (bilingual labels, nutrition facts).

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

Restaurants need a **county or city health permit** after a plan review and inspection. **Cottage food laws** in most states let you sell low-risk home-made foods directly to customers. Facilities that make, pack or hold food for wider sale register with the **FDA** (free, renewed in even years) and follow its food safety rules; meat and poultry go through the USDA.

</div>

## 3. Health products and services

Medicines, medical devices, supplements, cosmetics and health services each have a regulator: India's **CDSCO** and state drug controllers (and AYUSH for traditional medicines), **Health Canada**, and the US **FDA** plus state boards. Software that diagnoses or treats can itself be a regulated medical device. Get advice before you build anything that makes a health claim.

## 4. Alcohol

Alcohol needs licences at every step: making, importing, wholesaling, retail and serving.

- **India:** state excise departments license bars, shops and producers; rules and fees vary greatly by state, and some states ban alcohol.
- **Canada:** provincial regulators (AGCO in Ontario, LCRB in BC) license sale and service; provincial boards control most retail and wholesale.
- **USA:** federal **TTB** permits for producers, importers and wholesalers; states and localities license retail sale and service, under each state's "three-tier" rules.

> **Key idea:** Alcohol licences can take months and depend on location (distance from schools, local quotas). Check before you sign a lease.

## 5. Worked example

*Made-up founders.* A cloud kitchen in Hyderabad with ₹60 lakh projected turnover gets an FSSAI **state licence** on FoSCoS, a GHMC trade licence and a fire NOC, and lists its FSSAI number on delivery apps. A Vancouver hot-sauce maker selling only in BC uses an inspected commercial kitchen; when an Alberta store orders, she applies for an SFC licence first.

<div class="myth">

**"Small home food businesses do not need a licence."** India requires at least FSSAI basic registration for every food business; Canadian provinces and US states have specific home-food rules, and many foods are not allowed from a home kitchen at all.

</div>

## 6. Practice

1. Which Indian food businesses need a central FSSAI licence regardless of turnover?
2. What does a US home cook need to sell jam at a farmers' market in most states?
3. Name the US federal agency that issues permits to alcohol importers.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Importers and exporters, among others (also turnover above ₹20 crore and some specified activities).
2. To follow the state's cottage food law (allowed foods, label wording, any sales cap or registration).
3. The Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau (TTB).
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Permission comes before the first sale; plan 4 to 12 weeks.
- India: FSSAI basic registration up to ₹12 lakh, state licence above, central licence for large or multi-state.
- Canada: provincial and city food premises rules; Safe Food for Canadians licence for importing or interprovincial trade.
- USA: county health department; FDA registration for manufacturers; state cottage food laws.
- Alcohol licences depend on location and can take months.

</div>
