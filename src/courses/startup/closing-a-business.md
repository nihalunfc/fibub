**Module 12 · about 12 minutes.** You'll close a business in the right order, so no fees, tax or personal liability follow you afterwards.

<div class="bigidea">A business ends properly only when it is formally closed: pay everyone, file the final returns, cancel the registrations, dissolve the entity, and keep the records.</div>

## 1. Why close it properly

Most startups do not succeed, and that is part of the game. What hurts founders is not the closing but a half-closed company: annual fees and late penalties keep adding up, tax accounts stay open, and in India directors of a struck-off company can be **disqualified for 5 years**. A clean close also keeps your reputation with investors, staff and suppliers for the next venture.

## 2. The order matters

<!--fig:close-steps-->

### Watch it happen

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Month 0:</b> a Toronto corporation runs low on money. The founders decide, by board and shareholder resolution, to wind down, and tell staff and customers in writing.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Month 1:</b> they pay final wages and vacation pay, issue Records of Employment, sell equipment, and pay suppliers and CRA.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Month 3:</b> they file the final T2 and GST/HST returns, close the program accounts, and get a CRA clearance certificate.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Month 4:</b> they distribute the remaining C$12,000 to shareholders by percentage, file Articles of Dissolution, and store the records for 6 years.</p></div>
</div>

**Who is paid first?** Secured lenders, employees' wages and source deductions, taxes and other creditors are paid before shareholders. If the company cannot pay its debts in full, it is **insolvent**, and the closing goes through a formal process instead: India's **IBC**, Canada's **Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act** with a licensed insolvency trustee, or US **Chapter 7**. Directors can be personally liable for unpaid wages and deducted taxes in all three countries.

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

A company with no assets or liabilities can use **Fast Track Exit**: file overdue returns, close the bank account, then file **STK-2** with the **₹10,000** fee, directors' indemnity bonds and affidavits, and a statement of accounts certified by a chartered accountant. The Registrar publishes a notice and strikes the company off after the objection period. A company with assets to distribute uses **voluntary liquidation** under section 59 of the IBC with an insolvency professional. An LLP files **Form 24**. Cancel GST with **REG-16** and file the final return **GSTR-10**.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

File the final **T2**, GST/HST and payroll returns, issue final T4s and Records of Employment, and close each program account on the Business Number. Ask CRA for a **clearance certificate** (form **TX19**) before distributing property, or directors can be personally liable for unpaid tax. Then file **Articles of Dissolution**: federal Form 17 with Corporations Canada, or with your province (for example through the Ontario Business Registry). Keep records for 6 years.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

File **Form 966** within 30 days of the resolution to dissolve a corporation. File the final federal return (tick "final return" on 1120, 1120-S or 1065), final payroll returns (941, 940) and W-2s, and give shareholders **1099-DIV** for liquidating distributions. Pay all franchise tax, then file a **Certificate of Dissolution** (corporation) or **Certificate of Cancellation** (LLC) in the formation state, and withdraw from every state where you registered. Write to the IRS to close the EIN account.

</div>

## 3. Worked example

*Made-up founders.* A two-founder Indian private company never found product-market fit. It has ₹1.2 lakh in the bank and no debts after paying its last contractor. The founders file the two years of AOC-4 and MGT-7 they had skipped (with late fees), file the final ITR, cancel GST, split the remaining money by shareholding, close the bank account, and file STK-2 with ₹10,000. Seven months later the company is struck off, with no disqualification risk for either director. One of them starts a new company the next year.

<div class="myth">

**"If I stop using the company, it closes on its own."** A company exists until it is formally dissolved or struck off. Fees, returns and penalties keep coming, and in India the directors can be disqualified if the registry strikes it off for non-filing.

</div>

## 4. Practice

1. What must happen before shareholders get any money?
2. What is the Indian fast-track form to close a company with no assets or liabilities?
3. Which IRS form tells the IRS that a corporation has decided to dissolve?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. All debts, wages, taxes and closing costs are paid (and in Canada, a clearance certificate obtained).
2. STK-2 (Fast Track Exit), with a ₹10,000 fee.
3. Form 966, within 30 days of the resolution.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Decide formally, tell people in writing, then pay staff, creditors and tax before shareholders.
- Insolvent businesses go through a formal process (IBC, BIA trustee, Chapter 7).
- India: STK-2 (₹10,000) for a company with no assets or liabilities, IBC voluntary liquidation otherwise; LLP Form 24; GST REG-16.
- Canada: final T2, clearance certificate, Articles of Dissolution. USA: Form 966, final returns, Certificate of Dissolution, withdraw from other states.
- Keep records for the legal period after closing.

</div>
