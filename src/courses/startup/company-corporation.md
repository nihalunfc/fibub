**Module 3 · about 16 minutes.** You'll understand what makes a company different, who does what inside it, and how it looks in each country.

## 1. A separate legal person

A **company** (called a **corporation** in North America) is created by registering it with the government. From then on it is <mark>a legal person in its own right</mark>: it owns its bank account and assets, signs contracts, sues and is sued, owes its own debts and files its own tax return. If it fails, shareholders usually lose only what they paid for their shares.

It also **lasts**: founders can leave, sell or die and the company carries on, and it can raise money by selling new shares.

<!--fig:company-roles-->

> **Key idea:** The shield is real but not absolute. Directors can be personally liable for some unpaid employee wages and for tax they deducted but never paid over, and a personal guarantee on a loan bypasses the shield completely.

## 2. By country

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

*Under the Companies Act, 2013.*

- **Private limited company ("Pvt Ltd").** At least **2 directors** and **2 members** (shareholders; can be the same two people), at most 200 members. At least one director must have stayed in India for **182 days or more** during the financial year. No minimum capital. Shares cannot be offered to the public. Registered with the MCA through **SPICe+** (Lesson 4.2). Every company also needs a statutory auditor, board meetings and annual filings.
- **One Person Company (OPC).** One member (an Indian citizen; resident or not) and a nominee who takes over if the member dies. Since 2021 an OPC can convert into a private company at any time and has no capital or turnover cap.
- Tax: domestic companies can choose a 22 % rate (25.17 % with surcharge and cess) by giving up most deductions, or the older regime (Lesson 8.2).

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

Incorporate **federally** (Canada Business Corporations Act) or **provincially** (for example Ontario's Business Corporations Act). One director and one shareholder is enough. A company owned and controlled by Canadian residents is usually a **Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC)**, which gets the **small business deduction**: 9 % federal tax on the first $500,000 of active business income, plus a low provincial rate (Ontario 3.2 %, so 12.2 % combined). Federal corporations need at least 25 % resident Canadian directors; Ontario, BC, Alberta and Quebec have no residency rule.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

Incorporate with a **state** (Delaware is popular for startups, Lesson 3.6). One shareholder and one director can be enough. By default a corporation is a **C corporation**, taxed at a flat **21 %** federal rate, with shareholders taxed again on dividends. Eligible small corporations can elect **S corporation** status to pass profit through to owners instead (Lesson 3.4). Corporations keep **bylaws**, issue shares, hold board and shareholder meetings (or sign written consents) and file a state annual report.

</div>

## 3. What a company costs to run

More than a sole proprietorship, every year:

- A separate **corporate tax return** (India ITR-6; Canada T2; USA Form 1120 or 1120-S).
- **Annual filings** with the registrar (Lesson 12.1).
- **Minutes** and records of decisions, a share register.
- India also needs a statutory **audit** every year, whatever the size.

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founders.* Lena and Dev (Lesson 1.3) incorporate their booking app.

| | India | Canada | USA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entity | Private limited company | Federal CCPC | Delaware C corp |
| Minimum people | 2 directors, 2 shareholders | 1 director, 1 shareholder | 1 director, 1 shareholder |
| Their set-up | Both directors and 50/50 shareholders | Both directors, 50/50 | Both directors, 50/50, Lena CEO, Dev CTO |
| Profit tax | 25.17 % (new regime) | 12.2 % in Ontario on first $500k | 21 % federal plus state |

## 5. Practice

1. Name three things a company can do that a sole proprietorship can't.
2. Why might a solo Indian founder pick an OPC over a proprietorship?
3. Can a US corporation have just one person as shareholder, director and president?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Sign contracts in its own name, issue shares to investors, and continue after the founder leaves (also: limit the owners' liability).
2. Limited liability and an easy path to a private limited company later.
3. Yes, in every state.
