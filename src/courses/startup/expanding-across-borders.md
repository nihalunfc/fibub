**Module 12 · about 13 minutes.** You'll choose the next step into another country for your stage, and know which registrations and tax forms follow.

<div class="bigidea">Expand in steps: sell from home first, then put one person on the ground, and form a local company only when the business there is real. Each step brings its own registrations and tax rules.</div>

## 1. Three steps, not one leap

1. **Sell remotely** from your home company. You may still have to register for the other country's sales tax or GST.
2. **First person there** through an employer of record or a contractor. Watch for a **permanent establishment**.
3. **Local company** (a subsidiary) when you have a team, an office, or investors who want one.

<!--fig:expand-path-->

### Watch it happen

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Year 1:</b> an Indian SaaS company sells subscriptions to US small businesses from Pune. It registers for sales tax in two states where it passes economic nexus.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Year 2:</b> it hires a salesperson in Texas through an employer of record. The salesperson sets up demos but does not sign contracts, to limit permanent establishment risk.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Year 3:</b> a US investor wants to lead a round in a Delaware company. The founders form a US parent and, with advisers, plan the flip under FEMA and Indian tax rules.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>After:</b> the Indian company becomes a subsidiary doing development for the US parent, paid at an arm's length price (transfer pricing), with yearly reports in both countries.</p></div>
</div>

## 2. Tax follows activity

- **Sales tax and GST** follow the customer: register where the rules say, even without an office (Lesson 7.5).
- **Income tax** follows a permanent establishment: a fixed place of business, or someone who habitually concludes contracts for you. The India, Canada and US tax treaties set the tests.
- **Payroll** follows the worker: employees are taxed where they work.
- **Transfer pricing:** when related companies trade, prices must match what strangers would agree, with documentation.

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

**Going out from India:** investing in a foreign company is **Overseas Direct Investment (ODI)** under FEMA, done through your bank (the AD bank) with the required forms; set up the foreign company only through this route. Payments for exports must be received within the period your bank tells you. **Coming in:** foreign companies form an Indian private limited subsidiary under the automatic FDI route in most sectors, with at least one resident director, and report the investment to RBI on the **FIRMS** portal.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

**Going out from Canada:** report foreign affiliates on **Form T1134** each year and foreign property costing more than C$100,000 on **T1135**. A Canadian company controlled from abroad loses CCPC status (and the small business rate and enhanced SR&ED refunds). **Coming in:** register the foreign company extra-provincially where it operates, or form a subsidiary; non-residents selling digital services to Canadian consumers register for GST/HST above C$30,000 a year.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

**Going out from the USA:** owning a foreign subsidiary means **Form 5471** each year (penalties start at $10,000 a form) and US tax on some of its income under the **GILTI** rules. Foreign bank accounts over $10,000 in total go on the **FBAR**. **Coming in:** foreign founders usually form a Delaware C corporation, get an EIN (possible without a Social Security number), and register in each state where they have staff.

</div>

## 3. Worked example

*Made-up founders.* A Vancouver e-commerce brand sells to the USA from Canada. Its US sales pass $100,000 in Washington and California, so it registers for sales tax in both (Lesson 7.4). It ships from a US warehouse run by a fulfilment company, which also creates nexus in that state, so it registers there too. With no US staff or office and no one concluding contracts in the USA, it has no US permanent establishment, so its profits stay taxable in Canada under the Canada-US tax treaty.

<div class="myth">

**"Selling to the USA means I need a US company."** Many startups sell to US customers for years from home. You may need US sales tax registrations, but a US company is needed only when you have a team there or US investors require one.

</div>

## 4. Practice

1. What is the usual first step into a new country?
2. What creates a permanent establishment risk when you hire abroad?
3. Which yearly form reports a US company's foreign subsidiary?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Selling remotely from your home company, with any sales tax or GST registrations needed.
2. A fixed office, or a person who habitually signs contracts on your behalf.
3. Form 5471.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Expand in steps: sell remotely, then one person through an EOR, then a local company.
- Sales tax follows customers; income tax follows a permanent establishment; payroll follows the worker.
- India: ODI under FEMA to go out; FDI with a resident director and FIRMS reporting to come in.
- Canada: T1134 and T1135 for foreign holdings; foreign control ends CCPC benefits.
- USA: Form 5471, GILTI and FBAR for foreign subsidiaries; a Delaware C corp for US investors.

</div>
