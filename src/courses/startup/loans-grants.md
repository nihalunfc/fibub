**Module 6 · about 15 minutes.** You'll know the order to look for money in, and which government loans and grants may fit your business.

## 1. The funding ladder

1. **Your savings and revenue:** cheapest and keeps you in control.
2. **Customers' money:** pre-sales, deposits, annual plans paid upfront.
3. **Friends and family:** write it down as a loan or shares, with terms.
4. **Grants and competitions:** free money but slow, competitive and with reporting.
5. **Loans:** banks, government-backed schemes; need repayment from cash flow.
6. **Equity investors:** angels and VC (Lesson 6.5); expensive in ownership.

<!--fig:funding-match-->

> **Key idea:** A loan must be repaid from cash flow, so it suits businesses with revenue or assets. Equity suits fast-growing startups with no profit yet. Grants suit research and work with public benefit.

## 2. Applying well

- Prepare a business plan with cash flow forecasts (Lesson 2.6) and your registration documents.
- Know your credit score: CIBIL in India, Equifax/TransUnion in Canada and the USA. Banks check founders' personal credit for new businesses.
- Expect a **personal guarantee** on most small business loans, which bypasses the liability shield (Lesson 3.1).
- Read the reporting and repayment terms before you sign.

## 3. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Neha's jewellery business (Jaipur, sole proprietorship, Udyam-registered) needs ₹4 lakh for a kiln and stock. Her bank offers a **Mudra Kishore** loan with no collateral at the bank's rate; she shows two years of GST returns and her plan. A Calgary robotics startup with a working prototype gets **IRAP** support for an engineering project and claims **SR&ED** credits on its research salaries. A Miami bakery with two years of profits takes an **SBA 7(a)** loan through its bank for an oven.
