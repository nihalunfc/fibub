**Module 8 · about 15 minutes · USA.** You'll know how each US structure is taxed and when an S corp election or a C corp makes sense.

<div class="bigidea">In the USA the structure decides the tax: pass-through owners pay income tax plus 15.3 % self-employment tax, S corp owners pay payroll tax only on a reasonable salary, and C corps pay 21 % with dividends taxed again.</div>

## 1. How each structure files

| Structure | Federal return | Who pays |
|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietor, single-member LLC | Schedule C and Schedule SE with your Form 1040 | You: income tax + self-employment tax |
| Partnership, multi-member LLC | Form 1065 (due March 15) and a K-1 to each partner | Partners, on their share |
| S corporation (an election by a corporation or LLC) | Form 1120-S (March 15) and K-1s | Owners; the company runs payroll for owner salaries |
| C corporation | Form 1120 (April 15 for a calendar year) | The company at 21 %; owners again on dividends |

## 2. Self-employment tax and the QBI deduction

Sole proprietors and LLC owners pay **self-employment tax**: 15.3 % on 92.35 % of profit (12.4 % Social Security up to the $184,500 wage base in 2026, plus 2.9 % Medicare on everything, and 0.9 % more over $200,000). Half of it is deductible.

The **qualified business income (QBI) deduction**, made permanent in 2025, lets pass-through owners deduct up to 20 % of their business income. Above an income limit, it is restricted for service businesses (consulting, law, health and similar) and depends on wages paid and property owned.

## 3. S corp: salary plus distributions

An S corp owner who works in the business must take a **reasonable salary** through payroll; payroll taxes apply to that salary only. Profit above it comes out as **distributions**, free of the 15.3 %. Income tax is the same either way. The saving is real once profit is well above a fair salary, but it brings payroll, a separate return and often state costs. S corps allow at most 100 shareholders, all US individuals (and some trusts), with one class of stock, so they do not fit venture-funded startups.

## 4. C corp: the venture choice

Investors expect a **Delaware C corporation** (Lesson 3.4). It pays 21 % federal tax on profit, and dividends are taxed again (0 %, 15 % or 20 %). Most startups reinvest and pay no dividends for years. The big tax benefit is at exit: shares that qualify as **qualified small business stock (QSBS, section 1202)**, held long enough, can make most or all of the gain on sale tax-free (from 2025, partial exclusion from 3 years and 100 % from 5 years, up to $15 million per company for newer stock). Get advice when you issue founder shares so they qualify.

## 5. Estimated tax and state tax

Nobody withholds from profit, so pay **estimated tax** on April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15. You avoid a penalty if you pay at least 90 % of this year's tax or 100 % of last year's (110 % if last year's income was over $150,000). Most states also tax business income (nine states have no tax on wages), and some charge LLCs or corporations a yearly fee or franchise tax.

## 6. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Jordan's single-member LLC in Texas makes $120,000 profit. Federal self-employment tax is about $17,000 and income tax about $11,500. After an S corp election with a $60,000 salary, payroll tax on the salary is $9,180 and income tax about $14,100, a saving of roughly $5,000, against about $2,000 of extra payroll and accounting costs. Texas has no personal income tax, but its franchise tax report is still due (no tax below the revenue threshold).

<div class="myth">

**"An LLC is a tax type."** An LLC is a legal structure. For tax it is a sole proprietor, a partnership, an S corp or a C corp, depending on owners and elections.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Single-member LLCs file Schedule C and SE; multi-member LLCs file Form 1065 by March 15.
- Self-employment tax: 15.3 % on 92.35 % of profit, Social Security part up to $184,500 (2026); half is deductible.
- S corp owners take a reasonable salary through payroll; distributions above it avoid payroll tax.
- C corps pay 21 % federal; QSBS can make founders’ gains on sale largely tax-free.
- Estimated tax on April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15; safe harbor 90 % this year or 100 % (110 %) of last year.
