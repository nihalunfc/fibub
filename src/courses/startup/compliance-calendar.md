**Module 12 · about 10 minutes.** You'll turn every deadline from this course into one calendar with reminders and an owner for each date.

<div class="bigidea">Compliance is mostly a rhythm: a few dates every month, a few each quarter, a handful each year. Put them all in one calendar, give each an owner, and set reminders before they are due.</div>

## 1. The rhythm

| How often | India | Canada | USA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly | TDS (7th), GSTR-1 (11th), PF and ESI (15th), GSTR-3B (20th) | Payroll remittance (15th) | Payroll deposits (15th), many state sales tax returns (20th) |
| Quarterly | Advance tax, TDS returns | GST/HST returns (if quarterly), tax instalments | Form 941, estimated tax |
| Yearly | AGM, AOC-4, MGT-7, ITR, GSTR-9 | T4 and T5 (end of February), T2, annual return | W-2 and 1099 (January 31), 1120, state report |

## 2. Build yours

<div class="su-steps">

Set up your compliance calendar

1. Use the builder above, then add your own dates: licence renewals, insurance renewals, lease and contract notice dates, trademark renewals.
2. Import the .ics file into a shared calendar (Google, Outlook, Apple), not one person's phone.
3. Give each date an owner, and a backup.
4. Set a reminder a few days before; set two for tax payments.
5. Once a year, check the dates against the official calendars and your year-end.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

Most months have four dates: **TDS** payment by the 7th, **GSTR-1** by the 11th, **PF and ESI** by the 15th, **GSTR-3B** and GST payment by the 20th. Small taxpayers under ₹5 crore can choose the **QRMP** scheme (quarterly returns, monthly payments). Advance tax falls on 15 June, 15 September, 15 December and 15 March.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

Regular payroll remitters pay source deductions by the **15th** of the next month. GST/HST returns are annual, quarterly or monthly depending on sales (up to C$1.5 million, up to C$6 million, above). Corporations pay monthly or quarterly **instalments** once tax owed passes C$3,000; T4s and T5s are due by the **last day of February**.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

Most new employers deposit payroll tax **monthly, by the 15th** of the next month, and file **Form 941** each quarter. State sales tax returns are monthly, quarterly or annual by state and volume. Estimated tax for a calendar-year corporation is due on 15 April, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December. W-2s and 1099-NECs are due **January 31**.

</div>

## 3. Worked example

*Made-up founders.* An online tea shop in Jaipur is GST-registered, has 3 employees and is a private company. Its calendar has 4 monthly dates (TDS, GSTR-1, PF and ESI, GSTR-3B), 4 advance tax dates and about 6 yearly filings: roughly 58 dates a year. Using a payroll app for TDS and PF, and an accountant for GST and annual filings, the founders own only two: checking each month that the others were done, and the AGM.

<div class="myth">

**"My accountant handles all the deadlines."** Your accountant files what you send them, when you send it. Missing invoices or a late payroll file still makes the return late, and the penalty is yours. Keep your own calendar.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Compliance runs on a monthly, quarterly and yearly rhythm.
- India: TDS 7th, GSTR-1 11th, PF/ESI 15th, GSTR-3B 20th; Canada: payroll by the 15th, GST/HST by sales band; USA: payroll deposits by the 15th, Form 941 quarterly.
- Put every date in a shared calendar with an owner and a reminder.
- Add licences, insurance, leases, domains and trademarks, not just tax.
- Check the dates against the official calendars once a year.
