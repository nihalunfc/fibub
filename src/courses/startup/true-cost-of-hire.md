**Module 9 · about 11 minutes.** You'll work out what a hire costs in full, and compare it with a contractor before you commit.

<div class="bigidea">An employee costs about 1.2 to 1.4 times their salary once payroll contributions, benefits, tools, space and recruiting are added; plan runway on the full cost.</div>

## 1. More than salary

Every employee costs you their salary plus:

1. **Payroll contributions:** PF, ESI and gratuity (India); CPP, EI and vacation pay (Canada); FICA, FUTA and state unemployment (USA). Lessons 9.2 to 9.4.
2. **Insurance:** workers' compensation (Canada, USA); group health insurance, which in the USA is often the second biggest cost.
3. **Benefits** you choose to offer: health, dental, retirement matching, learning budget.
4. **Tools:** laptop, software seats, phone.
5. **Space:** a desk, co-working seat or home-office allowance.
6. **Recruiting:** job ads, recruiter fees (often 15 % to 25 % of first-year salary), your own interview time.
7. **Ramp-up:** new hires are rarely fully productive for the first one to three months.

## 2. Employee or contractor, by cost

A contractor's day rate looks high because they cover their own taxes, benefits, tools, holidays and gaps between contracts. Compare like with like: the employee's full yearly cost against the contractor's rate × the days you really need. For a short project, the contractor is usually cheaper; for steady, core work, the employee usually is (and contractors in that role may really be employees, Lesson 9.1).

> **Key idea:** Add the full cost of each planned hire to your runway model (Lesson 2.5) before you post the job. Many first-time founders plan on salary alone and run out of cash months early.

## 3. Making a hire affordable

- **Government programmes:** wage subsidies for students and young workers (Canada Summer Jobs, provincial programmes; in India, apprenticeship schemes such as NAPS; US Work Opportunity Tax Credit for some groups). Check eligibility before hiring.
- **Part-time or fixed-term** to start, with the same legal protections.
- **Equity** (Module 10) in place of part of the salary, for early team members who share the risk.
- **Hire in the order of need:** the role that frees the founder's time for selling usually pays back first.

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Diego plans a $70,000 analyst in Toronto. Vacation pay, CPP and EI add about $8,500; benefits $3,000; laptop and software $3,000; a co-working desk $4,800; a $6,000 job ad and agency fee spread over two years, $3,000. True cost: about **$92,300 a year**, 1.32 times the salary: $7,700 a month of runway. A contractor at $450 a day for 220 days would cost $99,000, so for a permanent role the hire is cheaper.

<div class="myth">

**"A contractor at $450 a day is far more expensive than a $70,000 employee."** Compare full costs: the employee costs about $92,000 with on-costs, tools and space. For a full year the gap is small, and for a 3-month project the contractor is far cheaper.

</div>

## 5. Practice

1. List four costs of a hire beyond salary.
2. Why compare a contractor's rate with the employee's full cost, not their salary?
3. Your runway is 14 months with a $50,000 hire at 1.3 times. What happens if you plan on salary only?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Payroll contributions, benefits, tools, space, recruiting, insurance, ramp-up time.
2. The contractor's rate already includes the taxes, benefits and tools the employer would pay for an employee.
3. You under-count burn by $15,000 a year, so cash runs out about a month or more earlier than planned.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- True cost = salary + payroll contributions + insurance + benefits + tools + space + recruiting.
- Typical multiples: 1.2 to 1.4 times salary in Canada and the USA (higher with US health insurance); India adds PF, gratuity and insurance.
- Contractors cost more per day but carry their own taxes and tools; compare yearly totals for the days you need.
- Add every planned hire’s full cost to your runway model before you hire.
- Wage subsidies, part-time starts and equity can make early hires affordable.
