**Module 12 · about 12 minutes.** You'll know every yearly filing your structure needs, when it is due and what a missed one costs.

<div class="bigidea">Registering the business is a one-time job; staying registered is a yearly one. A few forms a year keep the company alive, the liability shield up and investors happy.</div>

## 1. Two kinds of yearly filing

1. **Registry filings** keep the company itself in existence: accounts and an annual return to the corporate registry (MCA, Corporations Canada or your province, the US state).
2. **Tax filings** report the year's income to the tax agency (Income Tax India, CRA, IRS and state tax departments), even when there was no profit.

Miss the first and the company can be **struck off** or **dissolved**; miss the second and penalties and interest pile up. In both cases you lose **good standing**, which banks, investors and other states check before they deal with you.

## 2. Your country

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

A private company holds four board meetings a year (two for small companies and OPCs) and an **AGM** by **30 September**. Within 30 days of the AGM it files audited accounts on **AOC-4**, within 60 days its annual return on **MGT-7** (small companies and OPCs use the shorter **MGT-7A**), plus **ADT-1** when an auditor is appointed. Late fees are **₹100 a day per form** with no cap. Two years of missed returns can lead to the company being struck off and its directors disqualified for 5 years. An **LLP** files **Form 11** by 30 May and **Form 8** by 30 October.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

A federal corporation files an **annual return** with Corporations Canada within 60 days after each incorporation anniversary ($12 online), keeps its **register of individuals with significant control** up to date, and files the **T2** within 6 months of its year-end. Provinces add their own: Ontario corporations file an annual return through the **Ontario Business Registry**. A federal corporation that misses a year of returns can be dissolved. Keep directors' and shareholders' resolutions approving the accounts in the minute book.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

Each state sets its own report. **Delaware** corporations file an annual report and pay **franchise tax by March 1** (at least $175, or $400 under the assumed par value method, plus a $50 report fee); Delaware **LLCs** pay **$300 by June 1**. **California** charges $800 a year minimum franchise tax and a Statement of Information. Federal returns: **1120** by April 15 for a calendar-year C corp, **1120-S** and **1065** by March 15. Since FinCEN's March 2025 rule, US-formed companies no longer file beneficial ownership (BOI) reports; foreign companies registered in a state still do.

</div>

## 3. Make it routine

- Pick one month each year to close the books and file (with your accountant).
- Keep a minute book: resolutions, registers, share certificates.
- Renew the registered agent and any licences on the same day.
- Download a certificate of good standing once a year to check.

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founders.* A Delaware C corporation run from Seattle has 10 million authorised shares. Its first franchise tax bill under the authorised shares method shows about $85,000, which panics the founders. Their accountant recalculates under the **assumed par value capital method** using the company's gross assets, and the tax drops to the $400 minimum. They file by March 1, pay $450 with the report fee, and also file Washington's annual report for their foreign registration there.

<div class="myth">

**"If the company does nothing, there is nothing to file."** Dormant or not, a company files every year until it is formally closed (Lesson 12.5). Late fees and penalties keep growing even with zero sales.

</div>

## 5. Practice

1. Name the two Indian company forms filed after the AGM.
2. Within how many days of its anniversary does a Canadian federal corporation file its annual return?
3. Why does good standing matter when you raise money?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. AOC-4 (financial statements) and MGT-7 or MGT-7A (annual return).
2. 60 days.
3. Investors and their lawyers check it; a company not in good standing may not be able to sign contracts or issue shares cleanly.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Every company files with its registry and its tax agency every year, even with no sales.
- India: AGM by 30 September, AOC-4 within 30 days, MGT-7 within 60 days, ₹100 a day per form if late; LLP Forms 11 and 8.
- Canada: federal annual return within 60 days of the anniversary, provincial returns, T2 within 6 months.
- USA: state annual report and franchise tax (Delaware March 1), federal 1120, 1120-S or 1065.
- Missed filings cost good standing and can end the company.

</div>
