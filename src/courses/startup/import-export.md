**Module 5 · about 16 minutes.** You'll register to import or export, find the duty on a product, and work out its true landed cost.

<div class="bigidea">Every shipment across a border needs the right code, value and origin, and may owe duty and import tax. Price from the landed cost, and know your importer or exporter number.</div>

## 1. The basics everywhere

- **HS code:** every product has a Harmonized System code (the first 6 digits are global). It decides the duty rate and any permits.
- **Duty:** a tax on imports, a percentage of the customs value; trade agreements can reduce it to zero if the goods qualify by origin.
- **Import tax:** GST, IGST or similar is charged at the border (not in the USA) and registered businesses usually get it back.
- **Incoterms** (EXW, FOB, CIF, DDP) say who pays shipping, insurance and duties. Agree them in writing.
- **Customs brokers** file entries for you; most small importers use one, or a courier's brokerage service.

<!--fig:landed-cost-->

## 2. By country

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

Get an **IEC** from the DGFT portal (online, linked to your PAN; a small fee). Register your bank's **AD code** at the ports you export from. For exports, file a **LUT** on the GST portal each financial year to export without paying IGST; exports of services are zero-rated if paid in foreign currency (or permitted INR). Import duty is Basic Customs Duty plus a Social Welfare Surcharge, and **IGST** that you can claim back as input tax credit. Foreign payments follow **FEMA** rules through your bank (export proceeds must come back within the period the RBI allows).

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

Add an **RM** (import-export) program account to your Business Number, then register in **CARM** (the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management portal), which has been mandatory for commercial importers since October 2024; post security or pay at the time of release. GST is charged on imports and is recoverable as an input tax credit. Goods from the US or Mexico can be duty-free under **CUSMA** with proof of origin. Exports to most countries need an export declaration over $2,000 (or for controlled goods).

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

Importers use their **EIN** as the importer of record number (registered with CBP on Form 5106, usually by your broker). Formal entries (over $2,500, or for certain goods) need a **customs bond**. Duties come from the **Harmonized Tariff Schedule** plus any extra tariffs on goods from certain countries, which changed many times in 2025, so check before you order. The $800 **de minimis** exemption was suspended for most shipments from 29 August 2025. Some products need agency approval (FDA, FCC, CPSC, USDA). Exports over $2,500 per HS line, or licensed goods, are filed in AES.

</div>

> **Key idea:** Duty is a cost; import GST is usually not (for registered businesses). Price your product on the landed cost, and check tariffs again before every big order.

## 3. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* A Toronto startup imports 200 sensor kits from Taiwan: C$2,000 value (converted at the customs exchange rate), C$300 freight, duty 6.5 %. Duty is on the value without the international freight: ≈ $130. GST 5 % on value plus duty ≈ $107 (recoverable). Landed cost ≈ $2,537, or ≈ $12.68 per kit (≈ $12.15 after claiming the GST back). Their broker files the entry in CARM under their RM account.

<div class="myth">

**"Import GST or tax is a cost just like duty."** For a registered business, import GST is usually claimed back as an input tax credit. Duty is the real cost to price in.

</div>

## 4. Practice

1. What decides the duty rate on a product?
2. Which Canadian portal must commercial importers register in?
3. An Indian SaaS company bills a US client in dollars. Does it charge 18 % GST?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Its HS code (tariff classification), country of origin and any trade agreement or extra tariffs.
2. CARM, the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management portal.
3. Usually no: it is an export of services, zero-rated if the conditions are met; it files a LUT to export without paying IGST.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- India: IEC from DGFT; customs on ICEGATE; export earnings repatriated through your bank.
- Canada: RM account on your BN; CARM for duties; GST on imports.
- USA: EIN as importer number; CBP; tariffs change often.
- Classify every product with an HS code and check rules of origin.
- Price from the landed cost and recheck tariffs before big orders.
