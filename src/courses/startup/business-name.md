**Module 4 · about 13 minutes.** You'll choose a name that is likely to be accepted, check it properly, and know how to reserve it.

## 1. What makes a good name

- **Distinctive:** a coined word (Zomato, Shopify) or an unexpected word, plus a descriptive word if needed ("Kestrel Analytics").
- **Easy:** to say, spell and type; works in the languages your customers speak.
- **Available:** as a company name, a trademark, a domain and social handles.
- **Not misleading:** it shouldn't suggest a government link, a bank or a licence you don't have.

<!--fig:name-check-->

> **Key idea:** Four checks, one name: the **company registry**, the **trademark register**, the **domain**, and **social handles**. A registry accepting your name is not permission to use it as a brand if someone owns the trademark.

## 2. The rules by country

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

Names are approved by the MCA's Central Registration Centre. Rule 8 of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014 refuses names that are identical or too similar to existing companies, LLPs or registered trademarks, and restricts words such as "Bank", "Insurance", "Board", "Commission", "National", "Republic" or "Bharat" without approval. A private company must end with "**Private Limited**", an OPC with "**(OPC) Private Limited**", and an LLP with "**LLP**". Search with "Check company/LLP name" on the MCA portal, then reserve through **SPICe+ Part A** (or the separate RUN service), valid for 20 days for a new company.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

A corporate name needs a distinctive element, a descriptive element and a legal ending (**Inc., Ltd., Corp.**, or the French Ltée, Inc., Corp.). Federally, Corporations Canada checks a **NUANS** search report (a few dollars from a NUANS provider) or its own name search; you can also skip the name and incorporate as a "numbered company" (such as 1234567 Canada Inc.) and add an operating name later. Provinces run their own checks (Ontario also asks for a NUANS report for named corporations). Sole proprietors register a business name with the province if it isn't their own legal name.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

Each **state** checks only its own records, so the same name can exist in 50 states. Search the Secretary of State's business entity database, then the **USPTO** trademark database (federal trademarks apply nationwide). An LLC name needs "LLC" or "Limited Liability Company"; a corporation needs "Inc.", "Corp." or similar. Words like "Bank", "Insurance" or "Trust" need regulator approval. Most states let you reserve a name for a fee (about 30 to 120 days).

</div>

## 3. A careful search, in order

<div class="su-steps">

Checking a name

1. List 5 to 10 candidates; drop any that are only common words.
2. Search the company registry (MCA / NUANS or provincial registry / your Secretary of State).
3. Search trademarks in every country you plan to sell in (IP India, CIPO, USPTO), in your class of goods or services.
4. Search domains (.in, .ca, .com) and social handles.
5. Say it out loud to five people; ask them to spell it.
6. Reserve the name with the registry and buy the domain the same day.

</div>

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founders.* A Pune team likes "SmartClinic". The MCA search shows "Smart Clinic Solutions Private Limited" already exists, and a trademark "SMARTCLINIC" is registered in class 9. They try "Clinivo": no company, no trademark in classes 9 and 42, clinivo.in available. They reserve "Clinivo Health Private Limited" in SPICe+ Part A.

## 5. Practice

1. Turn "Quality Plumbing" into a name more likely to be accepted.
2. What is a Canadian "numbered company"?
3. Why can the same company name exist in Texas and Ohio?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Add a distinctive element: "Brannigan Quality Plumbing Ltd." or a coined word like "Pipewise Plumbing".
2. A corporation registered with a number instead of a name (for example 1234567 Ontario Inc.), often used for holding companies or to incorporate quickly.
3. Each state checks only its own register. A federal trademark is what protects a brand nationwide.
</details>
