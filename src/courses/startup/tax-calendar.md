<div class="bigidea">A founder has four kinds of tax deadline: sales tax returns, tax paid during the year, yearly returns and payroll. Put them all in one calendar and fund a tax account as you go.</div>

## 1. Four kinds of deadline

1. **Sales tax returns** (GST, GST/HST, state sales tax): monthly, quarterly or yearly.
2. **Paying income tax during the year:** advance tax (India), instalments (Canada), estimated tax (USA).
3. **Yearly income tax returns** for you and, if you have one, your company.
4. **Payroll:** sending deductions every month and year-end slips (once you hire, Module 9).

<!--fig:tax-calendar-->

## 2. A system that works

<div class="su-steps">

Never miss a date

1. Download your calendar above and put every date in your phone calendar with two reminders: one week and two days before.
2. Open a separate savings account for tax. Move the sales tax you collect and a share of each payment (20 % to 30 % to start) into it every time you are paid.
3. Do your bookkeeping monthly, before the earliest monthly return, so every return is a 30-minute job.
4. Pay electronically from the business account and save the confirmation with the return.
5. Once a year, after your return, check next year's dates and adjust the amounts you set aside.

</div>

> **Key idea:** Late filing usually costs more than late payment. If you cannot pay, still file on time and pay what you can: penalties for not filing are added on top of interest.

## 3. What lateness costs

| | Late filing | Late payment |
|---|---|---|
| India | GST: ₹50 a day (₹20 nil) up to a cap; ITR: up to ₹5,000 fee (₹1,000 if income ≤ ₹5 lakh) | 18 % a year interest on GST; 1 % a month on income tax and short advance tax |
| Canada | 5 % of the balance plus 1 % a month (up to 12 months); GST/HST 1 % plus 0.25 % a month | Daily compound interest at the prescribed rate |
| USA | 5 % of unpaid tax a month (up to 25 %) | 0.5 % a month plus interest; underpayment penalty on estimates |

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Priya, a Bengaluru consultant on 44ADA, expects tax of ₹2 lakh. She pays it all by 15 March as presumptive tax allows, files ITR-4 by 31 July, and files GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B quarterly under QRMP with monthly payments. Her phone calendar holds 18 dates for the year; a recurring transfer moves 25 % of each client payment into her tax account.

<div class="myth">

**"If I cannot pay, I should wait to file until I can."** Filing late adds a separate penalty on top of interest. File on time with whatever you can pay, then arrange the rest.

</div>

## 5. Practice

1. Which deadlines does a US single-member LLC with no employees face?
2. Why does a Canadian sole proprietor's tax bill often surprise them in the first year, and again in the second?
3. In India, by when must presumptive (44AD/44ADA) taxpayers pay advance tax?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Estimated tax (April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15), Form 1040 with Schedule C and SE by April 15, state income tax and any state sales tax returns.
2. First year: no tax was withheld, so the whole year's tax plus CPP is due on April 30. Second year: instalment reminders start once the tax owing was over $3,000.
3. All of it by 15 March.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- India: GSTR-1 by the 11th, GSTR-3B by the 20th; advance tax 15/45/75/100 % on 15 June, September, December, March; ITR by 31 July (31 October if audited).
- Canada: sole proprietor instalments quarterly over $3,000 owing; pay by April 30, file by June 15; T4s by the end of February.
- USA: estimated tax April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15; Form 1040 April 15; 1065 and 1120-S March 15; W-2 and 1099-NEC January 31.
- Late filing usually costs more than late payment: always file on time.
- A separate tax savings account funded from every payment prevents surprises.

</div>
