**Module 3 · about 14 minutes.** You'll know how a sole proprietorship works in your country, what to register and when it stops being the right choice.

<div class="bigidea">A sole proprietorship is you doing business: no separate legal person, so the profit is your income and the debts are your debts. It is the simplest start and fine while the risk is low.</div>

## 1. What it is

A **sole proprietorship** is one person doing business in their own name or a trade name. There is <mark>no separate legal person</mark>: the business's income is your income, its contracts are your contracts and its debts are your debts.

**Good:** cheapest and fastest to start, least paperwork, you keep all profit, losses can reduce your other taxable income.

**Not so good:** unlimited personal liability, harder to raise investment (there are no shares to sell), and it ends when you stop.

<!--fig:liability-shield-->

> **Key idea:** "Register a sole proprietorship" usually means registering the things around it (a trade name, tax accounts, licences), not the business itself.

## 2. What you register

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

There is no central registration for a proprietorship. It trades on your **PAN**. To prove the business exists (for a current account, payment gateway or government tender) you collect two or three of: **GST registration** (compulsory above the threshold, Lesson 7.2), **Udyam (MSME) registration** (free, online, with Aadhaar and PAN), a **Shops and Establishments** registration from your state, a municipal trade licence. Business income is taxed with your personal income at slab rates; small businesses can use the presumptive scheme (Lesson 8.2).

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

If you trade under a name other than your own legal name, register it with your **province** (Ontario: through the Ontario Business Registry, $60 online, renewed every 5 years). Get a **Business Number** from the CRA when you need a GST/HST, payroll or import-export account. Report income on your T1 return with form **T2125**, and pay both halves of CPP contributions on your self-employment earnings.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

No state filing is needed if you use your own legal name. To use another name, file a **DBA** ("doing business as", also called an assumed or fictitious name) with your county or state. You can use your Social Security number for tax, but a free **EIN** from the IRS keeps your SSN off invoices and is required once you hire or file excise returns. Report profit on **Schedule C** and pay **self-employment tax** (Schedule SE).

</div>

Every country: add any **licences** your activity needs (Module 5) and open a **separate bank account** (Lesson 6.1), even though the law treats it as yours.

## 3. When to move on

Consider a company, LLP or LLC when:

- You take on **real risk**: staff, a lease, loans, physical products, big contracts.
- **Profit is high** and you'd like to leave some in the business at a lower tax rate (Canada especially, Lesson 8.3).
- You want to **raise investment** or bring in co-owners.
- Big clients insist on dealing with a registered company.

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Meera, a freelance translator in Mississauga, trades as "Meera Iyer" from 2026. She needs no business name registration. In her second year she earns C$38,000 in four quarters, crossing C$30,000, so she registers for GST/HST and charges 13 % HST to Ontario clients. She opens a business chequing account and files T2125 with her T1 return. When she starts hiring other translators, she plans to incorporate.

<div class="myth">

**"Registering a business name gives me limited liability."** A name registration or DBA only records the name. As a sole proprietor you are still personally liable for every debt; only a company, LLP or LLC creates a shield.

</div>

## 5. Practice

1. Name two ways an Indian proprietor can prove the business exists to a bank.
2. A Texas cake maker trades as "Sweet Spot Bakes". What should they file?
3. True or false: a sole proprietor's business losses can reduce their other income.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Any two of: GST certificate, Udyam certificate, Shops and Establishments registration, trade licence (banks list what they accept).
2. An assumed name certificate (DBA) with the county clerk, plus any food permits (Texas cottage food rules cover some home bakers).
3. True in general in all three countries, subject to rules on hobby activities and loss limits.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- No separate legal person: business income, contracts and debts are yours.
- Register the things around it: trade name, tax accounts, licences.
- Losses usually reduce your other income; profits are taxed at your personal rates.
- Move on when you take real risk, profit is high, or you want investors.
- Insurance helps, but does not replace limited liability.

</div>
