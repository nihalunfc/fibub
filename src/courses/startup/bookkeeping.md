**Module 6 · about 15 minutes.** You'll understand what bookkeeping records, read the three main reports, and set up a routine that keeps tax time calm.

<div class="bigidea">Every money event has two sides, and profit and cash move separately: record each sale, cost and transfer, close the month, and read the three reports.</div>

## 1. Every event, two sides

Bookkeeping records each money event. **Double entry** means every event changes two things: a sale raises cash *and* revenue; buying a laptop lowers cash *and* raises equipment. That is why the books always balance:

```math
\text{assets} = \text{liabilities} + \text{equity}
```

<!--fig:ledger-sim-->

> **Key idea:** Profit and cash move separately. Invoices count as revenue before they are paid; equipment is spread over years; tax you collect is owed, not earned.

## 2. Cash or accrual

- **Cash basis:** record income when paid and expenses when paid. Simple; allowed for many small businesses (US small businesses; Canadian farmers and fishers; some Indian small taxpayers under presumptive schemes).
- **Accrual basis:** record income when earned and expenses when incurred. Required for companies in India and for most Canadian businesses; gives a truer picture.

## 3. The three reports

| Report | Shows | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| Profit and loss (income statement) | Revenue minus expenses over a period | Did we make money? |
| Balance sheet | Assets, liabilities, equity on a date | What do we own and owe? |
| Cash flow statement | Cash in and out by type | Where did the cash go? |

## 4. A month-end routine

<div class="su-check" data-name="Month-end bookkeeping routine">

Month-end routine (30 to 60 minutes)

- [ ] Record or import every bank and card transaction
- [ ] Attach a receipt or invoice to each expense
- [ ] Reconcile: the books' bank balance equals the bank statement
- [ ] Check unpaid invoices and chase late payers
- [ ] Move collected sales tax and estimated income tax to the tax account
- [ ] Record payroll and any owner payments correctly
- [ ] Review the profit and loss against your plan

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

Companies must keep books on the accrual, double-entry basis at the registered office, and since April 2023 accounting software must keep an **audit trail** (edit log) that can't be switched off. GST returns depend on accurate invoice records each month.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

Most businesses use accrual accounting for tax. Equipment is deducted through **capital cost allowance (CCA)** classes. Keep records in Canada (or electronically accessible from Canada) for six years.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

Small businesses can usually use the cash method. Equipment can often be expensed at once using **section 179** or bonus depreciation (100 % bonus depreciation was restored for property acquired after 19 January 2025).

</div>

## 5. Worked example

Use the simulator above: owner invests, a cash sale, an invoice, rent, a laptop, then remitting tax. Notice after "Buy a laptop" that cash falls a lot but profit doesn't move, and after "Invoice sent" that profit rises with no cash.

<div class="myth">

**"If there is money in the bank, the business is making a profit."** The bank holds tax you owe, deposits, loans and unpaid bills too. Profit comes from the profit and loss, not the balance.

</div>

## 6. Practice

1. Equation that always balances?
2. Name the three main reports.
3. Why reconcile the bank every month?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Assets = liabilities + equity.
2. Profit and loss, balance sheet, cash flow statement.
3. To catch missing or duplicate entries, bank errors and fraud early, while you still remember the details.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Every event has two sides; the books always balance.
- Accrual counts revenue when invoiced; cash basis when paid.
- Three reports: profit and loss, balance sheet, cash flow.
- Month-end: reconcile the bank, chase unpaid invoices, file receipts, check tax owed.
- Equipment is spread over years; collected tax is owed, not earned.

</div>
