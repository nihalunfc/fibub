**Module 3 · about 15 minutes.** You'll be able to tell a general partnership from an LLP, see how each is taxed, and list what a partnership agreement covers.

<div class="bigidea">Partners share profit and decisions; in a general partnership they also share unlimited liability. An LLP keeps the partnership feel with limited liability, and a written agreement prevents most fights.</div>

## 1. Three kinds of partnership

- **General partnership.** Two or more people sharing profit and running the business together. Simple, but <mark>every partner is personally liable for all the debts</mark>.
- **Limited partnership (LP).** At least one general partner with unlimited liability, plus "limited" partners who invest but don't manage and risk only what they put in. Used for funds and property deals.
- **Limited liability partnership (LLP).** Partners manage the business but are not personally liable for its debts (or for other partners' mistakes). Rules on who can form one differ a lot by country.

<!--fig:partner-split-->

> **Key idea:** A partnership is taxed "through" the partners: the partnership usually files an information return, and each partner pays tax on their share. India's LLPs are the main exception: an Indian LLP pays its own tax.

## 2. By country

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

- **Partnership firm** under the Indian Partnership Act, 1932, with a written partnership deed (stamped under state law). Registration with the state Registrar of Firms is optional, but an **unregistered firm cannot sue** outsiders to enforce a contract, so register. The firm gets its own PAN and is taxed at 30 % (plus surcharge and cess) on profit; partners' share of profit is then tax-free in their hands, and salary or interest paid to partners within limits is deductible for the firm.
- **LLP** under the LLP Act, 2008: registered with the MCA using form **FiLLiP**, with at least **two designated partners**, one of them **resident in India**, each holding a DPIN/DIN and a digital signature. The LLP agreement is filed within 30 days (Form 3). Each year it files a Statement of Account and Solvency (Form 8) and an Annual Return (Form 11). Tax is like a firm's: 30 % plus cess (and surcharge above ₹1 crore). No minimum capital.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

Partnerships are set by **provincial** law (for example Ontario's Partnerships Act). Register the partnership name with the province. The partnership itself does not pay income tax: each partner reports their share on their own return (individuals on form T2125). Partnerships above certain size tests must file a **T5013** information return. A partnership needs its own Business Number for GST/HST if it is registered. **LLPs** are mostly limited to professions (law, accounting) allowed by their regulators.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

Partnerships are set by **state** law; a general partnership often needs no filing except a DBA for its name. LPs and LLPs file with the Secretary of State. Federally, the partnership files **Form 1065** and gives each partner a **Schedule K-1**; partners pay tax (and usually self-employment tax) on their share. Most US founders who want a partnership with limited liability choose a **multi-member LLC** instead, which is taxed the same way by default.

</div>

## 3. The partnership agreement

Write it before you start, even with a best friend:

<div class="su-check" data-name="Partnership or LLP agreement checklist">

Partnership agreement checklist

- [ ] Names, business name and what the business does
- [ ] How much each partner contributes (money, equipment, time)
- [ ] Profit and loss shares, and any salary or interest to partners
- [ ] Who decides what; which decisions need everyone's agreement
- [ ] Bank signing authority
- [ ] Rules on new partners joining
- [ ] What happens if a partner leaves, dies or wants out (buy-out price formula)
- [ ] How disputes are settled (mediation, arbitration)
- [ ] Non-compete and confidentiality
- [ ] Who owns intellectual property made for the business

</div>

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founders.* Three designers in Bengaluru, Arjun, Bina and Chirag, want to work together. A partnership firm would make each of them liable for a client lawsuit against any of them. They form an **LLP** instead: FiLLiP filing with the MCA, Arjun and Bina as designated partners (both resident), profit split 40/35/25 in the LLP agreement filed in Form 3. When they later want outside investors, they plan to convert to a private limited company (Lesson 3.7).

<div class="myth">

**"A partnership needs no paperwork."** It needs no registration to exist, which is the danger. Without a written agreement, the law fills the gaps with equal shares and unlimited joint liability.

</div>

## 5. Practice

1. What is the main legal risk of a general partnership?
2. How is a US multi-member LLC taxed by default?
3. Why should an Indian partnership firm register with the Registrar of Firms?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Unlimited, shared personal liability: each partner can be made to pay all the partnership's debts.
2. As a partnership: Form 1065 and a K-1 for each member.
3. An unregistered firm cannot sue third parties to enforce contracts (section 69 of the 1932 Act).
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- General partners are each liable for all the debts.
- India: partnership firms (register to be able to sue) and LLPs via FiLLiP with two designated partners, one resident.
- Canada: LLPs are mainly for regulated professions; most others use a partnership or corporation.
- USA: multi-member LLCs are taxed as partnerships by default (Form 1065, K-1s).
- The agreement covers money in, profit shares, decisions, leaving, disputes and IP.

</div>
