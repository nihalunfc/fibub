**Module 4 · about 13 minutes.** You'll set up a proper business address, domain and email, show the details the law requires, and know how long to keep every record.

## 1. Which address

- **Home:** allowed in many cases, but check your lease, housing society or homeowners' rules and city zoning (Lesson 5.3), and remember it becomes public in registries.
- **Virtual office or coworking:** gives a business address and mail handling. Accepted as a registered office if it is a real street address you're entitled to use and the registry's rules are met.
- **Never a PO box** for a registered office.

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

The registered office needs proof (rent agreement or ownership papers, a utility bill under two months old, and a no-objection certificate from the owner). The MCA may verify it physically. GST also needs proof of each place of business.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

The registered office must be in the province of incorporation (federal: in the province named in the articles), and corporate records are kept there.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

The registered agent's address is public; many founders use the agent's address to keep their home address off state records. Some states also require a principal office address.

</div>

## 2. What must appear on your paperwork

- **India:** company name, CIN, registered office address, phone, email and website on letterheads, invoices and the website (Companies Act section 12); GSTIN on tax invoices.
- **Canada:** the full legal name (or registered business name) on contracts, invoices and the website; GST/HST number on invoices over $30 when claiming credits (Lesson 7.6).
- **USA:** the full legal name with "LLC" or "Inc." on contracts so you're signing for the company, not personally.

> **Key idea:** Sign contracts as the company: "Clinivo Inc., by Lena Park, CEO". A signature with just your name can make you personally liable.

## 3. Domain and email

Buy the domain the day you reserve the name (.in, .ca, .com; ideally the .com too). Use a business email on that domain, not a free webmail address. Register the domain **in the company's name** (or transfer it once the company exists) so it is a business asset.

## 5. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Meera's Ontario corporation closes its year on 31 December 2026. Its receipts, invoices and bank statements for 2026 must be kept until at least 31 December 2032. She scans paper receipts into a folder by month with an online backup, which the CRA accepts if the scans are complete and readable.

## 6. Practice

2. Why should the domain be registered in the company's name?
3. A US business filed its 2026 return on 15 April 2027. Until when should it normally keep the supporting records?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Any two of: letterhead, invoices and bills, official notices, the website.
2. So the domain belongs to the business: it moves with a sale of the company and can't be lost if a founder leaves.
3. At least until 15 April 2030 (3 years), longer for employment tax records (4 years) and in the special cases.
</details>
