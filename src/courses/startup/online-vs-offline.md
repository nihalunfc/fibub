**Module 1 · about 14 minutes.** You'll be able to list the rules your business triggers by what it does, not by whether it has a website.

## 1. Same core, different add-ons

Every business, online or offline, goes through the same core: **register it, get its tax numbers, keep records, file tax**. On top, the *activities* you do switch on extra rules:

- **Premises** (shop, office, kitchen, warehouse) bring zoning, fire safety and a local trade or business licence.
- **Selling to the public online** brings consumer protection and e-commerce rules: clear prices, refund terms, honest reviews.
- **Collecting personal data** (even just emails) brings privacy law.
- **Shipping goods** brings product safety, labelling and packaging rules.
- **Selling across borders** brings other places' sales taxes and export rules.

<!--fig:online-offline-->

> **Key idea:** Think in activities, not channels. "Do I store customer data? Do I have premises? Do I ship? Do I sell abroad?" Each "yes" adds a rule set covered later in the course.

## 2. Where online businesses get caught out

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

Online sellers face two early surprises:

- **GST from the first sale through marketplaces.** The normal turnover exemption (₹40 lakh for goods, ₹20 lakh for services in most states) generally does not apply if you sell goods through an e-commerce operator that must collect tax at source. A narrow relief lets very small sellers of goods within their own state sell through marketplaces without registering, subject to conditions; check the GST portal before you rely on it.
- **E-commerce rules.** The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 require sellers to show their legal name, address, return and refund terms, and country of origin, and ban fake reviews.
- **Privacy.** The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 applies to digital personal data; its Rules were notified in November 2025 with most duties phased in over 18 months.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

Online sellers must register for GST/HST once worldwide taxable sales pass **$30,000** over four consecutive calendar quarters, and then charge the rate of the customer's province. PIPEDA covers personal data collected in commercial activity (Quebec's Law 25 adds stricter rules for Quebec residents). Commercial emails must follow **CASL**: consent first, sender identification and an unsubscribe link.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

Since the 2018 *Wayfair* Supreme Court decision, a state can require you to collect its sales tax once your sales into that state pass its **economic nexus** threshold (often $100,000 a year), even with no office there. Marketplaces usually collect for you on their platform. The FTC enforces truthful advertising, the rule against fake reviews (2024) and the CAN-SPAM Act for email.

</div>

## 3. Where offline businesses get caught out

- **Zoning.** Not every street allows every business. Check before signing a lease.
- **Premises licences.** India: state Shops and Establishments registration and a municipal trade licence. Canada and USA: city or county business licence, plus fire and building inspections.
- **Health and food.** Anything you serve, cook or package for people to eat needs a food licence (FSSAI in India, provincial and municipal rules in Canada, county health departments and sometimes the FDA in the USA). Lesson 5.2.
- **Signs, parking and waste** rules from the local council.

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Ayesha sells handmade candles. She tests two plans.

| | Offline (a stall at weekend markets) | Online (her own site plus a marketplace) |
|---|---|---|
| Register | Proprietorship / sole proprietor | Same |
| Licences | Market vendor permit; city licence | Usually a home business permit only |
| Sales tax | Register when over the threshold | India: often from the first marketplace sale; Canada: at $30,000; USA: in her state, then in others past nexus |
| Product rules | Labels and safety warnings | Same, plus listing rules on the site |
| Privacy | Little data | Privacy policy, email consent |

## 5. Practice

1. A tutor teaches over video calls from home. Which rule sets apply?
2. A Canadian e-book seller made $12,000 this year. Must they charge GST/HST?
3. What US case lets states tax online sellers without a physical presence?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Core (register, income tax, records), privacy for student data, possibly a home business permit, and sales tax only past the threshold (and education is exempt in some cases).
2. Not yet: they are a small supplier under $30,000 in four consecutive quarters, though they may register voluntarily.
3. *South Dakota v. Wayfair* (2018).
</details>
