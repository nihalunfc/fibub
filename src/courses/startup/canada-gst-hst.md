**Module 7 · about 15 minutes · Canada.** You'll know when to register, what to charge customers in every province and how to file.

<div class="bigidea">Once sales pass $30,000 in four quarters, you charge GST or HST at your customer’s province rate, claim back the tax on your costs, and file on a schedule set by your size.</div>

## 1. The $30,000 small supplier test

You may stay unregistered as long as you are a **small supplier**: your worldwide taxable sales (with any associated businesses) are **$30,000 or less** in a single calendar quarter and in the last four consecutive quarters. Once you pass it, you have 29 days to register, and you charge GST/HST from the date shown below. Taxi and ride-share drivers, and non-residents performing in Canada, must register from their first sale.

Registering is free: add a GST/HST **RT program account** to your Business Number online through My Business Account or Business Registration Online (Lesson 4.5), or by phone.

## 2. What to charge

| Where the customer is | Tax | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Ontario | HST | 13 % |
| Nova Scotia | HST | 14 % |
| New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, PEI | HST | 15 % |
| Alberta, the territories | GST | 5 % |
| BC, Saskatchewan, Manitoba | GST + provincial PST/RST | 5 % + 7 %, 6 % or 7 % |
| Quebec | GST + QST | 5 % + 9.975 % |

The **place of supply** rules decide the rate, not where you live: goods are usually taxed where they are delivered; most services where the customer is. An Ontario designer billing a Calgary client charges 5 % GST, not 13 % HST.

> **Key idea:** HST is one tax collected by the CRA. PST in BC, Saskatchewan and Manitoba and QST in Quebec are **separate** taxes with their own registration and returns. Out-of-province sellers of software and digital services must register for BC and Saskatchewan PST, and the QST, once their sales there pass those provinces' limits.

## 3. Input tax credits

Each return you subtract the GST/HST you paid on business costs (rent, equipment, software, phone, professional fees) from what you collected. You need an invoice with the supplier's GST/HST number for anything over $100 (Lesson 7.6). Provincial PST is not recoverable; QST has its own credits (ITRs).

## 4. The quick method

Businesses with taxable sales up to **$400,000** can elect the **quick method**: remit a fixed lower percentage of tax-included sales instead of tracking each input credit (you can still claim credits on equipment and other capital purchases). It often saves time, and money, for service businesses with few costs. Check the rates for your province in CRA guide RC4058.

## 5. Filing periods and due dates

| Yearly taxable sales | Filing period |
|---|---|
| Up to $1.5 million | Annual (you may choose quarterly or monthly) |
| $1.5 million to $6 million | Quarterly |
| Over $6 million | Monthly |

Monthly and quarterly returns are due **one month after the period**. Annual filers: return and payment due **three months after the year end**, except individuals with business income and a December year end, who file by **June 15** but must pay by **April 30**. Annual filers whose net tax last year was $3,000 or more pay quarterly instalments. File online (GST/HST NETFILE, My Business Account or your accounting software), even when the return is nil.

## 6. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Noor, a Toronto web developer, passed $30,000 in March. From May she charges clients by location: $5,000 to an Ontario firm plus $650 HST, $3,000 to a Vancouver firm plus $150 GST (BC PST does not apply to her custom development service). Collected: $800. She paid $260 HST on a new monitor and software: credit $260. Net tax: **$540**, due with her return.

<div class="myth">

**"I charge the tax rate of my own province."** You charge the rate where the customer is (for most services) or where goods are delivered. An Ontario seller charges a Calgary client 5 % GST, not 13 % HST.

</div>

## 7. Practice

1. What date do you start charging tax if one big $35,000 contract in July takes you over the line?
2. Which customers mean you might owe a separate provincial registration?
3. Why can the quick method save money?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. On that very sale: a single quarter over $30,000 ends small supplier status at once.
2. Customers in BC, Saskatchewan, Manitoba (PST/RST) and Quebec (QST), once you pass their thresholds.
3. The remittance rate is set for typical costs; if your real costs are lower, you remit less than under the regular method.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Register within 29 days once taxable sales pass $30,000 in four consecutive calendar quarters, or in one quarter.
- Rates by customer: 5 % GST, 13 % HST Ontario, 14 % Nova Scotia, 15 % New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador and PEI; PST or QST on top in four provinces.
- Input tax credits return the GST/HST you paid on business costs.
- The quick method (sales up to $400,000) remits a flat share of sales instead of tracking credits.
- Annual filers: return due 3 months after year end; sole proprietors with a December year end file by June 15 and pay by April 30.
