**Module 3 · about 14 minutes.** You'll know the usual routes from one structure to another and when to get professional help.

## 1. Why businesses switch

Common triggers: profit grows (a corporation becomes tax-efficient), risk grows (staff, leases), co-founders join, or investors arrive and want shares. Switching is normal. What matters is doing it **without an accidental tax bill** and **moving everything over**: contracts, bank accounts, licences, GST or sales tax registrations, domain and trademarks.

> **Key idea:** A new entity is a new legal person. Customers' contracts, your licences and your tax registrations generally do not move by themselves: each must be assigned, re-issued or re-registered.

## 2. The usual routes

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

- **Proprietorship to private limited company:** incorporate the company, then transfer the business to it by a business transfer agreement (often as a "slump sale" for a lump sum, or in exchange for shares). Tax-neutral transfer is possible only if strict conditions are met (the proprietor holds at least 50 % of the shares for 5 years, and all assets and liabilities move). Apply for a new GST registration and transfer the input tax credit.
- **OPC to private limited:** pass resolutions, add a second member and director, and file the conversion with the MCA. Allowed at any time since 2021.
- **Partnership firm to LLP:** under Schedule II of the LLP Act by filing form 17 together with FiLLiP; all partners become partners of the LLP.
- **Private company to LLP:** possible under Schedule III (form 18) if no security interest is outstanding, but rarely done by startups.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

- **Sole proprietorship to corporation:** incorporate, then transfer the business assets (equipment, customer list, goodwill) to the corporation in exchange for shares using a **section 85 rollover**, electing on form **T2057** so no tax is due on any gain at the time. The corporation needs its own Business Number program accounts (and GST/HST registration; a section 167 election can let the transfer happen without charging GST/HST).
- **Provincial to federal (or between provinces):** "continuance" lets a corporation move its governing law without dissolving.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

- **Sole proprietor to LLC:** form the LLC; for a single member the federal tax treatment stays the same (Schedule C), but re-title the bank account, contracts and licences, and get a new EIN if the LLC will have employees or elects corporate tax.
- **LLC to C corporation:** most states (including Delaware) allow a **statutory conversion** by filing a certificate, or members can contribute their units to a new corporation. Usually tax-free if done properly (section 351), but the timing matters for **QSBS**: the five-year clock starts when stock is issued.
- **Any state to Delaware:** by conversion or merger into a new Delaware entity, common before a seed round.

</div>

<div class="su-steps">

Moving everything over: the order that works

1. Get advice on the tax route (CA, CPA or tax lawyer) and agree the value of what moves.
2. Form or convert the new entity and get its tax numbers.
3. Sign the transfer documents (business transfer agreement, rollover election, conversion certificate).
4. Open the new bank account; tell customers and suppliers the new legal name and banking details.
5. Re-register or transfer GST / GST-HST / sales tax, payroll, licences and permits.
6. Assign contracts, domain names, trademarks and other IP to the new entity.
7. Close the old registrations once nothing remains, and file final returns.

</div>

## 3. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Meera (the Ontario translator from Lesson 3.1) now earns C$140,000 profit and keeps half in the business. Her accountant sets up an Ontario corporation, transfers her equipment and client goodwill under section 85 for shares (T2057 filed), registers the corporation for GST/HST, and Meera starts paying herself a salary and dividends (Lesson 8.5). Profit she leaves in the company is now taxed at 12.2 % instead of her personal rate.

## 4. Practice

1. Name three things that do not move automatically to a new entity.
2. What form is filed to convert an Indian partnership firm into an LLP?
3. Why does the conversion date matter for a US founder hoping to use QSBS?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Any three of: customer and supplier contracts, licences and permits, tax registrations, bank accounts, trademarks and domains.
2. Form 17 (with FiLLiP), under Schedule II of the LLP Act.
3. The five-year holding period starts when the C corp stock is issued, not when the LLC was formed.
</details>
