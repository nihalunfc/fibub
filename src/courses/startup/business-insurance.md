**Module 12 · about 11 minutes.** You'll match each real risk in your business to the right cover, and know which ones the law requires.

<div class="bigidea">Insurance turns a loss that could close the business into a cost you can plan for: buy what the law requires first, then cover the risks you could not survive paying for yourself.</div>

## 1. Start from the risks

Insurance is not a shopping list; it follows your risks. Ask: what could go wrong, how likely is it, and could the business survive paying for it? Small, likely costs (a broken laptop) you pay yourself. Rare, large ones (a lawsuit, a fire, an injured worker) you insure.

## 2. The main covers

1. **General liability:** injury or damage to others. Landlords and big customers ask for it.
2. **Property and business interruption:** your stock and equipment, and lost income after a fire or flood.
3. **Professional liability (E&O):** mistakes in advice, design or software.
4. **Workers' compensation:** injured employees. Required by law.
5. **Commercial auto:** vehicles used for work. Third-party cover required.
6. **Cyber:** breaches, ransomware, customer notification.
7. **Product liability:** harm from products you make, import or sell.
8. **Directors and officers (D&O):** claims against directors personally; investors ask for it.
9. **Key person:** pays the company if a founder dies or is disabled; lenders sometimes require it.

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

Employers with 10 or more employees register with **ESIC**, which covers employees earning up to ₹21,000 a month for health and injury; above that, the **Employees' Compensation Act** makes you liable for workplace injuries, usually insured with a workmen's compensation policy. Motor third-party cover is compulsory. Shopkeepers' and office package policies bundle fire, burglary and liability cheaply. Buy from an IRDAI-registered insurer or broker.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

Register with your provincial workers' compensation board when you hire: **WSIB** in Ontario (most industries), **WorkSafeBC**, **WCB Alberta**, **CNESST** in Quebec. Premiums are a percentage of payroll by industry. Auto insurance is compulsory. Most small businesses buy a commercial general liability (CGL) package through a broker, with professional liability added for service firms.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

**Workers' compensation** is required in almost every state once you have employees (Texas lets employers opt out, with conditions); some states run their own funds. You also pay state **unemployment insurance** through payroll, and a few states require **disability** cover (such as California, New York and New Jersey). Many small businesses buy a **business owner's policy (BOP)** that bundles general liability and property.

</div>

## 3. Buying it well

- Use a broker who knows your industry, and compare at least three quotes.
- Read the **exclusions**, the **limit** (the most the insurer pays) and the **deductible** (what you pay first).
- Match limits to what contracts and leases require; keep certificates of insurance ready to send.
- Tell your insurer when the business changes: new products, countries, staff or premises.
- Review cover at every renewal.

<div class="meaning">

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founders.* A two-founder online skincare brand in New Jersey makes products in a rented unit and sells on its own site and a marketplace. It needs: product liability (the marketplace requires it once sales pass its threshold), a BOP for the unit's equipment and stock, workers' compensation and New Jersey disability once it hires its first employee, and cyber cover because it stores customer addresses. Total about $3,200 a year, quoted by a broker who compared four insurers.

<div class="myth">

**"We are a company with limited liability, so we do not need insurance."** Limited liability protects your personal assets from the company's debts; it does not pay the claim. A large claim can still empty the company's bank account and close it, and directors can be sued personally.

</div>

## 5. Practice

1. Which cover would a café need for a customer who slips on a wet floor?
2. When must a Canadian employer register for workers' compensation?
3. What is a deductible?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. General liability.
2. When it hires its first worker, in most industries (rules vary by province).
3. The part of each claim the business pays before the insurer pays the rest.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Insure rare, large losses you could not survive; pay small, likely ones yourself.
- Required by law: workers' compensation (or ESIC in India) once you have staff, and motor third-party cover.
- Commonly required by contracts: general liability, professional liability, product liability, D&O for investors.
- Check limits, deductibles and exclusions; compare at least three quotes.
- Update the insurer when the business changes.

</div>
