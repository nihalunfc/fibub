**Module 2 · about 12 minutes.** You'll write your whole business on one page and know when you need a longer plan.

<div class="bigidea">Put the whole business on one page in nine boxes, revisit it every few weeks, and test the box you find hardest to fill.</div>

## 1. Nine boxes

A one-page plan (often called a lean canvas) fits the whole business on a sheet. Fill it in fast, in pencil: it should change every few weeks as you learn.

<!--fig:lean-canvas-->

> **Key idea:** The box you find hardest to fill is the riskiest part of your idea. Test that next.

## 2. When you need a full plan

| Who asks | What they want |
|---|---|
| Banks and government loan schemes | Market, team, 3-year projections, how the loan is repaid |
| Grant programmes | How the money creates jobs, research or exports |
| Immigration and visa programmes | Proof the business is real and viable |
| Investors | Usually a pitch deck and a financial model instead |

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

Banks lending under schemes such as Mudra or with a CGTMSE guarantee usually ask for a project report with projected cash flows. The Startup India Seed Fund Scheme works through incubators, which ask for a business plan and pitch.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

Lenders under the Canada Small Business Financing Program and BDC ask for a business plan with cash flow forecasts. Futurpreneur (for founders aged 18 to 39) asks for a plan and gives a free template.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

SBA-backed lenders (7(a) and microloans) usually require a business plan. Free help writing one is offered by SCORE mentors and Small Business Development Centers.

</div>

## 3. Worked example

Press "Fill with the example" above to see a plan for a clinic reminder service. Notice the key number: **paying clinics retained after 3 months**. One number everyone on the team watches.

<div class="myth">

**"A serious business needs a 40-page business plan before it starts."** Early on, a one-page plan that changes every few weeks beats a long document. Write the long version when a lender, grant or visa programme asks for it.

</div>

## 4. Practice

1. Write your own nine boxes in under 20 minutes and download them.
2. Which box is weakest? What is one cheap test for it?
3. What does a bank want from a business plan that an investor may not?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Your own plan.
2. Your own answer: pair the weak box with an MVP from Lesson 2.3.
3. How and when the loan will be repaid from cash flow, often with security or a guarantee.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Nine boxes: problem, customers, value proposition, solution, channels, revenue, costs, key metric, unfair advantage.
- The hardest box to fill is your riskiest assumption: test it next.
- Pick one key number the whole team watches.
- Banks, grants and visa programmes ask for a full plan; investors usually want a deck and a model.
- Update the plan every few weeks as you learn.
