<div class="bigidea">Starting a business is a road with ten stops in a sensible order: test the idea first, then make it official, then add tax, people, protection and yearly upkeep as they become needed.</div>

## 1. The road

<!--fig:az-map-->

Most founders worry about doing steps "wrong". The real risks are doing them in the wrong order (hiring before payroll exists) or skipping one (never registering for sales tax after crossing the limit). This map fixes the order.

> **Key idea:** Test the idea before you spend on lawyers and filings. Many founders register in week one and then discover nobody wants the product. Registering can wait until you are about to take money from customers or sign contracts.

## 2. The ten stops in words

1. **Idea:** a problem people already pay to solve (Lesson 2.1).
2. **Test:** customer interviews and an MVP (Lessons 2.2 and 2.3).
3. **Structure:** sole proprietor, partnership, LLP, LLC or company (Module 3).
4. **Register:** make it official and get its numbers (Module 4).
5. **Licences:** permits for your activity and premises (Module 5).
6. **Bank:** a separate account and a way to get paid (Module 6).
7. **Sales tax:** register when required (Module 7).
8. **People:** payroll before your first hire (Module 9).
9. **Protect:** trademarks, IP assignments, privacy policy (Module 11).
10. **Keep going:** annual filings and taxes every year (Module 12).

## 3. The numbers your business collects

Each country gives a business a small set of identity numbers. You will meet all of them in Module 4.

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

Company or LLP identification number (CIN or LLPIN) from the MCA; **PAN** (tax ID) and **TAN** (for tax deducted at source), issued with SPICe+ for companies; **GSTIN** for GST; **Udyam** number for MSME benefits; DPIIT certificate if recognised.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

Corporation number (federal or provincial) or a business name registration; the **Business Number (BN)** from the CRA, with program accounts such as RT (GST/HST), RP (payroll), RC (corporate income tax) and RM (import-export).

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

State file number for the LLC or corporation; the **EIN** (Employer Identification Number) from the IRS, free; a state sales tax permit number; state payroll and unemployment account numbers when you hire.

</div>

## 4. Master checklist

<div class="su-check" data-name="Startup master checklist">

Startup master checklist

- [ ] Problem confirmed by at least 10 customer conversations
- [ ] First paying customer or pre-order
- [ ] Legal structure chosen
- [ ] Name checked and registered
- [ ] Business tax number received (PAN / BN / EIN)
- [ ] Licences and permits for your activity and premises
- [ ] Business bank account opened
- [ ] Bookkeeping set up from day one
- [ ] Sales tax registration (now, or a date to check the threshold)
- [ ] Co-founder agreement signed (if a team)
- [ ] IP assigned to the business
- [ ] Privacy policy and terms on the website
- [ ] Payroll registration before the first hire
- [ ] Compliance calendar with every yearly deadline

</div>

## 5. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Sam in Austin, Texas, sells a scheduling app.

Weeks 1 to 6: interviews and a pre-sale to 12 gyms. Week 7: single-member LLC filed with the Texas Secretary of State, EIN from irs.gov the same day, business checking opened. Week 8: Texas sales tax permit (Texas taxes many software subscriptions as data processing; checked with the Comptroller). Month 6: first hire, after registering with the Texas Workforce Commission and setting up payroll.

<div class="myth">

**"Register the company first; customers come later."** Registering takes days and can wait until you take money or sign contracts. Testing whether anyone wants the product takes longer and matters more.

</div>

## 6. Practice

1. Put in order: bank account, EIN, LLC filing.
2. Which Canadian program account is for payroll?
3. Name India's two income tax IDs issued to a new company through SPICe+.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. LLC filing, then EIN, then bank account (banks ask for both).
2. RP.
3. PAN and TAN.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- The ten stops: idea, test, structure, register, licences, bank, sales tax, people, protect, keep going.
- Order matters: test before filing, payroll before the first hire.
- India numbers: CIN or LLPIN, PAN, TAN, GSTIN, Udyam.
- Canada: corporation number or name registration, Business Number with RT, RP, RC and RM accounts.
- USA: state file number, EIN, sales tax permit, state payroll accounts.
