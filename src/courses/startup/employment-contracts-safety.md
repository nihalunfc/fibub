**Module 9 · about 13 minutes.** You'll hire with the right paperwork and run a workplace that is safe and fair from day one.

<div class="bigidea">Put every hire in writing (pay, notice, confidentiality and IP assignment), keep the workplace safe and free of harassment, and end employment by the rules.</div>

## 1. The offer letter and contract

A short offer letter plus a written employment agreement protects both sides. Include:

- job title, duties, start date, place of work (or remote), hours;
- pay, pay frequency, benefits, any equity (with the vesting schedule, Module 10);
- **probation** and **notice** terms that meet the minimum law;
- **confidentiality** and **IP assignment**: everything made for the job belongs to the company;
- leave and holidays, and the policies the employee must follow.

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

The labour codes require an **appointment letter** for every employee. Notice and probation follow the contract, the standing orders (if you have 300 or more workers) and your state's shops act. Courts in India do not enforce non-competes after employment ends (section 27 of the Contract Act), but confidentiality and non-solicitation clauses are fine.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

Each province's employment standards set minimum notice (Ontario: 1 week per year of service, up to 8 weeks, plus severance pay for larger employers). Without a clear, lawful termination clause, courts award **reasonable notice** that can be much longer, so a well-drafted clause matters. Ontario bans most employee non-competes since 2021; elsewhere courts enforce them only if narrow.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

Most US employment is **at will**: either side can end it at any time for any lawful reason, without notice, unless a contract says otherwise. Avoid promising "permanent" jobs in offer letters. Non-competes are void in California and limited in many states; the FTC's 2024 nationwide ban was set aside by a court and later withdrawn, so state law decides.

</div>

## 2. Safety and fair treatment

<div class="su-check" data-name="First-hire compliance checklist">

Before your first employee starts

- [ ] Written offer and employment agreement with confidentiality and IP assignment
- [ ] Payroll set up and registered (Lessons 9.2 to 9.4)
- [ ] Workers' compensation registration or insurance (Canada and USA)
- [ ] A health and safety policy, a first-aid kit and an emergency plan; workplace safety posters where required
- [ ] Harassment and discrimination policy, with how to report (India: POSH Internal Committee from 10 employees)
- [ ] Ergonomic home-office guidance for remote staff
- [ ] Personnel file: ID and work authorisation (USA: Form I-9), tax forms, signed policies
- [ ] Holiday and leave policy that meets the minimum law

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

The **OSH Code** sets duties for safe workplaces; the **POSH Act** requires an Internal Committee and policy at 10 or more employees (and a Local Committee handles smaller ones). Shops acts set hours, weekly off and leave.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

Provincial occupational health and safety laws require a written policy, training and, as you grow, a health and safety representative (6+ workers in Ontario) or joint committee (20+). Workplace harassment and violence policies are required in Ontario and federally regulated workplaces.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

OSHA's general duty clause requires a workplace free of recognised hazards; employers with more than 10 employees in most industries keep injury records. Federal anti-discrimination laws apply from 15 employees (20 for age), and many states apply them from the first employee.

</div>

## 3. When someone leaves

<div class="su-steps">

A clean exit

1. Give notice (or pay instead) as the contract and the minimum law require; put it in writing.
2. Pay final wages, unused vacation pay and any severance on time.
3. Issue the exit paperwork: Canada ROE; India relieving letter, full and final settlement, PF transfer; USA final paycheck by the state deadline and COBRA notice if you offer health insurance.
4. Recover equipment and switch off access the same day.
5. Remind them of their confidentiality duties; keep the personnel file for the required years.

</div>

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Neha's Bengaluru startup reaches 11 staff. She issues appointment letters with IP assignment to all, forms a POSH Internal Committee with an external member, registers for ESI (as she now has 10 or more staff), and writes a one-page remote-work safety guide. When a developer resigns, she pays full and final settlement within two working days, as the Code on Wages requires.

<div class="myth">

**"A non-compete will stop my employee joining a rival."** In India, Ontario and California non-competes for employees are generally unenforceable, and elsewhere they must be narrow. Protect yourself with confidentiality, IP assignment and non-solicitation clauses instead.

</div>

## 5. Practice

1. Why add an IP assignment clause if the law already gives employers most work?
2. What does "at will" mean in the USA?
3. Name two exit documents in Canada and India.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. To cover doubts: work made partly outside hours, inventions, moral rights, and work by people later found to be contractors.
2. Either side may end employment at any time without notice, for any reason that is not illegal (such as discrimination or retaliation).
3. Canada: Record of Employment, final pay statement. India: relieving letter, full and final settlement (and PF transfer).
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Every hire gets a written offer and agreement: duties, pay, probation, notice, confidentiality and IP assignment.
- India: appointment letters under the labour codes; non-competes after employment are void; POSH Internal Committee from 10 staff.
- Canada: provincial minimum notice, and courts may award longer reasonable notice without a lawful termination clause.
- USA: at-will employment; non-compete rules depend on the state.
- Safety duties apply from the first employee: policy, training, workers’ compensation (Canada, USA), harassment policy.

</div>
