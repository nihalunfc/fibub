**Module 7 · about 15 minutes · India.** You'll know whether you must register, what to charge, which returns to file and when.

<div class="bigidea">Register once your turnover passes ₹20 lakh (₹40 lakh for goods only), or at once if you sell goods across state lines; then file GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B on time every period.</div>

## 1. Do you have to register?

GST registration is by **PAN and state**: one GSTIN for each state where you have a place of business. You must register when your **aggregate turnover** (all taxable, exempt and export sales across India under one PAN, before tax) passes:

- **₹20 lakh** if you supply services (or goods and services),
- **₹40 lakh** if you supply only goods (in states that adopted the higher limit),
- **₹10 lakh / ₹20 lakh** in Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura.

Some businesses must register **whatever their turnover**: anyone making inter-state supplies of goods, e-commerce operators, people who must pay tax under reverse charge, casual taxable persons, and non-resident suppliers.

## 2. Registering

<div class="su-steps">

GST registration on gst.gov.in

1. Part A: PAN, mobile and email (verified by OTP) give you a temporary reference number (TRN).
2. Part B: business details, promoters or partners, place of business with proof (rent agreement or electricity bill and owner's consent), bank account and HSN/SAC codes for your main goods or services.
3. Aadhaar authentication (or biometric verification at a GST Suvidha Kendra in many states).
4. An officer may ask for clarification or visit the premises; reply within the time given.
5. Approval gives your 15-character GSTIN and certificate (Form REG-06). Display it at your place of business.

</div>

> **Key idea:** Registration is free on the government portal. Agents who charge a fee are selling their time, not a better registration.

## 3. What rate to charge

After the GST Council's September 2025 reform, most goods and services fall in two slabs: **5 %** (many daily goods, some textiles, many food items) and **18 %** (most services, including IT, consulting, design and marketing, and most other goods). A **40 %** rate covers a short list of luxury and sin goods, gold carries 3 %, and many essentials (fresh food, health care, education) are exempt.

Every item has a code: **HSN** for goods, **SAC** for services. The code decides the rate, so look yours up in the rate finder on the CBIC site and put it on your invoices.

For sales within your state charge **CGST + SGST** (half each, for example 9 % + 9 %); for sales to another state charge **IGST** (18 %). The **place of supply** rules decide which: for goods, usually where they are delivered; for most services to a registered business, where that business is registered.

## 4. The composition scheme

Small businesses can opt for **composition**: pay a flat low rate on turnover (1 % for traders and manufacturers, 5 % for restaurants, 6 % for small service providers up to ₹50 lakh) and file a simple quarterly statement. The catch: you cannot charge GST on invoices (you issue a *bill of supply*), cannot claim input credit, and cannot make inter-state supplies of goods. It suits shops selling to consumers, not businesses selling to other businesses.

## 5. Returns and payments

| Return | What | When |
|---|---|---|
| GSTR-1 | Every sale, invoice by invoice | 11th of next month (quarterly by the 13th under QRMP) |
| GSTR-3B | Summary: output tax, input credit, tax paid | 20th of next month (22nd or 24th quarterly under QRMP, with monthly payments) |
| GSTR-9 | Annual return | 31 December after the year (optional below ₹2 crore turnover) |
| CMP-08 / GSTR-4 | Composition: quarterly payment, yearly return | 18th after the quarter / 30 April |

**QRMP** lets businesses up to ₹5 crore turnover file quarterly while paying monthly. Your input credit depends on your suppliers filing their GSTR-1: you can see it in **GSTR-2B** each month. Claim only what appears there.

## 6. E-invoices, e-way bills and exports

- **E-invoicing** (an IRN and QR code from the government portal on each B2B invoice) is required once turnover passes ₹5 crore in any year since 2017-18.
- **E-way bill** for moving goods worth over ₹50,000 by road.
- **Exports** are zero-rated: file a **Letter of Undertaking (LUT)** online every financial year to export without paying IGST.

## 7. Late fees and interest

Missing a GSTR-3B costs a late fee (₹50 a day, ₹20 for a nil return, capped) plus **18 % a year** interest on tax paid late, and your buyers lose their input credit. After a few months of missed returns the registration can be suspended or cancelled. File nil returns even in months with no sales.

## 8. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Arjun's Kochi startup sells a ₹50,000 software service to a registered client in Chennai. Place of supply: Tamil Nadu, so he charges IGST 18 % = ₹9,000. In the same month he paid ₹3,600 CGST + SGST on a laptop. GSTR-3B: IGST ₹9,000 minus credit ₹3,600 = **₹5,400** to pay by the 20th (credit from CGST and SGST can be set against IGST in a set order).

<div class="myth">

**"I am under ₹20 lakh, so I never need GST."** Not if you sell goods to other states, sell through some marketplaces, or your clients insist on GST invoices. Many B2B startups register early because clients can only claim credit from registered suppliers.

</div>

## 9. Practice

1. Why might a startup selling only to big companies register below ₹20 lakh?
2. A café in Jaipur with ₹60 lakh sales: which GST option keeps paperwork simplest?
3. What document must an exporter of services file each year to avoid paying IGST?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Business clients want GST invoices to claim input credit, and the startup can claim credit on its own costs.
2. Composition at 5 % (restaurant), if it serves only within the state and accepts no input credit.
3. A Letter of Undertaking (LUT).
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Registration limit: ₹20 lakh turnover for services, ₹40 lakh for goods only (₹10 lakh / ₹20 lakh in a few states).
- Inter-state sellers of goods must register whatever their turnover.
- Main rates since 22 September 2025: 5 % and 18 % (40 % for a few luxury goods); CGST + SGST within a state, IGST between states.
- GSTR-1 (sales) by the 11th and GSTR-3B (summary and payment) by the 20th; quarterly under QRMP up to ₹5 crore.
- Claim input credit only when it shows in GSTR-2B; file an LUT each year to export without IGST.
