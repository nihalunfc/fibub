**Module 9 · about 16 minutes · USA.** You'll register, run compliant payroll for your first employee and file every form on time.

<div class="bigidea">US payroll means withholding federal and state income tax and FICA, matching FICA, paying federal and state unemployment tax, depositing on schedule and filing 941s and W-2s.</div>

## 1. Before the first payday

<div class="su-steps">

Hiring your first employee

1. Have an **EIN** (Lesson 4.7) and register with the state for **withholding** and **unemployment insurance** accounts (and in each state where an employee works).
2. New employee fills in **Form W-4** (federal withholding) and the state equivalent.
3. Complete **Form I-9** within 3 business days of the start date (E-Verify is required in some states).
4. Report the new hire to the state's new hire registry, usually within 20 days.
5. Buy **workers' compensation** insurance (required in nearly every state once you have employees), and choose a payroll provider.

</div>

## 2. What comes off each pay, and what you add

<!--fig:payslip-->

| | Employee | Employer |
|---|---|---|
| Social Security | 6.2 % up to $184,500 (2026) | 6.2 % |
| Medicare | 1.45 %, plus 0.9 % over $200,000 | 1.45 % |
| Federal income tax | Withheld from Form W-4 and IRS Publication 15-T | – |
| State and local income tax | Most states; a few cities | – |
| FUTA | – | 6 % of the first $7,000, usually 0.6 % ($42) after the state credit |
| State unemployment (SUTA) | A few states also charge employees | New-employer rate on the state's wage base |

## 3. Deposits and filings

- **Deposit** withheld tax and both FICA shares through EFTPS: monthly (by the 15th) for most small employers, semi-weekly once you are larger.
- **Form 941** each quarter (April 30, July 31, October 31, January 31); **Form 940** for FUTA by January 31.
- **Form W-2** to employees and the Social Security Administration by **January 31**.
- State withholding and unemployment returns on the state's schedule.

## 4. Contractors and wage rules

Contractors fill in a **W-9**; send a **1099-NEC** by January 31 to anyone paid $2,000 or more for services in 2026 (it was $600 through 2025). Employees must get at least the federal minimum wage ($7.25 an hour) or the higher state or city rate, and non-exempt employees get overtime at 1.5 times pay after 40 hours a week. Salaried staff are exempt from overtime only if they meet the duties tests and earn at least the federal salary threshold.

## 5. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Jordan in Texas hires a designer at $70,000. Each month: gross $5,833; Social Security $362 and Medicare $85 come off, and federal withholding about $548 (Texas has no income tax), leaving about $4,840. Jordan adds $447 of FICA, plus FUTA ($42 for the year) and Texas unemployment on the first $9,000. The cost is about **$6,300 a month**, before health insurance and workers' compensation.

<div class="myth">

**"A small startup with one employee can skip workers’ compensation and I-9s."** Nearly every state requires workers’ compensation from the first employee (Texas is the main exception), and every US employer must complete an I-9 for every hire.

</div>

## 6. Practice

1. Which two forms does a new employee complete on day one?
2. What is the maximum FUTA per employee after the usual state credit?
3. When is Form W-2 due?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Form W-4 and Form I-9 (plus any state withholding form).
2. $42 (0.6 % of $7,000).
3. January 31.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Register for state withholding and unemployment accounts; collect W-4 and complete I-9 within 3 business days; report new hires.
- FICA: 6.2 % (to $184,500) + 1.45 % from each side; FUTA usually $42 per employee; SUTA by state.
- Deposit monthly by the 15th (most small employers); Form 941 quarterly, Form 940 and W-2s by January 31.
- Contractors: W-9, and a 1099-NEC from $2,000 paid in 2026.
- Minimum wage, overtime and exempt status follow federal and state wage laws.

</div>
