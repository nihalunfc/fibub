**Module 11 · about 14 minutes.** You'll collect only the data you need, send marketing people agreed to, and know what to do if data leaks.

<div class="bigidea">Collect only what you need, say plainly why, get consent where the law asks, keep it safe, and make leaving as easy as joining.</div>

## 1. Personal data, in one paragraph

**Personal data** is anything that identifies a person: name, email, phone, address, IP address, device ID, location, photos, purchase history. A newsletter sign-up form or a contact page already makes you a **data fiduciary** (India) or an organisation responsible under privacy law. The rules in all three countries rest on the same ideas: tell people what you collect and why, use it only for that, protect it, let people see and correct it, and delete it when you no longer need it.

### Watch it happen

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Launch:</b> a startup adds a sign-up box. It asks for name, email, phone, date of birth and city "just in case".</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Privacy check:</b> it needs only an email to send the newsletter. It drops the rest, adds a one-line notice ("We use your email to send our weekly tips; unsubscribe any time"), and an unticked consent box.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Month 6:</b> a staff laptop is stolen. The data is encrypted and holds only emails, so the risk is low; the founders log the incident and tell the regulator and users where the law requires.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Result:</b> less data meant less to protect, less to explain and less to lose.</p></div>
</div>

## 2. The rules in each country

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

**DPDP Act, 2023.** The **DPDP Rules** were notified in November 2025 and phase in over about 18 months, so most duties apply from **May 2027**. Give a clear **notice** (what, why, how to withdraw and complain), take **consent** by a clear action (no pre-ticked boxes), use data only for that purpose, keep it secure, report **breaches** to the Data Protection Board and affected people, erase it when the purpose ends, and appoint a contact for questions. Children (under 18) need verifiable **parental consent** and no tracking or targeted ads. Penalties reach **₹250 crore** for failing to protect data.

**SMS and calls:** marketing messages must go through a telecom operator's **DLT** platform with a registered sender header and template, and must respect the **DND** (do not disturb) registry.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

**PIPEDA** covers private businesses in most provinces (Quebec, Alberta and BC have their own similar laws). Name a person **accountable** for privacy, get meaningful consent, limit collection, protect data, and report **breaches** that create a real risk of significant harm to the Privacy Commissioner and the people affected, keeping a record of every breach for 2 years. **Quebec Law 25** adds a published privacy officer, privacy impact assessments, and tracking turned off by default.

**CASL** covers every commercial electronic message (email, SMS, social DMs): you need **consent first** (express, or implied for 2 years after a purchase and 6 months after an inquiry), your identity and contact details, and an unsubscribe honoured within **10 business days**. Penalties reach **C$10 million** per violation for a business.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

There is no single federal privacy law. **State laws** (California's CCPA/CPRA and about 20 others) apply once you pass thresholds, such as California's: about **$26 million** annual revenue, or data on **100,000** or more consumers, or half your revenue from selling or sharing data. They require notices, opt-outs ("Do Not Sell or Share"), and responses to access and deletion requests. **CAN-SPAM** governs email (no prior consent, but honest headers, a postal address, an opt-out honoured within 10 business days; up to about $53,000 per email). The **TCPA** requires prior express written consent for marketing texts and automated calls ($500 to $1,500 per message). **COPPA** needs parental consent for children under 13. Every state has a **breach notification** law.

</div>

**Selling to Europe?** The EU and UK **GDPR** apply if you target people there: a lawful basis for each use, rights to access and delete, and fines up to €20 million or 4 % of worldwide turnover.

## 3. A privacy routine for a small team

1. List what personal data you collect, where it is stored and who can see it.
2. Delete what you do not need; set a deletion period for the rest.
3. Publish a clear privacy notice (Lesson 11.5) and name a privacy contact.
4. Use unticked consent boxes for marketing, and keep a record of each consent.
5. Turn on two-factor login, encryption and least-privilege access.
6. Sign data-processing terms with your email, payment and hosting providers.
7. Write a one-page breach plan: who decides, who is told, by when.

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founders.* A Montreal meal-kit startup sells across Canada and wants to email past customers a spring offer. Its list has 4,000 people: 2,500 ticked "send me offers" (express consent), 1,200 bought in the last 2 years (implied consent), and 300 bought 3 years ago. It emails the first 3,700, with the company name, address and a one-click unsubscribe, and sends the 1,200 a separate "stay subscribed?" message to turn implied consent into express consent. It does not email the 300. Its website loads analytics only after visitors accept, as Quebec's Law 25 expects.

<div class="myth">

**"Privacy laws are only for big companies."** India's DPDP Act, PIPEDA and CASL apply to businesses of every size, and CAN-SPAM and the TCPA apply to every sender. Only some US state laws have size thresholds.

</div>

## 5. Practice

1. Is a pre-ticked "send me offers" box valid consent under the DPDP Act or CASL?
2. A US customer replies "unsubscribe". How long do you have to stop emails?
3. What should you do first if a database of customer emails is exposed?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. No: consent needs a clear action by the person.
2. 10 business days under CAN-SPAM (CASL is the same).
3. Contain it (close the hole, change keys), assess the risk, then notify the regulator and people as your law requires, and record it.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Collect only what you need, say why, protect it and delete it when done.
- India: DPDP Act notice, consent and breach duties phase in to May 2027; DLT and DND for SMS.
- Canada: PIPEDA (Quebec Law 25 stricter); CASL needs consent before any commercial email or text.
- USA: state privacy laws above thresholds; CAN-SPAM for email; TCPA consent for texts; COPPA for under-13s.
- Every message needs your identity and a working unsubscribe, honoured within 10 business days.

</div>
