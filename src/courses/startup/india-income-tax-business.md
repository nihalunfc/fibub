**Module 8 · about 15 minutes · India.** You'll choose the cheapest legal route for your profit, and file and pay on time.

<div class="bigidea">An Indian founder’s profit can be taxed four ways: slabs on real profit, slabs on a presumed profit (44AD/44ADA), 30 % in an LLP, or 25.17 % in a company; pick with the numbers, not the label.</div>

> **New law, same tax.** From 1 April 2026 the **Income-tax Act, 2025** replaced the 1961 Act. It renames "previous year" and "assessment year" as one **tax year** and renumbers the sections, but the rates and schemes below carried over. We use the familiar old section numbers (44ADA, 80-IAC and so on), which you will still see on most websites and forms for a while.

## 1. Four routes for the same business

### Watch it happen

<div class="step"><p><b>Receipts ₹40 lakh, real profit ₹26 lakh</b> (a designer with ₹14 lakh of costs).</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Normal books:</b> slab tax on ₹26 lakh = ₹3.6 lakh + 4 % cess ≈ ₹3.74 lakh.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>44ADA:</b> profit presumed at 50 % = ₹20 lakh. Slab tax ₹2 lakh + cess ≈ ₹2.08 lakh, and no detailed books.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Company (115BAA):</b> 25.17 % of ₹26 lakh ≈ ₹6.54 lakh, and the founder still pays tax on what the company pays out.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Choice:</b> for a solo professional at this size, 44ADA costs least.</p></div>

### Proprietor, normal books

Your profit is added to any other income and taxed at slab rates. The new regime (default) for FY 2025-26: nil up to ₹4 lakh, then 5 %, 10 %, 15 %, 20 %, 25 % in ₹4 lakh steps and 30 % over ₹24 lakh, plus 4 % cess; the section 87A rebate means no tax if total income is up to ₹12 lakh. File **ITR-3**.

### Proprietor, presumptive tax

- **44ADA** for professionals (IT, engineering, design, consulting, law, medicine, accounting and similar): 50 % of receipts is treated as profit, up to ₹75 lakh receipts if cash is 5 % or less (otherwise ₹50 lakh).
- **44AD** for other small businesses: 6 % of receipts by bank or digital payment and 8 % of cash receipts, up to ₹3 crore turnover with little cash (otherwise ₹2 crore).

No detailed books or audit, and advance tax in one go by 15 March. File **ITR-4**. You can declare a higher profit, but if you declare less than the presumed figure you need books and an audit, and under 44AD you cannot return to it for five years once you leave.

### LLP or partnership firm

Taxed as a firm at **30 %** plus cess (and 12 % surcharge over ₹1 crore). Partners' profit share is then tax-free in their hands, but salary and interest paid to partners (within limits) are deductible for the firm and taxed on the partners.

### Private limited company

Most new companies choose **section 115BAA**: 22 % plus 10 % surcharge and 4 % cess = **25.17 %**, with no minimum alternate tax and no special deductions. Salary to founders is a deductible cost; dividends are paid from taxed profit and taxed again at your slab rate. File **ITR-6**, and the books always need a statutory audit by a chartered accountant.

## 2. The startup tax holiday (80-IAC)

A private company or LLP recognised by **DPIIT** (Lesson 4.3), incorporated before 1 April 2030, with turnover up to ₹100 crore, can apply to the Inter-Ministerial Board for a certificate. With it, the startup deducts **100 % of profit for any 3 consecutive years out of its first 10**. Apply on the Startup India portal; only a small share of applicants is approved, so plan without it and treat it as a bonus.

## 3. TDS: tax taken before you are paid

Business clients deduct **TDS** from many payments to you: 10 % on professional and technical fees (2 % on some technical services and call centres) under 194J, 1 % or 2 % on contract work under 194C, 10 % on rent. It appears in your **Form 26AS** and **AIS** on the e-filing portal and counts as tax you have already paid. Check it every quarter and chase clients whose TDS is missing.

Once your own business is large enough (a company always, a proprietor once turnover passed ₹1 crore or receipts ₹50 lakh in the previous year), **you** must deduct TDS when you pay contractors, professionals and rent above the thresholds, deposit it by the 7th of the next month, and file quarterly TDS returns. Get a **TAN** first.

## 4. Books, audit and filing

| | Proprietor (normal) | Proprietor (44AD/44ADA) | LLP | Company |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Books of account | Yes, above small limits | Not required | Yes | Yes |
| Tax audit (CA) | Turnover over ₹1 crore (₹10 crore if cash ≤ 5 %); professionals over ₹50 lakh receipts | No, if you declare at least the presumed profit | Over the same limits; statutory audit over ₹40 lakh turnover or ₹25 lakh contribution | Always (statutory audit) |
| Return | ITR-3 | ITR-4 | ITR-5 | ITR-6 |
| Due date | 31 July (31 October if audited) | 31 July | 31 October if audited | 31 October |

## 5. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Kavya, a UX designer in Pune, receives ₹40 lakh (all bank transfers) with ₹14 lakh of real costs: profit ₹26 lakh. Normal books: tax about ₹3.74 lakh. Under 44ADA her taxable profit is ₹20 lakh: tax about **₹2.08 lakh**, with simpler paperwork. As a company she would pay ₹6.54 lakh on the ₹26 lakh, before taking any money out. She stays a proprietor on 44ADA for now and revisits when she hires.

<div class="myth">

**"A private limited company always pays less tax."** At small profits a proprietor on the new regime or 44ADA usually pays less: the company pays 25.17 % from the first rupee, and dividends to you are taxed again. Companies win on raising money and limited liability, not on tax at small sizes.

</div>

## 6. Practice

1. Who can use 44ADA: a software developer, a grocery shop, a chartered accountant?
2. Where do you check the TDS clients deducted from your fees?
3. Why might a profitable startup company still choose 115BAA over the old rates?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. The developer (technical consultancy and IT professional services are covered) and the chartered accountant; the shop uses 44AD.
2. Form 26AS and the Annual Information Statement (AIS) on the e-filing portal.
3. 25.17 % with no minimum alternate tax is lower than 26 % to 34.94 % under the older rates for most startups that have few special deductions to lose.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- New regime (FY 2025-26): no tax up to ₹12 lakh income; 30 % over ₹24 lakh; plus 4 % cess.
- 44ADA: 50 % of professional receipts up to ₹75 lakh (little cash); 44AD: 6 % digital / 8 % cash turnover up to ₹3 crore.
- LLP 30 % plus cess; company 25.17 % under 115BAA, with dividends taxed again in your hands.
- DPIIT startups can apply for 80-IAC: 100 % of profit deducted for 3 of the first 10 years.
- Since 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies; rates carried over, section numbers changed.
