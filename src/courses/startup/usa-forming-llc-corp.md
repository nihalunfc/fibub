## 1. LLC, step by step

<div class="su-steps">

Forming an LLC

1. Choose the state (Lesson 3.6) and check the name (Lesson 4.1).
2. Appoint a **registered agent** with a physical address in that state (yourself, if you live there, or a paid service).
3. File **articles of organization** (called a certificate of formation in some states) online with the Secretary of State and pay the fee.
4. Sign an **operating agreement**: ownership percentages, who manages, how profit is split, what happens if a member leaves.
5. Get an **EIN** from irs.gov (free, minutes online; Lesson 4.7).
6. Open a business bank account (Lesson 6.1) and fund it with the members' contributions.
7. Handle state extras: publication (New York), initial reports (some states), and local licences.

</div>

## 2. Corporation, step by step

<div class="su-steps">

Forming a corporation

1. Choose the state (Delaware for venture capital) and appoint a registered agent there.
2. File the **certificate (articles) of incorporation**: name, authorised shares (startups often authorise 10,000,000 common shares at a tiny par value such as $0.00001), registered agent, incorporator.
3. The incorporator appoints the first **board of directors**; the board adopts **bylaws**, appoints officers and approves founder stock.
4. Issue **founder stock** under stock purchase agreements with **vesting** (Lesson 10.3), paying the tiny price in cash, and assign all IP to the company.
5. File any **83(b) elections within 30 days** of the stock grant.
6. Get an EIN, open the bank account, and qualify in your home state if different (Lesson 4.7).
7. Set up the stock ledger (cap table) and a minute book.

</div>

<!--fig:state-fees-->

> **Key idea:** The 83(b) deadline is 30 days and cannot be extended. Missing it can turn a founder's own vesting stock into a large tax bill later. The IRS now accepts the election on Form 15620, and founders keep proof of mailing or filing.

## 3. Beneficial ownership (BOI) reporting

The Corporate Transparency Act required most small companies to report their owners to **FinCEN**. In March 2025 FinCEN's interim rule **exempted companies formed in the USA** and US persons; only companies formed abroad that register to do business in a US state must report. Check fincen.gov for the current rule before relying on this.

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founders.* Lena and Dev form "Clinivo Inc." in Delaware: certificate of incorporation through a registered agent service, 10,000,000 authorised common shares at $0.00001 par. They each buy 4,000,000 shares for $40 with four-year vesting and a one-year cliff, sign IP assignments, and mail their 83(b) elections on day 12. They live in California, so they register Clinivo Inc. there as a foreign corporation, get an EIN, and open the bank account. Their Delaware franchise tax is calculated with the assumed par value method, so it stays at about $400 a year instead of tens of thousands.

<div class="myth">

**"An LLC does not need an operating agreement."** Most states do not require you to file one, but without it state default rules decide profit shares, decisions and exits, and banks and courts often ask for it.

</div>

## 5. Practice

1. Which document lists LLC members' shares and rules: articles of organization or operating agreement?
2. Within how many days must an 83(b) election be filed?
3. Why do startups authorise millions of shares at a tiny par value?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. The operating agreement.
2. 30 days from the date the stock is transferred.
3. So founders can buy their shares for very little, and so there is room to issue options and investor shares later; a low par value also keeps some state fees and taxes low.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- LLC: file articles, appoint a registered agent, sign an operating agreement.
- Corporation: certificate of incorporation, bylaws, board consent, stock purchase agreements.
- Founders on vesting file an 83(b) within 30 days; it cannot be extended.
- Most US-formed companies no longer file BOI reports since FinCEN’s March 2025 rule.
- Keep minutes, a stock ledger and signed agreements.

</div>
