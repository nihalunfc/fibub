**Module 8 · about 13 minutes.** You'll know what you can claim, what you cannot, and which records to keep so a claim holds up.

<div class="bigidea">A cost lowers your tax when it is spent to earn business income, is reasonable and is not personal; equipment is claimed over time, mixed costs only for the business share, and every claim needs a record.</div>

## 1. The test

A cost is deductible when it is spent **to earn business income**, is **reasonable**, and is **not personal or capital**. Capital items (equipment, vehicles, furniture) are claimed over time. Mixed personal and business costs are split, and only the business part is claimed.

<!--fig:expense-sort-->

### Watch it happen

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Aisha’s phone bill:</b> $1,200 a year. Business use about 70 % (she checked a typical month).</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Claim:</b> 70 % × $1,200 = $840. The personal 30 % stays personal.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Her car:</b> 10,000 km driven, 4,000 km for clients (from her log). Costs $5,000.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Claim:</b> 40 % × $5,000 = $2,000.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Proof:</b> the bills, the log and a note of the method, kept 6 years.</p></div>

## 2. The common cases

- **Running costs:** software, hosting, advertising, rent, professional fees, bank and payment fees, insurance, interest on business loans, salaries and contractor fees. Deduct in the year.
- **Equipment:** depreciation over its life.

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

Depreciation on the written-down value: 40 % a year for computers and software, 15 % for most plant and machinery, 10 % for furniture; half in the first year if the asset was used for under 180 days. On 44AD/44ADA you cannot claim costs or depreciation separately: the presumed profit already allows for them.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

**Capital cost allowance (CCA):** class 50 (computers) 55 %, class 12 (software, small tools under $500) 100 %, class 8 (furniture, equipment) 20 %, class 10 (vehicles) 30 %; the first year has a half-year rule, boosted by the accelerated investment incentive for property bought before 2028.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

**Section 179** and **bonus depreciation** let most small businesses deduct equipment in the year bought (100 % bonus depreciation for property acquired after 19 January 2025). Under the de minimis safe harbor you can expense items up to $2,500 each without depreciating them.

</div>

- **Home office:** the business share of rent or mortgage interest, utilities, insurance and property tax, by area. Canada and the USA require regular use; the USA requires exclusive use, and offers a simplified $5 per square foot method (up to 300 sq ft).
- **Car:** the business share of fuel, insurance, repairs, lease or depreciation, by kilometres or miles; or in the USA the standard mileage rate. A trip log is essential.
- **Phone and internet:** the business share.
- **Meals:** 50 % in Canada and the USA (entertainment not at all in the USA); in India, deductible when wholly for business.
- **Travel:** to clients, conferences or suppliers away from your usual place of business: fares, hotels, and meals (with the same limits).
- **Never:** fines and penalties, your own income tax, personal clothing, commuting between home and your regular workplace, gym and club memberships, and in India expenses that broke the law.

## 3. Records that survive an audit

<div class="su-check" data-name="Expense records checklist">

What to keep for every claim

- [ ] The invoice or receipt, in the business's name where possible (with GST/HST number for credits)
- [ ] Proof of payment (bank or card statement)
- [ ] A one-line note of the business purpose ("lunch with Acme to discuss pilot")
- [ ] For cars: a trip log (date, from and to, purpose, distance)
- [ ] For home office: floor areas and the bills you split
- [ ] For equipment: purchase invoice and the date it went into use
- [ ] Everything stored for 6 years (Canada), 8 years for Indian company books (6 years after the assessment year for income tax records), and 3 to 7 years (USA)

</div>

> **Key idea:** Photograph every receipt the day you get it, and match it to the bank line in your bookkeeping (Lesson 6.3). Digital copies are accepted by all three tax authorities if they are clear and complete.

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Deductible</dt><dd>subtracted from revenue before tax is worked out</dd>
<dt>Capital expense</dt><dd>something that lasts years, claimed through depreciation</dd>
<dt>Depreciation / CCA</dt><dd>the yearly share of an asset’s cost you may deduct</dd>
<dt>Business share</dt><dd>the part of a mixed cost used for the business, by area, distance or time</dd>
<dt>Business purpose</dt><dd>the short note that explains why a cost was for the business</dd>

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Aisha runs a consulting business from home in Vancouver. Her 120 sq ft office is 12 % of a 1,000 sq ft rented flat; rent and utilities are $28,800 a year, so she claims $3,456. Her phone is 70 % business ($840 of $1,200). Her log shows 4,000 of 10,000 km for business: 40 % of $5,000 car costs = $2,000. A $2,000 laptop goes in CCA class 50. Total claims: about $6,300 plus CCA, each backed by a receipt and a note.

<div class="myth">

**"If I put it through the business account, it is a business expense."** Which account paid does not matter. A family holiday paid by the business is still personal, and claiming it can bring penalties on top of the tax.

</div>

## 5. Practice

1. Is the cost of driving from home to your rented office deductible?
2. Your phone is used 50:50. You pay ₹12,000 a year. What can you claim?
3. Why is a parking ticket at a client's office not deductible, but the parking fee is?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. No, that is commuting.
2. ₹6,000, with bills and a basis for the split.
3. The fee is a cost of the trip; fines for breaking the law are never deductible.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Deductible: costs to earn business income that are reasonable and not personal or capital.
- Equipment is depreciated (India 40 % for computers, Canada CCA class 50 at 55 %); the US often allows the whole cost in year one.
- Mixed costs (home, car, phone) are split by area, kilometres or time used for business.
- Meals: 50 % in Canada and the USA; fines, your own income tax and commuting are never deductible.
- Keep receipt, proof of payment and a one-line purpose for every claim, for 6 years or more.

</div>
