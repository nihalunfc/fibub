**Module 5 · about 12 minutes.** You'll check whether your home business needs permission, and set it up safely for tax and insurance.

## 1. What the rules allow

Most cities allow a **home occupation**: a business in a home that stays mostly residential. Common limits: a share of floor space, no or few non-resident employees, limited client visits, no outside storage, small or no signs, no nuisance.

<!--fig:home-risk-->

## 2. By country

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

Municipal rules and your **housing society's bye-laws** decide what you can run from a flat; commercial activity in residential premises can attract commercial property tax and electricity rates. A home address works as a registered office with the owner's no-objection certificate (Lesson 4.8). Shops and Establishments registration may be needed if you employ people there.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

City zoning by-laws set home occupation limits; some cities require a business licence for any home business. Condo corporation rules and leases may restrict it. Sole proprietors can claim **business-use-of-home expenses** on T2125 if the space is the principal place of business or used only for business and to meet clients regularly.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

Cities issue **home occupation permits** under zoning codes; HOA covenants can be stricter. The IRS **home office deduction** needs a space used regularly and exclusively for business (simplified method: $5 per square foot up to 300 square feet).

</div>

> **Key idea:** Tell your insurer. A home policy usually limits business property and does not cover a client who slips on your stairs; a cheap rider or a small business policy does (Lesson 12.3).

## 3. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Jordan runs a bookkeeping business from a spare room in Columbus, Ohio: no signs, clients online, one visit a month. The city confirms no permit is needed for that level of activity; the HOA allows "home offices with no client traffic". Jordan adds a business rider to the homeowner's policy and measures the room (120 sq ft) for the simplified home office deduction: $600.

## 4. Practice

1. Name three things that make a home business more likely to need a permit.
2. What is the IRS simplified home office deduction for a 200 sq ft office?
3. Who else besides the city can restrict a home business?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Any three: clients visiting, employees on site, a sign, deliveries, noise or smells, food or child care.
2. 200 × $5 = $1,000.
3. Your landlord (lease), condo corporation, housing society or HOA.
</details>
