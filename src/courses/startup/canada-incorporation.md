**Module 4 · about 17 minutes · Canada.** You'll be able to register a business name or incorporate a corporation in Canada, and set up its records properly.

<div class="bigidea">In Canada you choose federal or provincial incorporation, file articles online, and from day one keep a minute book with by-laws, registers and the record of individuals with significant control.</div>

## 1. Sole proprietorship or partnership

<div class="su-steps">

Registering a sole proprietorship or partnership name (Ontario example)

1. Decide whether you need to register: only if you use a name other than your own legal name(s).
2. Search the name on the Ontario Business Registry.
3. Register the business name online ($60; valid for 5 years) and print the Master Business Licence.
4. Get a Business Number from the CRA if you need GST/HST, payroll or import-export accounts (Lesson 4.5).
5. Get any municipal licences and permits (Module 5) and open a business bank account.

</div>

Other provinces work the same way through their own registry (BC Registries, Alberta registry agents, Registraire des entreprises in Quebec), with their own fees.

## 2. Corporation: what you decide first

- **Federal or provincial** (Lesson 3.6) and the **name**, or a numbered company.
- **Registered office address** in the province (a real address, not a PO box).
- **Directors:** at least one; federal corporations need at least 25 % resident Canadian directors.
- **Share structure:** most startups authorise an unlimited number of **common shares**; some add classes (for example voting and non-voting, or preferred shares for investors later). Keep it simple at first.
- **Restrictions on share transfers**, so you stay a private company.

## 3. Federal incorporation, step by step

<div class="su-steps">

Incorporating with Corporations Canada

1. Get a NUANS report for your proposed name (or choose a numbered company).
2. Fill in the online incorporation form: Articles of Incorporation, registered office, first directors.
3. Pay the fee ($200 online, as of November 2026) and submit; a certificate usually comes the same day if the name is approved.
4. Register extra-provincially in every province where you will carry on business (for Ontario, file an initial return; other provinces charge a fee).
5. Sign by-laws and organisational resolutions: appoint officers, issue shares to the founders, set the financial year, pick a bank.
6. Set up the minute book and the register of individuals with significant control (ISC).
7. Get the Business Number (the CRA links federal corporations automatically) and the program accounts you need.

</div>

Ontario is similar through the **Ontario Business Registry** ($300 online, as of November 2026).

## 4. The minute book

Every corporation must keep records at its registered office. Start them on day one:

<div class="su-check" data-name="Canadian corporation minute book">

Minute book contents

- [ ] Certificate and Articles of Incorporation
- [ ] By-laws
- [ ] Directors' and shareholders' resolutions and minutes
- [ ] Securities (share) register and transfers
- [ ] Register of directors and officers
- [ ] Register of individuals with significant control (ISC), updated within 15 days of a change
- [ ] Shareholder agreement (Lesson 10.1)
- [ ] Copies of annual returns and notices filed with the registry

</div>

> **Key idea:** "Individuals with significant control" are people who own or control 25 % or more of the shares or votes, or otherwise control the corporation. Federal corporations file this information with Corporations Canada too.

## 5. Worked example

*Made-up founders.* Tom and Aisha (Calgary) incorporate federally as "Northlight Robotics Inc.": NUANS report, Articles online with unlimited common shares and transfer restrictions, $200 fee, certificate the same afternoon. They register extra-provincially in Alberta, issue 500,000 shares each at $0.0001, appoint Aisha CEO and Tom CTO, set a 31 December year-end, file their ISC information, and get the BN with RC and RT accounts.

<div class="myth">

**"Once the certificate arrives, the paperwork is done."** The corporation still needs by-laws, organisational resolutions, issued shares, registers and a Business Number, and must keep its minute book current every year.

</div>

## 6. Practice

1. What does a sole proprietor in Ontario get when registering a business name?
2. Name three things to decide before incorporating.
3. What is an ISC register?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. A Master Business Licence showing the registered name, valid for 5 years.
2. Any three: federal or provincial, name or number, registered office, directors, share structure, transfer restrictions.
3. A record of individuals with significant control (25 % or more of shares or votes, or control in fact), kept by the corporation and, for federal corporations, filed with Corporations Canada.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Federal: name protected nationally, about C$200 online, 25 % resident Canadian directors.
- Provincial: one province’s law; Ontario C$300 online.
- Decide share classes, directors, registered office and year-end first.
- Keep a minute book with by-laws, resolutions, registers and share certificates.
- Federal corporations file their individuals with significant control with Corporations Canada.

</div>
