**Module 11 · about 12 minutes.** You'll search, choose classes, file and keep a trademark in your country, and know what it costs.

<div class="bigidea">A registered trademark gives you the exclusive right to your name in your classes across the whole country; search first, file early, and use it consistently.</div>

## 1. Why register

Without registration, you have only the rights you earn by using a name in the places you trade, and proving them is slow and costly. A registration gives nationwide rights in the classes you filed, a public record that warns others off, and the power to take down copies on marketplaces, app stores and domain disputes. Investors expect the main brand to be filed.

## 2. Choose a strong name

The strongest marks are **made-up or unrelated words** (like a fruit name for computers). Descriptive names ("Fast Delivery") are weak and often refused. Before falling in love with a name, search:

1. The national register (IP India public search, the Canadian Trademarks Database, USPTO trademark search).
2. App stores, marketplaces, company registries and domain names.
3. The same name in similar classes: a clash with "close enough" goods also blocks you.

## 3. Filing, step by step

<div class="su-steps">

From search to registration

1. Search the register and the market for the same or similar marks.
2. Choose your classes from the 45 Nice classes (software is usually class 9 or 42; online retail class 35; food class 29, 30 or 43).
3. Write the goods and services, using the office's pre-approved wording where you can.
4. File online and pay the fee per class; you get a filing date and number.
5. An examiner checks it, and may send an objection you must answer by the deadline.
6. The mark is published for opposition, so others can object.
7. If nobody objects, it is registered; renew every 10 years.

</div>

<!--fig:trademark-fees-->

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

File Form **TM-A** on the IP India portal. Fees: **₹4,500 a class** for individuals, DPIIT-recognised startups and small enterprises (Udyam), **₹9,000** for others. After examination the mark is advertised in the **Trade Marks Journal** for 4 months of opposition. Start using **™** from filing; use **®** only after registration (misusing ® is an offence). Startups can use a free SIPP facilitator.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

File online with **CIPO** using the Nice classes. Fees are about C$460 for the first class and C$140 for each extra class (adjusted each January). Canada has a long backlog, often 2 to 3 years, so file as soon as you settle on a name. Canada has no ® rule in law, but use ® only after registration. Registration lasts 10 years.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

File on the USPTO's **Trademark Center**: **$350 a class** since 18 January 2025, plus $200 a class if you write your own description instead of using the ID Manual wording, and $100 if information is missing. You can file **intent-to-use** before launching, then prove use later. Foreign-domiciled applicants must use a US attorney. Between years 5 and 6 file a declaration of use, and renew every 10 years. State registrations exist but give far less.

</div>

## 4. Keep it alive

- Use the mark as registered, on the goods and services listed.
- Watch for copycats (set up alerts on the register and marketplaces) and send a firm letter early.
- File renewals and use declarations on time.
- Going abroad? The **Madrid Protocol** lets you extend a home filing to over 130 countries from one application (India, Canada and the USA are all members), within 6 months to keep your home date.

## 5. Worked example

*Made-up founders.* A two-person skincare startup in Austin picks a made-up name. The USPTO search shows no similar marks in class 3 (cosmetics) or 35 (online retail). They file intent-to-use in both classes with ID Manual wording: 2 × $350 = $700. Seven months later an examiner asks them to narrow "cosmetics" to listed items; they reply in a week. The mark is published, nobody opposes, they file the statement of use after launch, and the mark registers 14 months after filing. They extend it to Canada through the Madrid Protocol.

<div class="myth">

**"Registering my company name or domain protects my brand."** A company name or domain only stops an identical registration in that registry. Only a trademark gives you the right to stop others using a confusing name for similar goods.

</div>

## 6. Practice

1. Why is "Best Pizza" a weak trademark?
2. A software startup also sells online courses. Which classes might it need?
3. What is the risk of waiting two years to file in Canada?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. It describes the product and is used by many; examiners refuse descriptive marks.
2. Class 9 or 42 for the software and class 41 for education.
3. Someone else may file first, and the backlog means your own registration takes even longer.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Search the register, marketplaces and domains before choosing a name.
- File per Nice class, only where you sell; use the office's pre-approved wording to avoid extra fees.
- India ₹4,500 a class for startups (₹9,000 others); Canada about C$460 plus C$140 per extra class; USA $350 a class.
- Use ™ from filing and ® only after registration.
- Renew every 10 years, and use the Madrid Protocol to protect the mark abroad.

</div>
