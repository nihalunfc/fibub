**Module 10 · about 12 minutes.** You'll handle a co-founder's exit by the agreement, protect the company, and keep a dispute out of court.

<div class="bigidea">When a co-founder leaves, the agreement decides the shares; your job is a clean handover of shares, director roles, accounts and customers.</div>

## 1. Leaving by the agreement

<!--fig:leaver-calc-->

If you signed vesting and leaver terms (Lessons 10.1 and 10.3), most of the hard questions are already answered: how many shares the leaver keeps, at what price the rest come back, and by when. Without them, the leaver keeps every share and every right a shareholder has, and the remaining founders work for them.

## 2. The exit checklist

<div class="su-steps">

When a co-founder leaves

1. Agree the date and whether they are a good or bad leaver under the agreement; put it in a short separation letter.
2. Buy back or transfer unvested shares at the agreed price; update the share register and cap table.
3. Resign as director and file the change (India: DIR-11 and DIR-12 within 30 days; Canada: notice of change within 15 days federally; USA: board minutes and the next state report).
4. Hand over passwords, domain, code repositories, bank signing rights and customer relationships.
5. Remind them of their confidentiality, IP assignment and non-solicitation duties; collect equipment.
6. Tell the team and key customers together, with one simple message.

</div>

## 3. Tax on the buy-back

The leaver may have a capital gain or loss on shares sold back. In India a company buy-back is taxed as a deemed dividend in the shareholder's hands since October 2024; in Canada a redemption above paid-up capital is a deemed dividend; in the USA a repurchase of unvested stock at cost usually has no gain. Small amounts at the original price rarely create tax, but check before agreeing a higher price.

## 4. When founders fall out

- Talk early with a neutral person: a mentor, an investor, a coach.
- Follow the agreement's **deadlock** route: mediation, then arbitration (India's Arbitration and Conciliation Act; provincial arbitration acts; the US Federal Arbitration Act).
- As a last resort, one founder buys the other out, or the company is sold or closed (Lesson 12.5). Courts can step in for oppression of a minority shareholder, but it is slow and costly.

> **Key idea:** Separate the business from the friendship. The agreement handles the shares; your job is to keep customers served and the team calm while it is done.

## 5. Worked example

*Made-up founders.* After 20 months Ben leaves Asha's Pune startup to move abroad, a good leaver. He keeps 187,500 vested shares; the other 262,500 go to Asha and the option pool at the original ₹10 a share under the SHA. He resigns as director (DIR-11, DIR-12), hands over the AWS account, and stays as an adviser for three months. The investors see a clean cap table at the next round.

<div class="myth">

**"A founder who leaves loses their shares automatically."** Only if an agreement says so. Without vesting and leaver terms, a departed founder keeps every share and every shareholder right.

</div>

## 6. Practice

1. What protects the company if a leaver joins a competitor?
2. Which filings record a director's resignation in India?
3. Why keep the leaver as a shareholder rather than forcing them out entirely?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Bad-leaver terms (if joining a competitor is listed), confidentiality and non-solicitation clauses; non-competes are often unenforceable.
2. DIR-11 (by the director) and DIR-12 (by the company).
3. Vested shares were earned; taking them would breach the agreement and invite a dispute.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Leaver terms decide what a departing founder keeps: vested shares for a good leaver, often nothing for a bad leaver.
- Update the share register and cap table; file the director’s resignation (India DIR-11/DIR-12, Canada notice of change).
- Hand over passwords, domains, code, bank rights and customer relationships the same week.
- A buy-back can be taxed (India: deemed dividend since October 2024): check before agreeing a price.
- Use mediation and arbitration before court.

</div>
