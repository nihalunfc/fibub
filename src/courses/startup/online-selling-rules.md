**Module 5 · about 15 minutes.** You'll set up an online shop, app or subscription that follows the main consumer rules in your country.

## 1. The common core

Wherever you sell, online consumer law asks for the same basics:

- **Who you are:** legal name, address, contact.
- **What it costs:** the full price, with taxes and fees, before checkout.
- **What happens next:** delivery time, returns, refunds, cancellation.
- **No tricks:** honest reviews, no fake urgency or hidden subscriptions ("dark patterns").
- **Consent:** for marketing messages and personal data.

<!--fig:site-check-->

## 2. By country

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

The **Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020** require sellers to show legal name, address, contact and grievance officer details, total price and break-up, return, refund, exchange and warranty terms, delivery and shipment details, and country of origin; to acknowledge complaints within 48 hours; and not to post fake reviews. The **Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023** ban false urgency, basket sneaking, forced action and subscription traps. Pre-packaged goods sold online must show Legal Metrology declarations (MRP, net quantity, manufacturer, origin).

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

Provincial consumer laws (such as Ontario's Consumer Protection Act, 2002) require clear disclosure before an internet agreement, the chance to correct errors and accept, and a copy within 15 days; buyers can cancel if goods don't arrive within 30 days of the promised date. The federal **Competition Act** bans misleading advertising and "drip pricing" (advertising a price that can't be paid because fees are added). **CASL** covers commercial emails and texts.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

The **FTC** enforces truthful advertising, the Mail, Internet or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule (ship on time or offer a refund), the 2024 rule on **fake reviews**, and **ROSCA** for online subscriptions (clear terms, consent, simple cancellation), with many states adding automatic renewal laws (California's is strict). **CAN-SPAM** covers marketing email. Courts have applied the ADA to websites, so make yours accessible.

</div>

> **Key idea:** If you sell on a marketplace (Amazon, Flipkart, Etsy), its rules apply on top of the law, and the marketplace may collect sales tax or GST for you, but you are still the seller in law.

## 3. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Ayesha's candle site sells in Canada and the US. Her checklist: legal name and address in the footer; prices shown in each currency with tax estimated at checkout; delivery times by region; a 30-day return policy; order confirmation email with all terms (meets Ontario's copy requirement); an email sign-up box that is unticked by default with a clear purpose (CASL express consent); reviews only from verified buyers, none paid for.

## 4. Practice

1. Name two dark patterns banned in India.
2. What must a Canadian business give an online buyer within 15 days in Ontario?
3. What does ROSCA require for online subscriptions?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Any two: false urgency, basket sneaking, confirm shaming, forced action, subscription traps, drip pricing, disguised ads, nagging.
2. A copy of the internet agreement.
3. Clear disclosure of the terms before billing, the customer's express informed consent, and a simple way to stop recurring charges.
</details>
