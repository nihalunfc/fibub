## 1. Build your plan

Fill in the plan for a real idea, even a rough one. Each line links back to the module that explains it. Download it, share it with a co-founder or mentor, and review it every 90 days.

## 2. Your country checklist

Tick each item as you go. The list stays on this page only while it is open; download or print it to keep it.

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

<div class="su-check" data-name="India startup checklist">

India startup checklist

- [ ] Problem confirmed in 10 or more customer conversations; first paying customer or pre-order
- [ ] Structure chosen: sole proprietor, partnership, LLP, OPC or private limited
- [ ] Name checked on MCA and the trademark register; domain bought
- [ ] Company or LLP incorporated (SPICe+ or FiLLiP), with PAN and TAN
- [ ] Current account opened; payment gateway set up
- [ ] Udyam registration; Startup India (DPIIT) recognition if eligible
- [ ] Shop and establishment registration and trade licence; FSSAI or other licences if needed
- [ ] GST registration when required; invoices with GSTIN and HSN/SAC codes
- [ ] Bookkeeping set up; records kept for 8 years
- [ ] Co-founder agreement with vesting; IP assigned to the company
- [ ] Payroll: TDS, PF and ESI registrations once you hire
- [ ] Trademark filed; NDAs and contractor agreements with IP assignment
- [ ] Privacy notice and consent (DPDP Act); website legal pages and grievance officer
- [ ] Compliance calendar: TDS, GST, advance tax, AGM, AOC-4, MGT-7, ITR
- [ ] Insurance: ESIC or employees' compensation, property, liability as needed

Canada startup checklist

USA startup checklist

- [ ] Problem confirmed in 10 or more customer conversations; first paying customer or pre-order
- [ ] Structure chosen: sole proprietor, LLC, S corp election or C corporation
- [ ] Name checked with the state and USPTO; domain bought
- [ ] LLC or corporation formed; registered agent appointed; foreign qualification in your home state if formed in Delaware

- [ ] Trademark filed with USPTO; NDAs and contractor agreements with IP assignment

- [ ] Compliance calendar: payroll deposits, 941, sales tax, estimated tax, 1120 or 1040, state annual report
- [ ] Insurance: workers' compensation, general liability or BOP, professional or product liability

## 3. Reading your numbers

Runway is your cash divided by your net monthly burn (costs minus revenue), from Lesson 2.5. Start raising money or cutting costs while at least **6 months** remain, because raising usually takes 3 to 6 months. When revenue passes costs, you are at break-even and runway stops shrinking. Model it month by month with the [[cash-runway]] and [[break-even]] tools.

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founders.* Arjun and Priya plan a home-cooked meal subscription in Hyderabad, ₹3,000 a month per family. Their plan: 20 family interviews in month 1; a private limited company because they will raise money; SPICe+ with GST (they will deliver food, so FSSAI registration too); a co-founder agreement with 4-year vesting; a trademark in classes 29 and 43; a privacy notice and legal pages; a compliance calendar; and ₹6 lakh cash against ₹75,000 a month of costs: 8 months of runway. Their 90-day goal is 50 paying families. They download the India checklist and tick the first three items in week 2.

<div class="myth">

**"I should register the company first, then find customers."** Registration takes days; finding a real problem and a paying customer takes months. Validate first (Module 2), then register when you are about to take money or sign contracts.

</div>

## 5. Practice

1. Which comes first in the plan: trademark filing or customer interviews?
2. Cash ₹9 lakh, costs ₹1.5 lakh a month, revenue ₹30,000 a month. What is the runway?
3. Name three items from your country checklist you could do this week.

<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Customer interviews.
2. ₹9,00,000 ÷ (₹1,50,000 − ₹30,000) = 7.5 months.
3. For example: 10 customer interviews, a name search, and opening a business bank account (after registering).
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Validate the problem and get a first paying customer before spending on registration.
- Choose the structure by liability, tax and whether you will raise money; then register, get tax numbers and a bank account.
- Get the licences, sales tax or GST registration and payroll accounts the business needs, when it needs them.
- Protect the business: co-founder agreement with vesting, IP assignments, trademark, privacy and legal pages.
- Keep it running: a compliance calendar, insurance, and a plan reviewed every 90 days.
