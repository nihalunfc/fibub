**Module 11 · about 12 minutes.** You'll know which kind of IP protects each thing your startup makes, which ones to register, and how to make sure the company owns them.

<div class="bigidea">Your brand, code, inventions and secrets are property: trademarks guard the name, copyright the creative work, patents the invention, and secrecy the know-how, but only if the company owns them.</div>

## 1. Four kinds of protection

<!--fig:ip-picker-->

**Intellectual property (IP)** is the law's way of letting you own things you cannot hold: a name, a piece of code, an idea built into a machine. For most startups the brand and the code matter most in the first years; patents matter for hardware, biotech and deep tech.

| | Protects | Needs registering? | Lasts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trademark** | Name, logo, slogan | No, but registering makes it far stronger | 10 years, renewable |
| **Copyright** | Code, text, designs, video, music | No: automatic | Author's life + 60 (India) or + 70 (Canada, USA) |
| **Patent** | A new, useful, non-obvious invention | Yes, and before going public | 20 years from filing |
| **Trade secret** | Recipes, data, methods, customer lists | No: keep it secret | As long as it stays secret |
| **Design** | How a product looks | Yes | India 10 + 5 years; Canada up to 15; US 15 from grant |

## 2. Make sure the company owns it

IP belongs to whoever created it unless the law or a contract says otherwise. That catches many startups out:

- **Founders:** work done before the company existed belongs to the founder personally. Assign it to the company in writing at incorporation (Lesson 10.1).
- **Employees:** work done in the job normally belongs to the employer for copyright, but inventions and side projects can be disputed. Use an invention assignment in every offer letter.
- **Freelancers and agencies:** they usually own the copyright in what they make for you, even after you pay, unless they sign a written assignment (Lesson 11.3).

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

The Copyright Act, 1957 gives the employer copyright in works made by employees under a contract of service; work by a contractor stays with the author unless assigned in writing (section 19). Patents and designs need a patent agent; DPIIT-recognised startups get **80 % off patent fees**, expedited examination, and free facilitators under the **SIPP** scheme (the government pays the facilitator's fees). Software "per se" cannot be patented in India.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

The Copyright Act gives employers copyright in employees' works (section 13(3)); contractors keep copyright unless they assign it in writing, and authors keep **moral rights** unless they waive them. Copyright lasts life + 70 since December 2022. CIPO offers small-entity patent fees, and provinces such as Ontario fund IP help for startups (for example, the IP Ontario program).

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

Employee works are **works made for hire** owned by the employer; commissioned work by contractors is work for hire only in a few listed categories with a written agreement, so always add an assignment. Register copyright (about $45 to $65 online) before you sue to claim statutory damages. A **provisional patent application** ($65 to $325 depending on entity size) holds your filing date for 12 months while you test the market.

</div>

## 3. What to do first

1. List what you have made: name, logo, code, content, designs, inventions, data.
2. Get written assignments from every founder, employee and freelancer who made any of it.
3. Search and file a trademark for your main name (Lesson 11.2).
4. Lock up secrets: NDAs, access limits, confidentiality clauses.
5. Before showing an invention publicly, talk to a patent agent.

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founders.* Maya and Rohan build a budgeting app in Toronto. Rohan wrote the first version in his spare time before they incorporated, and a freelancer in Vancouver drew the logo. At their first investor meeting the lawyer asks: who owns the code and the logo? Rohan signs an assignment of his pre-incorporation code to the corporation; the freelancer signs a copyright assignment and moral-rights waiver for C$200 extra. They file the app name with CIPO in classes 9 (software) and 36 (financial services). Due diligence is clean.

<div class="myth">

**"If I paid for it, I own it."** Payment buys the work, not the copyright. Without a signed assignment, the freelancer or agency usually keeps the copyright and can stop you changing or reusing it.

</div>

## 5. Practice

1. Which IP protects a secret recipe, and what keeps it protected?
2. Your co-founder wrote code before you incorporated. Who owns it now?
3. Why might a startup choose a trade secret over a patent?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Trade secret law; keeping it secret with NDAs and limited access.
2. The co-founder personally, until they assign it to the company in writing.
3. A patent is published and expires after 20 years; a secret that cannot be reverse-engineered can last forever and costs nothing to register.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Trademarks protect names and logos, copyright creative work and code, patents inventions, trade secrets know-how.
- Copyright and trade secrets are automatic; patents and designs must be registered, and trademarks should be.
- Copyright lasts life + 60 years in India and life + 70 in Canada and the USA; patents 20 years; trademarks 10 years, renewable.
- Get written IP assignments from founders, employees and freelancers: paying is not owning.
- File a patent before any public showing; India gives startups 80 % off patent fees.

</div>
