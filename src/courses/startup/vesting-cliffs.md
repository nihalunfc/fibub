**Module 10 · about 11 minutes.** You'll set up vesting that protects the company and each founder, and avoid the most expensive paperwork mistake in US startups.

<div class="bigidea">Vesting means shares are earned over time: usually nothing for 12 months, a quarter at the cliff, then a slice each month for 3 more years.</div>

## 1. How vesting works

Founders get all their shares on day one, but the company can buy back **unvested** shares at the price paid if they leave. Each month more shares **vest** and can no longer be bought back. The standard schedule is **4 years, with a 1-year cliff**: nothing vests in the first year, a quarter vests at 12 months, then 1/48 each month.

<!--fig:vesting-timeline-->

### Watch it happen

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Day 1:</b> 1,000,000 shares issued. Vested: 0. The company could buy all of them back at cost.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Month 11:</b> still 0 vested. Leaving now means leaving with nothing.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Month 12, the cliff:</b> a quarter vests at once: 250,000 shares.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Each month after:</b> another 1/48, about 20,833 shares.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Month 48:</b> all 1,000,000 vested.</p></div>
</div>


Why a cliff? Teams usually find out within a year whether they work together. The cliff means someone who leaves at month 7 walks away without a quarter of the company.

## 2. Acceleration

- **Single trigger:** unvested shares vest when the company is sold. Buyers dislike it: the team they bought can leave at once.
- **Double trigger:** unvested shares vest only if the company is sold **and** the person is let go (or their role is cut) within a set time. This is the common compromise.
- Credit for time already worked: founders who worked a year before the agreement often start vesting from an earlier date.

## 3. How each country does it

<div class="cc" data-cc="in">

Companies issue founder shares outright, so vesting is written as **reverse vesting** in the shareholders' agreement: unvested shares must be transferred back (to the other founders or bought back by the company within the Companies Act buy-back rules) at the original price. Employee ESOPs have a statutory minimum vesting period of 1 year.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="ca">

Founders subscribe for shares at a nominal price; the shareholders' agreement gives the company or other founders a right to buy back unvested shares. Canada has no equivalent of the US 83(b) election, because the shares are owned outright from the start and gains are capital gains.

</div>

<div class="cc" data-cc="us">

Restricted stock purchase agreements give the company a **repurchase right** that lapses as shares vest. Each founder must file an **83(b) election** within **30 days** of receiving the shares (IRS Form 15620, or a signed letter) and keep proof of mailing. Without it, each month's vesting is taxed as income at the shares' value then; with it, tax is on today's tiny value, and the holding period for capital gains starts now.

</div>

> **Key idea:** Vesting protects the founders who stay. It feels like distrust when you sign it, but it is what lets the company replace a founder who leaves without the remaining team working for a large absentee shareholder.

## 4. Worked example

*Made-up founder.* Ben holds 450,000 shares on 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff. He leaves after 20 months: 450,000 × 20 ÷ 48 = 187,500 shares have vested; he keeps them. The company buys back the other 262,500 at the price he paid and grants them to his replacement.

<div class="myth">

**"Vesting means I don’t own my shares yet."** You own them and can vote them from day one. Vesting only gives the company a right to buy back the unvested part, at the price you paid, if you leave early.

</div>

## 5. Practice

1. Shares 960,000, 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff. How many have vested at month 30?
2. Why do buyers prefer double-trigger acceleration?
3. What happens if a US founder misses the 83(b) deadline?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. 960,000 × 30 ÷ 48 = 600,000.
2. The team cannot leave just because the sale happened; it must also lose its role.
3. Each vesting slice is taxed as ordinary income at its value when it vests, which can be large if the company has grown.
</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Standard: 4 years with a 1-year cliff, then monthly; vested = shares × months ÷ 48 after the cliff.
- The cliff protects the company from someone leaving early with a large stake.
- Double-trigger acceleration (sale plus loss of role) is the usual compromise.
- India and Canada write founder vesting as a buy-back or transfer right in the shareholders’ agreement.
- USA: file the 83(b) election within 30 days of the grant; it cannot be fixed later.

</div>
