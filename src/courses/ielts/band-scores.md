**Module 1 · about 15 minutes.** By the end you will be able to work out your overall band from four skill bands, turn a practice-test raw score into an approximate band, and know what an examiner looks for in Writing and Speaking.

<div class="bigidea">Every band is a description of what you can do, not a percentage. Moving up half a band means doing one more thing reliably, so find out exactly what that thing is.</div>

## 1. The nine bands in plain words

These short summaries are our own wording of what each level means in practice:

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 9 | Full, natural control of English. Rare slips only. |
| 8 | Very good. Occasional errors with unusual words or in unfamiliar situations; handles complex arguments well. |
| 7 | Good. Uses complex language and understands detail, with some mistakes and misunderstandings. |
| 6 | Competent. Gets the meaning across in familiar situations, but makes errors and sometimes misunderstands. |
| 5 | Modest. Understands the general meaning in most situations but makes many errors. |
| 4 | Limited. Copes only in familiar situations; frequent problems with understanding and expression. |
| 3 and below | Understands and uses only very basic language, or almost none. |

Most universities ask for 6.0 to 7.0. Many migration routes ask for 6.0 to 8.0 depending on the points you want (Lesson 1.4).

## 2. Listening and Reading: counting correct answers

Listening and Reading each have 40 questions worth one mark each. There is **no negative marking**, so never leave an answer blank. Your raw score (out of 40) is turned into a band with a conversion table. The table moves a little from test to test to keep each version equally fair, so the numbers below are **approximate**:

| Band | Listening | Academic Reading | General Training Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9.0 | 39 to 40 | 39 to 40 | 40 |
| 8.5 | 37 to 38 | 37 to 38 | 39 |
| 8.0 | 35 to 36 | 35 to 36 | 37 to 38 |
| 7.5 | 32 to 34 | 33 to 34 | 36 |
| 7.0 | 30 to 31 | 30 to 32 | 34 to 35 |
| 6.5 | 26 to 29 | 27 to 29 | 32 to 33 |
| 6.0 | 23 to 25 | 23 to 26 | 30 to 31 |
| 5.5 | 18 to 22 | 19 to 22 | 27 to 29 |
| 5.0 | 16 to 17 | 15 to 18 | 23 to 26 |
| 4.5 | 13 to 15 | 13 to 14 | 19 to 22 |
| 4.0 | 10 to 12 | 10 to 12 | 15 to 18 |

Notice two things. First, at the top the steps are tiny: one or two mistakes is the difference between 8.5 and 9.0. Second, in the middle each half band is worth three or four answers, so a better method for one question type can lift you half a band.

## 3. Writing and Speaking: four criteria each

Trained examiners mark Writing and Speaking. Each task is given a band for four criteria, and the four are averaged:

| Writing (each task) | Speaking |
|---|---|
| **Task achievement** (Task 1) or **task response** (Task 2): did you answer every part of the question and develop your ideas? | **Fluency and coherence**: can you keep talking smoothly and link ideas logically? |
| **Coherence and cohesion**: is it organised in clear paragraphs, with ideas linked naturally? | **Lexical resource**: do you have the words to talk about the topic precisely? |
| **Lexical resource**: is your vocabulary wide, precise and spelled correctly? | **Grammatical range and accuracy**: do you use a mix of sentence types, mostly without errors? |
| **Grammatical range and accuracy**: do you use complex sentences correctly, with good punctuation? | **Pronunciation**: are you easy to understand, with natural stress and intonation? |

In Writing, Task 2 counts about twice as much as Task 1. Your Writing band is worked out from both tasks and rounded to a half band.

<div class="myth">

**"Big words get a high band."** Rare words used wrongly lower your vocabulary score. Examiners reward words that are precise and natural for the topic, used correctly. "Many people own cars" beats "a plethora of individuals possess automobiles".

</div>

## 4. The overall band

Add your four skill bands, divide by 4, then round to the **nearest half band**. If the average ends in .25, it rounds **up** to .5; if it ends in .75, it rounds **up** to the next whole band.

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Example 1.</b> L 6.5, R 6.5, W 5.0, S 7.0. Total 25.0, average 6.25 → rounds up to <b>6.5</b>.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Example 2.</b> L 4.0, R 3.5, W 4.0, S 4.0. Total 15.5, average 3.875 → nearest half band is <b>4.0</b>.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Example 3.</b> L 7.0, R 6.5, W 6.0, S 6.0. Total 25.5, average 6.375 → nearest half band is <b>6.5</b>.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Example 4.</b> L 6.0, R 6.0, W 6.0, S 6.5. Total 24.5, average 6.125 → nearest half band is <b>6.0</b>.</p></div>

<div class="meaning">
<dl>
<dt>Raw score</dt><dd>The number of correct answers out of 40 in Listening or Reading.</dd>
<dt>Band</dt><dd>A level from 0 to 9 in half steps, describing what you can do in that skill.</dd>
<dt>Overall band</dt><dd>The average of the four skill bands, rounded to the nearest half band (.25 and .75 round up).</dd>
<dt>What it means for you</dt><dd>A weak skill pulls the overall down, and many organisations also set a minimum for each skill. Find your weakest skill early and give it the most time.</dd>

<div class="keypoints">

- Bands go from 0 to 9 in half steps and describe what you can do.
- Listening and Reading: one mark per question, no negative marking; the raw score out of 40 is converted with a table (about 30 correct gives 7.0 in Listening).
- General Training Reading needs more correct answers than Academic for the same band.
- Writing and Speaking are marked on four equal criteria; Writing Task 2 counts twice as much as Task 1.
- Overall band = average of the four, rounded to the nearest half band, with .25 and .75 rounding up.

</div>
