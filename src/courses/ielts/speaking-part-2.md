**Module 8 · about 16 minutes.** By the end you will turn any cue card into a two-minute talk with a clear structure.

<div class="bigidea">Treat the card as a story outline: set the scene, tell what happened in order, and finish with why it mattered to you. Notes are keywords; the talk is a story.</div>

## 1. The card

> **Describe a time when you helped someone.**
> You should say:
> - who you helped
> - why they needed help
> - what you did
>
> and explain how you felt about it.

You get 1 minute to prepare and paper for notes. Then you speak for 1 to 2 minutes. The examiner will stop you at about 2 minutes, so aim to still be talking then.

## 2. The one-minute plan

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>10 seconds: choose.</b> Pick the first idea you can talk about easily. True or invented, it doesn't matter.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>40 seconds: keyword notes</b> for each bullet: who, why, what, feeling. Three or four words each, plus one or two good vocabulary items.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>10 seconds: first sentence.</b> Decide how you will start, so you begin confidently.</p></div>
</div>

Example notes: *neighbour Mrs Ito, 80s · broken wrist, ice · shopping 6 weeks, bins, chats · glad, close now, "community"*.


Press Start (or Fast demo) to run the real clock for a cue card: one minute to prepare, then up to two minutes to speak.

## 3. Sample talk (about 2 minutes)

<div class="meaning">

I'd like to talk about the time I helped my neighbour, Mrs Ito, who lives in the flat below mine. She's in her eighties and lives on her own, and although she's very independent, last winter she slipped on some ice outside our building and broke her wrist.

She couldn't carry anything for about six weeks, and her family live in another city, so she really needed someone nearby. I knocked on her door the day after the accident and offered to do her shopping. At first she was a bit embarrassed, I think, because she doesn't like asking for help, but in the end she agreed.

So every Saturday I'd pick up her shopping list, which was always written in beautiful handwriting, and get everything with my own shopping. I also took her rubbish out, and quite often I stayed for a cup of tea afterwards. She told me stories about moving to this country in the nineteen sixties, which were absolutely fascinating.

As for how I felt, I was really glad I could help, and honestly I think I got as much out of it as she did. Before that, we'd only ever said hello on the stairs, but now we're genuinely close. Her wrist has healed completely, but I still pop in most weekends. It made me realise how easy it is to live next to someone for years without really knowing them.

</div>

Notice: every bullet in order, past tenses for the story, *used to / would* for repeated actions, feelings and a reflection at the end.

## 4. When you run out

Add one of these: a small detail (what the weather was like), a feeling, what you learned, what happened afterwards, or whether you would do it again.

<div class="myth">

**"I must cover each bullet for exactly 30 seconds."** The bullets are a guide. Spend longer where your story is richest, but do mention each one, and include the "explain" part.

</div>

## 5. Practise with the timer

Press Start: 1 minute to prepare, then 2 minutes to speak. Record yourself if you can.

<div class="keypoints">

- One minute: choose fast, keyword notes for each bullet, plan your first sentence.
- Tell it as a story: scene, events in order, feelings and reflection.
- Cover every bullet and the "explain" line; true or invented, both are fine.
- Keep talking until stopped, around 2 minutes; add details, feelings, results.
- Use past tenses accurately for stories; used to / would for repeated actions.

</div>
