**Module 3 · about 14 minutes.** Read first: [Skimming and scanning](/learn/courses/ielts/skim-and-scan/). By the end you will recognise the six common ways a question rewords the text, which is the key skill behind every Reading question type.

<div class="bigidea">The question and the text say the same thing in different clothes. Learn to recognise the person, not the clothes.</div>

## 1. The six ways text gets reworded

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>1. Synonyms.</b> Text: "a <i>rapid</i> rise". Question: "a <i>fast</i> increase". The most common change.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>2. Word form.</b> Text: "<i>Pollution</i> has worsened." Question: "The air has become more <i>polluted</i>." Same root, different part of speech.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>3. Active and passive.</b> Text: "Engineers <i>tested</i> the design." Question: "The design <i>was tested</i>."</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>4. General and specific.</b> Text: "apples, pears and plums". Question: "<i>fruit</i>". Or the other way round: "vehicles" becomes "lorries".</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>5. Negatives and opposites.</b> Text: "It is <i>not uncommon</i>." Question: "It happens <i>quite often</i>." Or "few people" becomes "most people did not".</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>6. Whole-sentence restructuring.</b> Text: "Despite its cost, the scheme proved popular." Question: "The scheme was popular <i>although</i> it was expensive." Clauses swap places and linking words change.</p></div>
</div>

## 2. Worked example

Text (from "Bees on the rooftops"): *"Early studies found that city honey bees often survived the winter at least as well as rural ones."*

| Question wording | Same meaning? |
|---|---|
| Urban honey bees were found to cope with winter no worse than country bees. | Yes: urban = city; country = rural; "no worse than" = "at least as well as" |
| City bees survived winter better than rural bees. | No: "at least as well" does not say "better" |
| City bees need less food in winter. | Not in the text |

## 3. Build your paraphrase bank

Every time you check a Reading answer, write the pair in a notebook:

| Text said | Question said |
|---|---|
| restricted | limited |
| refuge | safe place |
| in steeper decline | falling faster |
| could not show directly | did not prove |

After a few weeks you will notice that the same pairs come up again and again. Module 10 has a full paraphrase toolkit.

<div class="myth">

**"If I find the same word in the text, I've found the answer."** Often the same word is a trap: it appears in a nearby sentence that says something different. Read the meaning of the whole sentence.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Six kinds of paraphrase: synonyms, word form, active/passive, general/specific, negatives/opposites, restructured sentences.
- The answer is the sentence with the same meaning, not the same words.
- Same words in the text can be a trap.
- Keep a paraphrase bank from every practice test.
