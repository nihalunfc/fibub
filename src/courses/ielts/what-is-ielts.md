**Module 1 · about 12 minutes.** By the end you will know what IELTS measures, which version you need, and how this course and its practice room are organised so you can study alone from start to finish.

<div class="bigidea">IELTS does not test how much English you know. It tests what you can <i>do</i> with English: understand people talking, read real texts, write clearly and hold a conversation.</div>

## 1. What IELTS is

IELTS stands for the **International English Language Testing System**. It is an English test for people whose first language is not English, run jointly by the British Council, IDP and Cambridge University Press & Assessment. More than 12,000 organisations use it: universities, employers, professional bodies (such as nursing and medical councils) and governments deciding visas.

You do not pass or fail IELTS. You get a **band score from 0 to 9** for each of four skills, plus an overall band. The organisation you apply to decides which band is good enough. Lesson 1.3 explains how the bands are worked out, and Lesson 1.4 lists the bands people usually need.

## 2. The four skills

Every IELTS test has the same four parts. Step through them:

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Listening, 30 minutes, 40 questions.</b> Four recordings: a conversation about everyday life, a talk about a local service, a discussion in a study or training setting, and a short lecture. You hear each recording <b>once</b>.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Reading, 60 minutes, 40 questions.</b> Three long texts (Academic) or a set of shorter everyday and work texts plus one long text (General Training). Many question types: True/False/Not Given, matching headings, gap filling and more.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Writing, 60 minutes, 2 tasks.</b> Task 1 (at least 150 words) is a chart or diagram to describe (Academic) or a letter (General Training). Task 2 (at least 250 words) is an essay, and it counts twice as much as Task 1.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Speaking, 11 to 14 minutes, 3 parts.</b> A recorded, face-to-face conversation with an examiner (on a video call at some centres): questions about you, a 2-minute talk from a card, then a discussion of wider ideas.</p></div>
</div>

## 3. Academic or General Training?

This is the most important choice, and the only real difference is in Reading and Writing.

| | Academic | General Training |
|---|---|---|
| Who it is for | University and college study, professional registration (doctors, nurses, engineers, teachers) | Migration (Canada, Australia, New Zealand, the UK), work, training below degree level, secondary school |
| Listening and Speaking | Same for both | Same for both |
| Reading texts | Three long texts from books, journals and magazines, written for a general reader | Short notices, adverts and timetables, workplace texts such as policies and job ads, then one longer text |
| Writing Task 1 | Describe a graph, table, chart, diagram or map | Write a letter: formal, semi-formal or informal |
| Writing Task 2 | Essay on a general academic topic | Essay on a general topic (often a little more everyday) |

<div class="myth">

**"General Training is easier, so I'll take that."** The Reading texts are easier, but that is why you need **more correct answers** for the same band (Lesson 1.3 shows the numbers). And if a university asks for Academic, a General Training result will simply be refused. Take the one your organisation names.

</div>

**How to choose:** look at the website of the organisation you are applying to and find the exact words "IELTS Academic" or "IELTS General Training". If you are applying to more than one place, check each. Students applying for a study permit and later permanent residence in Canada sometimes need both, at different times.

## 4. IELTS for UKVI and IELTS Life Skills

- **IELTS for UKVI** is the same Academic or General Training test, with the same questions and scoring, taken at a centre approved by UK Visas and Immigration. Some UK visas require it instead of the normal test. If you are applying for a UK visa, check the UK government page in the sources before you book.
- **IELTS Life Skills** is a different, shorter test of Speaking and Listening only, at level A1, A2 or B1, for some UK family and settlement visas. It is pass or fail. This course does not cover it, but the Listening and Speaking modules will help.
- **IELTS Online** lets you take IELTS Academic (and in some places General Training) at home on your own computer, with a live examiner on video. It is accepted by fewer organisations, so check first.

## 5. Paper or computer?

You can take the written papers on paper or on a computer at a test centre. The questions, timing and scoring are the same. Lesson 1.6 compares them and helps you choose. Speaking is always a live conversation with a real examiner, whichever you pick.

<div class="keypoints">

- IELTS gives a band from 0 to 9 for each of Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking, plus an overall band. There is no pass or fail.
- Listening and Speaking are the same for everyone. Reading and Writing differ between **Academic** (study, professional registration) and **General Training** (migration, work).
- Take the module your university, employer or visa names. General Training needs more correct Reading answers for the same band.
- IELTS for UKVI is the same test at a UK-approved centre. Life Skills is a separate Speaking and Listening test.
- Study this course in order; tick lessons as you go and save your progress file.
