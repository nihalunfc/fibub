**Module 6 · about 15 minutes.** For General Training candidates. By the end you will decide the tone of any letter in ten seconds and keep it consistent from "Dear" to your name.

<div class="bigidea">Tone follows the reader. A stranger or an organisation gets a formal letter; someone you know in a work or official role gets semi-formal; a friend or relative gets informal. Decide once, then keep it all the way.</div>

## 1. The task

> You should spend about 20 minutes on this task.
> You recently bought a coffee machine online, but it stopped working after a week.
> Write a letter to the company. In your letter
> - describe the problem
> - explain what you have already done
> - say what you would like the company to do
>
> Write at least 150 words. You do NOT need to write any addresses at the top of your letter. Begin your letter as follows: Dear Sir or Madam,

Every task has the same parts: a situation, a reader, **three bullet points** you must cover, and often the greeting.

## 2. Find the tone from the reader

| Reader | Tone | Greeting | Sign-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| A company, an official, someone whose name you don't know | Formal | Dear Sir or Madam, | Yours faithfully, |
| Someone you don't know personally, but whose name you know | Formal | Dear Mr Patel, / Dear Ms Okafor, | Yours sincerely, |
| Someone you know in a work, school or neighbour role | Semi-formal | Dear Mr Patel, or Dear Anna, | Kind regards, / Best regards, |
| A friend or relative | Informal | Dear Sam, / Hi Sam, | Best wishes, / Love, / See you soon, |

Use Ms for a woman unless you know she prefers Mrs or Miss. Never "Dear Mr Anna" or "Dear Mr John Smith" (title + surname only).


Pick a situation and slide Formality from very casual to very formal.

## 3. What changes with the tone

| Feature | Formal | Informal |
|---|---|---|
| Contractions | I am, I would, I have not | I'm, I'd, I haven't |
| Opening line | I am writing to complain about... | Thanks so much for your letter! |
| Requests | I would be grateful if you could... | Could you...? / Can you...? |
| Vocabulary | purchase, receive, inform, require | buy, get, tell, need |
| Phrasal verbs | fewer | more: catch up, put up with, sort out |
| Closing line | I look forward to hearing from you. | Write back soon! / Can't wait to see you. |

Semi-formal sits in between: polite and complete, with some contractions allowed, no slang.

## 4. Same message, three tones

Purpose: you can't come to an event.

- **Formal (to an organiser you don't know):** *I regret to inform you that I will be unable to attend the conference on 12 May due to a prior commitment.*
- **Semi-formal (to your manager):** *I'm afraid I won't be able to come to the team lunch on Friday, as I have a medical appointment that I can't move.*
- **Informal (to a friend):** *I'm so sorry, but I can't make it to your party on Saturday. I've got my cousin's wedding that day!*

## 5. Purpose first

In all three tones, the **first or second sentence says why you are writing**. The examiner should not have to guess.

<div class="myth">

**"Informal letters are easier, so I can use text-message English."** Informal does not mean careless. No "u", "gonna" or emojis, and grammar and spelling still count. Write as you would to a friend you respect.

</div>

## 6. Practise choosing the tone

<div class="keypoints">

- The reader decides the tone: formal (strangers, organisations), semi-formal (known people in a role), informal (friends, family).
- Pairs: Dear Sir or Madam → Yours faithfully; Dear Mr X → Yours sincerely; Dear Sam → Best wishes.
- Formal: no contractions, formal words, "I would be grateful if". Informal: contractions, phrasal verbs, warmth, no slang.
- Say why you are writing in the first or second sentence.
- Keep one tone from greeting to sign-off.

</div>
