**Module 5 · about 18 minutes.** Read first: [The overview](/learn/courses/ielts/task1-overview-sentence/). By the end you will describe any line graph with precise, varied language of change and a clear structure.

<div class="bigidea">A line graph is a story of change. Tell it in groups ("the two that rose, the one that fell"), give the start, the end and the turning points, and choose words that match the size of each change.</div>

## 1. Read the graph before you write

Spend two minutes on:

- **The axes.** What is measured, in what unit (millions, %, tonnes), over which years?
- **The shape of each line.** Up, down, flat, peak, dip, fluctuating?
- **Crossings.** Where one line overtakes another is almost always a main feature.
- **Groups.** Which lines move together?

## 2. Words for change

Move the sliders. The table gives one natural way to describe the change. The thresholds are a rough guide for choosing words, not an IELTS rule.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="change-words"><figcaption>Change the 2010 and 2020 values and see the percentage change and the phrases that fit it.</figcaption></figure>

| Direction | Verbs | Nouns |
|---|---|---|
| Up | rise, increase, grow, climb, go up | a rise, an increase, growth |
| Down | fall, decrease, decline, drop, go down | a fall, a decrease, a decline, a drop |
| No change | remain stable, stay the same, level off, plateau | a plateau |
| Top / bottom | peak at, reach a high of, hit a low of | a peak, a low point |
| Up and down | fluctuate, vary | fluctuations |

| Size | Adverbs | Adjectives |
|---|---|---|
| Big | sharply, dramatically, significantly, considerably | sharp, dramatic, significant, considerable |
| Medium | steadily, moderately | steady, moderate |
| Small | slightly, marginally | slight, marginal |
| Speed | rapidly, gradually | rapid, gradual |

Two sentence patterns, and you should use both:

- **Verb + adverb:** *Visitor numbers rose sharply between 2015 and 2020.*
- **There was + adjective + noun:** *There was a sharp rise in visitor numbers between 2015 and 2020.*


Tap a stretch of the line (or its button) and pick the words that describe it.

## 3. Prepositions that carry the numbers

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| from ... to | start and end | rose **from** 20% **to** 35% |
| by | the size of the change | rose **by** 15 percentage points |
| at | a level, a peak | peaked **at** 50,000 in 2018 |
| of | after a noun | a rise **of** 15% |
| between ... and / over | a period | **between** 2010 **and** 2020; **over** the decade |

**Percent or percentage points?** If a share goes from 20% to 35%, it rose by **15 percentage points**, which is a 75% increase. Many candidates write "rose by 15%", which is a different claim. Saying "from 20% to 35%" avoids the problem.

## 4. Worked example

The task: *The graph shows the percentage of commuters in one city who travelled to work by car, bus and bicycle between 2000 and 2020.*

The data: car 62% → 58% → 49%; bus 28% → 26% → 19%; bicycle 4% → 11% → 26% (for 2000, 2010 and 2020; the rest walked or used other means).

Main features: car still largest but falling; bus falling; bicycle rising fast and overtaking bus around 2018.

<div class="meaning">

**Model answer (about 180 words)**

The line graph compares the proportion of commuters in one city who used three types of transport to get to work over a twenty-year period from 2000.

Overall, cycling grew dramatically in popularity, while both car and bus use declined. Despite this, the car remained the most common way to commute throughout the period.

In 2000, almost two thirds of commuters (62%) drove to work. This figure fell slightly to 58% over the next decade and then dropped more steeply to 49% by 2020, meaning that for the first time fewer than half of all commuters went by car. Bus use followed a similar downward pattern, decreasing gradually from 28% to 26% before falling to 19% at the end of the period.

Cycling, in contrast, rose steadily from a very low base of just 4% in 2000 to 11% in 2010. It then increased sharply, overtaking the bus around 2018 and reaching just over a quarter of commuters (26%) in 2020, more than six times its original level.

</div>

Notice: two groups (falling: car and bus; rising: bicycle), start and end values for every line, one crossing point, varied verbs and nouns, and no reasons.

## 5. Common mistakes

- **Describing year by year**: "In 2001... In 2002... In 2003..." Pick the start, end and turning points.
- **Every line in its own paragraph.** Group them.
- **Wrong tense** for past years, or mixing tenses.
- **"The graph increased."** The graph does not rise; the number of commuters does. Name the subject.
- **Reasons:** "because of new bike lanes". Not in the graph.

<div class="myth">

**"I need fancy words like 'skyrocketed' and 'plummeted' for a high band."** Precise, natural words matter more than dramatic ones. Use a strong word only for a strong change, and spell it correctly.

</div>

## 7. Write one

<div class="keypoints">

- Read the axes, units and years; find crossings and groups.
- Use both patterns: verb + adverb, and there was + adjective + noun.
- From/to for levels, by for the size of change, at for a peak; percentage points for changes in a share.
- Group lines that behave alike; give start, end and turning points.
- Past simple for past years; the subject is the number, not the graph.
