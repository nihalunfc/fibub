**Module 2 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will know what you will hear in each of the four parts, how the questions follow the recording, and the habits that raise a Listening band quickest.

<div class="bigidea">The answers come in the same order as the questions, and the recording is played once. Your job is to know where you are at every moment.</div>

## 1. The four parts

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Part 1: two people, everyday situation.</b> Someone books, buys, joins or reports something: a hotel room, a course, a lost bag. You fill in a form or notes with names, numbers, dates and short facts. It is the easiest part, so aim for 9 or 10 out of 10.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Part 2: one speaker, everyday situation.</b> A guide, a manager or a radio presenter gives information: a tour of a museum, changes at a library, a local festival. Often a map or plan, multiple choice and matching.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Part 3: two to four people, study or training.</b> Students talk with each other or with a tutor about an assignment, a project or a field trip. You must follow opinions: who thinks what, who agrees, what they decide.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Part 4: one speaker, academic lecture.</b> A short talk on a subject such as animal behaviour, the history of a material or a new technology. Usually 10 gaps in notes, with no pause in the middle.</p></div>
</div>

## 2. How the time works

For each part, the recording first tells you which questions to look at and gives you about 30 seconds to read them. Then you hear the recording once. Parts 1 to 3 may have a short pause half way to look ahead; Part 4 does not. On paper you get 10 minutes at the end to copy your answers onto the answer sheet; on computer you type as you go and get 2 minutes to check.

## 3. Word limits

Every completion task tells you the maximum, such as **ONE WORD ONLY**, **NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS** or **ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER**.

- Articles and small words count: "a garden" is two words.
- Numbers written as digits count as a number, not a word: "15 June" is one word and a number.
- Hyphenated words count as one word: "part-time".
- Going over the limit makes the answer wrong, even if the right word is in it.

## 4. Question types you will practise

| Type | What you do | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Form, note, table, flow-chart completion | Write words or numbers in gaps | 2.4 |
| Multiple choice | Pick one answer from A, B, C, or two from five | 2.5 |
| Map, plan and diagram labelling | Match places to letters on a plan | 2.6 |
| Matching | Match a list of items to options (such as people to opinions) | 2.7 |
| Sentence completion and short answers | Write a few words that complete or answer | 2.4, 2.8 |

## 5. Five habits that raise your band

1. **Predict before you listen** (Lesson 2.2): for every gap, decide the kind of answer, such as a number, a place or a plural noun.
2. **Follow the order**: answers come in order. If you miss one, let it go and listen for the next, or you will lose three.
3. **Wait for the final answer**: speakers change their minds ("Tuesday... no, Wednesday"). This is the most common trap in Part 1.
4. **Listen for paraphrase**: the question says "cost", you hear "you'll pay". The exact words of the question are rarely the words you hear.
5. **Check spelling and grammar**: a plural you miss or a misspelled word is wrong.

## 6. Hear it for yourself

The practice room reads every recording aloud using your device's own voices. Nothing is downloaded or sent anywhere. Choose a speed, and switch on "exam mode" to hear each recording once only, as in the real test. After you answer, the transcript appears with the answers highlighted, so you can see exactly where each one was.

<div class="myth">

**"I need to understand every word."** You don't. You need to understand the words around each answer. Native speakers miss words too; they follow the meaning and the structure. Prediction tells you which words matter.

<div class="keypoints">

- Four parts: everyday conversation, everyday talk, study discussion, academic lecture. They get harder.
- 40 questions in about 30 minutes; each recording is played once; answers follow the order of the questions.
- Obey the word limit exactly: articles count, digits count as numbers.
- Predict, follow the order, wait for the final answer, listen for paraphrase, check spelling.
