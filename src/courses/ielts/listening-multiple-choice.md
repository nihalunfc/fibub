**Module 2 · about 15 minutes.** Read first: [Predicting the answer](/learn/courses/ielts/predict-the-answer/). By the end you will recognise the distractors in multiple choice questions and know how to stay with the right answer.

<div class="bigidea">In multiple choice, every option is usually mentioned. The skill is not hearing the words; it is hearing which option actually answers the question.</div>

## 1. Two formats

- **One answer from three** (A, B or C): one mark.
- **Two answers from five** (A to E), or three from seven: one mark for each correct letter, in any order.

## 2. The five kinds of distractor

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>1. Change of mind.</b> "We were going to take the train, but in the end we drove." The first plan is wrong.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>2. Mentioned, but not the answer to the question.</b> Q: What does the speaker <i>dislike</i> about the flat? She says the kitchen is small (true, but she says it doesn't bother her) and the street is noisy (that is what she dislikes).</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>3. Someone else's opinion.</b> Q: What does <i>Tom</i> think? Anna gives one view; Tom gives another. Track who says what.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>4. Past versus now.</b> "It used to open at nine; now it opens at ten." The question decides which time matters.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>5. Same words, different meaning.</b> An option repeats exact words from the recording but changes the meaning. The correct option usually paraphrases.</p></div>
</div>

## 3. The method

1. **Read the question stem first** and underline its key idea: who? reason? problem? most? first?
2. **Compare the options** and note how they differ (cost, time, place). Give each a one-word label.
3. **Listen for the question's idea**, not for the option words. When the speaker reaches it, the answer follows.
4. **Cross out options** as they are ruled out ("not the train") and keep listening until the speaker settles.
5. **Don't change** your answer later unless you are sure you misheard. First instincts in Listening are usually linked to something you heard.

## 4. Worked example

*Why did Maya choose the evening course?*
A because it was cheaper · B because of her work timetable · C because a friend recommended it

> *"My friend Lucy did the day course and loved it, so I nearly signed up for that. The price is the same either way. But I start work at eight every morning, so the evening was the only option for me."*

- A is ruled out: "the price is the same".
- C is a distractor: Lucy recommended the **day** course.
- **B** is the answer: her work hours, paraphrased as "I start work at eight every morning".

<div class="myth">

**"Pick the option whose words you heard."** Exact matching is often the trap. The right answer is normally expressed in different words, as in the example above.

</div>

## 5. Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="ielts" data-topics="listening-part2,listening-part3" data-mode="practice" data-n="10" data-facets="qtype=mcq"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Expect all options to be mentioned; two are distractors.
- Know the five traps: change of mind, mentioned but not the answer, someone else's opinion, past versus now, same words with a different meaning.
- Underline the key idea of the question and label how the options differ.
- The right option is usually a paraphrase of what you hear.
- In "choose TWO", each letter is a mark, in any order; never leave a blank.

</div>
