**Module 8 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will know which kinds of vocabulary actually raise a Speaking score, and how to use idioms without sounding memorised.

<div class="bigidea">In Speaking, good vocabulary sounds natural: precise topic words, everyday collocations and phrasal verbs, a few idioms used in the right place, and paraphrase when a word is missing.</div>

## 1. Topic words, ready to use

Make a short list for each common topic: home, work, study, travel, food, technology, health, environment. Not single words, but phrases:

| Topic | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Home | a two-bedroom flat, a quiet residential area, within walking distance of, cramped, spacious |
| Work | a demanding job, flexible hours, work-life balance, meet deadlines, a supportive manager |
| Travel | off the beaten track, a packed itinerary, jet-lagged, a breathtaking view, a budget airline |
| Technology | I'm glued to my phone, screen time, keep in touch, a user-friendly app |

## 2. Phrasal verbs and spoken collocations

Spoken English uses many: *catch up with friends, look after my nephew, put off my homework, take up a hobby, run out of time, make a decision, have a go, take a break*. They sound natural in Speaking (more than in essays).

## 3. Idioms: few, right, natural

Use an idiom only if you are sure of the meaning, the form and the situation:

- *It's not my cup of tea* (I don't enjoy it).
- *Once in a blue moon* (very rarely).
- *I was over the moon* (very happy, about a specific event).
- *It costs an arm and a leg* (very expensive).

One or two in the whole test, used naturally, is enough. Forced idioms ("It's raining cats and dogs" when asked about your favourite season) sound memorised.

## 4. Paraphrase when you forget

*What's the word... it's the person who checks your teeth* (dentist). *A kind of machine that...* Paraphrase is a skill the examiner hears positively; silence is not.

<div class="myth">

**"I should learn a list of advanced idioms to use in every answer."** Idioms out of context sound strange and can lower the impression of control. Precise, natural everyday vocabulary is far more valuable.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Prepare topic phrases, not single words, for common topics.
- Phrasal verbs and everyday collocations sound natural in Speaking.
- One or two well-chosen idioms are enough; never force them.
- Paraphrase when you forget a word: "it's a kind of..."
- Precision and naturalness beat rare words.
