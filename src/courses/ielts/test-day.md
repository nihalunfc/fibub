**Module 11 · about 12 minutes.** By the end you will know what happens on the day, so nothing surprises you.

<div class="bigidea">On test day you should only be thinking about English. Prepare everything else (documents, travel, timing, food) the day before.</div>

## 1. The day before

- Check the **confirmation email** from your centre: address, arrival time, rules.
- Put your **ID** (the same one you booked with) by the door.
- Plan the journey and arrive early. Late arrivals may not be allowed in.
- No new study. A light review of your error list, then rest.

## 2. At the centre

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Check-in:</b> ID check, photo and sometimes a fingerprint. Personal items go into storage, including phones and watches.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Listening (about 30 minutes):</b> instructions, then four recordings played once. Paper test: about 10 minutes at the end to transfer answers. Computer test: a short time to check.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Reading (60 minutes):</b> no transfer time on paper; write answers straight onto the answer sheet.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Writing (60 minutes):</b> you choose the order and manage your own time.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Speaking (11 to 14 minutes):</b> same day or within about a week, depending on the centre.</p></div>
</div>

Usually there are no breaks between Listening, Reading and Writing. Use the toilet before you start.

## 3. Rules that catch people out

- On paper: pencil for Listening and Reading; pen or pencil for Writing (check your centre).
- Write answers in the right box; spelling counts; capitals are fine.
- Raise your hand for anything you need. Don't talk to other candidates.

## 4. Staying calm

- **One question at a time.** A hard section doesn't mean the next one will be.
- **If you miss a Listening answer, let it go** and catch the next. Chasing it costs two more.
- **In Speaking, breathe and slow down.** The examiner is not trying to trick you.

<div class="myth">

**"If the examiner looks serious, I'm doing badly."** Examiners are trained to stay neutral with every candidate. Their face tells you nothing about your score.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Day before: ID, confirmation email, route, rest.
- Phones and watches go into storage; the clock in the room is your guide.
- Listening has transfer time on paper only; Reading answers go straight onto the sheet.
- No breaks between the written papers.
- Stay with the current question; let missed answers go.

</div>
