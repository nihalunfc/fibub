**Module 8 · about 15 minutes.** By the end you will know the features that make speech easy to understand and have a way to practise them.

<div class="bigidea">Clear English is about stress and rhythm as much as individual sounds. Stress the right syllable, stress the important words in a sentence, and let the small words go quickly.</div>

## 1. Word stress

Every word of two or more syllables has one stressed syllable: louder, longer and a little higher. Getting it wrong can make a word hard to recognise, even with perfect sounds. Tap a word to hear it:

<figure class="fig" data-fig="stress-player"><figcaption>Your device reads each word aloud with its own voice; nothing is sent anywhere. Capitals mark the stressed syllable.</figcaption></figure>

Patterns that help:

- **-tion, -sion, -ic, -ical:** stress the syllable before: inforMAtion, deCIsion, ecoNOmic, poLItical.
- **-ity, -ify:** stress two before: abILity, elecTRIcity.
- **Two-syllable nouns vs verbs:** a REcord / to reCORD, an INcrease / to inCREASE.

## 2. Sentence stress and rhythm

In a sentence, stress the content words (nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs) and say the small words (a, the, to, of, and, can) quickly and weakly.

> I **WENT** to the **MARket** on **SATurday** to **BUY** some **FRUIT**.

English has a "beat" on the stressed words. Read sentences aloud tapping the table on each stress.


Tap the syllable you think is stressed, then see the answer. Press Next word to continue.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="@lang-syllable-beats"><figcaption>Notice the patterns: -tion and -ic pull the stress to the syllable before them.</figcaption></figure>

## 3. Endings that carry grammar

| Ending | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| -ed | /t/ after p, k, f, s, sh, ch | worked, stopped, watched |
| | /d/ after voiced sounds | played, lived, opened |
| | /ɪd/ after t, d | wanted, needed |
| -s | /s/, /z/ or /ɪz/ | cats, dogs, buses |

Dropping these endings makes your grammar sound wrong even when it is right.


Pick a sentence and press Play: the ball lands on every stressed syllable.

## 4. Chunking and intonation

Pause between ideas, not in the middle of them: *"When I was a child | I used to spend every summer | at my grandparents' farm."* Let your voice fall at the end of statements and rise for yes/no questions. Flat intonation can make you sound uninterested.

<div class="myth">

**"I must lose my accent to get band 7."** Many high-band speakers have clear regional or first-language accents. Work on the features that block understanding: stress, endings, and sounds that change meaning (ship/sheep, light/right in some languages).

</div>

## 5. Practise

<div class="keypoints">

- Any accent is fine; clarity is what counts.
- Word stress: one strong syllable per word; it moves in word families.
- Sentence stress: content words strong, small words weak, with a steady beat.
- Pronounce -ed and -s endings: they carry grammar.
- Pause between ideas, and let intonation rise and fall naturally.

</div>
