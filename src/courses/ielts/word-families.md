**Module 10 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will build four words from one, and choose the right form from the grammar around a gap.

<div class="bigidea">Learn words in families. One root gives you a noun, a verb, an adjective and an adverb, and the sentence tells you which one it needs.</div>

## 1. One root, four jobs

| Noun | Verb | Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|---|---|
| economy, economist | economise | economic, economical | economically |
| benefit | benefit | beneficial | beneficially |
| reliability | rely | reliable | reliably |
| significance | signify | significant | significantly |
| competition, competitor | compete | competitive | competitively |

## 2. Clues from the sentence

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>After a/the/adjective</b> → noun: "a significant <u>increase</u>".</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>After a modal or to</b> → base verb: "can <u>improve</u>", "to <u>compete</u>".</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Before a noun, or after be/seem/become</b> → adjective: "a <u>reliable</u> service", "it became <u>competitive</u>".</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Describing a verb or adjective</b> → adverb: "rose <u>significantly</u>", "<u>highly</u> competitive".</p></div>
</div>

## 3. Where it matters in IELTS

- **Listening and Reading gaps:** the right form is the only one that fits.
- **Writing Task 1:** switch between "rose sharply" and "a sharp rise" for variety.
- **Paraphrase:** change the form to change the structure: "people rely on cars" → "car reliance".

<div class="myth">

**"I know the word, so I know all its forms."** Many forms are irregular: economic (about the economy) vs economical (saving money). Learn the family, not the single word.

</div>

## 4. Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="english" data-topics="word-families,prefixes-suffixes" data-mode="practice" data-n="15"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Learn each word with its noun, verb, adjective and adverb.
- The words around a gap tell you the form.
- Noun suffixes: -tion, -ment, -ness, -ity. Adjective: -ful, -ous, -ive, -able. Adverb: -ly.
- Switch forms for variety and paraphrase.
- Watch tricky pairs: economic/economical, historic/historical.

</div>
