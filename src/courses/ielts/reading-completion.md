**Module 4 · about 16 minutes.** Read first: [Form, note and table completion](/learn/courses/ielts/form-note-completion/) from Listening; the skills carry over. By the end you will fill gaps in summaries, notes, tables and flow charts with the exact word the marker expects.

<div class="bigidea">A completion task is a short version of one part of the passage. Your job is to find that part, then copy the exact word that fits both the meaning and the grammar of the gap.</div>

## 1. Four shapes, one skill

| Shape | What you see | Order |
|---|---|---|
| Summary | a paragraph with gaps | usually follows the text, but may cover only one section |
| Notes | headings and bullet points with gaps | follows the text |
| Table | rows and columns | read across rows; may jump around the text |
| Flow chart | boxes and arrows (a process) | follows the steps of the process |

There are two kinds of summary:

- **Words from the passage**: "Choose NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS from the passage." You copy.
- **Words from a box**: "Choose the correct letter, A to J." You pick from a list. The box words are usually paraphrases, so you will not find them in the text.

## 2. The method

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>1. Read the instructions twice.</b> Circle the word limit: ONE WORD ONLY, NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS, ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER. Going over the limit scores zero, even if the word is right.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>2. Read the title and the whole summary</b> (not just the gap). It tells you which part of the passage it covers.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>3. Predict each gap.</b> Noun or verb? Singular or plural? A number, a person, a place? Look at the words just before and after: "a ______ of" needs a noun; "were ______ by" needs a past participle.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>4. Find the place</b> by scanning for the words next to the gap, or their synonyms. Read the sentence carefully.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>5. Copy the word exactly</b>, with the same spelling, then read the completed sentence. It must be grammatical and say what the passage says.</p></div>
</div>


Tap a phrase from the question, then the passage phrase that means the same.

## 3. Worked example: words from the passage

Passage (from "Bees on the rooftops"): *"Companies that want to help can plant native wildflowers, leave patches of bare earth for ground-nesting bees and stop mowing grass verges so often."*

Summary: *Businesses can support wild bees by planting native (1) ______, leaving bare (2) ______ where bees can nest, and cutting (3) ______ less often.* NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS.

1. After "native", a plural noun: **wildflowers**.
2. After "bare", a noun: **earth**. "Patches of bare earth" would be four words and the wrong grammar.
3. A noun that is cut: **grass verges** (two words, allowed). "Verges" alone is also usually accepted; "grass verges so" is wrong.

## 4. Worked example: a box of words

Summary: *Rising hive numbers in one capital led to (4) ______ of food for each colony, and each hive produced (5) ______ honey.*

Box: **A** less **B** a shortage **C** more **D** a surplus **E** better

The passage says "the amount of nectar and pollen available per colony fell, and so did the amount of honey each hive produced". Fell = (4) **B** a shortage; (5) **A** less. "Shortage" is not in the passage: that is normal with a box.

## 5. Tables and flow charts

- **Tables**: read the column heading and the row heading. The gap must fit both. If the "Material" column has "steel" and "rubber" above the gap, the gap is a material.
- **Flow charts**: each box is one step of a process. Find the step before and the step after in the text; the gap is between them. Signal words in the passage help: *first, then, once, after this, finally*.

<div class="myth">

**"I'll write the answer in my own words; it means the same."** Without a word box, the answer must be the exact words from the passage. A synonym scores zero, and so does a changed form ("build" for "building").

</div>

## 6. Practise

<div class="keypoints">

- Obey the word limit exactly; too many words scores zero.
- Read the whole summary first to find which part of the passage it covers.
- Predict the type of word for each gap from the grammar around it.
- Copy the passage's exact word and spelling; with a word box, expect paraphrases.
- Tables: fit both the row and the column. Flow charts: find the step before and after.
