**Module 2 · about 14 minutes.** Read first: [How the Listening test works](/learn/courses/ielts/listening-overview/). By the end you will be able to use the 30 seconds before each part to predict the type, grammar and wording of every answer.

<div class="bigidea">If you know what kind of answer you are waiting for, you hear it. If you don't, it passes by like a station you didn't know was yours.</div>

## 1. Why prediction works

Think about waiting for a friend at a busy station. If you know they wear a red coat, your eyes jump to every red coat and ignore everything else. Prediction does the same for your ears. You can't listen to every word with equal attention for 30 minutes, so you decide in advance which words matter.

In the real test you get about 30 seconds before each part (and a short pause half way through Parts 1 to 3). Use every second of it.

## 2. The three questions to ask for every gap

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>1. What kind of information?</b> A number, price, date, time, name, place, object, reason, adjective? Look at the words around the gap: "£", "at ___ pm", "Mr ___", "located near the ___".</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>2. What grammar?</b> Singular or plural noun ("a ___" or "several ___")? A verb in "-ing"? An adjective before a noun? The answer must fit the sentence exactly, so the grammar narrows it down.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>3. How might it be said?</b> Think of one or two other ways the speaker could express the words near the gap. "Cost" might be "you'll pay", "it comes to" or "the fee is". This is the paraphrase you will actually hear.</p></div>
</div>


Pick a phrase and slide Speed from careful to natural.

## 3. Worked example

Here are notes from a Part 2 question about a nature reserve. Before reading the predictions, try them yourself.

> **Riverside Nature Reserve**
> - Open every day except (1) ______
> - Best place to see birds: the (2) ______ near the lake
> - Visitors should not bring (3) ______
> - Guided walks cost £(4) ______ for adults
> - Walk leaders are mostly (5) ______

| Gap | Kind | Grammar | Possible paraphrase |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A day, or a holiday | A noun (probably a weekday, or "Christmas Day") | "closed on...", "the only day we shut is..." |
| 2 | A place or building | A singular noun after "the" | "the hide", "the tower": listen for "if you want to watch birds" |
| 3 | Things | Probably a plural noun ("dogs", "bicycles") | "please leave your ___ at home", "___ are not allowed" |
| 4 | A price | A number | "adults pay", "it's ___ for grown-ups" |
| 5 | People | A plural noun, perhaps a job or group ("volunteers", "students") | "most of our guides are..." |

Now notice how much easier the recording becomes: you are listening for a day, a place, some things, a price and a group of people, in that order.

<div class="myth">

**"I should read the questions slowly and carefully."** You don't have time. Skim the instructions (word limit), then the words around each gap, and make a tiny note: "#" for a number, "pl" for plural, a question mark for a place. Speed beats depth here.

</div>

## 4. Prediction for multiple choice and matching

For multiple choice, underline the **key idea** of the question and notice how the options differ. If the question is "Why did Sara choose this course?", the options might be about cost, location and timetable. Then you listen for **her reason**, and expect the others to be mentioned too (they are distractors, Lesson 2.5).

For matching, read the list of options first and give each a one-word label in your head. When you hear the speaker discuss each item, you only have to match it to a label.

## 5. Practise

Use the reading time exactly as in the test: look at the questions for 30 seconds, note your predictions, then press play. After answering, compare your predictions with the transcript.

<div class="keypoints">

- Use every second of reading time before each part and in the mid-part pause.
- For each gap ask: what kind of information, what grammar, how might it be said?
- Words around the gap are clues: £, "at", "a/an", "Mr", "several".
- For multiple choice, find how the options differ; for matching, label each option.
- Make tiny notes (#, pl, ?) instead of reading slowly.
