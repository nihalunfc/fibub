**Module 10 · about 14 minutes.** The last vocabulary lesson. Read with: [Spotting the paraphrase](/learn/courses/ielts/spot-the-paraphrase/) in Reading. By the end you will paraphrase any sentence in five ways.

<div class="bigidea">Paraphrase is the skill underneath the whole test. Listening and Reading questions paraphrase the text; Writing and Speaking reward you for not repeating words.</div>

## 1. Five techniques

| Technique | Original | Paraphrase |
|---|---|---|
| Synonym | Many **people** **buy** food online. | Many **consumers** **purchase** groceries online. |
| Word form | The government **decided** to... | The government's **decision** to... |
| Structure | **Some people think** that... | **It is sometimes argued** that... |
| Active ↔ passive | The council **built** a new library. | A new library **was built** by the council. |
| General ↔ specific | **Cars, buses and lorries** cause... | **Road traffic** causes... |

## 2. Combine them

> *Some people believe that children should learn a second language at primary school.*
>
> *It is sometimes argued that young pupils ought to start studying a foreign language before secondary school.*

Structure (it is argued), synonyms (pupils, foreign language), specific → general (primary school → before secondary school).

## 3. Watch the meaning

- Synonyms are rarely perfect: *big* and *major* are close; *big* and *wide* are not.
- Keep quantities and certainty: "some" ≠ "most"; "may" ≠ "will".
- Technical words with no synonym (internet, carbon dioxide) can stay.

<div class="myth">

**"A paraphrase must change every word."** Change enough to show your own language, and keep the meaning exact. Forced synonyms that change the meaning lose more than they gain.

</div>
