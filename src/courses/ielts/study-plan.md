**Module 1 · about 14 minutes.** By the end you will have a printed week-by-week plan, a daily routine, and a simple system to learn from your own mistakes, so you can prepare well without a teacher.

<div class="bigidea">Practice tests measure. Learning happens when you study why you got something wrong and fix that pattern.</div>

## 1. Start with a diagnostic

Before you study anything, find out where you are. Take one full timed practice test (Lesson 11.1 shows how, and the practice room has full sets). Do it under test conditions: one sitting, a timer, no dictionary. Then write down four numbers, your band in each skill, next to your target from Lesson 1.4.

The biggest gap is your **priority skill**. It gets the most hours, every week.

## 2. How many hours?

A common rough guide is that a learner needs **about 150 to 200 hours** of focused study to rise one band from around 5 or 6 (it is slower higher up, and it varies a lot from person to person). Use it to check your plan is realistic:

<div class="problem" data-problem="study-hours"></div>

If you have a fixed test date and not enough hours, you have three choices: add hours, move the test, or first aim for a smaller step such as half a band.

## 3. Build your plan

<figure class="fig" data-fig="study-plan"><figcaption>Set your weeks, hours and weakest skill to get a week-by-week plan. It is made in this page and nothing is saved; print it or copy it.</figcaption></figure>

Every good plan has the same three phases:

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Phase 1, learn the test (about a quarter of your time).</b> Work through Modules 1 to 4 in order. For each question type, learn the method and do the practice in the lesson. You should finish this phase knowing exactly what every question will look like.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Phase 2, build the skills (about half).</b> Writing and Speaking lessons, grammar and vocabulary every day, and daily practice-room sets focused on your weakest question types. Give your priority skill about 40% of the time.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Phase 3, test yourself (the last quarter).</b> One full timed mock a week, then a careful review of every mistake. Finish with Module 11: test day and the last two weeks.</p></div>
</div>

## 4. A daily routine (90 minutes)

| Minutes | What | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | Vocabulary and spelling review: 10 words from your notebook | Module 10, practice room |
| 25 | One lesson, or one section of a lesson, from your plan | This course |
| 30 | Timed practice in the practice room: one Listening part or one Reading passage, or one writing task | Practice room |
| 15 | Mistake review: log every wrong answer (see below) | Your notebook |
| 10 | Speaking: answer three Part 1 questions out loud, recording yourself on your phone | Module 8 |

On a busy day, do the 10-minute vocabulary review and the 10-minute speaking practice. Short daily practice beats one long session a week.

## 5. The mistake log: your teacher

Without a teacher, your mistake log is how you get feedback. For every wrong answer, write one line:

| Date | Skill and type | My answer | Right answer | Why I got it wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 Mar | Listening, form | 15th | 50th | Mixed up fifteen and fifty |
| 3 Mar | Reading, TFNG | False | Not Given | Used my own knowledge, not the text |
| 4 Mar | Reading, completion | lighthouses | lighthouse | Plural: checked grammar of the gap |

After a week, read the last column. You will see the same three or four reasons again and again. Those reasons are what you study next: each lesson in this course has a "traps" section for exactly this.

<div class="myth">

**"I'll just do lots of practice tests."** Taking test after test without reviewing mistakes mostly measures the same band again. Learners who study fewer tests but review every mistake improve faster.

</div>

## 6. Writing and Speaking without a teacher

- **Writing**: after each task, compare yours with the model answer in the practice room and tick the self-check list (word count, an overview for Task 1, a clear position for Task 2, paragraphs, a range of sentences). Leave your essay for a day, then read it aloud: you will hear mistakes you could not see.
- **Speaking**: record yourself answering the cue cards in the practice room, with the timers. Listen back once for fluency (long pauses, "umm"), once for grammar, once for pronunciation. Lesson 8.5 gives you a scoring sheet.

<div class="keypoints">

- Begin with one timed diagnostic test; your biggest gap is your priority skill.
- Plan with realistic hours (a rough guide: 150 to 200 focused hours per band from band 5 or 6).
- Three phases: learn the test, build skills, test yourself with weekly mocks.
- Study a little every day; a 90-minute routine covers all four skills.
- Keep a mistake log and study the reasons that repeat: it is your teacher.

</div>
