<div class="bigidea">Task 1 is a short factual report. Choose the most important features, give an overview of them, and support it with accurate numbers. You are a reporter, not an analyst: no reasons, no opinions.</div>

## 1. The task

You see one visual (sometimes two) and an instruction like this:

> The chart below shows the number of visitors to three museums in one city between 2010 and 2020. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant. Write at least 150 words.

Three phrases in that instruction tell you what to do:

| Phrase | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| **Summarise** | Do not copy the chart into words. Group and shorten. |
| **Selecting and reporting the main features** | Choose what matters most (the biggest, the smallest, the trend, the exception) and leave small details out. |
| **Make comparisons where relevant** | Put things side by side: higher, lower, twice as many, while, whereas. |

You have about **20 minutes** and must write **at least 150 words**. Task 2 counts twice as much as Task 1, so do not let Task 1 eat its time.

## 3. How it is marked, in plain words

Examiners score four areas equally. These are our own explanations of what each area looks at; the official public descriptions are on the IELTS website (see the sources).

| Area | The examiner asks | You show it by |
|---|---|---|
| **Task achievement** | Did you report the main features accurately, with an overview, and enough detail? | a clear overview, accurate numbers, nothing invented, at least 150 words |
| **Coherence and cohesion** | Is it organised and easy to follow? | logical paragraphs, linking words used naturally, clear references (it, this, the former) |
| **Lexical resource** | How wide and accurate is your vocabulary? | varied words for change and comparison, correct collocations, correct spelling |
| **Grammatical range and accuracy** | How varied and correct is your grammar? | a mix of simple and complex sentences, correct tenses, few errors |

Because the four areas count equally, a report with perfect numbers but poor grammar cannot score highly, and neither can beautiful English with no overview.

## 4. The four-paragraph shape

Almost every Task 1 report can use the same shape:

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>1. Introduction (1 sentence).</b> Paraphrase the task: what the visual shows, where and when. Do not copy the question.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>2. Overview (1 to 2 sentences).</b> The two or three biggest features, with no numbers: the main trend, the highest and lowest, the biggest change. This is the most important paragraph (Lesson 5.2).</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>3. Detail paragraph 1 (2 to 3 sentences).</b> One group of the data, with figures and comparisons.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>4. Detail paragraph 2 (2 to 3 sentences).</b> The other group, with figures and comparisons.</p></div>
</div>

No conclusion is needed: the overview does that job. Some writers put the overview at the end, which is also fine; what matters is that it is there and easy to find.

## 5. A 20-minute plan

| Minutes | Do |
|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | Read the task and the visual: title, units, years, categories. Circle the 2 or 3 main features. Decide how to group the details. |
| 3 to 5 | Introduction and overview. |
| 5 to 16 | Two detail paragraphs. |
| 16 to 20 | Check: numbers, units, tense, articles, spelling, word count. |

<div class="myth">

**"Writing more words gets a higher band."** Length is not one of the four areas. A focused report of 170 to 200 words usually does better than 280 words of every number, because selecting is part of the task. Write at least 150, aim for about 180.

Open a few tasks from the bank and, before you write anything, practise steps 1 and 2: say the main features out loud in one sentence. Then read the model answer and compare.

<div class="keypoints">

- Task 1 is a factual report of at least 150 words in about 20 minutes; Task 2 counts twice as much.
- Summarise, select the main features and compare. Do not explain causes or give opinions.
- Four equally weighted areas: task achievement, coherence and cohesion, vocabulary, grammar.
- Shape: introduction, overview, two detail paragraphs. No conclusion is needed.
- Spend 3 minutes reading and planning, and 4 minutes checking.

</div>
