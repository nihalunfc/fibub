**Module 4 · about 15 minutes.** Read first: [Skimming and scanning](/learn/courses/ielts/skim-and-scan/). By the end you will find the main idea of any paragraph and match it to the right heading, avoiding the headings that only match a detail.

<div class="bigidea">A heading is the title of the whole paragraph, not of one sentence in it. Ask: "What is this paragraph mostly about?"</div>

## 1. What it looks like

You get a list of headings (i, ii, iii...) and a passage with lettered paragraphs. You choose the heading that fits each paragraph. There are more headings than paragraphs, and the answers do **not** follow any order.

## 2. The method

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Read the headings first</b> and underline the key idea of each: "the original reason", "a second concern", "practical advice".</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Read one paragraph</b>: first sentence, last sentence, and skim the middle. Say its main idea to yourself in five words.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Pick the heading</b> that matches your five words. If two seem to fit, ask which one covers the <i>whole</i> paragraph.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Cross it off</b> and move to the next paragraph. Leave the hardest paragraph to last, when fewer headings remain.</p></div>
</div>


Try matching: read each one-line summary and tap the heading that gives its main idea.

## 3. Worked example

Headings: **i** A fashion that may cause harm · **ii** Why cities seemed safe for bees · **iii** The many kinds of bee · **iv** How honey is made · **v** Competition for food · **vi** The risk of illness spreading · **vii** Flowers matter more than hives · **viii** First steps by local authorities

Using "Bees on the rooftops":

| Paragraph | Main idea in five words | Heading |
|---|---|---|
| A | Rooftop hives boom; maybe a problem | i |
| B | Bees died; cities offered refuge | ii |
| C | Most bees are wild, not honey | iii |
| D | Dense hives take wild bees' food | v |
| E | Viruses spread between species | vi |
| F | Plant flowers rather than hives | vii |
| G | Cities map flowers, register hives | viii |

Heading **iv** is the distractor: honey is mentioned (paragraph D: "the amount of honey each hive produced"), but no paragraph is about how honey is made.

<div class="myth">

**"The heading must use words from the paragraph."** Good headings almost always paraphrase. A heading that repeats a word from the paragraph is often a trap based on a detail.

</div>

## 4. Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="ielts" data-topics="reading-academic" data-mode="practice" data-n="8" data-facets="qtype=headings"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- The heading matches the whole paragraph's main idea, not a detail.
- Read the headings first; summarise each paragraph in five words.
- First and last sentences carry the main idea; skim the middle.
- Spare headings are distractors, often built on details.
- Answers are in no particular order; leave the hardest paragraph to last.

</div>
