**Module 2 · about 12 minutes.** Read first: [Part 4: following a lecture](/learn/courses/ielts/lecture-section-4/). By the end you will know the accent features that most often change how an answer sounds, and how to train for them in ten minutes a day.

<div class="bigidea">An accent changes some sounds, never the structure. If you know which sounds shift, you can still catch the answer.</div>

## 1. Which accents?

IELTS recordings use speakers with a range of accents: British (several regions), Australian, New Zealand, North American (US and Canadian) and others. Speakers are clear and speak at a natural pace; you won't hear a very strong regional accent, but you will hear real differences.

## 2. Differences that change answers

| Feature | British / Australian / NZ (common) | North American (common) | Answer it can affect |
|---|---|---|---|
| "r" after a vowel | silent: "car" = "cah" | said: "car" | "car park", "Thursday", "forty" |
| "t" between vowels | clear "t": "water" | soft "d" sound: "wadder" | "thirty", "twenty", "city", "Peter" |
| vowel in "bath, dance, can't" | long "ah" (southern England, Australia mixed) | short "a" | "half", "past" |
| "ay" vowel | Australian: "day" can sound close to "die" | "day" | "eight", "date", "Monday" |
| "z" | "zed" | "zee" | spelled postcodes and names |
| dates | "the fifth of May" | "May fifth" | dates in Part 1 |
| words | "flat, lift, mobile, timetable, postcode" | "apartment, elevator, cell, schedule, zip code" | Part 1 and 2 vocabulary |

## 3. Train your ear in ten minutes a day

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Pick one accent a week.</b> Listen to radio news, podcasts or university lectures from that country for ten minutes a day (public broadcasters and university lecture channels are free).</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Dictate one minute.</b> Choose 60 seconds, play it in short pieces and write down every word. Then check against a transcript where one exists. The words you miss show which sounds you need to learn.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Shadow it.</b> Play it again and speak along with the speaker, copying their rhythm. Saying the sounds helps you hear them.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Use the practice room voices.</b> Your device usually has several English voices (British, American, Australian, Indian and more). In the practice room, choose a different voice each day.</p></div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"I should learn one accent perfectly."** You need to understand all of them, not speak like any of them. Mixed exposure beats deep study of one.

</div>

## 4. Practise with different voices

<div class="keypoints">

- IELTS uses a range of accents: British, Australian, New Zealand, North American and others.
- Know the shifts: silent "r", soft "t", vowel changes, "zed/zee", and different words for the same thing.
- British and American spellings are both accepted.
- Ten minutes a day: listen, dictate a minute, shadow, and switch practice voices.
