**Module 4 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will answer direct questions and label diagrams with short, exact answers taken from the passage.

<div class="bigidea">Short answers are scanning with a word limit. The question word tells you the type of answer; the passage gives you the exact words.</div>

## 1. Short-answer questions

> Answer the questions below. Choose NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER from the passage.
> 1. What do the bearings in base isolation bend in response to?
> 2. How many workers can a strong colony contain in summer?

The questions **follow the order of the passage**. Turn each question word into the answer type:

| Question word | Answer type |
|---|---|
| What / Which + noun | a thing of that kind ("which material" → a material) |
| Who | a person, a job, a group |
| Where | a place |
| When / In which year | a time or date |
| How many / How much | a number (and maybe a unit) |
| How long | a length of time or distance |
| Why | a cause, often after "because" or "due to" in the text |

## 2. The method

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>1. Underline the key words</b> and decide the answer type from the question word.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>2. Scan</b> for the key words or their synonyms, after the place of the last answer.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>3. Read the sentence</b> and find the words of the right type.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>4. Cut to the limit.</b> Keep the words that answer the question; drop articles and extra adjectives if the limit is tight. Never add your own words.</p></div>
</div>

Example answers: 1. **the ground shakes** or **shaking ground** (copied as in the text). 2. **50,000** (a number alone is fine; "50,000 workers" is also within the limit).

## 3. Diagram and flow-chart labels

You get a drawing (a machine, a building, a plant, a process) with numbered gaps. The labels come from a section of the passage that describes the object.

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>1. Study the diagram first</b> for 20 seconds: what is it, which parts are already labelled, where are the gaps?</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>2. Find the describing section</b> by scanning for the labelled parts: if the diagram already says "foundations", find "foundations" in the text.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>3. Use position words</b> in the text: above, below, between, at the top, on the left, attached to, inside. They tell you which gap is which.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>4. Copy the exact words</b> within the limit, as in completion tasks.</p></div>
</div>

Example, from the base isolation passage: a diagram shows a building, a gap between it and the foundations, and a gap at the top. Text: *"flexible bearings, often layers of rubber and steel, between a building and its foundations"* and *"a heavy weight near the top"*. The gap between: **(flexible) bearings**. The gap at the top: **heavy weight** (in towers with a tuned mass damper).

<div class="myth">

**"I should explain the answer fully to get the mark."** Extra words over the limit score zero, even if every word is correct. Short is safe.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- The question word gives the answer type: who, where, when, how many, why.
- Short answers follow the passage order; diagram labels may not.
- Study the diagram first and use the labels already given to find the right section.
- Position words in the text (between, above, at the top) tell you which gap is which.
- Copy exact words and stay within the limit; numbers can be written as digits.
