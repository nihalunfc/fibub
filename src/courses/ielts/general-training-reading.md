**Module 4 · about 17 minutes.** For General Training candidates; Academic candidates can skip it. Read first: [The Reading paper](/learn/courses/ielts/reading-overview/) and [A time plan for 60 minutes](/learn/courses/ielts/reading-time-plan/). By the end you will know what each of the three sections contains, how to read short everyday texts fast, and how to plan your hour.

<div class="bigidea">General Training Reading starts easy and gets harder. Win the easy marks fast in Sections 1 and 2, and carry the time you save into Section 3.</div>

## 1. The three sections

Like Academic, you get 60 minutes and 40 questions, with the same question types you met in this module. The texts are different:

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Section 1, social survival.</b> Two or three short texts from everyday life: adverts, notices, timetables, leaflets, a hotel or gym information page. Often several short texts labelled A to E with matching questions ("Which advert mentions free parking?"), plus True/False/Not Given and short answers. About 14 questions.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Section 2, workplace survival.</b> Two texts about work: a job advert, a staff handbook, a training guide, a health and safety notice, a page about pay or leave. Sentence and note completion, matching headings, short answers. About 13 questions.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Section 3, general reading.</b> One long text (700 to 900 words) on a topic of general interest, written for a general reader: history, nature, a social trend. All the Academic question types appear here. About 13 questions.</p></div>
</div>

## 2. Why you need more correct answers

The texts are easier, so the conversion from raw score to band is stricter. For example, roughly 30 correct answers out of 40 is about band 7 in Academic but only about band 6 in General Training (Lesson 1.3 has the table, and the conversion varies a little from test to test).

<div class="problem" data-problem="reading-raw"></div>

In practice this means you **cannot afford careless mistakes in Sections 1 and 2**. They are the marks everyone at your level gets.

## 3. Reading notices and adverts (Section 1)

Short everyday texts are built to be scanned, so use their layout:

- **Headings, bold words, prices and times** are where the answers are. Notices put the important information in the first line and in bold.
- **Matching across several adverts**: read the question, then scan all of A to E for that one detail. Do not read each advert in full. Watch for "except", "only", "not included" and asterisks (*) with small print.
- **Numbers are traps.** A notice may give two prices (member, non-member) or two times (summer, winter). Check which one the question asks about.

Example. Two pool notices:

> **A** Riverside Pool · Lane swimming 6:30 to 9:00 every weekday · Adults 6 euros, under-16s free with a paying adult
> **B** Hillcrest Leisure · Lane swimming 7:00 to 10:00, weekends only · Adults 5 euros, members free

"Which pool would suit an adult who wants to swim before work on Tuesday?" **A** (weekdays from 6:30). "Which pool is free for some children?" **A**. "Which is cheaper for an adult non-member?" **B**.

## 4. Workplace texts (Section 2)

Workplace texts use formal, rule-like language. Learn the signal words:

| Text says | It means |
|---|---|
| must, are required to, it is compulsory | an obligation (TRUE for "staff have to...") |
| should, are advised to, are encouraged to | a recommendation, not a rule |
| may, are entitled to, are eligible for | a right or a choice |
| no later than, at least ... in advance | a deadline |
| unless, except, provided that | a condition: read it carefully |

"Staff must give at least two weeks' notice" and "Staff should try to give two weeks' notice" are different rules. A statement saying "Staff are required to give two weeks' notice" is TRUE for the first and FALSE (or NOT GIVEN) for the second.

## 5. Section 3

Treat it like an Academic passage: skim for the main idea of each paragraph, then use the methods from this module. The main difference is the topic, which is usually lighter and closer to everyday life.

## 6. A time plan

| Section | Minutes | Aim |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | about 15 | all correct; move fast |
| 2 | about 20 | careful with rules and conditions |
| 3 | about 22 | the long text, harder questions |
| Check | about 3 | blanks, spelling, the answer sheet (paper test) |

If Section 1 takes you more than 18 minutes in practice, you are reading the short texts in full. Scan.

<div class="myth">

**"Section 3 is worth more marks."** Every question is worth one mark. That is exactly why the easy marks in Sections 1 and 2 matter so much.

</div>

## 7. Practise

<div class="keypoints">

- Three sections: everyday notices, workplace texts, one long general text. Difficulty rises.
- General Training needs more correct answers for the same band, so Sections 1 and 2 must be near perfect.
- Scan short texts for the one detail; watch for two prices, two times and small print.
- Workplace rules: must (obligation), should (advice), may (choice), unless (condition).
- Plan about 15, 20 and 22 minutes, plus 3 to check.
