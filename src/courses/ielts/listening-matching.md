**Module 2 · about 12 minutes.** Read first: [Multiple choice without the traps](/learn/courses/ielts/listening-multiple-choice/). By the end you will handle matching questions, where you link a list of items to a set of options while the speakers move quickly from one to the next.

<div class="bigidea">Matching is multiple choice with one shared set of options. Know the options so well that you can match a paraphrase in two seconds.</div>

## 1. What it looks like

> What does the tutor say about each part of the project? Choose your answers from the box and write the correct letter, A to F, next to questions 21 to 25.
>
> **A** needs more detail · **B** should be shorter · **C** is the strongest part · **D** has a mistake · **E** needs a source · **F** can be removed
>
> 21 Introduction · 22 Survey · 23 Graphs · 24 Interviews · 25 Conclusion

It is common in Parts 2 and 3: features of places, opinions about items, people and their roles.

## 2. The method

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Label the options.</b> A = more detail, B = cut, C = best, D = error, E = source, F = delete. Short, so you can scan them in a glance.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Expect paraphrases.</b> "It's a bit long" = B; "I'd take it out altogether" = F; "That's where your work really shines" = C.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Follow the numbered items</b>, which are in order. When the speakers move to "the graphs", your eyes go to 23.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Wait for the final verdict.</b> "At first I thought the graphs had an error, but no, they're fine. They just need a source." Answer: E, not D.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Cross out used options</b> if each can be used only once. It makes the last ones quicker.</p></div>
</div>

## 3. The traps

- **Two options for one item**: speakers discuss two ideas about the survey and reject one.
- **Options mentioned for the wrong item**: "The introduction is fine. Now the survey: that could be much shorter." B goes with 22, not 21.
- **A different speaker disagrees**: in Part 3, a student's opinion is not the tutor's. Check whose view the question asks for.

<div class="myth">

**"If I miss one, the rest are lost."** Only if you keep waiting for it. When the speakers clearly move to the next item, move with them and leave the gap; guess at the end from the unused options.

</div>

## 4. Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="ielts" data-topics="listening-part2,listening-part3" data-mode="practice" data-n="10" data-facets="qtype=matching"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- One shared option list; check whether options can be used more than once.
- Give each option a one-word label; expect paraphrase, not the same words.
- The numbered items come in order; the options do not.
- Wait for the final verdict and for the right speaker's view.
- Cross out used options; guess from the unused ones at the end.

</div>
