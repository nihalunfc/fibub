**Module 4 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will join sentence beginnings to the right endings using two quick filters, grammar and meaning, and you will know why the most attractive wrong ending is usually a true fact from the passage.

<div class="bigidea">The beginning tells you where to look and what kind of words must come next. The passage decides which ending is true.</div>

## 1. What it looks like

> Complete each sentence with the correct ending, A to G.
> 1. Base isolation protects a building by...
> 2. Tuned mass dampers are most useful in...
>
> A tall, slender towers. · B allowing the ground to move beneath it. · C making the walls thicker. · D older stone buildings. · E reducing the cost of steel. · F separating it from the movement of the ground. · G bridges with long spans.

There are more endings than beginnings. Unlike the matching types in the last two lessons, the beginnings **follow the order of the passage**.

## 2. Two filters

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Filter 1, grammar.</b> Read the beginning and ask what must come next. "...by" needs an -ing verb or a noun. "...most useful in" needs a place or a situation. "...because" needs a clause with a subject and verb. Cross out endings that cannot follow. This often removes half of them in seconds.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Filter 2, meaning.</b> Scan for the key words of the beginning ("base isolation"), read the sentences around them, and pick the ending that says what the passage says, in other words.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Read the whole sentence aloud in your head</b>, beginning plus ending. It must be grammatical and true to the passage.</p></div>
</div>

In the example: beginning 1 ends in "by", so only B, C, E and F (all -ing) survive the grammar filter. Beginning 2 ends in "in", so A, D and G survive. Now meaning decides.

## 3. Worked example

Passage extract:

> Base isolation places flexible bearings, often layers of rubber and steel, between a building and its foundations. When the ground shakes, the bearings bend, so that much less of the motion reaches the floors above. Tall, slender towers, which sway in strong winds as well as earthquakes, often use a different device: a tuned mass damper, a heavy weight near the top that moves against the building's motion.

1. **Base isolation protects a building by...** Grammar leaves B, C, E, F. The passage: bearings bend "so that much less of the motion reaches the floors above", which means the building is separated from the ground's movement. **F**. B is the trap: the ground *does* move beneath it, but that is not how it protects the building.
2. **Tuned mass dampers are most useful in...** Grammar leaves A, D, G. The passage: "Tall, slender towers... often use a different device: a tuned mass damper". **A**. G (bridges) may be true in real life, but the passage does not say it.

## 4. The traps

- **True but wrong subject.** An ending can be a fact from the passage that belongs to a different beginning. Ask: "Does the text say this about *this* subject?"
- **Your own knowledge.** An ending can be true in the world but not in the text. Only the text counts, exactly as in [True, False, Not Given](/learn/courses/ielts/true-false-not-given/).
- **Repeated words.** An ending that copies a word from the beginning or the passage is not automatically right. The correct ending is usually a paraphrase.
- **Half-match.** "A building of any height" vs "tall towers": check every part of the ending, including size, number and time words.

<div class="myth">

**"If it is grammatically correct, it must be right."** Grammar only removes the impossible endings. At least two endings will usually fit the grammar; meaning decides.

</div>

## 5. Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="ielts" data-topics="reading-academic-2,reading-general" data-mode="practice" data-n="10" data-facets="qtype=endings"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Beginnings follow the passage order; endings are a mixed list with extras.
- Filter 1: the last word of the beginning decides what grammar must follow.
- Filter 2: scan for the beginning's key words and choose the ending that paraphrases the passage.
- Distractors are often true facts from the passage about something else, or true in life but not in the text.
- Read beginning plus ending as one sentence before you decide.

</div>
