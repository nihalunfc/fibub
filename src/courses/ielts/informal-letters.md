**Module 6 · about 14 minutes.** By the end you will write a letter to a friend that sounds natural and warm and still covers the task completely.

<div class="bigidea">Write as if you are talking to a friend you respect: warm, relaxed, natural English with contractions and phrasal verbs, but still clear paragraphs, every bullet covered and no text-message spelling.</div>

## 1. The shape

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Warm opening (1 to 2 sentences).</b> A friendly line, then the reason: "Thanks so much for your letter. I'm writing because I've got some exciting news!"</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Bullet 1, bullet 2, bullet 3</b>, one paragraph each, with details that make it feel real: names, places, a small story.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Friendly close.</b> "Can't wait to hear what you think." / "Let me know if that works for you." Then "Best wishes," or "Love," and your first name.</p></div>
</div>

## 2. Natural informal language

| Instead of (formal) | Write (informal) |
|---|---|
| I am writing to inform you | I just wanted to let you know |
| I would like to invite you | I'd love it if you could come |
| Unfortunately, I am unable to | I'm really sorry, but I can't |
| Please inform me | Let me know |
| I apologise for | I'm so sorry about |
| In addition | Also, / On top of that, |
| However | But / Still, |
| I look forward to hearing from you | Write back soon! / Can't wait to hear from you. |

Phrasal verbs fit well here: *catch up, come over, look forward to, sort out, put you up, drop by*.


Pick a situation and slide Formality from very casual to very formal.

## 3. Model answer

Task: *A friend is coming to visit your city for the first time. Write a letter to your friend. In your letter, suggest when they should come, describe what you can do together, and give some advice about what to bring.* Begin: Dear Leo,

<div class="meaning">

**Model answer (about 185 words)**

Dear Leo,

I was so happy to get your message saying you're finally coming to visit! I've been hoping you'd make it here for ages.

If you can, try to come in late September. The summer crowds have gone by then, but it's still warm enough to sit outside in the evenings. The weekend of the 27th would be perfect, because there's a food festival down by the harbour that I think you'd really love.

There's loads we can do together. I'd like to take you on a boat trip around the islands, and if the weather's good we could hike up the hill behind my flat, which has an amazing view of the whole city. In the evenings I'll introduce you to some of my friends at our favourite little café.

As for packing, bring comfortable walking shoes, because the old town is very hilly, and a light jacket, as it can get windy near the sea. You don't need to bring towels or anything like that, since you'll be staying with me.

Can't wait to see you!

Best wishes,

Maya

</div>

Note the details that make it vivid (food festival, boat trip, hilly old town) and the reasons with *because*, *as* and *since*.

<div class="myth">

**"Informal letters can be shorter."** The 150-word minimum is the same. Aim for about 170 to 190 words, with details that bring each bullet to life.

</div>

## 4. Write one

<div class="qbank" data-bank="ielts" data-topics="writing-letters" data-mode="practice" data-n="4" data-sub="informal"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Warm opening, then the reason for writing.
- One paragraph per bullet, with small vivid details.
- Contractions and phrasal verbs are natural; slang, "u" and emojis are not.
- Give reasons with because, as and since.
- Friendly close, then Best wishes or Love and your first name.

</div>
