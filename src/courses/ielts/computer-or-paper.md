**Module 1 · about 11 minutes.** By the end you will know which delivery mode suits you, how to book, and what to check before you pay.

<div class="bigidea">Same test, different tools. Pick the mode that lets you spend your time on English, not on the pen or the keyboard.</div>

## 1. What is the same

Whichever you choose, the **questions, the timing, the marking and the bands** are the same, and the **Speaking test is always a live conversation** with a trained examiner. Organisations that accept IELTS accept both.

## 2. What is different

| | Paper | Computer (at a test centre) |
|---|---|---|
| Listening | Write on the question paper, then **10 minutes** to copy answers to the answer sheet | Type answers as you listen, then **2 minutes** to check. Headphones for everyone |
| Reading | Underline and write notes on the paper; answers on the answer sheet | Text on the left, questions on the right; you can highlight and make notes on screen |
| Writing | Handwritten in pencil; you count your own words | Typed; word counter on screen; you can cut, copy and paste |
| Results | Usually about 13 days after the test | Usually 1 to 5 days after the test |
| Test dates | Fewer dates, often Saturdays and Thursdays | More dates and times, often several a week |

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Choose computer if</b> you type at least as fast as you write, you like editing your writing, you want results quickly, or you need a date soon.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Choose paper if</b> you read better on paper, you like to underline and write all over the text, you rarely use a keyboard, or screens tire your eyes.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Try both before you decide.</b> Do one Reading passage on paper (print it from the practice room) and one on screen, timed. Do one Task 2 essay by hand and one typed. Compare your speed and accuracy.</p></div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Computer IELTS is easier."** The questions are the same and so is the marking. It only feels easier if typing and reading on screen suit you. The 10 minutes of Listening transfer time on paper can also be a help, since you can tidy up spelling then.

</div>

## 3. Booking checklist

1. **Confirm the module** (Academic or General Training) and whether you need **IELTS for UKVI** (Lesson 1.1).
2. **Pick a date that leaves time for a resit** before your deadline. Results take up to about two weeks, and you may want a second try or a One Skill Retake (Lesson 11.3).
3. **Book only on the official IELTS site** or the official British Council or IDP site for your country, never through a stranger or an unofficial agent. Fees vary by country.
4. **Use your passport (or the ID your centre accepts)** and type your name exactly as it appears on it. You must bring the same document on test day.
5. **Choose the Speaking slot**: on the same day as the written papers, or up to about a week before or after, depending on the centre.
6. **Ask for special arrangements early** if you need them (for example extra time, a larger print paper or a separate room for a medical reason). Centres usually need several weeks' notice and medical evidence.
7. **Choose who receives your result**: you can usually send results electronically to several organisations for free when you book or soon after.

<div class="keypoints">

- Computer and paper IELTS have the same questions, timing, marking and live Speaking test.
- Paper: 10 minutes to copy Listening answers; handwritten essays. Computer: typed essays with a word counter, faster results and more dates.
- Try both on practice material and pick the one where you are faster and more accurate.
- Book only through official IELTS, British Council or IDP sites, with the ID you will bring on the day, and leave time for a resit.

</div>
