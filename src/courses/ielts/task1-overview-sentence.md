**Module 5 · about 14 minutes.** Read first: [What Task 1 asks](/learn/courses/ielts/task1-academic-overview/). By the end you will find the main features of any visual in under two minutes and write a clear overview every time.

<div class="bigidea">If someone covered the numbers and asked "So what does the chart show?", your answer is the overview. Big picture only: trends, the highest and lowest, the biggest change.</div>

## 1. Why it matters so much

A report without a clear overview has not really summarised the information, which limits the task achievement score however good the rest is. It is the one sentence most candidates at band 5 and 6 forget, and the easiest one to fix.


Tap a stretch of the line (or its button) and pick the words that describe it.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="@lang-writing-trend"><figcaption>Notice that the size of the change picks the word: "sharply" for big and fast, "slightly" for small, "levelled off" for flat.</figcaption></figure>

## 2. Five questions that find the main features

Ask these about any chart. You need only two or three answers.

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>1. What is the general trend?</b> Up, down, stable, up then down? For several lines: do they move together or apart?</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>2. What is the biggest and the smallest?</b> The highest bar, the largest slice, the top row.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>3. What changed most?</b> The steepest line, the slice that grew most.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>4. Is there an exception?</b> The one line that went the other way, the one country that was different.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>5. For processes and maps:</b> how many stages, where it starts and ends; what the overall change to the place was.</p></div>
</div>

## 3. Overview patterns

Start with "Overall," or "In general," then one of these patterns:

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Two trends | Overall, the number of cyclists rose steadily, whereas car journeys fell. |
| Trend + biggest | Overall, all three museums became more popular, with the Science Museum attracting the most visitors throughout the period. |
| Biggest + smallest | In general, rent was by far the largest household expense, while transport accounted for the smallest share. |
| Exception | Overall, energy use rose in every sector except industry, which saw a slight decline. |
| Process | Overall, there are seven stages in the process, beginning with the collection of used glass and ending with new bottles ready for use. |
| Map | Overall, the village became more residential, with farmland giving way to housing and the transport links improving. |

## 4. Worked example

The data (thousands of visitors per year):

| Museum | 2010 | 2015 | 2020 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Science | 410 | 520 | 640 |
| Art | 300 | 290 | 350 |
| History | 220 | 180 | 120 |

Answer the five questions: (1) Science and Art up, History down. (2) Science highest throughout; History lowest. (3) Science grew most; History fell steadily. (4) History is the exception.

Overview: *"Overall, the Science and Art museums attracted more visitors over the decade, whereas the History museum became steadily less popular. The Science museum was the most visited throughout."*

No numbers, two sentences, and an examiner reading only these lines would know what the table shows.

## 5. Common mistakes

| Mistake | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers in the overview | "Overall, Science rose from 410 to 640." | Save numbers for the details: "Overall, Science grew the most." |
| A copy of the introduction | "Overall, the table shows visitors to three museums." | Say what happened, not what is shown. |
| A reason or an opinion | "Overall, History fell because people prefer science." | Report only. Causes are not in the table. |
| Too much | five features crammed into one sentence | Two or three features. |
| Hidden | the overview is in the middle of a detail paragraph | Give it its own short paragraph, starting "Overall,". |

<div class="myth">

**"The overview is a conclusion, so it goes at the end and repeats what I said."** It is a summary of the data, not a repeat of your paragraphs. It can go after the introduction or at the end. What matters is that it states the main features clearly.

</div>

## 6. Practise

Take any task from the bank. Give yourself two minutes and write only the overview, then compare with the model answer's overview.

<div class="qbank" data-bank="ielts" data-topics="writing-task1" data-mode="practice" data-n="6"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- The overview states the main features: trends, biggest and smallest, biggest change, exceptions.
- Two or three features, usually no numbers, starting "Overall," or "In general,".
- Give it its own paragraph after the introduction (or at the end).
- Not a copy of the introduction, not a reason, not an opinion.
- Without a clear overview, the task achievement score stays low.

</div>
