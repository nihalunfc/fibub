**Module 9 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will avoid the punctuation errors that make essays harder to read and lower accuracy.

<div class="bigidea">Punctuation shows where ideas begin and end. The one error to eliminate first is the comma splice: two sentences joined only by a comma.</div>

## 1. The comma splice

> ~~Many people cycle to work, it is cheaper than driving.~~

Fix it one of four ways:

1. Full stop: *Many people cycle to work. It is cheaper than driving.*
2. Joining word: *Many people cycle to work **because** it is cheaper.*
3. Semicolon: *Many people cycle to work; it is cheaper than driving.*
4. Subordinate clause: ***As** it is cheaper, many people cycle to work.*

## 2. Commas that are needed

| Where | Example |
|---|---|
| after an introductory phrase or clause | In 2020, sales fell. / Although it is expensive, it is popular. |
| around non-defining relative clauses | My manager, who is Canadian, speaks French. |
| after sentence linkers | However, ... Therefore, ... For example, ... |
| in lists | buses, trains and trams |

## 3. Linkers: however, therefore, moreover

These connect **sentences**, not clauses: *Prices rose. **However,** sales stayed high.* or *Prices rose; **however,** sales stayed high.*
Inside a sentence, use **but**, **so**, **although**: *Prices rose, **but** sales stayed high.*

## 4. Apostrophes, capitals, hyphens

- **its** (possessive) vs **it's** (it is); **students'** (plural possessive) vs **student's** (one student).
- **Capitals:** days, months, nationalities, languages, countries: *Monday, March, Canadian, English*. Not seasons: *summer*.
- **Hyphens** in compound adjectives before a noun: *a well-paid job, a 20-minute walk, a two-bedroom flat*.

<div class="myth">

**"A comma goes wherever I would pause."** Commas follow grammar, not breathing. Many long pauses need no comma, and a comma cannot join two sentences.

</div>

## 5. Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="english" data-topics="punctuation" data-mode="practice" data-n="15"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Never join two sentences with just a comma.
- Comma after introductory phrases and sentence linkers; commas round non-defining clauses.
- However / therefore start a new sentence or follow a semicolon.
- its vs it's; student's vs students'.
- Capitals for days, months, nationalities and languages; hyphens in compound adjectives.

</div>
