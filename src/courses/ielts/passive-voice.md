**Module 9 · about 14 minutes.** By the end you will form the passive in any tense and know when it helps your writing.

<div class="bigidea">The passive puts the thing affected first: "The bridge was built in 1998." Use it when the action matters more than who did it, which is often true in processes, reports and formal essays.</div>

## 1. Form: be + past participle

| Tense | Passive |
|---|---|
| Present simple | The glass **is crushed**. |
| Present continuous | The road **is being repaired**. |
| Past simple | The factory **was converted** into flats. |
| Present perfect | A new station **has been built**. |
| Past perfect | The trees **had been cut down** by 2010. |
| Future / modal | Fees **will be raised**. Cars **should be banned**. |

## 2. Where IELTS needs it

- **Process diagrams** (Lesson 5.6): *The leaves are collected, dried and then ground into powder.*
- **Maps** (Lesson 5.7): *The woodland has been replaced by a car park.*
- **Essays:** *More money should be spent on prevention.* / *It is often argued that...*
- **Reporting:** *It is believed that... / X is thought to be...*

## 3. When not to use it

- When the doer is important: *The government should fund it* is clearer than *It should be funded*.
- When it makes the sentence wordy: *A decision was made by me to leave* → *I decided to leave*.
- Mostly active in Speaking; the passive sounds formal in conversation.

<div class="myth">

**"Formal writing should be mostly passive."** Good academic writing mixes both. Too many passives hide who is responsible and make sentences heavy.

</div>

## 4. Practise

<div class="keypoints">

- Form: be (in the right tense) + past participle.
- Use it for processes, maps, reports and formal proposals.
- Modals: should be / must be / could be + past participle.
- "It is believed / argued that..." for reporting views.
- Prefer the active when the doer matters or the passive sounds heavy.
