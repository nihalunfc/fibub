**Module 7 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will write a two-sentence introduction for any essay type in about three minutes.

<div class="bigidea">An introduction does two jobs: show you understood the topic (paraphrase) and tell the examiner your answer (thesis). Two sentences is enough.</div>

## 1. Sentence 1: paraphrase the topic

Change the words and the structure, keep the meaning.

| Prompt | Paraphrase |
|---|---|
| More and more people are working from home. | A growing number of employees now do their jobs from home rather than in an office. |
| Some people think that university education should be free. | It is sometimes argued that students should not have to pay tuition fees. |
| Children today spend too much time on screens. | Young people now spend a large part of their day using phones, tablets and computers. |

Tools: synonyms (people → individuals, employees), word forms (educate → education), and structure changes (active ↔ passive, "some people think" → "it is sometimes argued").

## 2. Sentence 2: the thesis

The thesis is your answer, matched to the type:

| Type | Thesis pattern |
|---|---|
| Opinion | I (largely / completely) agree / disagree, because... |
| Discussion | While there are good arguments for both views, I believe that... |
| Advantages outweigh | In my view, the benefits clearly outweigh the drawbacks, because... |
| Problem / solution | This causes several problems, but they can be reduced by... |
| Two-part | This is mainly due to X, and I believe it is a largely positive / negative development. |

## 3. Worked examples

Prompt: *Some people believe that cities should ban cars from their centres. To what extent do you agree?*

> Many people argue that private cars should no longer be allowed in town and city centres. I strongly agree with this idea, mainly because it would make urban areas healthier and safer for everyone who lives and works there.

Prompt: *More and more people are working from home. Why? Is this a positive or negative development?*

> In recent years, a growing number of employees have started doing their jobs from home instead of travelling to an office. This trend is largely the result of faster internet and new attitudes among employers, and I believe it is mostly a positive development, despite some drawbacks.

## 4. What to leave out

- **No general background:** "Nowadays, technology is everywhere in our modern world." It says nothing.
- **No outline of the essay:** "This essay will first discuss... then..." It wastes words; the thesis is better.
- **No copied prompt.**
- **No examples or details**: they belong in the body.

<div class="myth">

**"A longer introduction makes a better first impression."** A long introduction steals time and words from the body, where the marks for developing ideas are. Two strong sentences, 40 to 50 words.

</div>

- Sentence 1: paraphrase the topic with new words and a new structure.
- Sentence 2: a thesis that answers the exact question, matched to the essay type.
- 40 to 50 words; no background filler, no outline, no copying, no examples.
- Your thesis must agree with your conclusion.
- Partial agreement is fine if it is clear: "I largely agree, although..."
