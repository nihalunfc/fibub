**Module 5 · about 17 minutes.** Read first: [Line graphs and the language of change](/learn/courses/ielts/line-graphs/). By the end you will turn any bar chart or table into a clear report built on comparisons.

<div class="bigidea">Bar charts and tables are mostly about comparison: which is bigger, by how much, and which pattern the numbers make. Find the pattern first, then pick the numbers that prove it.</div>

## 1. Two kinds of bar chart

| Kind | What it shows | Language |
|---|---|---|
| Bars with **no time** (e.g. spending by country) | comparison | higher, lower, the largest, twice as much |
| Bars **over time** (groups of bars per year) | comparison and change | the language of change from Lesson 5.3 as well |

A table is the same as a bar chart without the picture. It is harder only because you must find the pattern yourself.

## 2. Finding the pattern in a table

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>1. Read the headings and units.</b> Rows and columns: what are they?</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>2. Find the extremes.</b> Circle the highest and lowest number in the whole table.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>3. Look along each row.</b> Mark it ↑, ↓ or → (rising, falling, stable).</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>4. Group.</b> Rows with the same arrow go together. That grouping is your paragraph plan, and the arrows give you the overview.</p></div>
</div>

## 3. Comparison language

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| -er than / more ... than | Canada spent **more** on education **than** Mexico. |
| the -est / the most | Japan had **the highest** rate **of** the five countries. |
| as ... as / not as ... as | Rail was **not as popular as** road. |
| twice / three times as ... as | Spain had **twice as many** visitors **as** Portugal. |
| much / far / slightly + comparative | **far** higher, **slightly** lower |
| while / whereas | Exports rose, **whereas** imports fell. |
| compared with | **Compared with** 2010, spending was 20% higher. |
| the former / the latter | Men and women... **the former** ..., **the latter** ... |
| respectively | Men and women spent 4 and 6 hours **respectively**. |

## 4. Worked example: a table

*The table shows average weekly hours spent on four activities by teenagers in one country in 2005 and 2025.*

| Activity | 2005 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Online (phones, games) | 9 | 24 |
| Sport | 7 | 5 |
| Reading for pleasure | 5 | 2 |
| Homework | 8 | 8 |

Arrows: online ↑ (biggest change); sport ↓, reading ↓; homework →. Groups: the big rise / the falls / no change.

<div class="meaning">

**Model answer (about 175 words)**

The table compares how many hours per week teenagers in one country spent on four activities in 2005 and twenty years later.

Overall, time spent online rose dramatically and became by far the largest activity, while sport and reading both declined. Homework was the only activity that did not change.

In 2005, teenagers spent around 9 hours a week online, only slightly more than the 8 hours they spent on homework. By 2025, however, this figure had almost tripled to 24 hours, three times as much as homework and more than the other three activities put together.

In contrast, the time given to sport fell from 7 to 5 hours, and reading for pleasure dropped even more sharply, from 5 hours to just 2. Reading therefore went from being almost as popular as sport to the least common activity by a wide margin. Homework, meanwhile, remained stable at 8 hours a week in both years.

</div>

## 5. Bar chart over time: grouping choices

When bars show several categories over several years, you can group by **category** ("Country A, then Country B") or by **time** ("In 2010..., by 2020..."). Choose the one that shows the pattern most simply. If the categories moved in different directions, group by category. If they all moved together and the interesting thing is the ranking at each point, group by time.

<div class="myth">

**"I must mention every number to be accurate."** Accuracy means the numbers you give are right, not that you give them all. In a table of 20 numbers, 8 to 12 well chosen ones are plenty.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Bar charts and tables are about comparison; over time they are also about change.
- In a table: circle the extremes, arrow each row, group rows with the same arrow.
- Use varied comparison patterns: -er than, the -est, twice as many as, whereas, respectively.
- Group by category or by time, whichever shows the pattern most simply.
- Select numbers; do not list them all.
