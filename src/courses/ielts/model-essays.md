**Module 7 · about 20 minutes.** The last Task 2 lesson. By the end you will see, sentence by sentence, what changes as an essay moves from band 6 to band 8, so you know exactly what to work on.

<div class="bigidea">The jump from band 6 to 7 is mostly development and accuracy; the jump from 7 to 8 is precision and naturalness. The ideas can be almost the same.</div>

**Prompt.** *Some people believe that the best way to reduce traffic in cities is to make public transport free. Others think there are better solutions. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.*

The band labels below are our estimates, written to show typical differences. Only a trained examiner can give an official band.

## 1. About band 6

<div class="meaning">

Nowadays traffic is a big problem in many cities. Some people think public transport should be free to reduce traffic, but others think there are better ways. In this essay I will discuss both views and give my opinion.

Firstly, free public transport has many advantages. People will save money. Moreover, they will leave their cars at home. Furthermore, there will be less pollution in the city. For example, in some cities the buses are free and many people use them.

On the other hand, some people think there are better solutions. Free transport is very expensive for the government. In addition, if the buses are bad, people will not use them even if they are free. Also, the government can make the cars pay to enter the city center, this is a good way.

In conclusion, both views have advantages and disadvantages. I think free public transport is a good idea but the government should also improve the buses and trains.

</div>

**Why about 6:** all parts of the question are addressed, and there is a position, but it appears only at the end and is a little unclear. Ideas are **listed, not developed** (save money, leave cars, less pollution: no explanation of why). Linking is **mechanical** (Firstly, Moreover, Furthermore, In addition, Also). There is a **comma splice** ("...city center, this is a good way") and vague words ("a big problem", "bad", "a good idea"). It is also under 250 words, which is penalised.

## 2. About band 7

<div class="meaning">

Traffic congestion has become a serious problem in many large cities. While some people argue that making public transport free is the most effective solution, others believe that different measures would work better. In my view, free transport can help, but only if it is combined with other policies.

Supporters of free public transport argue that cost is one of the main reasons people choose to drive. If buses and trains were free, many commuters, especially those on low incomes, would have a strong reason to leave their cars at home. This would reduce the number of vehicles on the roads and, as a result, cut air pollution in city centres. Some smaller cities that have removed fares have reported a noticeable rise in passenger numbers.

However, critics point out that free transport is extremely expensive, and the money has to come from taxes. They also argue that price is not the only factor: if services are slow, crowded or unreliable, drivers will not switch even when travel costs nothing. For this reason, they suggest that charging drivers to enter busy areas, and spending that income on better services, would be more effective.

In conclusion, although free public transport may encourage some people to stop driving, I believe it works best as part of a wider plan that also improves the quality of services and discourages car use in city centres.

</div>

**Why about 7:** a **clear position in the introduction** and the conclusion. Each body paragraph has **one main idea, explained** (why cost matters, who benefits, what follows). Linking is varied and fewer (However, as a result, For this reason). Vocabulary is more precise (traffic congestion, commuters, on low incomes, unreliable). A few sentences could be more concise, and the example is fairly general.

## 3. About band 8

<div class="meaning">

Few problems frustrate city dwellers more than traffic congestion. Some argue that abolishing public transport fares would solve it, while others favour alternative measures. I believe that free fares alone are a blunt and costly tool, and that cities would achieve more by making driving less attractive and public transport genuinely better.

The case for free transport rests on a simple idea: if the cheapest option is also the most convenient, people will choose it. For low-income households in particular, removing fares could make a real difference, both to their budgets and to the number of cars on the road. Cities that have experimented with fare-free buses have often seen ridership rise, which suggests that price does influence behaviour.

Yet the evidence also suggests that many of these new passengers were previously walking or cycling, not driving. Motorists tend to value speed, comfort and reliability more than the price of a ticket, so a free but infrequent bus is unlikely to tempt them. Moreover, the lost fare income has to be replaced, often by cutting investment elsewhere. A congestion charge, by contrast, targets drivers directly and raises money that can fund more frequent and reliable services, tackling the problem from both sides.

In short, while free fares have a certain appeal, I am convinced that charging for road use and investing the proceeds in high-quality public transport is a more effective and sustainable way to reduce urban traffic.

</div>

**Why about 8:** a **nuanced position** stated precisely and held throughout. Ideas are **developed with reasoning**, including a counter-point that weakens the first view (new passengers were walking, not driving). Cohesion comes from ideas and reference ("these new passengers", "tackling the problem from both sides") rather than stacked linkers. Vocabulary is **precise and natural** (abolishing fares, a blunt and costly tool, ridership, the proceeds). Grammar is varied (conditionals, relative clauses, passives) and essentially error-free.

## 4. What changed, in one table

| | Band 6 | Band 7 | Band 8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Position | only at the end, vague | clear in intro and conclusion | precise, nuanced, consistent |
| Development | listed points | each idea explained | explained, weighed, counter-argued |
| Linking | one linker per sentence | varied, fewer | mostly through ideas and reference |
| Vocabulary | vague (big, bad, good) | precise | precise and natural, good collocation |
| Grammar | some errors, simple | mix, few errors | varied, almost error-free |

<div class="myth">

**"To reach band 8 I need completely different, cleverer ideas."** Look again: all three essays use similar ideas (cost, pollution, congestion charge). The difference is how clearly they are argued, developed and expressed.

</div>

- Band 6 to 7: a clear position from the start, ideas explained and supported, fewer errors.
- Band 7 to 8: precise, natural vocabulary; nuanced argument; cohesion through ideas, not linkers.
- Similar ideas can score very differently depending on development and language.
- Write at least 250 words; the band 6 essay here would also lose marks for length.
