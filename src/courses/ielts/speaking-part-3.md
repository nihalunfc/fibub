**Module 8 · about 15 minutes.** By the end you will answer abstract questions with a clear opinion, a reason, an example and a second point of view, in about 30 to 45 seconds.

<div class="bigidea">In Part 3 you are discussing ideas, not your life. Give a view, explain it, support it with an example, and show you can see the other side.</div>

## 1. The question types

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Opinion | Do you think people help their neighbours less than in the past? |
| Compare | How is helping people in a city different from in a village? |
| Cause | Why do some people volunteer and others don't? |
| Predict | Will communities become closer or more distant in the future? |
| Evaluate | Should schools make community service compulsory? |

## 2. The O-R-E-C pattern

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Opinion.</b> "I think it's true to some extent."</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Reason.</b> "People move more often now, so they don't get to know their neighbours."</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Example.</b> "In my building, most people rent for a year or two and then leave."</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Contrast or conclusion.</b> "Having said that, online neighbourhood groups have made it easier to ask for help, so in some ways it's changing rather than disappearing."</p></div>
</div>

## 3. Sample answers

**Should schools make community service compulsory?**

> I'm in two minds about that. On the one hand, it could teach young people that they have responsibilities to others, and some of them might discover a real interest in, say, working with elderly people. On the other hand, if it's compulsory, it might feel like a punishment, and that could put students off volunteering for life. So I think schools should offer it and give credit for it, but not force everyone to do it.

**Will communities become closer or more distant in the future?**

> It's hard to say, but I suspect they'll become closer in some ways. As more people work from home, they'll spend more time in their own neighbourhoods, using local shops and parks, and that tends to create connections. Mind you, if people also do everything online, there's a risk they'll never actually meet. It probably depends on how cities are designed.

## 4. Useful language

- **Opinion:** I'd say that..., As far as I'm concerned..., I'm convinced that...
- **Partly agree:** To some extent..., It depends on..., I'm in two minds about...
- **Contrast:** Having said that..., On the other hand..., Mind you...
- **Speculate:** I suspect..., It's quite likely that..., There's a good chance that..., might, could.
- **Generalise:** Generally speaking..., In most cases..., People tend to...

<div class="myth">

**"I should give a short answer because the examiner has many questions."** Part 3 rewards longer, developed answers. The examiner will interrupt if needed; that is normal and not a bad sign.

</div>

- Opinion, reason, example, contrast: about 30 to 45 seconds.
- Speculate naturally: I suspect, it's likely that, might, could.
- No opinion? Say so briefly and discuss what different people think.
- Longer, developed answers are expected; interruptions are normal.
