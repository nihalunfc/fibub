**Module 2 · about 14 minutes.** Read first: [Names, spellings, numbers and dates](/learn/courses/ielts/spelling-and-numbers/). By the end you will have a method for every gap-filling task in Listening, and you will know the four reasons people lose marks on them.

<div class="bigidea">Completion tasks test whether you can catch one exact word in a stream of speech. The words around the gap tell you when it is coming.</div>

## 1. The five forms of completion

| Task | Looks like | Usually in |
|---|---|---|
| Form | A booking or registration form with labels | Part 1 |
| Notes | Headings and bullet points | Parts 1, 2, 4 |
| Table | Rows and columns | Parts 1, 2 |
| Flow chart | Boxes with arrows (a process or steps) | Parts 2, 3 |
| Sentence completion | Separate sentences with one gap each | Parts 2, 3, 4 |

All are marked the same way: one mark if the word is right, correctly spelled, within the word limit, and fits the grammar.

## 2. The method

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Before: read the instructions.</b> Circle the word limit. "ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER" means "15 June" is fine, "the garden" is not.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Before: predict every gap</b> (Lesson 2.2). Kind of word, grammar, and a possible paraphrase of the label next to it.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Before: find the signposts.</b> In notes and tables, headings are the speaker's map. When you hear "Now, about the costs...", your eyes should already be on the "Costs" row.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>During: write the word fast, even half-spelled.</b> You can fix spelling later. Keep your eyes one gap ahead so you never miss the next answer while writing.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>After: check grammar and spelling.</b> Plural or singular? Does the sentence read correctly with your word? Is it within the limit?</p></div>
</div>

## 3. Worked example: a table

> | Tour | Starts at | Length | Price |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Old Harbour | the (1) ______ | 90 minutes | £12 |
> | Street Food | Market Square | (2) ______ hours | £(3) ______ |

The recording says: *"The Old Harbour tour starts at the lighthouse, not the bus station as it says in the old leaflet... The Street Food tour used to be two hours, but now it is three hours. It's thirty-five pounds, and that includes the food."*

- (1) **lighthouse**: "the bus station" is mentioned but ruled out.
- (2) **3** (or three): "two hours" is the old length, a distractor.
- (3) **35**: the £ sign is printed, so write only the number.

## 4. The four reasons people lose marks

1. **Over the word limit.** "the lighthouse" when the gap already says "the".
2. **Wrong form of the word.** The notes say "Visitors should avoid ___" and you hear "please don't bring bicycles". The answer is "bicycles": the plural you heard.
3. **Distractors.** The first thing you hear is often corrected or ruled out (Lesson 2.5 explains how test writers build them).
4. **Losing your place.** You wait for answer 4, it passes, and you miss 5 and 6 too. If two signposts go by with no answer, move on.

<div class="myth">

**"I should write a synonym if I'm not sure of the spelling."** No. A completion answer must be the word that fits the gap; a synonym that the recording did not use may be marked wrong. Write the word you heard, then check its spelling against your mistake log.

</div>

## 5. Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="ielts" data-topics="listening-part1,listening-part4" data-mode="practice" data-n="10" data-facets="qtype=form"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Circle the word limit; articles count, digits count as a number.
- Predict every gap and find the headings that signpost each section.
- Write fast, look one gap ahead, fix spelling at the end.
- Use the exact word you hear, in the form the grammar needs (often a plural).
- If two signposts pass with no answer, move on.

</div>
