**Module 4 · about 16 minutes.** Read first: [Spotting the paraphrase](/learn/courses/ielts/spot-the-paraphrase/). By the end you will decide True, False or Not Given with a reason you can point to in the text, every time.

<div class="bigidea">Compare the statement with the text like a judge with evidence. TRUE needs a matching sentence, FALSE needs a contradicting sentence, and NOT GIVEN means there is no evidence either way.</div>

## 1. The three answers

| Answer | Meaning | Test |
|---|---|---|
| TRUE | The text says the same thing (in other words) | You can point to a sentence that means the same |
| FALSE | The text says the opposite | You can point to a sentence that contradicts it |
| NOT GIVEN | The text does not say | You looked in the right place and there is no information either way |

**Only the text counts.** Not what you know, not what is likely, not what is true in real life.

## 2. The method

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>1. Underline the key words</b> in the statement, especially findable ones (names, numbers) and the "meaning" words: most, all, only, always, increased, first, because.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>2. Scan</b> for the place in the text. Questions follow the text order, so it is after the place for the previous question.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>3. Read the place carefully</b>, the sentence before and after too.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>4. Compare piece by piece.</b> Does every part of the statement match? One part contradicted = FALSE. One part missing from the text = NOT GIVEN.</p></div>
</div>


Pick a statement and walk it through the two questions. Press Next statement for a new one.

## 3. Words that change everything

Small words are where FALSE and NOT GIVEN hide:

| Type | Examples | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Quantity | all, most, some, few, none, only | "Most" in the text, "all" in the statement = FALSE |
| Frequency | always, usually, sometimes, never | "Usually" ≠ "always" |
| Certainty | proved, suggested, may, might, could | "Studies suggest" ≠ "it is proved" |
| Comparison | more than, the first, the largest, the only | "One of the largest" ≠ "the largest" |
| Cause | because, led to, as a result of | Two facts side by side ≠ one causing the other: often NOT GIVEN |
| Time | now, used to, by 2010, until | Past and present mixed up |

## 4. Worked examples

Text: *"Early studies found that city honey bees often survived the winter at least as well as rural ones."*

1. "Some research showed city honey bees did not survive winter worse than rural ones." **TRUE**: "at least as well" = "did not survive worse".
2. "City honey bees always survive winter better than rural bees." **FALSE**: "often" and "at least as well" contradict "always" and "better".
3. "City honey bees are fed by their keepers in winter." **NOT GIVEN**: feeding is never mentioned.

<div class="myth">

**"If the statement is not exactly in the text, it's FALSE."** That is the most common mistake. If the text simply doesn't mention it, the answer is NOT GIVEN. Choose FALSE only when you can point to the words that contradict it.

</div>

## 5. Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="ielts" data-topics="reading-academic" data-mode="practice" data-n="15" data-facets="qtype=tfng"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- TRUE = same meaning; FALSE = contradicted; NOT GIVEN = no information.
- Only the text counts, never your own knowledge.
- Watch quantity, frequency, certainty, comparison, cause and time words.
- Answers follow the order of the text.
- Choose FALSE only when you can point to the contradicting words.
