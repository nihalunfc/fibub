**Module 9 · about 16 minutes.** By the end you will choose the right tense in every IELTS situation: answering about your life, telling a story, describing a graph and arguing in an essay.

<div class="bigidea">You don't need every tense; you need the right one each time. The question's tense and the time words (since, last year, by 2030) tell you which.</div>

## 1. The eight you need

| Tense | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present simple | habits, facts, general truths | I **walk** to work. Water **boils** at 100 °C. |
| Present continuous | now, temporary, arrangements | I'**m studying** for IELTS this month. |
| Past simple | finished past, with a time | I **moved** here in 2021. |
| Past continuous | background in a story | I **was waiting** for the bus when it started to rain. |
| Present perfect | past to now; experience; result now | I'**ve lived** here since 2021. I'**ve** never **tried** sushi. |
| Past perfect | earlier past in a story | The film **had** already **started** when we arrived. |
| will / going to | predictions, decisions, plans | I think prices **will** rise. I'**m going to** apply next year. |
| used to / would | past habits | I **used to** play football every weekend. |

## 2. The big mix-up: past simple or present perfect?

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Is the time finished and named?</b> (yesterday, in 2019, last week, ago, when I was a child) → <b>past simple</b>: "I visited Rome in 2019."</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Does the time reach now?</b> (since, for, so far, this year, recently) → <b>present perfect</b>: "I have visited three countries this year."</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Is it experience, with no time?</b> (ever, never, before) → <b>present perfect</b>: "Have you ever been to Canada?"</p></div>
</div>

## 3. Tenses by IELTS task

| Task | Main tenses |
|---|---|
| Writing Task 1, past data | past simple; past perfect with "by 2010, it had doubled" |
| Task 1, data to now / projections | present perfect; will / is expected to / is predicted to |
| Task 1, process | present simple (passive) |
| Task 2 essays | present simple for general truths, present perfect for recent change, conditionals for proposals |
| Speaking Part 1 | match the question: do / did / would / have you ever |
| Speaking Part 2 stories | past simple, past continuous, past perfect, used to / would |

<div class="myth">

**"Using many different tenses raises my grammar score."** Range helps only when each tense is the right one. A wrong tense is an error, however advanced it looks.

</div>

## 4. Practise

<div class="qbank" data-bank="english" data-topics="tenses" data-mode="practice" data-n="15"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Finished time named (last year, in 2019, ago) → past simple.
- Time up to now (since, for, so far) or experience (ever, never) → present perfect.
- Stories: past simple for events, past continuous for background, past perfect for earlier events.
- Graphs: past simple for past years, present perfect for "since", will / is expected to for projections.
- Speaking: answer in the tense of the question.

</div>
