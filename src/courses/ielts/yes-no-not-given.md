**Module 4 · about 13 minutes.** Read first: [True, False or Not Given](/learn/courses/ielts/true-false-not-given/). By the end you will separate the writer's own view from the views the writer reports, which is the whole skill in Yes/No/Not Given.

<div class="bigidea">In an argument text, many voices speak. Yes/No/Not Given only cares about one: the writer.</div>

## 1. YES, NO, NOT GIVEN

- **YES**: the writer agrees with the statement (states it, or clearly implies it).
- **NO**: the writer disagrees with it.
- **NOT GIVEN**: you cannot tell what the writer thinks about it.

The method is the same as TFNG (Lesson 4.1). The new skill is recognising whose view each sentence gives.

## 2. Whose voice is it?

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Reported views:</b> "Some critics claim...", "It is often said that...", "Supporters argue..." These are other people. The writer may agree or disagree next.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>The writer's turn:</b> "However, this ignores...", "In fact...", "It seems clear that...", "Unfortunately...", "Surprisingly..." Now the writer speaks.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Attitude words:</b> adjectives and adverbs that judge: "a welcome change", "a costly mistake", "rightly", "wrongly", "sadly". They show the writer's view in a single word.</p></div>
</div>

## 3. Worked example

> *Critics of rooftop beekeeping often say that it should simply be banned. This goes too far. Experienced keepers who limit their hives cause little harm, and the real priority, as ecologists rightly point out, is planting more flowers.*

1. "Rooftop beekeeping should be banned." **NO**: "This goes too far."
2. "Planting flowers is more important than adding hives." **YES**: "the real priority, as ecologists rightly point out" (rightly = the writer agrees).
3. "Most rooftop beekeepers are inexperienced." **NOT GIVEN**: the writer mentions experienced keepers but says nothing about how many are inexperienced.

<div class="myth">

**"If someone in the text says it, the answer is YES."** Only if the writer agrees with them. Look for the writer's response after a reported view.

</div>

## 4. Practise

<div class="keypoints">

- YNNG tests the writer's views and claims, not facts.
- "Some argue", "it is said" = other people's views; "however", "in fact", "rightly" often mark the writer's.
- Attitude words show the writer's view in one word.
- NO needs the writer to disagree; NOT GIVEN means the writer's view is not shown.

</div>
