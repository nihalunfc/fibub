**Module 7 · about 15 minutes.** By the end you will know what "good vocabulary" means to an examiner, and it is not a list of rare words.

<div class="bigidea">Higher-band vocabulary is precise, natural and accurate: the right word for the exact meaning, in the combination native writers use, spelled correctly. Rare words help only when they are all three.</div>

## 1. Precision

| Vague | Precise |
|---|---|
| a lot of people | most commuters / many elderly residents |
| bad for the environment | increases carbon emissions / pollutes rivers |
| good for students | improves students' concentration / reduces stress |
| things | items, issues, factors, possessions, activities |
| big problem | serious problem, major issue, widespread concern |

## 2. Collocation

Words travel in pairs. Learn the pair, not the single word.

| Verb + noun | Adjective + noun | Adverb + adjective |
|---|---|---|
| address a problem | a heavy burden | highly effective |
| raise awareness | a growing number | widely accepted |
| pose a threat | a significant impact | increasingly common |
| reach a decision | a key factor | deeply concerned |
| meet a demand | a vicious circle | strictly controlled |

## 3. Paraphrase, not repetition

Repeating the same noun six times shows a narrow range. Use synonyms, word forms and reference: *cars → private vehicles → motorists → traffic → they*.

## 4. Register

Task 2 is a formal essay:

- **Avoid informal words:** kids (children), a lot of (many, a great deal of), get (obtain, become, receive), stuff (things, possessions, material).
- **Avoid contractions:** don't → do not.
- **Limit "I think":** use "In my view", "I would argue that", or a strong statement without it.

## 5. Accuracy

Spelling and word form count. Common errors: *environment, government, necessary, accommodation, responsibility*; *economic* (adjective) vs *economy* (noun); *effect* (noun) vs *affect* (verb).

<div class="myth">

**"Idioms like 'a piece of cake' raise the writing score."** In a formal essay, idioms usually sound out of place. Save them for Speaking, where natural idiomatic language helps.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Precise words beat vague ones: no "things", "stuff", "a lot of" in an essay.
- Learn collocations: address a problem, play a role, a growing number.
- Paraphrase and use reference words instead of repeating nouns.
- Formal register: no contractions, no slang, few "I think"s.
- Accuracy first: a correct common word beats a wrong rare one.
