**Module 8 · about 14 minutes.** By the end you will answer any Part 1 question with a natural, extended answer of the right length.

<div class="bigidea">Answer, reason, example. Say yes or no (or what you think), say why, and add one real detail. That is a perfect Part 1 answer.</div>

## 1. What happens

The examiner checks your name and ID, then asks about your home, work or studies, then two or three short topics, about four questions each. Questions are simple and personal: *Do you...? How often...? What kind of...? Did you... when you were a child?*

## 2. The A-R-E pattern

| Step | Example: "Do you enjoy reading?" |
|---|---|
| **Answer** directly | Yes, I really do, |
| **Reason** | mainly because it helps me switch off after work. |
| **Example / detail** | At the moment I'm reading a crime novel set in Iceland, and I usually read a chapter on the train home. |

About 20 seconds. Natural, personal, complete.

## 3. Watch the tense

Part 1 questions use different tenses on purpose. Answer in the same one:

- *Do you...?* → present simple (habits): "I usually..."
- *Did you... as a child?* → past simple: "When I was little, I used to..."
- *Would you like to...?* → would: "I'd love to, because..."
- *Have you ever...?* → present perfect, then past simple: "Yes, I have. I went..."

## 4. Sample answers

**Work.** *"What do you do?"*
> I work as a pharmacy assistant in a busy shopping centre. It's quite demanding, because we're often short of staff, but I enjoy it, especially helping older customers understand their medicine.

**Home town.** *"What's your home town like?"*
> It's a small coastal town in the south, so it's very quiet most of the year. In summer, though, it gets crowded with tourists, mostly because of the beaches and the seafood restaurants.

**Weather.** *"What kind of weather do you like?"*
> I prefer cool, dry days, to be honest. Hot weather makes me feel tired, so I'm happiest in autumn, when I can go for long walks without getting sunburnt.

## 5. If you don't know or don't do it

It's fine. Say so and explain: *"Actually, I don't really play any sport, mainly because I don't have time, but I do try to walk to work every day."* You've turned a "no" into a full answer.

<div class="myth">

**"Short answers are safer because I make fewer mistakes."** One-word answers give the examiner nothing to assess. Extend every answer, even if it means a small mistake.

</div>

Each card has four questions on one topic and a 2-minute timer. Answer all four out loud, then compare with the model.
