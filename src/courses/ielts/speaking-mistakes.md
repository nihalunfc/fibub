**Module 8 · about 12 minutes.** The last Speaking lesson. By the end you will recognise the habits that hold a score down and what to do instead.

<div class="bigidea">Most Speaking scores are lowered by habits, not knowledge: answers that are too short, scripts that sound rehearsed, and tenses that don't match the question. All of them can be fixed with practice.</div>

## 1. Ten common mistakes

| Mistake | Instead |
|---|---|
| One-word or one-sentence answers in Part 1 | Answer, reason, example |
| Memorised scripts | Prepare ideas and phrases; speak freshly each time |
| Wrong tense for the question | Listen for do / did / would / have you ever |
| Long silences | Natural thinking phrases: "Let me think..." |
| Stopping at 1 minute in Part 2 | Add details, feelings, what you learned |
| Going off topic | Stay with the question; link back to it |
| Over-formal written language ("Firstly, furthermore") | Spoken linking: "Well", "actually", "the thing is" |
| Forced idioms | One or two, only where they fit |
| Speaking too fast | A steady pace with pauses between ideas |
| Dropping word endings (-ed, -s) | Practise them slowly, then at normal speed |

## 2. Over-formal vs natural

> **Over-formal:** "Firstly, I would like to state that my hometown is a place of considerable historical significance. Furthermore..."
>
> **Natural:** "Well, my home town's actually quite historic. There's a castle right in the centre, and..."

Speaking is a conversation. Sound like a person, not an essay.


Pick a phrase and slide Speed from careful to natural.

## 3. On the day

- Speak to the examiner as you would to a polite stranger: friendly, relaxed.
- If your mind goes blank, say so and paraphrase the question back: "Something I'm proud of... let me think..."
- Don't try to judge your score from the examiner's face. They are trained to stay neutral.

<div class="myth">

**"The examiner interrupted me, so I must have done badly."** In Parts 1 and 3, examiners have to keep to time and often interrupt long answers. It is normal and usually means you were talking enough.

</div>

## 4. Practise the whole test

Do one card from each part in order, with the timers, and record yourself.
