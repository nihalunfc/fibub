<div class="bigidea">The Speaking test is a structured conversation. The examiner wants to hear you talk: long, natural, connected answers, not perfect ones.</div>

## 1. The three parts

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Part 1, 4 to 5 minutes: questions about you.</b> Your home, work or studies, and two or three everyday topics (weekends, food, weather). Short questions; answer in 2 to 4 sentences.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Part 2, 3 to 4 minutes: the long turn.</b> You get a card ("Describe a place you enjoyed visiting..."), a pencil and paper, and <b>1 minute to prepare</b>. Then you speak for <b>1 to 2 minutes</b> without interruption. The examiner may ask one or two short questions after.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Part 3, 4 to 5 minutes: discussion.</b> Broader, more abstract questions linked to the Part 2 topic: compare, explain, predict, evaluate. Longer answers with opinions and reasons.</p></div>
</div>

The test is recorded. It may be face to face or, at some centres, with the examiner on a video call. The examiner follows a fixed script, which is why they may sound formal or move on suddenly: it is not a sign that you did badly.

## 2. The four marking areas, in plain words

Our own explanations; the official public descriptions are on the IELTS website.

| Area | The examiner listens for | You show it by |
|---|---|---|
| **Fluency and coherence** | Can you keep talking at a natural speed, with ideas that connect? | extended answers, few long pauses, linking (because, so, actually, the thing is) |
| **Lexical resource** | How wide and precise is your vocabulary? | topic words, natural collocations, paraphrasing when you don't know a word |
| **Grammatical range and accuracy** | Do you use varied structures correctly? | a mix of tenses, conditionals, complex sentences; few errors |
| **Pronunciation** | How easy are you to understand? | clear sounds, word and sentence stress, natural rhythm and intonation |

An accent is fine. What matters is being easy to understand.

## 3. Practising alone

1. **Use the timers** in the practice room: Part 2 gives you 1 minute to prepare and 2 to speak.
2. **Record yourself** on your phone's voice recorder. Listen once for fluency (pauses, "umm"), once for grammar, once for pronunciation.
3. **Compare with the model answer**, then answer the same question again. The second try is where you learn.
4. **Speak out loud**, always. Answering in your head does not train your mouth.

Nothing you say on fibub is recorded or sent anywhere: the timers run in your browser only.

<div class="myth">

**"I need to give clever, correct opinions."** The examiner never marks your ideas. A simple opinion explained in fluent English scores better than a brilliant one you struggle to say.

</div>

- Three parts in 11 to 14 minutes: questions about you, a 2-minute talk from a card, a discussion.
