**Module 3 · about 13 minutes.** Read first: [How the Reading test works](/learn/courses/ielts/reading-overview/). By the end you will skim a passage for its structure in about two minutes and scan for any word in seconds.

<div class="bigidea">Skimming is looking at a map of a city. Scanning is looking for one street name on that map. You need both, in that order.</div>

## 1. Skimming: the two-minute map

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>Title and subtitle (10 seconds).</b> What is the topic? Is there a hint of an argument ("Why X may be wrong")?</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>First sentence of each paragraph (60 seconds).</b> Most paragraphs announce their topic here. Write a 2 to 3 word note beside each: "A: rooftop trend", "B: why bees died", "C: wild bees".</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Last paragraph (30 seconds).</b> It often gives the writer's conclusion or view.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Stop.</b> Do not read the details. You now know where everything is, which is what the questions need.</p></div>
</div>

## 2. Scanning: finding it fast

1. Pick the **most findable word** in the question: a name, number, date, technical term or word with a capital letter.
2. Hold its **shape** in your mind, and move your eyes quickly down the text (a zigzag or straight down the middle works).
3. When you find it, **stop and read carefully** the sentence before, the sentence itself and the sentence after.
4. If the question has no findable word, use your paragraph notes from skimming to choose where to look.

## 3. Worked example

Question: *"Britain has around 270 species of bee."* True, False or Not Given?

- Findable word: **270**. Scan for the number, ignoring everything else.
- Found in paragraph C: "Britain alone has around 270 species of bee, most of them solitary bees..."
- Read carefully: the number and the country match. **TRUE**.

That took about 15 seconds, without reading the rest of the passage.

<div class="myth">

**"Skimming means reading fast."** Skimming means reading **selectively**: titles, first sentences, the end. If you read every word quickly, you understand less and remember little.

</div>

## 4. Drills

- **Daily skim**: take any news article, give yourself 60 seconds, then write one sentence summarising it. Check by reading it properly.
- **Scan race**: give yourself 10 numbers or names from a long article and time how long it takes to find all of them. Repeat with a new article each day.
- **Practise here**: in the passage below, before you look at the questions, skim it in two minutes and write paragraph notes.
