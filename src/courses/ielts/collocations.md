**Module 10 · about 12 minutes.** By the end you will notice, learn and use collocations, the word partnerships that make English sound natural.

<div class="bigidea">Native speakers say "heavy rain", not "strong rain", for no logical reason. Learn words with their partners, and your English sounds natural.</div>

## 1. Main patterns

| Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|
| verb + noun | make progress, do research, raise awareness, meet a need, pose a risk |
| adjective + noun | heavy traffic, a strong argument, a key factor, a vast majority |
| adverb + adjective | highly likely, deeply concerned, widely available |
| noun + noun | traffic congestion, energy consumption, job security |
| verb + adverb | rise steadily, vary considerably, depend largely on |
| noun + preposition | a rise in, the reason for, the impact on, a solution to |

## 2. How to learn them

1. **Read for phrases.** Underline two- or three-word partnerships in reading passages.
2. **Keep a phrase notebook by topic**, not single words.
3. **Use them** in your next essay or Speaking answer the same week.

## 3. In the test

- **Writing:** "pose a serious threat", "address the problem" score better than "make a big danger", "solve the problem bad".
- **Speaking:** everyday collocations sound natural: "catch a cold", "take a break", "a tight budget".
- **Reading and Listening:** collocations help you predict a missing word: "a sharp ______" is probably rise, fall or increase.

<div class="myth">

**"Collocations are only for advanced learners."** Basic ones (make a mistake, do homework, take a photo) matter from band 5 upward, and errors in them are very noticeable.

</div>
