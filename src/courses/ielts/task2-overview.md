**Module 7 · about 15 minutes.** For both Academic and General Training. By the end you will know exactly what the examiner looks for in the essay and how to spend your 40 minutes.

<div class="bigidea">Task 2 is an argument, not a display of knowledge. Answer exactly the question asked, take a clear position, and support it with explained, relevant ideas in well-organised paragraphs.</div>

## 1. The task

> You should spend about 40 minutes on this task.
> Write about the following topic:
> *Some people think that all university students should study whatever they like. Others believe that they should only be allowed to study subjects that will be useful in the future, such as those related to science and technology.*
> *Discuss both these views and give your own opinion.*
> Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.
> Write at least 250 words.

- **At least 250 words** (aim for 270 to 310). Fewer than 250 is penalised.
- **About 40 minutes.** Task 2 is worth twice as much as Task 1, so do it with full attention. Many candidates do it first.
- **No specialist knowledge needed.** Examples from your own life and general knowledge are fine. Never invent precise statistics.

## 2. The four marking areas, in plain words

Like Task 1, four areas count equally. These are our own plain explanations, not the official wording (see the sources for that).

| Area | The examiner asks | You show it by |
|---|---|---|
| **Task response** | Did you answer every part of this question, with a clear position, developed ideas and relevant support? | a clear thesis in the introduction; every part of the prompt covered; ideas explained, not just listed |
| **Coherence and cohesion** | Is it logically organised and easy to follow? | one main idea per paragraph; clear topic sentences; natural linking; clear reference words |
| **Lexical resource** | How wide, precise and accurate is your vocabulary? | topic words, collocations, paraphrase instead of repetition; correct spelling |
| **Grammatical range and accuracy** | How varied and accurate is your grammar? | a mix of complex sentences used correctly; good control of articles, tenses and agreement |

A beautiful essay on a slightly different question loses heavily on task response. A brilliant argument full of grammar mistakes loses on accuracy. You need all four.

Step through a body paragraph and watch each move (Point, Explain, Example, Link) add one sentence.

<div class="steps" data-fig="@lang-peel-steps">
<div class="step"><p><strong>Point.</strong> Open with one sentence that states the idea of the paragraph.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><strong>Explain.</strong> Say why it is true or how it works, in one or two sentences.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><strong>Example.</strong> Give one specific example that proves the point.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><strong>Link.</strong> Close by tying the paragraph back to the question.</p></div>
</div>

Notice that the Point and the Link say nearly the same thing; the Explain and Example in between do the proving.

## 3. A 40-minute plan

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>0 to 5 minutes: plan.</b> Identify the essay type and every part of the question, decide your position, and note two main ideas with an example each (Lesson 7.3).</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>5 to 8: introduction.</b> Paraphrase the topic and state your position (Lesson 7.4).</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>8 to 30: two body paragraphs.</b> Point, explain, example, link back (Lesson 7.5). About 10 minutes each.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>30 to 34: conclusion.</b> Your answer again in new words (Lesson 7.6).</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>34 to 40: check.</b> Articles, plurals, verb agreement, tense, spelling, and that the position is the same at both ends.</p></div>
</div>

## 5. Look at the prompts

Read three prompts from the bank. For each, say out loud: the type, every part you must answer, and your position. Don't write yet.
