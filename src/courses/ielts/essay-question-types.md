**Module 7 · about 16 minutes.** By the end you will recognise any Task 2 prompt in seconds and know exactly what it requires.

<div class="bigidea">The last sentence of the prompt is the instruction. Read it word by word: it tells you the type, and every part you must answer.</div>

## 1. The five types

<figure class="fig" data-fig="essay-shape"><figcaption>Switch between the five types and compare their plans.</figcaption></figure>

| Type | Typical instruction | Required |
|---|---|---|
| Opinion | To what extent do you agree or disagree? / Do you agree or disagree? | Your position, which can be full or partial, held consistently |
| Discussion | Discuss both these views and give your own opinion. | Both views and your opinion |
| Advantages and disadvantages | Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages? / Is this a positive or negative development? | A judgement weighing the two sides |
| Problem and solution | What problems does this cause? What solutions can you suggest? (also causes and effects) | Each part named in the question |
| Two-part (direct questions) | Why is this the case? What can be done? | A full answer to each question |

## 2. One topic, five instructions

Topic: *More and more people are working from home.*

1. **Opinion:** "Working from home is better for both employees and employers. To what extent do you agree?" → Your view: agree, disagree or partly agree, and why.
2. **Discussion:** "Some say working from home benefits employees; others say it harms teamwork. Discuss both views and give your opinion." → View A, view B, your view.
3. **Advantages and disadvantages:** "Do the advantages of working from home outweigh the disadvantages?" → Both sides, then which weighs more.
4. **Problem and solution:** "What problems can working from home cause, and how can they be solved?" → Problems, then matching solutions.
5. **Two-part:** "Why are more people working from home? Is this a positive development?" → Reasons; then your evaluation.

## 3. Traps in the instruction

- **"To what extent"** allows partial agreement, but your position must still be clear: "I largely agree, although..."
- **"Advantages outweigh"** needs a verdict. Ending with "there are both advantages and disadvantages" does not answer it.
- **Hidden parts.** "Discuss the causes of this problem and **suggest some solutions**." Two parts. Underline every verb in the instruction.
- **Narrow topics.** "Children should learn to cook **at school**." The essay is about school, not cooking in general. Staying on the exact topic is part of task response.

<div class="myth">

**"I can prepare one essay structure and use it for every question."** The basic four-paragraph shape works for every type, but what goes in each paragraph changes. An opinion essay structure used for a two-part question will miss one of the questions.

</div>

## 4. Practise identifying the type

<div class="qbank" data-bank="ielts" data-topics="writing-essay-skills" data-mode="practice" data-n="12" data-sub="question-type"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Read the instruction (last sentence) word by word; underline every verb and question.
- Opinion: a clear position. Discussion: both views plus yours. Advantages outweigh: a verdict.
- Problem/solution and two-part: answer every named part, each in its own paragraph.
- Stay on the exact topic, including narrow words like "at school" or "in cities".
- The four-paragraph shape fits every type; the content of each paragraph changes.

</div>
