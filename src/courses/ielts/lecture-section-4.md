**Module 2 · about 14 minutes.** Read first: [Form, note and table completion](/learn/courses/ielts/form-note-completion/). By the end you will follow a four- to five-minute academic talk, using signposts and note headings to keep your place for all ten answers.

<div class="bigidea">Lecturers tell you where they are going. Signpost phrases are the road signs; the note headings are the map.</div>

## 1. What Part 4 is

One speaker gives a short talk on an academic subject: the history of a material, an animal's behaviour, a design idea, a study's findings. You usually complete ten gaps in notes or sentences. There is **no pause** half way through, so you read all ten questions in the 30 or so seconds before it starts (or at the end of Part 3 on paper).

Topics are chosen so that no special knowledge is needed: everything you need is in the talk.

## 2. Signpost language

| The lecturer is... | You hear |
|---|---|
| starting | "Today I'd like to look at...", "I'll begin with..." |
| listing | "First of all...", "Another factor...", "Finally..." |
| changing topic | "Turning now to...", "Let's move on to...", "As for..." |
| giving an example | "For instance...", "Take the case of...", "A good illustration is..." |
| contrasting | "However...", "On the other hand...", "Surprisingly..." |
| giving a reason or result | "This is because...", "As a result...", "This meant that..." |
| emphasising | "What's really important here is...", "The key point is..." |
| ending | "To sum up...", "So, in conclusion..." |


Pick a lecture sentence and slide Compress from the full sentence to short notes.

## 3. Watch it happen

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><p><b>The notes.</b> Heading: <i>Uses of seaweed</i>. Gaps: (31) In Japan, eaten as a ______ in soups. (32) Farmers spread it on fields as a ______. (33) A substance from it thickens ______ such as ice cream.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Predict.</b> 31: a noun, maybe "flavour" or "ingredient". 32: a noun for something farmers use, maybe "fertiliser". 33: a plural noun, a type of food.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p><b>Listen.</b> "In Japan, seaweed is a basic <i>ingredient</i> in soups..." → 31 <b>ingredient</b>.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p>"For centuries, coastal farmers have used it to feed the soil, in other words as a <i>fertiliser</i>..." → 32 <b>fertiliser</b> (or fertilizer). "Feed the soil" was the paraphrase signal.</p></div>
<div class="step"><p>"Turning now to industry. A gel extracted from red seaweed is used to thicken <i>desserts</i>, ice cream being the best known." → 33 <b>desserts</b>. The signpost "Turning now to" told you to move on.</p></div>
</div>

## 4. Keeping your place for ten answers

1. **Read all ten gaps first** and underline the headings.
2. **Keep one eye on the next gap** while writing the current one.
3. **Use headings as checkpoints**: if the lecturer reaches a new heading, any gaps before it are finished; move on.
4. **Write short**: you can fix spelling at the end.
5. **Expect technical words to be explained**: a lecturer who says a difficult word usually spells it or defines it.

<div class="myth">

**"Part 4 is the hardest, so give up on it."** Part 4 has the clearest structure in the test. Many learners score better on it than on Part 3, because only one person speaks and the notes follow the talk exactly.

</div>
