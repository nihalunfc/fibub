**Module 6 · about 15 minutes.** By the end you will ask for things politely and clearly: time off, information, a job, a change, a favour.

<div class="bigidea">A request letter succeeds when the reader knows exactly what you want, why, and how to say yes easily. Be polite, give a reason, and make it simple to agree.</div>

## 1. Kinds of request letter

| Kind | Example task | Usual tone |
|---|---|---|
| Request | Ask your manager for time off / ask a neighbour to keep the noise down | semi-formal |
| Enquiry | Ask a college about a course; ask a hotel about a booking | formal |
| Application | Apply for a part-time job or a volunteer role | formal |
| Change | Ask your landlord to repair something; ask to change a booking | formal or semi-formal |

## 2. Polite request patterns, from direct to very polite

| Level | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Direct | Could you send me...? / Can I...? (informal) |
| Polite | Would it be possible to...? / Would you mind + -ing? |
| More polite | I would be grateful if you could... / I would appreciate it if you could... |
| Most polite | I was wondering whether it might be possible to... |

Indirect questions are polite too: *Could you tell me **when the course starts**?* (not "when does the course start").


Pick a situation and slide Formality from very casual to very formal.

## 3. Model answer: semi-formal request

Task: *You need to take a week off work next month. Write a letter to your manager. In your letter, explain why you need the time off, say what dates you need, and explain how your work will be covered while you are away.* Begin: Dear Ms Rahman,

<div class="meaning">

**Model answer (about 180 words)**

Dear Ms Rahman,

I am writing to ask whether I could take a week of annual leave next month.

My sister is getting married in Lisbon on 18 April, and as the only member of our family living abroad, I would very much like to be there to help with the preparations. I realise this is quite short notice, and I apologise for not asking sooner, but the date was only confirmed last weekend.

Ideally, I would need to be away from Monday 14 April until Friday 18 April, returning to work the following Monday. I still have nine days of leave left this year, so this would not go over my allowance.

As for my work, I have already spoken to Tom, who has agreed to look after my clients while I am away. I will also make sure the monthly report is finished before I leave and will write a short handover note for him.

I would be very grateful if you could let me know whether this would be possible.

Kind regards,

Priya Nair

</div>

Note: the purpose is in the first line, each bullet gets a paragraph, the reasons are specific, and the writer makes it easy to agree (leave allowance, cover arranged).

## 4. Applications

For a job or volunteer role: say which role and where you saw it, give two or three relevant experiences or skills, say when you are available and how they can contact you. Stay modest and specific: *"I worked for two years as a receptionist at a busy dental clinic, where I handled bookings for six dentists."*

<div class="myth">

**"Being polite means being long and indirect all the time."** Be polite in the request itself, but give facts directly: dates, times, what you need. A clear letter is a polite letter.

</div>

## 5. Write one

<div class="qbank" data-bank="ielts" data-topics="writing-letters" data-mode="practice" data-n="4" data-sub="semi-formal"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Purpose first; one paragraph per bullet.
- Use polite patterns: Would it be possible to...? I would be grateful if you could... I was wondering whether...
- Give a specific reason, and make it easy for the reader to say yes.
- Indirect questions are polite: Could you tell me when the course starts?
- Applications: role, where you saw it, 2 or 3 relevant experiences, availability.

</div>
