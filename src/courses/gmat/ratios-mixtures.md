**Module 4 · about 16 minutes.** By the end you will solve ratio and mixture questions with the "parts" method.

<div class="bigidea">A ratio a : b means the total is made of a + b equal parts. Find the size of one part and everything else follows.</div>

## 1. The parts method

<figure class="fig" data-fig="ratio-bars"><figcaption>Set a ratio and a total. Each block is one part, and every block holds the same amount.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: 90 sweets shared in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4">
<div class="step">Count the parts: 2 + 3 + 4 = 9.</div>
<div class="step">One part: 90 ÷ 9 = 10.</div>
<div class="step">Shares: 2 × 10 = 20, 3 × 10 = 30, 4 × 10 = <b>40</b>.</div>
</div>

A useful check: the total must be a **multiple of the number of parts** when the items are whole things. Boys to girls 4 : 5 means the class size is a multiple of 9.

<div class="problem" data-problem="ra-g01"></div>

## 2. Joining two ratios

When two ratios share a term, make that term the same number in both.

a : b = 2 : 3 and b : c = 4 : 5. The b values are 3 and 4; make both 12: a : b = 8 : 12 and b : c = 12 : 15, so **a : b : c = 8 : 12 : 15**.

## 3. Ratios that change

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: red to blue is 3 : 5. After 9 red are added it is 3 : 2. How many blue?">
<div class="step">Write red = 3k and blue = 5k (k is the size of one part).</div>
<div class="step">New ratio: (3k + 9) : 5k = 3 : 2.</div>
<div class="step">Cross-multiply: 2(3k + 9) = 3(5k), so 6k + 18 = 15k and k = 2.</div>
<div class="step">Blue = 5 × 2 = <b>10</b>. Check: red 6 + 9 = 15, and 15 : 10 = 3 : 2. ✓</div>
</div>

<div class="problem" data-problem="ra-g04"></div>

<div class="myth">

**"The ratio numbers are the actual counts."** A ratio of 3 : 5 could be 3 and 5, or 30 and 50, or 300 and 500. Always use 3k and 5k until something fixes k.

</div>

## 4. Proportion and scale

If two quantities grow together (twice as many hours, twice the pay), set up a proportion: a/b = c/d and cross-multiply. Maps, recipes and unit prices all work this way.

## 5. Mixtures

The strength of a mixture is a **weighted average**: total amount of the ingredient ÷ total volume.

10 L of 20% juice + 30 L of 60% juice: juice = 2 + 18 = 20 L in 40 L, so 50%. The answer is closer to 60% because there is more of that liquid.

<div class="problem" data-problem="ra-g02"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- a : b → a + b equal parts; one part = total ÷ (a + b).
- Whole items: the total must be a multiple of the number of parts.
- Join ratios by making the shared term equal.
- Changing ratios: write the parts as 3k and 5k and solve for k.
- Mixture strength = total ingredient ÷ total volume, a weighted average.

</div>
