**Module 4 · about 14 minutes.** By the end you will handle every business-style money question, the kind the GMAT loves because business schools do.

<div class="bigidea">Profit questions are percent questions in a suit: markups and discounts are multipliers, and they multiply one after another.</div>

## 1. The words

- **Cost:** what the seller pays. **Revenue** (or price): what the buyer pays.
- **Profit = revenue − cost.** A negative profit is a loss.
- **Markup %** is profit as a percent of **cost**. **Margin %** is profit as a percent of **price**. Check which one the question means.
- **Break-even:** revenue exactly covers costs.

## 2. Markup then discount

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: cost 80, marked up 50%, sold at 25% off">
<div class="step">Marked price: 80 × 1.5 = 120.</div>
<div class="step">Sale price: 120 × 0.75 = 90.</div>
<div class="step">Profit: 90 − 80 = <b>10</b>, a 12.5% profit on cost. (Multipliers: 1.5 × 0.75 = 1.125.)</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"50% up and 25% off leaves 25% profit."** Percents of different amounts do not subtract. Multiply the multipliers.

## 3. Break-even

Each unit sold brings in **price − variable cost** (the contribution). Fixed costs (rent, salaries) are paid no matter what. Break-even quantity = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit, rounded **up** to a whole unit.

## 4. Simple and compound interest

- **Simple interest:** the same interest every year, on the original amount only. Interest = P × r × t.
- **Compound interest:** each year's interest is added and then earns interest too. Value = P × (1 + r)ᵗ.

With 10% a year on 1,000: simple gives 1,100, 1,200, 1,300; compound gives 1,100, 1,210, 1,331. If interest compounds more often (say twice a year), use half the rate twice as many times: (1 + 0.05)² per year.

<div class="problem" data-problem="pf-g02"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Profit = revenue − cost; markup is on cost, margin is on price.
- Chain markups and discounts by multiplying.
- Break-even = fixed costs ÷ (price − variable cost), rounded up.
- Simple interest = Prt; compound value = P(1 + r)ᵗ.
- Compounding n times a year: rate ÷ n, n times as many periods.

</div>
