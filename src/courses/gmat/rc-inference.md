**Module 7 · about 12 minutes.** By the end you will choose inferences that the text proves and applications that share its logic.

<div class="bigidea">An inference is one short step from the text, never a leap. An application keeps the passage's logic and changes only the setting.</div>

## 1. Inference

Stems: "suggests", "implies", "it can be inferred", "the author would most likely agree". Find the relevant lines and ask: **what else must be true if these words are true?**

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: street plans">
<div class="step">Question: the author would most likely agree a city is easiest to redesign when…</div>
<div class="step">Lines: redesign has worked "where land ownership was already concentrated in a few hands, or where a government could take land".</div>
<div class="step">Small step: when few people own most of the land. ✓</div>
<div class="step">Tempting leap: "when it has been completely destroyed". The passage gives destroyed cities that kept their plans, so this contradicts it. ✗</div>
</div>

The same rules as Critical Reasoning inference (Lesson 6.5) apply: modest claims, no outside facts.

## 2. Application

Stems: "which situation is most similar", "which example best illustrates". Describe the passage's idea in topic-free words, then look for a case with the same shape.

Fertiliser passage, topic-free: *people would gain from spending later, but struggle to hold money until then; letting them commit when they have cash fixes it.* The automatic pension deduction has the same shape, even though it is about retirement, not farming.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="pattern-match"><figcaption>Strip the topic from an idea and match it to cases from other fields. The right match shares the pattern, not the words.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="myth">

**"Choose the example on the same topic as the passage."** Application questions often put a same-topic trap among the choices (another farming example with a different logic). Match the pattern, not the subject.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Inference: one supported step from specific lines; modest wording; no outside facts.
- "Would most likely agree" is an inference question.
- Application: restate the idea without its topic, then match the structure.
- Same-topic choices are often traps in application questions.
