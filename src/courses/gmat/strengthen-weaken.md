**Module 6 · about 15 minutes.** By the end you will weaken or strengthen any argument by working on its assumption.

<div class="bigidea">To weaken an argument, attack its assumption. To strengthen it, support its assumption. The evidence itself is never the target.</div>

## 1. Treat every choice as true

"Which of the following, **if true**, most weakens…" means: accept each choice as a fact and measure how much it changes your belief in the conclusion. Do not argue with the evidence; the passage's facts stand.

## 2. How to weaken

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: speed cameras">
<div class="step"><i>"After speed cameras went up on Route 9, crashes fell by a third. So the cameras made Route 9 safer."</i></div>
<div class="step">Conclusion: the cameras caused the drop. Assumption: nothing else caused it.</div>
<div class="step">Prediction: a weakener will give another cause of the drop.</div>
<div class="step">The right answer: "In the same period, a new highway drew most of the traffic away from Route 9." Fewer cars, fewer crashes, cameras or not.</div>
</div>

The usual weakeners match the gaps from Lesson 6.1:

| Argument type | Weaken by showing |
|---|---|
| Cause from a link | another cause; the cause runs the other way; the link is chance |
| Plan | a side effect cancels the benefit; the plan cannot be carried out |
| Sample | the sample is not typical |
| Numbers | the numbers measure different things (sales vs profit, rate vs total) |
| Analogy | the cases differ in a way that matters |

## 3. How to strengthen

Strengtheners **close the gap**: rule out other causes, show the side effect is small, show the sample is typical, or show the effect changes when the cause changes ("in colder springs, the birds nested later").

<figure class="fig" data-fig="evidence-scale"><figcaption>Tap each statement to add it to the argument and watch the scale. Statements that rule out other causes lift the conclusion; statements that offer other causes sink it; off-topic ones do nothing.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="myth">

**"A weakener must prove the conclusion false."** It only has to make the conclusion less likely. "Most weakens" means the biggest effect among the five, not a knock-out blow.

</div>

## 4. Traps

- **Out of scope:** a true-sounding fact about something next to the topic (the cost of the cameras).
- **Opposite direction:** a strengthener in a weaken question, or the reverse. Mark each choice +, − or 0 as you read.
- **Shifting the measure:** the conclusion is about profit and the choice talks about sales.
- **Trend before:** "crashes had been rising for years" does not give another cause of the drop.

## 5. Practice

<div class="keypoints">

- Accept every choice as true; judge only its effect.
- Weaken = attack the assumption (other cause, side effect, bad sample, wrong measure).
- Strengthen = support the assumption (rule out other causes, show cause and effect move together).
- Mark choices +, − or 0 to avoid picking the opposite job.
- A weakener lowers the conclusion's likelihood; it need not disprove it.

</div>
