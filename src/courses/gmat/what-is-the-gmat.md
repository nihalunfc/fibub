<div class="bigidea">The GMAT is a 2-hour-15-minute computer test of three skills business schools care about: working with numbers, reasoning with words, and making sense of data.</div>

## 1. Who takes it and why

The GMAT (Graduate Management Admission Test) is the entrance test most business schools use for MBA, Master's in Finance, Master's in Management and Business Analytics programmes. Schools use it because it puts every applicant, from any country or degree, on the same scale. A good score does not prove you will be a good manager, but it tells a school that you can handle the maths and the reading in the first year.

The test you will sit is the one GMAC launched in 2023 as the "GMAT Focus Edition"; since 2024 it is simply called the GMAT. If you see old books with Sentence Correction, geometry or an essay, they describe the older test. This course teaches only the current one.

## 2. The three sections

<figure class="fig" data-fig="gmat-map"><figcaption>Pick an order and a section to read what is in it. Every section is 45 minutes.</figcaption></figure>

| Section | Questions | Time | What it tests | Lessons |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quantitative Reasoning | 21 | 45 min | Arithmetic, algebra, word problems, statistics, counting, probability. Multiple choice, no calculator. | Modules 2 to 5 |
| Verbal Reasoning | 23 | 45 min | Reading Comprehension and Critical Reasoning. | Modules 6 and 7 |
| Data Insights | 20 | 45 min | Data Sufficiency, Table Analysis, Graphics Interpretation, Two-Part Analysis, Multi-Source Reasoning. Calculator allowed. | Module 8 |

That is **64 questions in 135 minutes**, roughly two minutes per question. You may take one optional 10-minute break after the first or second section.

## 3. Things that make the GMAT different from a school exam

1. **It is adaptive.** The computer picks each question based on how you are doing. Get questions right and they get harder; that is how the test separates scores at the top. Lesson 1.2 explains what this means for you.
2. **You cannot skip around freely.** Questions come one at a time. You can **bookmark** a question and, if time is left at the end of the section, go back and **change up to three answers**. Lesson 9.1 shows how to use that review screen well.
3. **There is no negative marking, but there is a penalty for not finishing.** An unanswered question at the end costs more than a wrong guess, so you must always answer every question.
4. **It tests reasoning, not facts.** There is no business knowledge, no formula sheet to memorise beyond school maths, and no grammar rules. Every question gives you what you need; the skill is using it fast and accurately.

<div class="myth">

**"The GMAT is a maths test."** Only a third of it is. Verbal Reasoning and Data Insights count just as much toward your total score, and Data Insights mixes numbers with reading. Strong maths students often lose most of their points in Verbal.

</div>

## 4. A taste of each question style

<div class="steps" data-title="One question from each section (original examples)">
<div class="step"><b>Quantitative Reasoning.</b> "A price rises 20% and then falls 20%. How does the final price compare with the original?" Five numeric or worded answer choices; you pick one. (Answer: 4% lower. Lesson 4.1 shows why.)</div>
<div class="step"><b>Verbal: Critical Reasoning.</b> A short argument, such as "Sales rose after the new logo, so the logo caused the rise", then a question like "Which of the following, if true, most weakens the argument?" (Lessons 6.1 to 6.6.)</div>
<div class="step"><b>Verbal: Reading Comprehension.</b> A passage of 200 to 350 words on business, science or society, followed by 3 or 4 questions about its main point, details and logic. (Lessons 7.1 to 7.4.)</div>
<div class="step"><b>Data Insights: Data Sufficiency.</b> "Is x &gt; 0? (1) x² = 9. (2) x³ = 27." You do not solve; you decide which statements give enough information. (Lesson 8.1.)</div>
<div class="step"><b>Data Insights: the others.</b> A sortable table, a chart with fill-in-the-blank menus, a two-column answer grid, or three tabs of information. Each is read like a real work task. (Lessons 8.3 to 8.6.)</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- The GMAT has three 45-minute sections: Quantitative Reasoning (21 questions), Verbal Reasoning (23) and Data Insights (20).
- You choose the section order; one optional 10-minute break.
- It is computer-adaptive: right answers lead to harder questions.
- You can bookmark questions and change up to three answers per section at the end.
- No negative marking, so never leave a question blank.
- A calculator is allowed only in Data Insights.
