**Module 9 · about 12 minutes.** By the end you will have a pacing plan for each section and a rule for when to guess.

<div class="bigidea">The GMAT rewards finishing calmly more than solving the hardest question. Spend your minutes where they buy marks, and let one or two questions go.</div>

## 1. Time per question

| Section | Questions | Minutes | Per question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quantitative Reasoning | 21 | 45 | about 2 min 10 s |
| Verbal Reasoning | 23 | 45 | about 2 min |
| Data Insights | 20 | 45 | about 2 min 15 s |

Check the official site before your test, because formats change.

## 2. Checkpoints

Rather than timing every question, glance at the clock at a few checkpoints. In Quant: question 7 with about 30 minutes left, question 14 with about 15. If you are behind by more than a question's worth, make **one** smart guess on the next question that looks long, and you are back on pace.

## 3. Smart guessing

<div class="steps" data-title="How to guess well">
<div class="step">Rule out what you can: answers of the wrong size, wrong sign, or that break a condition.</div>
<div class="step">In Data Sufficiency, if you know (1) alone fails, A and D are gone: one in three.</div>
<div class="step">Pick from what is left and move on without looking back.</div>
<div class="step">Never leave questions unanswered at the end: the penalty for unfinished questions is heavier than for wrong ones.</div>
</div>

## 4. Review and edit

You may bookmark questions, then review and change up to three answers per section at the end, if time is left. Use it for questions you bookmarked with a real doubt, not to redo the section.

## 5. Section order and test day

You choose the order of the three sections. Many people start with their strongest section to settle in; others start with the one that tires them most. Try both in the practice tests and keep what gives your best score. Sleep well, eat before, arrive early (or set up your room early for the online test), and plan your optional break.

<div class="myth">

**"I should get every question right."** Nobody does. Strong scores come from getting the questions you can do right, and guessing smartly on a few that would eat your time.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- About 2 minutes per question in every section.
- Use checkpoints, not a stopwatch on every question.
- Behind by a question? Guess smartly on one long question.
- Never leave questions blank at the end.
- Use review-and-edit only for real doubts; try section orders in practice.

</div>
