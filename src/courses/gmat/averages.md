**Module 5 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will solve average questions by thinking in totals.

<div class="bigidea">An average is a total shared out equally. Turn every average into a total (average × count) and the problem becomes adding and subtracting.</div>

## 1. Think in totals

**Average = sum ÷ count**, so **sum = average × count**.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="dot-plot"><figcaption>Drag the five values. The mean is the balance point of the dots; one far-away value pulls it a long way.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: four tests average 78. What score on the fifth makes the average 80?">
<div class="step">Total needed for five tests: 5 × 80 = 400.</div>
<div class="step">Total so far: 4 × 78 = 312.</div>
<div class="step">Fifth score: 400 − 312 = <b>88</b>.</div>
</div>

## 2. Adding or removing a value

Total goes up or down by that value, and the count changes by one. If 10 people average 30 and a 52-year-old joins, the new average is (300 + 52) ÷ 11 = 32.

## 3. Weighted averages

When groups of different sizes are combined, the bigger group pulls harder.

**Combined average = (n₁a₁ + n₂a₂) ÷ (n₁ + n₂)**. The answer always lies between the two averages, closer to the bigger group's.

A fast check: if 1/4 of the people average 60 and 3/4 average 80, the answer is 3/4 of the way from 60 to 80: 75.

<div class="problem" data-problem="av-g02"></div>

<div class="myth">

**"The average of two averages is the overall average."** Only when the groups are the same size. Always weight by the number in each group.

</div>

## 4. Evenly spaced numbers

For evenly spaced numbers (consecutive integers, multiples of 5, etc.) the **average = (first + last) ÷ 2**, and it equals the median. Then **sum = average × count** (Lesson 3.4 uses this for sequences).

<div class="keypoints">

- Sum = average × count; solve in totals.
- Adding or removing a value changes the total by that value and the count by one.
- Weighted average lies between the two group averages, nearer the bigger group.
- Evenly spaced numbers: average = (first + last) ÷ 2 = median.

</div>
