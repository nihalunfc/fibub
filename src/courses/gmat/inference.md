**Module 6 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will pick the one choice the facts actually prove.

<div class="bigidea">An inference must be proven by the passage. If you need even one outside fact to believe it, it is wrong.</div>

## 1. Spot the type

Stems: "If the statements above are true, which must also be true?", "most strongly supported", "which conclusion can be drawn?". The passage usually has **no conclusion** of its own: just facts. Your answer is the conclusion.

## 2. Combine the facts

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: restaurants on Pine Street">
<div class="step">Facts: all Pine Street restaurants open on Sundays; some serve only vegetarian food; no vegetarian-only restaurant opens after 10 p.m.</div>
<div class="step">Chain: those vegetarian-only Pine Street restaurants open on Sundays <b>and</b> close by 10 p.m.</div>
<div class="step">So: "Some restaurants open on Sundays are not open after 10 p.m." Proven.</div>
<div class="step">"No Pine Street restaurant opens after 10 p.m." goes too far: the non-vegetarian ones might.</div>
</div>

## 3. Hidden arithmetic

Many inference passages hide a calculation. Price down 20% and customers up 10%: revenue is 0.8 × 1.1 = 0.88, a fall. Fewer farms on the same land: bigger farms. Always ask, "what do these numbers force?"

<div class="myth">

**"The answer should be an insightful conclusion."** The right answer is often dull: a careful restatement or a small combination of two facts. Exciting choices usually go beyond the passage.

</div>

## 4. Elimination checklist

Cross out a choice if it:

1. needs outside information;
2. uses stronger words than the passage supports (all, never, most, the main);
3. compares things the passage does not compare;
4. predicts the future when the passage is about the past.

<div class="keypoints">

- Inference = what the facts prove, with no outside knowledge.
- Chain statements together; look for hidden arithmetic.
- Prefer modest wording; strong words need strong proof.
- Eliminate outside information, extreme wording, new comparisons and predictions.
