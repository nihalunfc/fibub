**Module 1 · about 11 minutes.** By the end you will be able to read a GMAT score report and set a realistic target.

<div class="bigidea">Your GMAT score depends on how many questions you get right and how hard they were, combined equally across the three sections.</div>

## 1. The numbers on the report

- **Total score: 205 to 805**, in steps of 10 (every score ends in 5, such as 655).
- **Three section scores: 60 to 90 each**, for Quantitative Reasoning, Verbal Reasoning and Data Insights.
- **Percentile ranks** for the total and for each section.

All three sections count **equally** toward the total. A strong Verbal score can make up for a weaker Quant score, and the other way round.

## 2. Why hard questions are worth more

<figure class="fig" data-fig="adaptive"><figcaption>Press the buttons to answer questions right or wrong. The test moves to harder questions after right answers and easier ones after wrong answers, and settles near your level. This is a teaching picture; GMAC's real method is more detailed.</figcaption></figure>

The GMAT is **computer-adaptive**. The first question is of medium difficulty. Each answer changes the computer's estimate of your level, and the next question is chosen to match. Two students can both get 15 of 21 questions right and receive different scores, because one of them was answering harder questions.

What this means in practice:

1. **Every question counts.** Early questions move the estimate most, but a run of careless mistakes late in the section still pulls your score down.
2. **A question feeling hard is a good sign.** It usually means you are doing well. Do not panic.
3. **You cannot "save" time for later easy questions.** There is no easy section at the end. Spend about the same time on each question, as Lesson 9.1 explains.
4. **Do not leave blanks.** Each unanswered question is penalised more than a wrong answer. If time is running out, guess.

<div class="myth">

**"A percentile is the percent of questions I got right."** No. A percentile tells you what share of recent test takers you scored above. A 70th-percentile Verbal score means you did better than about 70% of people, not that you got 70% of the questions right. On an adaptive test most people get a similar share right, because the questions adapt to them.

</div>

## 3. What score should you aim for?

Each school publishes the middle 80% range of its admitted students' scores. Look up your target schools and aim a little above their middle. Two practical rules:

- **Balance matters.** Many schools look at the section scores too. A very low Quant score can worry an MBA programme even with a good total.
- **Plan for one retake.** You can take the GMAT up to five times in any 12 months (eight times in total), with at least 16 days between attempts. Book your first test early enough to leave room for a second.

## 4. Scores after the test

You see your unofficial total and section scores on screen right after the test, and you may then accept or cancel them. Scores are valid for five years. Schools see only the scores you choose to send.
