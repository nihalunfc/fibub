**Module 8 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will map a three-tab set in under two minutes and know which tab answers each question.

<div class="bigidea">Multi-Source Reasoning is Reading Comprehension with tables. Map each tab in one line, then for each question ask: which tab, or which two tabs, hold the answer?</div>

## 1. What you get

Two or three tabs of information: emails, memos, tables, a chart. Then about three questions on the set. Some are single choices; others give three statements to mark yes or no.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="msr-tabs"><figcaption>A three-tab set about a delivery firm. Switch tabs, then try each question: the figure lights up the tabs it needs.</figcaption></figure>

## 2. Map the tabs

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: the delivery firm">
<div class="step"><b>Tab 1, Policy:</b> standard delivery in 5 working days; express in 2; express costs 12 dollars extra.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Tab 2, Email from the operations manager:</b> since March, standard parcels to the north region take 7 days because of a depot closure.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Tab 3, Table:</b> parcels sent per region and the share sent express.</div>
<div class="step">Question: "A customer in the north orders standard delivery in April. Will the parcel arrive within the policy's promise?" Needs tabs 1 and 2: the policy says 5, the email says 7. <b>No.</b></div>
</div>

## 3. Conflicts and assumptions

Sources sometimes disagree on purpose. Read the question carefully: "according to the policy" points to one tab; "most likely" asks you to weigh them (a later, specific email usually beats a general policy). Never bring in outside facts.

<div class="keypoints">

- One line per tab before any question.
- For each question, name the tab or tabs it needs.
- Conflicts are deliberate: the question tells you which source matters.
- Yes-no statement sets: judge each statement on its own.
- Inference rules from Critical Reasoning apply: no outside facts, no big leaps.

</div>
