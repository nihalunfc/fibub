**Module 1 · about 15 minutes.** By the end you will multiply, divide and find percents of GMAT-sized numbers in your head, and estimate well enough to rule out wrong answers in seconds.

<div class="bigidea">GMAT numbers are chosen to be friendly. Break them into easy pieces, and most "calculations" become two or three small steps you can do in your head.</div>

## 1. Why this matters

Quantitative Reasoning has no calculator, and you have about two minutes per question. Students who know the methods but calculate slowly run out of time. The aim here is not to become a human calculator; it is to spot the shortcut the question writer built in.

## 2. Multiply by splitting

<figure class="fig" data-fig="area-model"><figcaption>Any two-digit product is four small rectangles: tens × tens, tens × ones, ones × tens and ones × ones. Move the sliders and add the pieces.</figcaption></figure>

Most of the time you only need to split **one** number:

- 18 × 14 = 18 × 10 + 18 × 4 = 180 + 72 = 252.
- 49 × 12 = 50 × 12 − 1 × 12 = 600 − 12 = 588. (Round up, then take back the extra.)
- 25 × 36 = 25 × 4 × 9 = 100 × 9 = 900. (Look for pairs that make 10 or 100: 2 × 5, 4 × 25, 8 × 125.)

## 3. Squares you should just know

Know the squares from 1² to 20² (1, 4, 9, ..., 361) and 25² = 625. For numbers near a round number, use (a ± b)² = a² ± 2ab + b²:

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: 48² in your head">
<div class="step">Write 48 as 50 − 2.</div>
<div class="step">50² = 2,500.</div>
<div class="step">2 × 50 × 2 = 200. Because it is 50 <b>minus</b> 2, subtract: 2,500 − 200 = 2,300.</div>
<div class="step">2² = 4 is always added: 2,300 + 4 = <b>2,304</b>.</div>
</div>

A number ending in 5: multiply the tens digit by the next whole number and put 25 after it. 35² → 3 × 4 = 12 → 1,225. 65² → 6 × 7 = 42 → 4,225.

<div class="problem" data-problem="mm-g03"></div>

## 4. Percents in two steps

Every percent is built from **10%** (move the decimal point one place left), **5%** (half of 10%) and **1%** (move it two places).

| Want | Build it from | Example: of 340 |
|---|---|---|
| 15% | 10% + 5% | 34 + 17 = 51 |
| 35% | 3 × 10% + 5% | 102 + 17 = 119 |
| 12% | 10% + 2 × 1% | 34 + 6.8 = 40.8 |
| 75% | whole − 25% (a quarter) | 340 − 85 = 255 |

A useful flip: **x% of y = y% of x**. 16% of 25 is the same as 25% of 16, which is 4.

## 5. Dividing without long division

- **By 5:** double, then divide by 10. 435 ÷ 5 = 870 ÷ 10 = 87.
- **By 25:** multiply by 4, then divide by 100. 1,350 ÷ 25 = 5,400 ÷ 100 = 54.
- **By 4 or 8:** halve two or three times. 744 ÷ 8 → 372 → 186 → 93.
- **By 15:** double both numbers first. 690 ÷ 15 = 1,380 ÷ 30 = 46.

## 6. Estimate to kill answers

GMAT answer choices are usually far enough apart that a rough estimate picks the right one. Round to one significant figure, and remember which way you rounded.

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: which is closest to 0.48 × 61.9 × 19.7?">
<div class="step">0.48 is almost 1/2, 61.9 is almost 62 and 19.7 is almost 20.</div>
<div class="step">1/2 × 62 × 20 = 31 × 20 = 620.</div>
<div class="step">If the choices are 60, 200, 600, 2,000 and 6,000, the answer is clearly <b>600</b> (exact value 585.3). No long multiplication needed.</div>
</div>

<div class="problem" data-problem="fr-g03"></div>

<div class="myth">

**"Estimating is a guess."** On the GMAT it is a method. When the choices differ by a factor of 2 or more, an estimate within 10% is as good as an exact answer and takes a quarter of the time. Only calculate exactly when two choices are close together.

</div>

## 7. Fractions you should know as decimals

1/2 = 0.5, 1/3 ≈ 0.333, 1/4 = 0.25, 1/5 = 0.2, 1/6 ≈ 0.167, 1/8 = 0.125, 1/9 ≈ 0.111, 1/10 = 0.1, 1/12 ≈ 0.083, and their multiples (3/8 = 0.375, 5/6 ≈ 0.833). Knowing these lets you compare fractions and percents instantly.

<div class="keypoints">

- Split one number to multiply: 49 × 12 = 50 × 12 − 12.
- Look for pairs that make 100: 4 × 25, 8 × 125, 2 × 50.
- Squares near a round number: (a ± b)² = a² ± 2ab + b²; numbers ending in 5 have a shortcut.
- Percents from 10%, 5% and 1%; and x% of y = y% of x.
- Divide by 5, 25 and 15 by first multiplying both numbers to make a round divisor.
- Estimate first; calculate exactly only when two choices are close.

</div>
