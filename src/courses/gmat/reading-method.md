**Module 7 · about 14 minutes.** By the end you will read a 300-word passage in about 3 minutes and know where to find every answer.

<div class="bigidea">You are not reading to remember facts. You are reading to understand the author's point and the job of each paragraph, so you can find any detail in seconds.</div>

## 1. What Reading Comprehension looks like

Each passage has 200 to 350 words on business, science, history or society, followed by 3 or 4 questions. Expect about 4 passages in the Verbal section, so roughly 13 to 14 questions: more than half of Verbal.

No outside knowledge is needed or wanted. If you know the topic, be careful: answer from the passage, not from what you know.

## 2. Read for structure

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: mapping the street-plan passage">
<div class="step"><b>Paragraph 1:</b> Planners hope to rebuild with wide streets, but rebuilt cities keep old plans (London 1666). → <i>The puzzle.</i></div>
<div class="step"><b>Paragraph 2:</b> The usual answer is money or will; the author's answer is property rights and slow land swaps. → <i>The explanation.</i></div>
<div class="step"><b>Paragraph 3:</b> Street plans are like contracts; redesign works where land is concentrated. → <i>Wider point and support.</i></div>
<div class="step"><b>Main point:</b> Old street plans survive mainly because changing them means overriding many owners' property claims.</div>
</div>

Your map should be that short: one line per paragraph, and the main point. Spend most of your time on topic sentences and turns; skim lists of examples.

## 3. Signal words

| Signal | Means | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Contrast | the direction changes; often the author's view follows | however, yet, but, although, in fact |
| Continue | more of the same | moreover, also, similarly |
| Example | support for the claim before it | for instance, such as |
| Conclusion | the point | thus, therefore, then, so |
| Opinion | the author's attitude | surprisingly, unfortunately, convincing, flawed |

## 4. Go back for every answer

The right answer is always supported by specific words in the passage. For each question, find the lines, reread them, then choose. Answering from memory is the biggest source of errors.

<div class="myth">

**"I must understand every sentence before moving on."** Hard passages are written to be dense. Get the main point and the paragraph jobs; when a question asks about a difficult detail, reread just those lines then.

</div>

## 5. Timing

About 2.5 to 3 minutes to read and map, then about 1 to 1.5 minutes per question. A four-question passage takes about 7 to 8 minutes.

## 6. Practice

<div class="qbank" data-bank="grad-verbal" data-topics="rc-passages" data-mode="practice" data-n="4" data-exam="gmat"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Read for the main point and each paragraph's job, not for every fact.
- Make a short map: one line per paragraph plus the main point.
- Contrast words (however, yet, but) often lead to the author's view.
- Go back to the passage for every answer.
- About 3 minutes to read, then 1 to 1.5 minutes per question.

</div>
