**Module 3 · about 16 minutes.** By the end you will factor quadratics in your head and use the three special products to skip whole calculations.

<div class="bigidea">If two numbers multiply to zero, one of them is zero. Factoring a quadratic turns one hard equation into two easy ones.</div>

## 1. Factoring x² + bx + c

Find two numbers that **multiply to c** and **add to b**.

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: x² + 2x − 15 = 0">
<div class="step">Need two numbers with product −15 and sum +2.</div>
<div class="step">Pairs for −15: (1, −15), (−1, 15), (3, −5), (−3, 5). The pair −3 and 5 adds to 2.</div>
<div class="step">So x² + 2x − 15 = (x − 3)(x + 5).</div>
<div class="step">(x − 3)(x + 5) = 0 means x − 3 = 0 or x + 5 = 0: <b>x = 3 or x = −5</b>.</div>
</div>

If the x² has a number in front, divide it out first when you can (2x² − 8x + 6 = 0 → x² − 4x + 3 = 0).

<div class="problem" data-problem="qu-g02"></div>

## 2. See it on a graph

<figure class="fig" data-fig="parabola"><figcaption>Choose the two roots. The curve y = (x − p)(x − q) crosses the x-axis exactly there, and the expanded form shows that the roots add to −b and multiply to c.</figcaption></figure>

So for x² + bx + c = 0: **sum of roots = −b, product = c**. A question asking for the sum of the solutions needs no solving at all.

<div class="problem" data-problem="qu-g01"></div>

## 3. The three special products

| Pattern | Use it when you see |
|---|---|
| (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b² | a + b and ab given, a² + b² asked |
| (a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b² | a − b and ab given |
| (a + b)(a − b) = a² − b² | squares subtracted, or numbers like 51 × 49 |

51 × 49 = (50 + 1)(50 − 1) = 2,500 − 1 = 2,499. And 87² − 13² = (100)(74) = 7,400.

<div class="problem" data-problem="qu-g03"></div>

<div class="myth">

**"(a + b)² = a² + b²."** The middle term 2ab is the most forgotten term in algebra. Check with numbers: (2 + 3)² = 25, but 2² + 3² = 13.

</div>

## 4. Never divide by a variable

x² = 6x has two solutions, 0 and 6. Dividing both sides by x would silently throw away x = 0, because you cannot divide by zero. Always move everything to one side and factor.

## 5. The top or bottom of a parabola

y = ax² + bx + c turns at **x = −b ÷ (2a)**. If a is negative the curve opens downward and that point is the **maximum**: the best price, the biggest area. Put the x back in to get the value.

<div class="problem" data-problem="qu-g04"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Factor x² + bx + c with two numbers that multiply to c and add to b.
- Product zero → a factor is zero: each bracket gives a root.
- Sum of roots = −b and product = c (when the x² coefficient is 1).
- Use (a ± b)² and (a + b)(a − b) to skip calculations.
- Never divide by a variable; move terms to one side and factor.
- A parabola turns at x = −b/(2a).

</div>
