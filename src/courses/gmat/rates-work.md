**Module 4 · about 16 minutes.** By the end you will solve travel and work problems with one table and one rule: rates add, times do not.

<div class="bigidea">Distance = rate × time, and work = rate × time. When things move or work together, add their rates, never their times.</div>

## 1. The rate table

Make a small table for every rate question: one row per traveller or worker, columns **rate × time = distance (or work)**. Fill what you know; the empty cell is your equation.

| | Rate | Time | Distance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Car | 60 km/h | t | 60t |
| Bus | 40 km/h | t + 1 | 40(t + 1) |

**Units first:** turn minutes into hours (45 min = 0.75 h) before multiplying.

## 2. Average speed

**Average speed = total distance ÷ total time**, never the average of the speeds.

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: 120 km at 40 km/h, back at 60 km/h">
<div class="step">Time out: 120 ÷ 40 = 3 h. Time back: 120 ÷ 60 = 2 h.</div>
<div class="step">Total: 240 km in 5 h.</div>
<div class="step">Average speed: 240 ÷ 5 = <b>48 km/h</b>, less than 50, because more time was spent going slowly.</div>
</div>

<div class="problem" data-problem="rt-g02"></div>

<div class="myth">

**"Average speed is the middle of the two speeds."** That only works when equal <i>times</i> are spent at each speed. For equal <i>distances</i> the average is always below the middle.

</div>

## 3. Closing speed

<figure class="fig" data-fig="rate-race"><figcaption>Toward each other, the gap shrinks at the sum of the speeds. When one chases the other, it shrinks at the difference.</figcaption></figure>

- Moving **toward** each other (or apart): add the speeds.
- Moving the **same way** (catching up): subtract the speeds.

## 4. Work problems

Treat a job as 1 whole. If someone finishes it in 6 hours, their rate is 1/6 of the job per hour.

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: Ana paints a room in 6 h, Ben in 3 h. Together?">
<div class="step">Rates: 1/6 and 1/3 room per hour.</div>
<div class="step">Add: 1/6 + 2/6 = 3/6 = 1/2 room per hour.</div>
<div class="step">Time = 1 ÷ (1/2) = <b>2 hours</b>. Faster than either alone, as it must be.</div>
</div>

Shortcut for two workers: **together = ab ÷ (a + b)** (6 × 3 ÷ 9 = 2). A pipe that empties a tank has a **negative** rate.

<div class="problem" data-problem="rt-g03"></div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Rate × time = distance (or work). Convert units first.
- Average speed = total distance ÷ total time.
- Toward each other: add speeds. Chasing: subtract.
- Work: rate = 1/time; add rates, then time = 1 ÷ total rate. Two workers: ab/(a + b).
- An emptying pipe has a negative rate.

</div>
