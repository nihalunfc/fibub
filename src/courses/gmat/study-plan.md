**Module 1 · about 10 minutes.** By the end you will have a schedule that fits your week and a simple system for learning from every mistake.

<div class="bigidea">The fastest way to a higher score is a steady routine: learn one method, practise it at once, and study your mistakes more than your successes.</div>

## 1. Pick your plan

Most people need 80 to 120 hours of focused study. Choose the plan that fits your life; each one covers the whole course in order.

| Plan | Time a day | Weeks | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fast | 2 hours | 6 | Strong maths and reading, or an early deadline |
| Standard | 1.5 hours | 10 | Most people with a job or classes |
| Steady | 1 hour | 14 | Busy schedules, or rebuilding school maths |

## 2. The week-by-week map (standard 10-week plan)

| Week | Lessons | Also do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Module 1, then 2.1 to 2.3 | Full practice test 1 (Lesson 9.2) on day 1 |
| 2 | 2.4, 2.5, Module 3 | Start your error log |
| 3 | Module 4 | Mixed review of Modules 2 and 3 |
| 4 | Module 5 | Quant review (Lesson 5.5) |
| 5 | Module 6 (Critical Reasoning) | Daily: 10 quant questions to stay sharp |
| 6 | Module 7 (Reading Comprehension) | One passage a day from a quality newspaper or science magazine |
| 7 | Lessons 8.1 to 8.3 | Data Sufficiency every day |
| 8 | Lessons 8.4 to 8.6 | Full practice test 2 |
| 9 | Lesson 9.1, then your three weakest topics from the error log | Timed sets of 10 |
| 10 | Light review, Lesson 9.3 | Full practice test 3 early in the week; rest the day before the exam |

For the **6-week plan**, do about 1.7 weeks of the table each week. For the **14-week plan**, stretch Modules 2 to 5 over six weeks, since the maths base matters most.

## 3. One study session, done right

<div class="steps" data-title="A 90-minute session">
<div class="step"><b>10 minutes: warm up.</b> Redo three questions from your error log without looking at your notes.</div>
<div class="step"><b>30 minutes: learn.</b> Read one lesson, play its figures and follow its worked example step by step.</div>
<div class="step"><b>30 minutes: practise.</b> Do its live problems until you get three right in a row, then the quiz.</div>
<div class="step"><b>15 minutes: review.</b> For every question you missed or guessed, write one line in the error log.</div>
<div class="step"><b>5 minutes: plan.</b> Mark the lesson as done and note what to do tomorrow.</div>
</div>

## 4. The error log

Keep a simple table (paper, a spreadsheet or a notes app). For each missed or guessed question write:

1. **Topic** (for example "weighted averages").
2. **Why I missed it**, in one of four words: **concept** (I did not know the method), **careless** (I knew it but slipped), **timing** (I ran out of time) or **trap** (I fell for a tempting wrong choice).
3. **The fix**, in one sentence ("Always divide by the old value for percent change").

Every week, count the reasons. Mostly *concept*? Go back to the lessons. Mostly *careless*? Slow down and write each step. Mostly *timing*? Practise with a clock. Mostly *trap*? Reread the trap boxes in each lesson.

<div class="myth">

**"Doing thousands of questions is the way to improve."** Volume without review just repeats the same mistakes. Thirty questions with careful review beat a hundred without it. Research on learning shows that testing yourself and spacing practice over days work far better than rereading.

</div>

## 5. When are you ready?

You are ready when your last two full practice tests are at or above your target and your error log shows few *concept* mistakes. If you are 30 or more points short a week before the test, think about moving the date; rescheduling costs less than a retake.
