**Module 4 · about 15 minutes.** By the end you will solve every percent question with one idea: a percent change is a multiplier.

<div class="bigidea">Increasing by 20% means multiplying by 1.2; decreasing by 20% means multiplying by 0.8. Think in multipliers and percent problems become one-line calculations.</div>

## 1. Percent means "per hundred"

35% = 35/100 = 0.35. "What is 35% of 80?" → 0.35 × 80 = 28. "28 is what percent of 80?" → 28 ÷ 80 = 0.35 = 35%.

<div class="problem" data-problem="pc-g04"></div>

## 2. Percent change

**Percent change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100.** Always divide by the **old** (starting) value.

From 50 to 60 is a 20% increase; from 60 to 50 is a 16.7% decrease. Same gap, different starting point.

<div class="problem" data-problem="pc-g01"></div>

## 3. Multipliers

| Change | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| up 20% | × 1.20 |
| down 20% | × 0.80 |
| up 5% | × 1.05 |
| down 35% | × 0.65 |
| up 100% (doubles) | × 2 |

<figure class="fig" data-fig="percent-bar"><figcaption>Start at 100 and apply two changes. The second percent is taken of the new amount, so +20% then −20% ends at 96, not 100.</figcaption></figure>

**Successive changes multiply.** Up 10% then up 10% is 1.1 × 1.1 = 1.21: a 21% rise, not 20%.

<div class="problem" data-problem="pc-g02"></div>

## 4. Working backwards

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: after a 15% discount a phone costs 340 dollars. Find the original price.">
<div class="step">A 15% discount is a multiplier of 0.85: original × 0.85 = 340.</div>
<div class="step">Divide: original = 340 ÷ 0.85.</div>
<div class="step">340 ÷ 0.85 = 34,000 ÷ 85 = <b>400 dollars</b>. Check: 15% of 400 is 60, and 400 − 60 = 340. ✓</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Add 15% of the sale price back on."** 15% of 340 is 51, giving 391, which is wrong. The discount was 15% of the <i>original</i>, so you must divide by 0.85, not multiply by 1.15.

</div>

<div class="problem" data-problem="pc-g03"></div>

## 5. Percent of a percent, and percentage points

- 40% of employees are remote and 30% of those live abroad: 0.3 × 0.4 = 0.12, so 12% of all employees.
- **Percentage points** are a plain difference of two percents; **percent change** compares them. A rate from 4% to 5% rose 1 percentage point, which is a 25% increase.

## 6. Smart number: start with 100

When a question gives only percents and no amounts, pretend the starting value is 100. Every percent is then a plain number, and the answer drops out.

<div class="keypoints">

- Percent change = (new − old) ÷ old.
- Turn changes into multipliers: up p% → × (1 + p/100); down p% → × (1 − p/100).
- Successive changes multiply; they do not add or cancel.
- To find the original, divide by the multiplier.
- Percentage points are differences; percent change is relative. Start at 100 when no amounts are given.

</div>
