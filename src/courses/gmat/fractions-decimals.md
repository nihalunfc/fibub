**Module 2 · about 14 minutes.** By the end you will work with fractions as fast as with whole numbers and avoid the classic "fraction of the remainder" trap.

<div class="bigidea">A fraction is a division waiting to happen. Keep it as a fraction while you calculate, and turn it into a decimal only to compare or estimate.</div>

## 1. The four operations

- **Add or subtract:** use a common denominator. 2/3 + 1/4 = 8/12 + 3/12 = 11/12.
- **Multiply:** top × top, bottom × bottom, and **cancel first**: 9/14 × 7/12 = (9 × 7)/(14 × 12) → cancel 7 and 3 → 3/8.
- **Divide:** multiply by the flip. (3/4) ÷ (9/8) = 3/4 × 8/9 = 2/3.
- **Mixed numbers:** turn into improper fractions first: 2½ = 5/2.

## 2. Comparing fractions

<figure class="fig" data-fig="fraction-bars"><figcaption>Set two fractions and compare the bars. The cross-multiplication rule below the bars always agrees with the picture.</figcaption></figure>

To compare a/b with c/d (positive numbers), **cross-multiply**: compare a × d with c × b. The fraction with the bigger cross product is bigger. Also useful: compare each fraction with 1/2, or see how far each is from 1 (7/8 is 1/8 short of 1, 8/9 is 1/9 short, so 8/9 is bigger).

## 3. "Of the remainder"

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: 2/5 of a budget goes to rent, then 1/3 of what is left to food. What fraction is left?">
<div class="step">After rent, 1 − 2/5 = <b>3/5</b> of the budget is left.</div>
<div class="step">Food takes 1/3 <b>of that 3/5</b>: 1/3 × 3/5 = 1/5 of the whole budget.</div>
<div class="step">Left: 3/5 − 1/5 = <b>2/5</b>. (Shortcut: multiply what stays each time: 3/5 × 2/3 = 2/5.)</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"2/5 + 1/3 of the budget is spent."** The 1/3 is of the remainder, not of the whole. Read every "of" and ask: of what?

</div>

## 4. Terminating decimals

A fraction **in lowest terms** has a terminating decimal exactly when its denominator has no primes except 2 and 5. 7/40 = 0.175 terminates (40 = 2³ × 5); 1/6 = 0.1666… repeats (6 has a 3). Simplify first: 3/12 = 1/4 terminates even though 12 has a 3.

## 5. Estimate first

When the answer choices are far apart, round and estimate (Lesson 1.4). When they are close, keep exact fractions to the end, since early rounding can push you to the wrong choice.

<div class="keypoints">

- Multiply: cancel first. Divide: multiply by the flip.
- Compare fractions by cross-multiplying, or by distance from 1/2 or 1.
- "Of the remainder": multiply the fractions that stay each time.
- Lowest-terms denominators with only 2s and 5s give terminating decimals.
- Estimate when choices are far apart; stay exact when they are close.
