**Module 7 · about 12 minutes.** By the end you will answer "why" and "how" questions straight from your passage map.

<div class="bigidea">Function, structure and tone questions are about the author's choices, not the facts. Your map already holds the answers.</div>

## 1. Function: why is this here?

Stems: "in order to", "serves mainly to", "the author mentions X because". Look at what the detail **supports** or **contrasts with**. Examples support claims; concessions admit a point before rejecting it; analogies make an idea vivid.

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: the harvest-time experiment">
<div class="step">What does the experiment say? Farmers bought more when allowed to pay at harvest, at the same price.</div>
<div class="step">What claim is near it? The timing explanation, which the author favours over the information explanation.</div>
<div class="step">So its job: to support the timing explanation. Not "to show farmers respond to price" (price did not change).</div>
</div>

## 2. Structure: how is it built?

Most GMAT passages follow one of a few shapes:

| Shape | Signals |
|---|---|
| Puzzle → explanations → preferred answer | "Why, then…?", "a more convincing explanation" |
| Old view → new view | "traditionally", "recent research shows" |
| Claim → evidence → limits | "for example", "however, this applies only" |
| Compare two things | "by contrast", "similarly" |

## 3. Tone

Authors on the GMAT are academic: they argue, doubt, and approve, but rarely rage or gush. Prefer **moderate** words: measured, sceptical, cautiously optimistic, mildly critical. Eliminate extremes (scornful, ecstatic) and "indifferent", since an author who wrote the passage cares about the topic.

<div class="myth">

**"A critical author is hostile."** Critical on the GMAT means weighing evidence. An author who says an explanation "changed behaviour very little" is sceptical of it, not angry.

</div>
