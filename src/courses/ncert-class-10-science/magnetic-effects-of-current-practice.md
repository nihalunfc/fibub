## How to set out your answers

- Draw the diagram first when the question has a picture (field lines, circuit) and label it.
- For direction questions, name the rule you use (right-hand thumb rule or Fleming's left-hand rule) and state the three directions.
- For numbers, write the formula, put in the values with units, and give the answer with its unit.
- Match the length to the marks: one fact for 1 mark, a reason for 2, a short explanation for 3, a labelled diagram plus steps for 5.

## 1-mark questions

**1.** Name the device used to find the direction of a magnetic field.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A magnetic compass (a small magnetic needle).</details>

**2.** Can two magnetic field lines cross each other? Why?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>No. At a crossing point the compass would have to point in two directions at once.</details>

**3.** Which rule gives the direction of the force on a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Fleming's left-hand rule.</details>

**4.** What is the function of the earth wire in a house?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It carries any leakage current safely to the ground so that the metal body of an appliance never stays at a dangerous voltage.</details>

**5.** Give one example of a device that works as an electromagnet.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>An electric bell, or a crane that lifts scrap iron.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

**6.** (2 marks) State the right-hand thumb rule.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Hold the current-carrying wire in the right fist with the thumb stretched out along the current. The fingers curl in the direction of the magnetic field lines around the wire.</details>

**7.** (2 marks) What is meant by a short circuit? Why is it dangerous?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>A short circuit happens when the live and neutral wires touch, so the resistance becomes almost zero. The current then becomes huge, heats the wires and can cause a fire.</details>

**8.** (3 marks) A wire carries 4 A. What is the magnetic field at 2 cm from it? Use $\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}$ T m/A.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Use `$B = \mu_0 I / (2\pi r)$`.

`$B = (2\times10^{-7} \times 4) / 0.02 = 4\times10^{-5}$` T.

</details>

**9.** (3 marks) List three ways to make the magnetic field of a solenoid stronger.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Increase the current; increase the number of turns per unit length; put a soft iron core inside the coil.</details>

## 5-mark questions

**10.** Explain how a fuse, an MCB and an earth wire protect a household circuit. What causes (a) overloading and (b) a short circuit?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Overloading: too many appliances on the same circuit, so the total current exceeds what the wire can carry.

(b) Short circuit: live and neutral touch because of damaged insulation, so resistance is almost zero.

Protection:

- A **fuse** wire melts when the current is too high and breaks the circuit.
- An **MCB** trips off at the same condition and can be reset.
- The **earth wire** joins the metal body of the appliance to the ground. If the live wire touches the body, a large current flows to the earth, the fuse or MCB cuts off the supply, and nobody gets a shock.

</details>

**13.** Describe an activity to show that a current-carrying wire produces a magnetic field, and how the direction of the field can be found. Also draw the field of a solenoid and say why an iron core makes an electromagnet.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Activity: place a compass under a straight wire, switch on the current and the needle turns away from north. Reverse the current and it turns the opposite way. So a current makes a magnetic field and the field reverses with the current.

Direction: the right-hand thumb rule. The thumb points along the current and the curled fingers show the field circles.

Solenoid: the field inside is strong and uniform along the axis, and outside it looks like a bar magnet (field lines leave the north end and enter the south end). A soft iron core is magnetised by the coil and adds its own field, so the combination is a strong magnet that can be switched on and off with the current.

</details>

## Case-based question

A student winds 40 turns of insulated copper wire on an iron nail and joins the ends to a 3 V battery. She finds that the nail picks up pins. When she removes the nail from the coil, it picks up fewer pins. When she reverses the battery, the end of the nail that attracted pins now repels a compass needle's north end.

**11a.** Name the device she has made. (1 mark)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>An electromagnet (a solenoid with a soft iron core).</details>

**11b.** Why did it pick up fewer pins without the nail? (1 mark)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The iron core becomes magnetised by the coil's field, and this greatly strengthens the total field.</details>

**11c.** What happens to the pins when the battery is disconnected, and why? (1 mark)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>They fall. Soft iron loses almost all its magnetism when the current stops.</details>

**11d.** Why did the poles swap when the battery was reversed? (1 mark)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The right-hand rule: reversing the current reverses the direction of the field, so the north and south ends exchange.</details>

## Assertion-reason questions

Choices: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true but R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

**12.** **Assertion:** The earth wire is connected to the metal body of a geyser. **Reason:** It lets leakage current flow to the ground and keeps the body at zero potential.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). Both are true and the reason is the purpose of the connection.</details>

**14.** **Assertion:** Two magnetic field lines can never intersect. **Reason:** At a point of intersection a compass needle would have to point in two directions.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(A). Both are true and the reason explains the assertion.</details>
