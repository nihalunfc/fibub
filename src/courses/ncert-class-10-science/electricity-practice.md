**Practice · Class 10 Science · Chapter 11.** Read the chapter lesson, [Electricity](/learn/courses/ncert-class-10-science/electricity/), first. Try each question on paper before you open the answer. Board answers are marked for steps, so write the formula, put in the numbers with units, then the answer.

<div class="bigidea">Most electricity questions need only four ideas: V = IR, R = ρL/A, the series and parallel rules, and P = VI = I²R. Draw the circuit, mark what is the same (current in series, voltage in parallel), and the rest follows.</div>

## How to set out a numerical

1. Write what is given, with units, and what you must find.
2. Write the formula in symbols.
3. Put in the numbers, convert units first (minutes to seconds, mm² to m², W to kW).
4. Give the answer with its unit, and check that it makes sense (a parallel total must be smaller than the smallest resistor).

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the instrument used to measure potential difference and say how it is connected.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A voltmeter, connected in parallel across the part.</details>

2. What is the resistance of a wire that carries 2 A when 10 V is applied across it?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>R = V/I = 10/2 = 5 Ω.</details>

3. Why is tungsten used for bulb filaments?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>It has a very high melting point (about 3400 °C), so it can glow white-hot without melting.</details>

4. How many joules are there in one unit of electricity?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>1 unit = 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. A wire of resistance 8 Ω is cut into two equal halves, and the halves are joined in parallel. Find the new resistance. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Each half has 4 Ω (resistance is proportional to length). In parallel: R = (4 × 4)/(4 + 4) = 2 Ω.</details>

6. Give two reasons why household appliances are connected in parallel. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(1) Each appliance gets the full mains voltage. (2) Each can be switched on and off on its own, and if one fails the others keep working. (Also: each draws the current it needs.)</details>

7. A 100 W bulb and a 1 kW heater both work on 220 V. Which has the higher resistance? Show your working. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>R = V²/P. Bulb: 220²/100 = 484 Ω. Heater: 220²/1000 = 48.4 Ω. The bulb has ten times the resistance: the higher the power at a fixed voltage, the lower the resistance.</details>

8. Explain why the coil of an electric toaster is made of an alloy and not of copper. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The coil must turn electrical energy into heat. An alloy such as nichrome has a much higher resistivity than copper, so it produces much more heat (H = I²Rt) for the current; it also does not oxidise (burn) easily when red hot. Copper would carry the current with little heating, and would oxidise at high temperature.</details>

## 5-mark questions

9. (a) State Ohm’s law. (b) Draw a circuit to verify it, naming each part. (c) Sketch the V–I graph you expect and explain how to get the resistance from it. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>(a) The current through a metal conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across its ends, provided its temperature stays the same: V = IR. (b) A battery, a plug key, an ammeter, a rheostat and the resistor in series, with a voltmeter in parallel across the resistor. Change the rheostat to get several pairs of V and I. (c) A straight line through the origin. The resistance is the slope: pick two points far apart and divide the change in V by the change in I.</details>

10. Three resistors of 2 Ω, 3 Ω and 6 Ω are available. (a) How would you join all three to get the least resistance, and what is it? (b) the most? (c) How would you join them to get 4 Ω? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>(a) All in parallel: 1/R = 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6 = 1, so R = 1 Ω. (b) All in series: 2 + 3 + 6 = 11 Ω. (c) 3 Ω and 6 Ω in parallel give (3 × 6)/(3 + 6) = 2 Ω; put that in series with the 2 Ω: 2 + 2 = 4 Ω.</details>

## Case-based question

Riya’s family uses these every day: four LED bulbs of 10 W for 6 hours, a 75 W fan for 10 hours, a 150 W fridge running 24 hours (its compressor runs about half the time), and a 2000 W geyser for 30 minutes. The tariff is ₹6 per unit.

11. (a) Find the energy used per day by the fan. (b) Which uses the most energy per day? (c) Find the monthly bill (30 days) for all four. (d) Suggest one change that saves the most money.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) 75 W × 10 h = 750 Wh = 0.75 kWh. (b) Bulbs: 4 × 10 × 6 = 240 Wh. Fan: 750 Wh. Fridge: 150 × 24 × ½ = 1800 Wh. Geyser: 2000 × 0.5 = 1000 Wh. The fridge uses the most (1.8 kWh). (c) Daily total = 0.24 + 0.75 + 1.8 + 1.0 = 3.79 kWh; 30 days = 113.7 kWh; bill = 113.7 × 6 = ₹682.20. (d) Use the geyser for less time or switch to a solar heater, or keep the fridge door shut and its coils clean; each kWh saved saves ₹6.</details>

## Assertion and reason

12. **A:** The resistance of a wire increases when it is heated. **R:** At a higher temperature the atoms vibrate more and the electrons collide with them more often.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.</details>

13. **A:** An ammeter should have a very low resistance. **R:** An ammeter is connected in parallel.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is true, R is false. An ammeter is joined in series, so it must have a very low resistance in order not to change the current it is measuring.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Series: same I, V adds, R = R₁ + R₂. Parallel: same V, I adds, 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂.
- Cutting a wire into n equal parts gives n pieces of R/n each; joined in parallel they give R/n².
- R = V²/P at a rated voltage: higher power means lower resistance.
- Bill: kWh = kW × hours; 1 unit = 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J.
- Always give units and set out formula, substitution and answer.
