**Practice · Class 10 Science · Chapter 7.** Read the chapter lesson, [How do Organisms Reproduce?](/learn/courses/ncert-class-10-science/how-organisms-reproduce/), first. Try each question on paper before you open its answer.

<div class="bigidea">Most questions ask you to compare asexual and sexual reproduction, to name the method from a description, to trace pollen to seed, to follow the human system from gamete to birth, and to explain birth control and health in a sensible way.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For "differences" questions, use two columns or two clear lines for each point.
2. Name the method or organ first, then say what it does.
3. For flower and human-system questions, draw a simple labelled diagram if the marks allow.
4. For reproductive health, give facts, not opinions; mention doctors and safe practice.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the type of reproduction in which the offspring are genetically identical to the parent.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Asexual reproduction (the offspring are clones).</details>

2. Which structure carries pollen grains in a flower?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The anther (of the stamen).</details>

3. In which part of the female reproductive system does fertilisation usually take place?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The oviduct (fallopian tube).</details>

4. What is the name of the organ through which the embryo gets food and oxygen from its mother?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The placenta.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. How is budding different from binary fission? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>In binary fission the parent splits into two equal parts, each of which becomes a new individual. In budding a small outgrowth (bud) forms on the parent, grows and then separates, so the parent stays larger than the bud at first.</details>

6. Why do spores of Rhizopus have a tough coat? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The coat protects the spore from heat and dryness while it waits in the air or on a surface. When it lands in warm, damp conditions it germinates.</details>

7. Why is variation useful to a species even if it does not help every individual? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>When the environment changes (hotter, a new disease), some individuals with a slightly different trait may survive while others die. Those survivors reproduce, so the species continues. A population of identical individuals could be wiped out by a single change.</details>

8. A farmer wants to grow banana plants and seedless oranges. Which method of reproduction would he use and why? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Vegetative propagation (cutting or grafting). These plants do not make viable seeds, and the new plants are clones, so they have the same fruit quality as the parent. It is also faster than growing from seed.</details>

## 5-mark questions

9. (a) Distinguish pollination from fertilisation. (b) Describe how the pollen tube helps. (c) What do the ovule and ovary become after fertilisation? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) Pollination is the transfer of pollen from the anther to the stigma. Fertilisation is the fusion of the male gamete with the egg cell inside the ovule. (b) After landing, the pollen grain sprouts a tube that grows down the style to the ovule and carries the male gamete there. (c) The ovule becomes the seed (with the embryo from the zygote) and the ovary becomes the fruit.

</details>

10. (a) What is the placenta? (b) Describe two functions. (c) Why do the mother's and the embryo's blood not mix? (d) Name the tube that joins the foetus to it. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) A disc-like tissue in the uterus wall, rich in blood vessels, with villi. (b) Gives the embryo food and oxygen; takes away carbon dioxide and wastes. (c) Substances pass across thin walls by diffusion, so the two blood supplies stay separate; this protects the embryo from the mother's higher blood pressure and many harmful agents. (d) The umbilical cord.

</details>

## Case-based question

Anita watched a pond. A flatworm of the size of her finger was accidentally cut into three pieces by a boy's net. A week later she saw three small flatworms. In the school lab she also saw yeast in sugar solution under a microscope: some cells had small round outgrowths.

11. (a) Name the process shown by the flatworm. (b) Are the three new worms genetically different from the first? (c) Name the method shown by the yeast. (d) Name one difference between the two processes.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Regeneration. (b) No; they are clones of the original worm. (c) Budding. (d) In regeneration the pieces rebuild missing parts after the body is cut; in budding the parent makes the new individual as a growth on its own body, without being cut.</details>

## Assertion and reason

12. **A:** Self-pollination in a plant produces more variation than cross-pollination. **R:** Self-pollination brings together DNA from two different plants.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false and R is false. Cross-pollination (pollen from a different plant) brings DNA of two plants together and so gives more variation. Self-pollination uses DNA from one plant.</details>

13. **A:** A condom helps to prevent the spread of AIDS. **R:** A condom stops sperm and semen from touching the partner's body.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.</details>

14. **A:** The number of chromosomes in a human egg is 23. **R:** The body cells have 46 chromosomes, and gametes carry half the number.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Asexual: one parent, clones. Methods: fission, fragmentation, regeneration, budding, spores, vegetative propagation.
- Sexual: gametes fuse. Variation comes from DNA copying errors and from mixing two parents.
- Flower to fruit: pollination, pollen tube, fertilisation, zygote, embryo; ovule becomes the seed; ovary becomes the fruit.
- Human: testes, ovaries, oviduct, uterus; fertilisation, implantation, placenta, birth after about 40 weeks.
- Only barrier methods (condom) protect against STIs.

</div>
