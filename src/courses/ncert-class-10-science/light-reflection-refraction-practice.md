Try each question on paper before you open the answer. Optics questions are marked for steps and for signs, so show both.

<div class="bigidea">Every numerical in this chapter is the same four steps: draw a quick sketch, write u, v and f with their signs, put them into 1/v + 1/u = 1/f (mirror) or 1/v − 1/u = 1/f (lens), then read the nature of the image from the signs of v and m.</div>

## How to set out a numerical

1. Write what is given with signs (object on the left, so u is negative) and what you must find.
2. Write the formula in symbols, then substitute.
3. Solve for 1/v first, then flip it to get v. Keep the sign.
4. Use m to say whether the image is real or virtual, erect or inverted, enlarged or diminished.

## 1-mark questions

1. State the relation between the radius of curvature and the focal length of a spherical mirror.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>f = R/2.</details>

2. Which type of mirror always gives a virtual, erect and diminished image?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A convex mirror.</details>

3. A ray of light is incident along the normal on a glass surface. What is the angle of refraction?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>0°. The ray goes straight on without bending.</details>

4. What is the unit of the power of a lens, and what is it for a lens of focal length 1 m?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The dioptre (D). A lens of focal length 1 m has a power of 1 D.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Why is a convex mirror used as a rear-view mirror in vehicles? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>It always forms an erect (upright) image, and it gives a wide field of view, so the driver can see a large area of the road behind. The image is smaller than the object, so more of the scene fits in.</details>

6. A ray of light travels from air into water. Draw the ray and say how its speed and direction change. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The ray bends towards the normal (the angle of refraction is smaller than the angle of incidence). Its speed decreases, because water is optically denser (n = 1.33), v = c/n.</details>

7. A concave mirror of focal length 20 cm forms an image of an object placed 30 cm in front of it. Find the position and nature of the image. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>u = −30 cm, f = −20 cm. 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = −1/20 + 1/30 = −1/60, so v = −60 cm. The image is 60 cm in front of the mirror: real. m = −v/u = −(−60)/(−30) = −2, so it is inverted and twice the size of the object.</details>

8. The refractive index of glass is 1.5. Find the speed of light in glass (c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s). If light of the same colour goes from air into glass at i = 30°, find the angle of refraction. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>v = c/n = 3 × 10⁸/1.5 = 2 × 10⁸ m/s. sin r = sin 30°/1.5 = 0.5/1.5 = 0.333, so r ≈ 19.5°.</details>

## 5-mark questions

9. (a) State the two laws of refraction. (b) Draw a labelled diagram to show the refraction of a ray through a rectangular glass slab and mark the lateral displacement. (c) Why is the emergent ray parallel to the incident ray? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>(a) The incident ray, the refracted ray and the normal at the point of incidence are in the same plane. For light of a given colour in a given pair of media, sin i / sin r is a constant (the refractive index, Snell’s law). (b) A rectangular slab, the incident ray bending towards the normal at the first face, travelling straight across the glass, bending away from the normal at the second face; the dashed line from the point of entry shows the undeviated path, and the perpendicular distance between it and the emergent ray is the lateral displacement. (c) At the first face the ray bends towards the normal by (i − r); at the second face, whose normal is parallel to the first, it bends away by the same amount, so e = i and the emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray, only shifted sideways.</details>

10. A convex lens of focal length 12 cm is used to form an image of a 5 cm tall object kept 18 cm from the lens. (a) Find the image distance. (b) Find the height of the image. (c) State its nature. (d) Name an instrument that uses this arrangement. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>u = −18 cm, f = +12 cm. (a) 1/v = 1/f + 1/u = 1/12 − 1/18 = 1/36, so v = +36 cm. (b) m = v/u = 36/(−18) = −2, so h′ = −2 × 5 = −10 cm. (c) Real, inverted, enlarged (twice the size), formed beyond 2F on the other side of the lens. (d) A film or slide projector (object between F and 2F).</details>

## Case-based question

Aarav’s uncle is an optician in Kozhikode. He says: “A prescription tells me the power of the lens in dioptres. A child with a prescription of −2.0 D cannot see the blackboard clearly, so I fit concave glasses. Her grandfather needs +2.5 D to read the newspaper, so his glasses are convex. If a person needs more correction than one lens gives, I can put two thin lenses together.”

11. (a) What is the focal length of the child’s lens? (b) What is the focal length of the grandfather’s lens? (c) A +2.5 D and a −0.5 D lens are placed in contact. What is the power and focal length of the pair? (d) Why does the uncle choose a concave lens for the child and a convex one for the grandfather?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) f = 1/P = 1/(−2.0) = −0.5 m = −50 cm. (b) f = 1/2.5 = 0.4 m = +40 cm. (c) P = 2.5 + (−0.5) = +2.0 D, so f = 1/2 = 0.5 m = +50 cm (a convex lens). (d) A concave lens diverges light, which helps a short-sighted eye whose own lens focuses too strongly; a convex lens converges light, which helps a long-sighted eye focus on near objects. (You will study these defects in the next chapter.)</details>

## Assertion and reason

12. **A:** A concave mirror can form both real and virtual images. **R:** The nature of the image depends on the position of the object.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A. An object beyond F gives a real image; between F and P it gives a virtual image.</details>

13. **A:** The refractive index of a medium is always greater than 1. **R:** Light travels faster in any medium than in vacuum.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is true, R is false. n = c/v, and v is less than c in every medium, which is why n &gt; 1.</details>

14. **A:** A convex lens made of glass behaves as a diverging lens when it is placed in a liquid of higher refractive index than the glass. **R:** The behaviour of a lens depends on the refractive index of the lens relative to its surroundings.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A. If the lens is optically rarer than its surroundings, the rays bend the opposite way.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Mirror: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f, m = −v/u. Lens: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f, m = v/u. Always use signs: u negative.
- Real images are inverted and can be caught on a screen; virtual images are erect and cannot.
- Refraction: n = c/v = sin i / sin r; denser medium bends the ray towards the normal.
- Slab: e = i, sideways shift only. Power P = 1/f (metres); powers in contact add.
- Convex mirror and concave lens: always virtual, erect, diminished.
