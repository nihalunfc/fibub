<div class="bigidea">Most questions test the path of something: food along the gut, blood through the heart, water through the plant, blood through the nephron. Say each step in order and name the organ, the juice or the enzyme.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Name the process and the organ; then say what happens in order.
2. For comparison questions, use two columns or a table.
3. Name the enzyme, the substrate and the product in digestion answers.
4. For a labelled diagram, draw large and name the parts with straight lines.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the pores in a leaf through which gases are exchanged.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Stomata.</details>

2. Which enzyme in saliva digests starch?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Salivary amylase.</details>

3. What is the energy currency of the cell?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>ATP.</details>

4. Which blood vessel has valves to stop backflow of blood?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Veins (and the heart has valves between its chambers).</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Why are the walls of the ventricles thicker than those of the atria? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The atria only receive blood and push it a short way into the ventricles. The ventricles must pump blood out to the lungs (right) or to the whole body (left) under pressure, so they need thick muscular walls. The left is thickest.</details>

6. How do guard cells open and close the stomata? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>When water enters the guard cells they swell and curve, opening the pore. When water leaves they become flaccid and the pore closes.</details>

7. Compare aerobic and anaerobic respiration. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Aerobic needs oxygen, occurs in the mitochondria, breaks pyruvate down completely into CO₂ and water, and gives about 30 ATP per glucose. Anaerobic occurs without oxygen, in the cytoplasm, gives ethanol and CO₂ (yeast) or lactic acid (muscle), and gives only about 2 ATP per glucose.</details>

8. What are the functions of the following in the small intestine: (a) bile, (b) villi, (c) intestinal juice? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Bile emulsifies fats and makes the food alkaline. (b) Villi increase the surface area and absorb the digested food into the blood. (c) Intestinal juice completes digestion of carbohydrates, proteins and fats into glucose, amino acids and fatty acids and glycerol.</details>

## 5-mark questions

9. (a) Describe double circulation in humans. (b) Why is it necessary for warm-blooded animals? (c) Name the blood vessel that brings oxygen-rich blood to the heart. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>(a) Oxygen-poor blood enters the right atrium, goes to the right ventricle, and is pumped to the lungs by the pulmonary artery. In the lungs it takes oxygen and returns by the pulmonary vein to the left atrium, then the left ventricle, which pumps it round the body by the aorta. Blood returns to the right atrium through the vena cava. It passes through the heart twice in one complete circuit. (b) Warm-blooded animals need a lot of energy to keep body temperature constant; keeping oxygen-rich and oxygen-poor blood separate gives the tissues a good supply of oxygen. (c) The pulmonary vein.</details>

10. (a) What is transpiration? (b) How does it help the plant? (c) Name the factors that change its rate. (d) How is translocation different? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>(a) The loss of water vapour from the leaves, mainly through stomata. (b) It pulls water and minerals up the xylem from the roots, cools the leaf, and keeps the cells firm. (c) Light, temperature, wind (increase it) and humidity (decreases it). (d) Translocation is the movement of food made in the leaves through the phloem to all parts; it needs energy (ATP) and goes both up and down, while transpiration pull is a passive upward flow of water in dead xylem.</details>

## Case-based question

A sprinter runs 100 m and then rests. Straight after the race her muscles ache and her breathing and heart rate are high. After ten minutes her breathing is back to normal.

11. (a) Name the type of respiration that took place in her muscles during the race. (b) Name the product that causes the aching. (c) Why does she keep breathing hard after stopping? (d) Name the organelle where aerobic respiration takes place.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Anaerobic respiration (with some aerobic). (b) Lactic acid. (c) She needs extra oxygen to break down the lactic acid built up (the oxygen debt). (d) Mitochondria.</details>

## Assertion and reason

12. **A:** Plants close their stomata in hot, dry weather. **R:** Closing stomata reduces loss of water by transpiration.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.</details>

13. **A:** Veins have valves. **R:** Veins carry blood under high pressure.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is true but R is false. The pressure in veins is low, which is why valves are needed to prevent the backflow of blood.</details>
