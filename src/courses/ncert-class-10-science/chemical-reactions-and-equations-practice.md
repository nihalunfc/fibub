**Practice · Class 10 Science · Chapter 1.** Read the chapter lesson, [Chemical Reactions and Equations](/learn/courses/ncert-class-10-science/chemical-reactions-and-equations/), first. Try each question on paper before you open its answer.

<div class="bigidea">Every question in this chapter comes down to three habits: count atoms on both sides, name the pattern (combination, decomposition, displacement, double displacement, redox), and ask what has gained or lost oxygen.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For an equation: write the skeleton with correct formulas, count each atom, then change coefficients only; add state symbols if asked.
2. For a type of reaction: write the pattern (A + B → AB) and match it to the equation.
3. For “why” questions: give the reason in one sentence, then the example.
4. For calculations: write the formula masses, set up the ratio, and give the unit.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the gas evolved when zinc granules are added to dilute sulphuric acid.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Hydrogen gas. Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂.</details>

2. What does (aq) written after a formula mean?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The substance is dissolved in water (an aqueous solution).</details>

3. Is the burning of natural gas exothermic or endothermic?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Exothermic: it gives out heat.</details>

4. What is the name of the brown-red coating on iron?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Rust (hydrated iron(III) oxide, Fe₂O₃·xH₂O).</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Balance: Fe + O₂ → Fe₂O₃. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Fe is 1 on the left against 2; O is 2 against 3. Use 4Fe and 3O₂ with 2Fe₂O₃: 4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃ (4 Fe and 6 O each side).</details>

6. Why is respiration an exothermic reaction? Write the equation in words. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Glucose is oxidised in the cells and releases the energy stored in it as heat and usable energy. glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy.</details>

7. A shiny brown copper wire is heated in a flame and becomes black. When it is placed in a stream of hydrogen it becomes brown again. Explain with equations. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Heating: 2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO; copper gains oxygen, so it is oxidised and the black copper(II) oxide forms. In hydrogen: CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O; CuO loses oxygen and is reduced, giving brown copper back, while H₂ gains oxygen and is oxidised.</details>

8. Why does a nail in boiled water covered with a layer of oil not rust? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Boiling removes the dissolved air (oxygen) and the oil layer stops fresh air entering. Rusting needs both oxygen and water, so without oxygen the iron stays unchanged.</details>

## 5-mark questions

9. (a) Define a displacement reaction. (b) Write the balanced equation for zinc added to copper sulphate solution. (c) State what you would observe. (d) Which is more reactive, zinc or copper? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>(a) A reaction in which a more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its compound. (b) Zn(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + Cu(s). (c) The blue colour of the solution fades and a reddish-brown deposit of copper forms on the zinc. (d) Zinc is more reactive, because it pushed copper out.</details>

10. Classify each as combination, decomposition, displacement or double displacement and balance it: (a) H₂ + Cl₂ → HCl, (b) KClO₃ → KCl + O₂ (heat), (c) Mg + HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂, (d) AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>(a) Combination: H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl. (b) Decomposition: 2KClO₃ → 2KCl + 3O₂. (c) Displacement: Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂. (d) Double displacement (precipitation of white AgCl): already balanced.</details>

## Case-based question

Rohan’s uncle runs a small factory that makes whitewash and cement near Kota. He heats limestone (CaCO₃) in a kiln to get quicklime (CaO), and then adds water to the quicklime in a tank to make slaked lime. The tank becomes very hot. For cement he also needs CO₂ to be removed from the kiln, so there is a chimney.

11. (a) Write the equation for the kiln. (b) What type of reaction is it? (c) Write the equation for the tank and say why it is hot. (d) How many tonnes of CaO come from 10 tonnes of CaCO₃? (Ca = 40, C = 12, O = 16)
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂. (b) Thermal decomposition (endothermic). (c) CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + heat; it is an exothermic combination reaction. (d) 10 × 56/100 = 5.6 tonnes of CaO (and 4.4 tonnes of CO₂).</details>

## Assertion and reason

12. **A:** Rusting of iron is a slow oxidation reaction. **R:** Iron reacts with oxygen and moisture to form hydrated iron oxide.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.</details>

13. **A:** Hydrogen collected at the cathode in the electrolysis of water is twice the volume of oxygen at the anode. **R:** Water contains hydrogen and oxygen in the mass ratio 2 : 1.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is true but R is false. The ratio of 2 : 1 is by volume (by number of atoms in H₂O), not by mass. By mass hydrogen to oxygen is 1 : 8.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Balance by coefficients only. Count every atom on both sides.
- Patterns: A + B → AB (combination); AB → A + B (decomposition); A + BC → AC + B (displacement); AB + CD → AD + CB (double displacement).
- Oxidation = gain of oxygen; reduction = loss of oxygen; they happen together.
- Exothermic gives out heat; endothermic takes it in.
- Rusting needs air and water; rancidity is oxidation of fats; prevent both by keeping oxygen away.
