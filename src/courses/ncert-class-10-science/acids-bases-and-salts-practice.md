**Practice · Class 10 Science · Chapter 2.** Read the chapter lesson, [Acids, Bases and Salts](/learn/courses/ncert-class-10-science/acids-bases-and-salts/), first. Try each question on paper before you open its answer.

<div class="bigidea">Most questions need four things: the three reactions of acids (with a metal, with a carbonate, with a base), the pH scale (log scale, 7 is neutral), how to predict a salt and its nature, and the formulas of the common salts.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Write the equation with correct formulas and balance it.
2. Give the observation (colour, gas, bubbles) and the test for the gas.
3. For pH questions, say whether it is acid or base and give the reason (H⁺ or OH⁻).
4. For preparation questions, give the raw material, the condition, and the equation.

## 1-mark questions

1. What is the pH of pure water at room temperature?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>7 (neutral).</details>

2. Name the gas liberated when baking soda is heated.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Carbon dioxide: 2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂.</details>

3. Which ion is common to all acids?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The hydrogen ion, H⁺ (H₃O⁺ in water).</details>

4. What is the formula of bleaching powder?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>CaOCl₂ (calcium oxychloride).</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Why should acid be added to water and not water to acid when diluting? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Dissolving a concentrated acid in water gives out a lot of heat. If water is poured onto the acid, it may boil and spurt out the acid, causing burns. Adding the acid to water slowly spreads the heat through the larger volume of water.</details>

6. A dry piece of blue litmus does not change in dry HCl gas but turns red in HCl solution. Explain. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>HCl gives H⁺ ions only in the presence of water. In the dry gas there are no free H⁺ ions, so there is no acid action; in solution the H⁺ ions turn the litmus red.</details>

7. Why does tooth decay start when the pH in the mouth falls below 5.5? How can it be prevented? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Below 5.5 the acid made by bacteria from sugar dissolves the calcium phosphate of the enamel, and the tooth begins to decay. Brushing after eating, using a basic toothpaste (which neutralises the acid) and avoiding sweets between meals keep the mouth pH higher.</details>

8. Write the equations for the reaction of dilute HCl with (a) magnesium and (b) sodium hydrogencarbonate. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂↑. (b) NaHCO₃ + HCl → NaCl + H₂O + CO₂↑.</details>

## 5-mark questions

9. (a) What is meant by neutralisation? (b) Write the equation for the reaction of sulphuric acid with calcium hydroxide. (c) Name the products. (d) Give one use of neutralisation in daily life and one in industry. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>(a) The reaction of an acid with a base to give a salt and water. (b) H₂SO₄ + Ca(OH)₂ → CaSO₄ + 2H₂O. (c) Calcium sulphate (a salt) and water. (d) Daily life: antacid tablets for acidity, or baking soda on a bee sting. Industry: treating acidic factory waste with lime before release.</details>

10. (a) How is plaster of Paris prepared? (b) Write the equation. (c) Why must it be stored in a moisture-proof container? (d) Give two uses. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>(a) By heating gypsum at 373 K. (b) CaSO₄·2H₂O → CaSO₄·½H₂O + 1½H₂O. (c) POP reacts with moisture from the air and sets into hard gypsum, so it would be useless. (d) Plaster casts for fractures, toys and statues, false ceilings and smoothing walls.</details>

## Case-based question

Meera tests five liquids from her kitchen with universal indicator paper: lemon juice (red-orange), tap water (green), baking soda solution (blue-green), soap solution (blue) and vinegar (orange). She also puts a few drops of lemon juice on a baking soda and notices fizzing.

11. (a) Arrange the five in increasing order of pH. (b) Which is closest to neutral? (c) Name the gas formed in the fizzing and give a test for it. (d) Why is baking soda used for a bee sting?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Lemon juice (about 2) < vinegar (3) < water (7) < baking soda solution (8.3) < soap (10). (b) Tap water. (c) CO₂; it turns lime water milky. (d) The sting has methanoic acid; the mild base baking soda neutralises it without harming the skin.</details>

## Assertion and reason

12. **A:** Bleaching powder is used to disinfect drinking water. **R:** Bleaching powder gives chlorine when it reacts with water and acids.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.</details>

13. **A:** The pH of a solution of washing soda is above 7. **R:** Washing soda is a salt of a strong acid and a weak base.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is true but R is false. Washing soda (Na₂CO₃) is a salt of a strong base (NaOH) and a weak acid (carbonic acid), which is why its solution is basic.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Acid + metal → salt + H₂; acid + carbonate → salt + water + CO₂; acid + base → salt + water.
- pH < 7 acid, 7 neutral, > 7 base; each unit is ten times the H⁺.
- Always add acid to water. Dilution moves pH toward 7 only.
- NaCl brine → NaOH + Cl₂ + H₂. Cl₂ + Ca(OH)₂ → bleaching powder. NaHCO₃ baking soda; Na₂CO₃·10H₂O washing soda; CaSO₄·½H₂O POP.
