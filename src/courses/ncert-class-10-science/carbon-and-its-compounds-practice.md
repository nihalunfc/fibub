**Practice · Class 10 Science · Chapter 4.** Read the chapter lesson, [Carbon and its Compounds](/learn/courses/ncert-class-10-science/carbon-and-its-compounds/), first. Try each question on paper before you open its answer.

<div class="bigidea">Most questions test four skills: drawing and naming a compound, using CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ and its cousins, writing the equation for one of the four reaction types, and explaining soap with the head-and-tail picture.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For a structure, draw it so every carbon has four bonds, and count the hydrogens.
2. For a reaction, write a balanced equation, name the type, and give the condition (catalyst, sunlight, heat).
3. For a test, say what you will do and what you will see.
4. For soap, always say: head (water), tail (oil), micelle.

## 1-mark questions

1. Write the formula of the alkane with five carbon atoms.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>C₅H₁₂ (pentane).</details>

2. What is the functional group of ethanol?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The hydroxyl group, –OH (alcohol).</details>

3. Name the gas given off when ethanoic acid reacts with sodium carbonate.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Carbon dioxide.</details>

4. Why are covalent compounds poor conductors of electricity?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>They contain no free ions or free electrons; their molecules are neutral.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Draw the electron-dot structure of methane and state how many shared pairs it has. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Carbon in the centre with one shared pair (two dots) to each of four hydrogens. Four shared pairs, so four single bonds. Carbon then has 8 electrons and each H has 2.</details>

6. What are isomers? Draw two isomers of butane, C₄H₁₀. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Isomers have the same molecular formula but different structures. (i) Butane: CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–CH₃. (ii) 2-Methylpropane: CH₃–CH(CH₃)–CH₃, a three-carbon chain with a CH₃ branch on the middle carbon. Both are C₄H₁₀.</details>

7. Why does a saturated hydrocarbon burn with a blue flame and an unsaturated one with a sooty yellow flame? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A saturated hydrocarbon has a lower percentage of carbon and burns completely, giving a blue flame. An unsaturated one has a higher percentage of carbon that does not all burn, so unburnt carbon glows as a yellow sooty flame.</details>

8. Write the equation for the reaction of ethanol with (a) sodium and (b) hot concentrated sulphuric acid. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) 2CH₃CH₂OH + 2Na → 2CH₃CH₂ONa + H₂↑. (b) CH₃CH₂OH → CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O at 443 K with conc. H₂SO₄ (dehydration).</details>

## 5-mark questions

9. (a) Name the process in which an unsaturated hydrocarbon is converted into a saturated one. (b) Write the equation for ethene. (c) State the catalyst. (d) Name an industry that uses it. (e) How does the product differ in flame? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>(a) Addition (hydrogenation). (b) CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ → CH₃–CH₃. (c) Nickel (or palladium or platinum). (d) The vanaspati ghee industry turns vegetable oils into fats. (e) The saturated product burns with a clean blue flame; the unsaturated reactant with a sooty yellow flame.</details>

10. (a) Explain how soap cleans a greasy cloth. (b) Why does soap fail in hard water? (c) How does a detergent solve this? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>(a) A soap molecule has an ionic head that is water-loving and a long hydrocarbon tail that is oil-loving. In water the tails go into the grease and the heads stay in the water, surrounding the grease in a micelle. Rubbing breaks the grease into droplets and micelles repel each other because of the charged heads, so they are rinsed away. (b) The Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions of hard water react with soap to give an insoluble scum, so the soap is wasted and no lather forms. (c) A detergent forms soluble salts with calcium and magnesium, so no scum forms and it lathers in hard water.</details>

## Case-based question

A student adds a few drops of ethanol and ethanoic acid to separate test tubes. She then adds a pinch of sodium hydrogencarbonate to each. Only one fizzes. In a second test she warms ethanol and ethanoic acid together with a few drops of concentrated sulphuric acid, and pours the mixture into a beaker of water. A sweet smell is noticed.

11. (a) Which tube fizzes and what is the gas? (b) How can the gas be tested? (c) Name the compound that gives the sweet smell. (d) What is the role of sulphuric acid? 
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) The ethanoic acid tube; the gas is carbon dioxide. (b) Pass it through lime water, which turns milky. (c) Ethyl ethanoate, an ester. (d) It acts as a catalyst (and removes water, which pushes the reaction forward).</details>

## Assertion and reason

12. **A:** Ethene decolourises bromine water, but ethane does not. **R:** Ethene is unsaturated and adds bromine across its double bond.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.</details>

13. **A:** Detergents are better than soaps in hard water. **R:** Detergents contain a hydrocarbon tail, soaps do not.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is true but R is false. Both have a hydrocarbon tail. The difference is that detergent calcium salts are soluble, so there is no scum.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Alkane CₙH₂ₙ₊₂, alkene CₙH₂ₙ, alkyne CₙH₂ₙ₋₂; every carbon has four bonds.
- Four reaction types: combustion, oxidation, addition, substitution.
- Ethanol → ethene (dehydration), → ethanoic acid (oxidation); ethanoic acid + alcohol → ester.
- Soap: head in water, tail in grease, micelle. Hard water gives scum with soap, not with detergent.
