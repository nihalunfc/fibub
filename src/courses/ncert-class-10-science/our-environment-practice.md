**Practice · Class 10 Science · Chapter 13.** Read the chapter lesson, [Our Environment](/learn/courses/ncert-class-10-science/our-environment/), first. Try each question on paper before you open its answer.

<div class="bigidea">Most questions ask you to draw or read a food chain, apply the 10 per cent law to numbers, explain biological magnification, give the cause and effects of ozone depletion, and sort and manage waste. Always show the energy at each level.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. For food-chain questions, write the chain with arrows from food to eater and label the trophic levels.
2. For energy questions, write the starting energy and multiply by 0.1 for each step.
3. For ozone, give the cause (CFCs), the effect (more UV) and the remedy.
4. For waste, name the kind (biodegradable or not) and give a way to manage it.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the producers in a pond ecosystem.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Green plants and algae (they make food by photosynthesis).</details>

2. What percentage of energy passes from one trophic level to the next?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>About 10 per cent.</details>

3. Name two chemicals that damage the ozone layer.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons), and other ozone-depleting substances such as halons.</details>

4. What is a non-biodegradable substance? Give one example.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A substance that cannot be broken down by living organisms, for example plastic.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. What is the role of decomposers in an ecosystem? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>They break down dead plants, animals and wastes into simple substances like minerals, which return to the soil for the producers. Without them, dead matter would pile up and nutrients would be locked away.</details>

6. Why is a food web more stable than a single food chain? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>In a web, most animals have more than one food source and more than one predator. If one species disappears, others can take its place, so the whole system is not as easily upset.</details>

7. Explain why the number of trophic levels in a food chain is limited. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Only about 10 per cent of the energy is passed on at each step; the rest is lost as heat or used for life processes. After four or five steps the energy left is too little to support another level, so food chains are short.</details>

8. How can a person's own choices reduce the garbage they produce? Give three ways. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Use cloth bags instead of plastic ones; reuse containers and bottles; separate wet and dry waste, compost the kitchen waste, and give paper, glass and metal for recycling.</details>

## 5-mark questions

9. (a) What is biological magnification? (b) Why is the concentration of a pesticide higher in a fish-eating bird than in algae? (c) Why do biodegradable chemicals not show this? (d) Which organisms are most at risk? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) The increase in the concentration of a harmful, non-biodegradable chemical at each higher trophic level. (b) Each consumer eats many of the organisms below it and keeps the chemical in its body, so it is multiplied at every step. (c) A biodegradable chemical is broken down or removed by the body, so it does not build up. (d) The top carnivores, such as large fish, birds of prey, and humans.

</details>

10. (a) What is ozone and where is the ozone layer? (b) What does it do? (c) How do CFCs damage it? (d) Name an international agreement to protect it. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) Ozone (O₃) is a molecule of three oxygen atoms; the layer is in the stratosphere, about 15 to 35 km above the ground. (b) It absorbs most of the Sun's harmful UV radiation. (c) UV breaks down CFCs and frees chlorine atoms; each chlorine atom destroys many ozone molecules in a repeating cycle, so the layer thins. (d) The Montreal Protocol (1987).

</details>

## Case-based question

In a lake sprayed with a pesticide, the water had 0.002 ppm. Algae had 0.02 ppm, small fish 0.2 ppm, big fish 2 ppm and a fish eagle 20 ppm. Villagers eat the big fish from this lake.

11. (a) Name the producer and the top carnivore. (b) By what factor does the concentration rise at each step? (c) Name this phenomenon. (d) Why are the villagers at risk?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Algae are the producer; the fish eagle is the top carnivore. (b) A factor of 10 at each step. (c) Biological magnification. (d) They eat big fish near the top of the chain, which carry a high concentration of a chemical that the body does not break down.</details>

## Assertion and reason

12. **A:** Energy flows in one direction in an ecosystem. **R:** Energy lost as heat at each trophic level cannot be reused by the living organisms.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.</details>

13. **A:** The ozone layer protects us from ultraviolet rays. **R:** Ozone strongly absorbs UV radiation.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.</details>

14. **A:** Biodegradable waste can be safely dumped anywhere. **R:** Decomposers break it down at once.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are false. Large heaps of biodegradable waste rot slowly, smell, and breed pests; it should be composted properly.</details>
