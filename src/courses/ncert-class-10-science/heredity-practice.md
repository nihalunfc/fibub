**Practice · Class 10 Science · Chapter 8.** Read the chapter lesson, [Heredity](/learn/courses/ncert-class-10-science/heredity/), first. Try each question on paper before you open its answer.

<div class="bigidea">Most questions ask for a Punnett square (with the genotype and phenotype ratios), the meaning of dominant and recessive, the difference between acquired and inherited traits, and how sex is determined. Always write the cross, the gametes and the square.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Write the genotypes of the parents, then the gametes each can make.
2. Draw the Punnett square and fill it in.
3. Give the genotype ratio and the phenotype ratio separately.
4. Use the words dominant, recessive, homozygous, heterozygous correctly.

## 1-mark questions

1. What is a gene?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A stretch of DNA on a chromosome that has the instructions to make one protein, and so controls a trait.</details>

2. If a plant has the genotype Tt, what is its phenotype if T is dominant for tall?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Tall.</details>

3. How many pairs of chromosomes does a human body cell have?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>23 pairs (46 chromosomes).</details>

4. Which sex chromosomes does a woman have?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Two X chromosomes (XX).</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Differentiate between an inherited trait and an acquired trait, with one example each. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>An inherited trait is passed on through the DNA in the gametes, for example eye colour. An acquired trait is gained during life and is not passed on, for example big muscles from exercise or a scar.</details>

6. A tall plant (Tt) is crossed with a short plant (tt). Write the genotypes and the ratio of the offspring. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Gametes: T and t from Tt; t from tt. Offspring: Tt (tall) and tt (short) in the ratio 1 : 1.</details>

7. Why did all the F1 plants in Mendel's tall × short cross look tall, yet the F2 had some short plants? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The F1 plants are Tt. T is dominant, so they look tall, but each carries one t allele. When they self-pollinate, a t sperm can meet a t egg, giving tt, which is short. So the recessive trait was hidden, not lost.</details>

8. Why is the sex of a baby decided by the father and not by the mother? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Every egg has an X chromosome. A sperm can carry X or Y. An X sperm gives a girl (XX) and a Y sperm gives a boy (XY), so the father's sperm decides.</details>

## 5-mark questions

9. A cross is made between pure round yellow seeded peas (RRYY) and pure wrinkled green peas (rryy). (a) What is the F1? (b) What is the ratio in the F2? (c) Which of these combinations are new? (d) What does this show? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) All F1 plants are RrYy: round and yellow. (b) 9 round yellow : 3 round green : 3 wrinkled yellow : 1 wrinkled green. (c) Round green and wrinkled yellow, which neither parent had. (d) The traits of shape and colour are inherited independently of each other.

</details>

10. (a) Explain how a gene leads to a trait, using plant height. (b) Why is one working copy of the gene enough to make a plant tall? (c) Why can a hybrid tall plant give short offspring? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

(a) The height gene has the instructions for an enzyme, which helps to make a growth hormone; plenty of hormone makes the plant tall. (b) One working copy makes enough enzyme, so T is dominant. (c) A Tt plant makes gametes with t. If two t gametes meet, the offspring is tt (short).

</details>

## Case-based question

Mala and Ravi have three daughters. Ravi's mother keeps saying that Mala has "no luck for a son". Mala's school friend Asha, who studies biology, explains the matter to them, and says the chance of the next baby being a boy is the same as it ever was. Mala, as a girl, had no Y chromosome and Ravi has one.

11. (a) Which chromosomes do Mala's eggs carry? (b) Which sperm gives a boy? (c) What is the chance that the next child is a boy? (d) Who determines the sex of the baby?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Every egg carries an X chromosome. (b) A sperm carrying a Y chromosome. (c) 1/2 (50%), whatever the earlier children were. (d) The father, because his sperm may carry X or Y.</details>

## Assertion and reason

12. **A:** A recessive trait can appear in the F2 even though it was absent in the F1. **R:** F1 plants are hybrids that carry the recessive allele.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.</details>

13. **A:** A boy who loses a finger in an accident will pass on the missing finger to his children. **R:** Traits acquired in life change the DNA in the gametes.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are false. Acquired traits do not change the DNA of the gametes, so they are not inherited.</details>

14. **A:** The F2 ratio in a monohybrid cross is 3 : 1 in every family. **R:** The ratio is a probability that shows clearly only in large numbers.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false but R is true. The 3 : 1 ratio shows up on average in many offspring; in a small family the numbers can be quite different.</details>
