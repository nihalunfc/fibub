<div class="bigidea">Most questions come back to the reactivity series: it predicts displacement, reaction with acids and water, how a metal is extracted, and what protects iron. For ionic compounds, remember electron transfer, charge balance and the properties that follow.</div>

## How to set out answers

1. Use the reactivity series and write it out before you predict a reaction.
2. For ionic compounds, show the electron arrangement before and after, and the charges.
3. For extraction, name the group of metals and the method, then give the key equations.
4. Always write balanced equations with state symbols if asked.

## 1-mark questions

1. Name the most reactive metal that is stored under kerosene.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Sodium (potassium is stored the same way).</details>

2. What is the name for a metal that dissolves in mercury?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>An amalgam.</details>

3. Name the process in which a carbonate ore is heated in limited air.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Calcination.</details>

4. Which non-metal is a good conductor of electricity?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Graphite.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. Why is a thin layer of aluminium oxide on aluminium useful? *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>It is a tough, unreactive layer that stops air reaching the metal beneath, so the aluminium does not corrode further. (It can be thickened by anodising.)</details>

6. What happens when an iron nail is dipped in copper sulphate solution? Write the equation and name the type of reaction. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A brown coating of copper forms on the nail and the blue solution turns pale green. Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu. It is a displacement reaction, because iron is more reactive than copper.</details>

7. Show the formation of magnesium oxide by transfer of electrons. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Mg is 2,8,2 and O is 2,6. Mg loses two electrons to become Mg²⁺ (2,8), and O gains two to become O²⁻ (2,8). The ions Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ attract, giving MgO.</details>

8. Why do ionic compounds conduct electricity in solution but not as solids? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Conduction needs charged particles that can move. In a solid, the ions are locked in the lattice. When dissolved in water or melted, the ions are free to move, so the liquid carries current.</details>

## 5-mark questions

9. (a) What is the difference between roasting and calcination? (b) How is zinc extracted from zinc blende (ZnS)? Give the equations. (c) Why is carbon not used to extract sodium? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>(a) Roasting is heating a sulphide ore strongly in excess air to make the oxide (2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂); calcination is heating a carbonate ore in limited air (ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂). (b) Concentrate the ore by froth flotation, roast to ZnO, then reduce with carbon: ZnO + C → Zn + CO. (c) Sodium is so reactive that carbon cannot remove oxygen from sodium oxide (or chlorine from NaCl); electrolysis of molten NaCl is used.</details>

10. (a) What is meant by galvanisation? (b) Why does it protect iron even when the zinc is scratched? (c) Name two alloys, their components and a use for each. *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>(a) Coating iron with a layer of zinc. (b) Zinc is more reactive than iron, so it corrodes in preference (sacrificial protection) and the exposed iron is spared. (c) Brass: copper and zinc, for taps and musical instruments. Steel: iron and carbon, for bridges and tools. (Bronze, solder or stainless steel also fine.)</details>

## Case-based question

A school in Kerala builds a rain-water tank from galvanised iron sheet. After 3 years, the sheets near the sea show white powder on the surface, and in one place where a scratch removed the zinc, the iron under it is not rusted, but the zinc nearby has a white powdery coating. The school also uses a tin-coated food container which rusted quickly after a dent.

11. (a) What is the white powder? (b) Why has the iron in the scratch not rusted? (c) Why did the tin-coated container rust quickly after the dent? (d) Name one other method to protect the iron tank.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Zinc oxide (and zinc carbonate), formed as the zinc corrodes. (b) Zinc is more reactive than iron, so it corrodes instead (sacrificial protection). (c) Tin is less reactive than iron, so once the coat is broken the iron corrodes faster than bare iron would. (d) Painting, or making the tank of stainless steel.</details>

## Assertion and reason

12. **A:** Aluminium is extracted by electrolysis of molten aluminium oxide in cryolite. **R:** Aluminium is very reactive and carbon cannot reduce its oxide.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.</details>

13. **A:** Alloys are harder than pure metals. **R:** An alloy is a compound of the metals in a fixed ratio.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is true but R is false. An alloy is a mixture (the ratio can vary); it is harder because the differently sized atoms stop the layers from slipping.</details>

<div class="keypoints">

- Reactivity series: K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb, H, Cu, Hg, Ag, Au. Use it to predict displacement and reaction with acid.
- Ionic compounds: electron transfer, charge balance, high melting, brittle, conduct when molten or dissolved.
- Extraction: low group heat; middle group roast or calcine and reduce with C; top group electrolysis.
- Zinc coating protects iron even when scratched; tin does not.
- An alloy is a mixture, not a compound.
