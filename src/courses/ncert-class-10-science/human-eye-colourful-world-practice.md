Try each question on paper before you open the answer. Give reasons, not just names: most marks go for the “because”.

<div class="bigidea">Two kinds of question appear again and again: “name the defect and the lens” (work out where the image forms and which way it must be moved) and “explain the colour phenomenon” (is it refraction, dispersion or scattering?). Decide that first and the answer writes itself.</div>

## How to answer an explanation question

1. Name the phenomenon (refraction, dispersion, scattering, accommodation).
2. Say what is different from the ordinary case (denser air near the ground, a longer path, a point source).
3. Finish with the result in one clear sentence.

## 1-mark questions

1. What is the function of the iris in the human eye?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The iris controls the size of the pupil and so the amount of light entering the eye.</details>

2. What is the near point of a normal eye?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>About 25 cm, the closest distance at which the eye can see clearly without strain.</details>

3. Name the phenomenon responsible for the visibility of a sunbeam entering a dusty room.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The Tyndall effect (scattering of light by the dust particles).</details>

4. Which colour is deviated the least by a prism?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Red.</details>

## 2-mark and 3-mark questions

5. A student cannot see the writing on the blackboard clearly from the last bench. Name the defect and the type of lens needed. *(2 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The defect is myopia (short-sight): the image of the far board forms in front of the retina. A concave lens of suitable power is needed.</details>

6. Why do stars twinkle but planets do not? *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Starlight passes through layers of air whose density keeps changing, so the path of the light and the amount of light reaching the eye keep changing: the star twinkles. A star is so far away that it is a point source, so all the light follows one path. A planet is closer and acts as an extended source, a collection of many point sources; the changes in their paths cancel on average, so the total light stays steady and the planet does not twinkle.</details>

7. A farsighted person has a near point at 100 cm. Calculate the power of the lens needed to read at 25 cm. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The lens must form a virtual image at 100 cm of the book at 25 cm: u = −25 cm, v = −100 cm. 1/f = 1/v − 1/u = −1/100 + 1/25 = 3/100, so f = 33.3 cm = 0.333 m and P = 1/f = +3.0 D (a convex lens).</details>

8. Explain why the Sun appears red at sunrise and sunset. *(3 marks)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>At sunrise and sunset, light from the Sun passes through a much thicker layer of the atmosphere to reach us. Air molecules scatter short wavelengths (blue, violet) much more than long wavelengths (red), so most of the blue is scattered out of the beam on the way. The light that reaches the eye is mostly red and orange, so the Sun looks red.</details>

## 5-mark questions

9. (a) What is dispersion of light? (b) Explain with a diagram how a rainbow is formed. (c) Why are the colours in the rainbow in the order red on the outside to violet on the inside? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>(a) Dispersion is the splitting of white light into its component colours when it passes through a medium such as glass, because each colour has a different speed (and refractive index) in that medium. (b) The Sun is behind the observer; sunlight enters a raindrop and is refracted (and dispersed) at the first surface, is totally reflected at the back surface, and is refracted again as it leaves, spreading the colours more. Each drop sends one colour to the eye; raindrops at different heights send different colours, so an arc of colours is seen. A labelled diagram shows one drop with white light entering and the red and violet rays leaving at slightly different angles. (c) Red is deviated the least and leaves the drop at about 42° from the direction opposite the Sun, violet is deviated the most and leaves at about 40°; so the drops that send red to the eye are higher in the sky than those that send violet. Red is on the outer edge of the arc, violet on the inner edge.</details>

10. (a) What is accommodation? (b) How does the shape of the eye lens change when you look from a distant tree to a book in your hand? (c) What is the function of the retina? (d) Why can’t we see objects clearly at 10 cm? *(5 marks)*
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>(a) The ability of the eye lens to change its focal length so that objects at different distances are focused on the retina. (b) The ciliary muscles contract and the lens becomes thicker (more curved), so its focal length becomes shorter. (c) The retina is the screen where the real, inverted image forms; its rod and cone cells convert light into electrical signals sent to the brain through the optic nerve. (d) The eye lens cannot become thick enough to focus an object closer than the near point (about 25 cm), so the image forms behind the retina and is blurred.</details>

## Case-based question

Meera lives in Munnar. She notices the following. (i) In the evening, the Sun looks large and orange when it is near the hills. (ii) After a morning shower, a rainbow appears when she stands with her back to the Sun. (iii) At night, stars twinkle but Venus shines with a steady light. (iv) Her brother Arjun, who wears glasses of power −2.5 D, cannot read the signboards without them.

11. (a) Why does the Sun look orange near the horizon? (b) Why must she stand with her back to the Sun to see the rainbow? (c) Name the defect Arjun has and find the focal length of his lens. (d) Why does Venus not twinkle?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Its light has to travel through a long path of air, so the blue light is scattered away and the red and orange reach her eyes. (b) Light must enter the raindrops from behind her and be reflected back to her eyes after dispersion and internal reflection; so the Sun must be behind her and the rain in front. (c) Myopia (short-sight; a concave lens of negative power corrects it). f = 1/P = 1/(−2.5) = −0.4 m = −40 cm. (d) Venus is close enough to be an extended source: its tiny disc has many points whose light follows many paths, and the changes average out, so its brightness stays steady.</details>

## Assertion and reason

12. **A:** The sky is dark for an astronaut in space. **R:** There is no atmosphere there to scatter sunlight.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.</details>

13. **A:** A person with myopia should use a convex lens. **R:** Myopia is the inability to see distant objects clearly.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is false, R is true. A short-sighted eye needs a concave lens, which diverges the rays so the image shifts back onto the retina.</details>

14. **A:** Red light is used in danger signals. **R:** Red light has the shortest wavelength of visible light and is scattered the most.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A is true, R is false. Red has the longest wavelength and is scattered the least, which is why it can be seen from far away.</details>
