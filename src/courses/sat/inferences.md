<div class="bigidea">An inference question gives you the steps of an argument and leaves out the last one. Write the last step yourself first, as the smallest claim the facts support. Then match it.</div>

## 1. Build the chain

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">Fact 1: glass frogs have see-through bellies.</div>
<div class="step">Fact 2: while sleeping, they hide 90% of their red blood cells in a reflective liver.</div>
<div class="step">Fact 3: red cells are what make blood visible.</div>
<div class="step">So: hiding the cells makes the sleeping frogs harder to see. That is the smallest conclusion all three facts support.</div>
</div>

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Inference</dt><dd>a conclusion that must be (or is very likely to be) true if the text is true</dd>
<dt>Too far</dt><dd>a conclusion bigger than the evidence: "never", "all", "the only"</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>A detective does the same: muddy shoes plus a wet coat means "was outside in the rain", not "robbed the bank".</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<details class="try"><summary>Try it: "Coins minted in winter were found along a mountain route once thought to be used only in summer." What follows?</summary>

The route was probably used in some winters too. Not that it was used every winter, or that the passes never had snow.

</details>

<div class="myth">

**"A smart answer adds something new."** On the SAT, the right inference adds the least. If an option needs facts the text does not give, it is wrong.

</div>
