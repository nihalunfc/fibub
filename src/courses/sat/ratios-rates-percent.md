**Module 4 · about 20 minutes · read first: [2.1 Linear equations](/learn/courses/sat/linear-equations/).** By the end you will solve any ratio, rate or percent question with one idea: write it as a multiplication.

<div class="bigidea">A percent is a multiplier. "Up 15%" means × 1.15, "down 15%" means × 0.85, "15% of" means × 0.15. Turn every percent into a multiplier and the hard questions become one line of arithmetic.</div>

## 1. Ratios are parts of a whole

A recipe uses flour and sugar in the ratio 3 : 2. Think of the bowl as 3 + 2 = 5 equal scoops. If the bowl holds 400 grams, one scoop is 400 ÷ 5 = 80 grams: flour is 3 scoops (240 g) and sugar 2 scoops (160 g).

<div class="problem" data-problem="rp-g05"></div>

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Ratio</dt><dd>how many of one thing for each of another: 3 : 2 means 3 flour for every 2 sugar, not 3 grams and 2 grams</dd>
<dt>Rate</dt><dd>a ratio with units that differ: miles per hour, dollars per pound; a "unit rate" is per 1 of something</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Comparing two shops? Work out the unit price (price ÷ amount) for each. The smaller unit price is the better deal, whatever the pack size.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

## 2. Proportions: scale it up

If 4 litres of paint cover 30 square metres, how many litres for 75 square metres? The ratio stays the same, so 4/30 = x/75. Since 75 is 2.5 times 30, x = 4 × 2.5 = 10 litres. Cross-multiplying gives the same: 30x = 300.

## 3. Percent of, and percent change

"What is 35% of 240?" is 0.35 × 240 = 84. "By what percent did it change?" always divides by the **original**:

`$\text{percent change} = \dfrac{\text{new} - \text{original}}{\text{original}} \times 100$`

## 4. Two changes in a row

<div class="steps" data-fig="percent-grid" data-p="40" data-a="25" data-b="20">
<div class="step">A grid of 100 squares: 40 shaded squares is 40%. "Percent" literally means "per hundred".</div>
<div class="step">A price of 100 goes up 25%: multiply by 1.25 to get 125.</div>
<div class="step">Now it drops 20%. The 20% is taken of the <b>new</b> price, 125: 125 × 0.80 = 100. Up 25% then down 20% lands exactly where it started, not 5% higher.</div>
</div>

Multiply the multipliers: up a% then down b% is × (1 + a/100)(1 − b/100). Never add or subtract the percents.

## 5. Going backwards

"After a 30% discount the price is $56. What was it before?" The sale price is 70% of the original: 0.70P = 56, so P = 80. Adding 30% of $56 back on gives $72.80, the classic wrong answer.

<div class="problem" data-problem="rp-g06"></div>

<div class="myth">

**"Up 10% then down 10% gets you back to the start."** It does not: 1.10 × 0.90 = 0.99, a 1% loss. The second 10% is taken of a bigger number. The same goes for population, prices and test scores.

</div>

## 6. How the SAT asks it

- Percent of, percent change and reverse percent, often in a word problem with a table.
- Ratios split into parts, and proportions to scale a recipe, map or model.
- Unit rates to compare deals or speeds.
- Two percents in a row: multiply, never add.

**Try it:** Set three percent changes in a row and compare the result with the sum of the percents.
