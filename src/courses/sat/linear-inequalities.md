<div class="bigidea">An inequality is solved just like an equation, with one extra rule: multiply or divide by a negative number and the sign flips. The answer is not one number but a whole range.</div>

## 1. Why the sign flips

5 > 2 is true. Multiply both sides by −1 and you get −5 and −2, but −5 is <i>less</i> than −2. Multiplying by a negative mirrors the number line, so "bigger" and "smaller" swap. That is the only new rule.

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><b>Solve:</b> 7 − 3x ≥ 19.</div>
<div class="step">Subtract 7 from both sides: −3x ≥ 12.</div>
<div class="step">Divide by −3. It is negative, so <b>flip</b> the sign: x ≤ −4.</div>
<div class="step">Check with x = −5 (should work): 7 + 15 = 22 ≥ 19 ✓. Check with x = 0 (should fail): 7 ≥ 19 ✗.</div>
</div>

## 2. Words into symbols

| Words | Symbol |
|---|---|
| at most, no more than, maximum, cannot exceed | ≤ |
| at least, no less than, minimum | ≥ |
| more than, greater than | > |
| fewer than, less than | < |

**Whole-number answers:** if the answer is "x ≤ 13.6 tickets", the greatest number of tickets is 13 (round <b>down</b>, or you go over budget). If "at least 7.2 hours" are needed, you need 8 (round <b>up</b>).

## 3. Inequalities on a graph

An inequality in x and y, like y < −x + 2, is a **region**: every point on one side of the line.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="graph" data-mode="inequality" data-m="-1" data-b="2"><figcaption>Shade above for "y >" or below for "y <". A solid edge includes the line (≤, ≥); a dashed edge leaves it out (<, >).</figcaption></figure>

A **system** of inequalities is the overlap of two shaded regions. To check whether a point is a solution, put it into <b>both</b> inequalities.

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>&lt;, &gt;</dt><dd>strict: the boundary is not included (dashed line, open circle)</dd>
<dt>≤, ≥</dt><dd>the boundary is included (solid line, filled circle)</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Real limits are inequalities: a lift that can carry at most 1,000 kg, a grade you need at least 70 for. The solution set is every choice that stays within the limit.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<div class="problem" data-problem="li-g04"></div>

<div class="myth">

**"I only flip the sign when there is a minus in the question."** You flip only when you <b>multiply or divide both sides by a negative number</b>. Subtracting a number, even a negative one, never flips the sign.

</div>

## 4. How the SAT asks it

- Solve, then pick the greatest or least integer that fits.
- Turn a budget or limit into an inequality (pick the right one of four).
- Which point satisfies a system of inequalities (test each option).
- Read an inequality from a graph: slope, intercept, shading side and solid or dashed.

<div class="keypoints">

- Solve like an equation; flip the sign when you multiply or divide by a negative.
- "At most" is ≤, "at least" is ≥; round whole-number answers in the direction that stays within the limit.
- A two-variable inequality is a shaded half-plane; dashed means strict, solid means included.
- A point solves a system only if it makes every inequality true.
