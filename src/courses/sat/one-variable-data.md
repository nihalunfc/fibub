<div class="bigidea">Two numbers describe most data: a centre (where the values sit) and a spread (how far apart they are). The mean and median are centres; the range and standard deviation are spreads.</div>

## 1. Mean: the balance point

Add the values and divide by how many there are. Picture the values as weights on a ruler: the mean is where it balances. The fastest SAT trick is to think in totals: mean × count = total.

## 2. Median: the middle value

Sort the values. With an odd count, the median is the middle one; with an even count, it is the mean of the two middle ones.

## 3. Outliers pull the mean, not the median

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Mean</dt><dd>the fair share: if everyone pooled their values and split them evenly, each would get the mean</dd>
<dt>Median</dt><dd>the value that half the data is below and half above</dd>
<dt>Range</dt><dd>largest minus smallest; one extreme value changes it a lot</dd>
<dt>Standard deviation</dt><dd>roughly the typical distance of a value from the mean; the SAT asks you to compare it, not calculate it</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>House prices are reported as medians because a few mansions would drag the mean far above what a typical home costs.</dd>
</dl>

## 4. Spread

Two classes both average 75. In one, every score is between 70 and 80; in the other, scores run from 40 to 100. Same centre, very different spread. The second has the larger range and the larger standard deviation.

## 5. Tables and plots

Frequency tables, dot plots and histograms all list how many times each value (or interval) appears. To find the median of 20 values from a table, count along to the 10th and 11th values.

<div class="myth">

**"The median is the middle number in the list."** Only once the list is sorted. And in a frequency table, the middle of the <i>value column</i> is not the median: count the data, not the rows.

</div>

- Outliers move the mean and range a lot and the median very little.
- Standard deviation measures spread: compare it by eye, do not calculate it.
