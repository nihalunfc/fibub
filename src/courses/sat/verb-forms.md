**Module 9 · about 12 minutes · read first: [9.3 Subject-verb agreement](/learn/courses/sat/subject-verb-agreement/).** By the end you will read the time clues in a sentence and pick the verb form they demand.

<div class="bigidea">Time words in the sentence choose the tense: "in 1928" wants the past, "since" wants has/have, "by the time" (in the past) wants had. Then check that the sentence still has a main verb and that verbs in a list match.</div>

## 1. Time clues

| Clue | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a past date | simple past | In 1928, Fleming noticed... |
| since, for (until now) | present perfect | has grown |
| by the time (past) | past perfect | had waited |
| next week, by 2030 | future | will present |

## 2. Main verbs and parallel lists

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">"The violin, crafted in Italy in the 1700s and restored last spring, ______ at the hall on Friday."</div>
<div class="step">Remove the description between the commas: "The violin ______ at the hall on Friday."</div>
<div class="step">It needs a main verb. "Being played" and "to be played" are not main verbs.</div>
<div class="step">Friday is in the future: <b>will be played</b>.</div>
</div>

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Present perfect (has/have + verb)</dt><dd>started in the past and still true now</dd>
<dt>Past perfect (had + verb)</dt><dd>finished before another past moment</dd>
<dt>Parallel</dt><dd>items in a list share the same form (weighs, kneads, shapes)</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Tense is a timeline. Getting it right tells the reader which event came first without extra words.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"-ing words are verbs."** "Being played" or "kneading" cannot be a sentence's main verb on its own. A sentence needs a verb like "is", "was" or "will be".

</div>
