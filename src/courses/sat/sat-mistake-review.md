**Module 10 · about 25 minutes, then a few minutes a day · read first: [10.1 Practice test 1](/learn/courses/sat/sat-practice-test-1/).** By the end you will have a mistake log and a plan for every topic in it.

<div class="bigidea">Every miss has a cause: you did not know it, you misread it, you ran out of time, or you slipped. Each cause has a different fix. Students who sort their mistakes by cause improve much faster than students who just redo questions.</div>

## 1. Build a mistake log

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">For each missed or guessed question, write the topic (for example "4.1 percent change").</div>
<div class="step">Write the cause: <b>K</b> (knowledge: did not know the rule), <b>R</b> (read the question wrong), <b>T</b> (time), or <b>S</b> (slip: arithmetic or clicking).</div>
<div class="step">Write the fix in one line: "reread 4.1 section 3", "underline what is asked", "skip and return after 2 minutes", "check signs".</div>
<div class="step">In three days, do fresh questions on each K topic. If you get them right, cross the line out.</div>
</div>

| Cause | Fix |
|---|---|
| K: did not know | reread the lesson, then fresh practice on that topic |
| R: misread | underline what is asked; answer that, not a nearby question |
| T: ran out of time | first pass fast, mark and move on after about 2 minutes |
| S: slip | write steps down; put the answer back in to check |

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Spaced practice</dt><dd>coming back to a topic after a few days, which makes it last far longer than one long session</dd>
<dt>Retrieval practice</dt><dd>answering fresh questions from memory, which builds skill better than rereading</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>A mistake log is data about your own test-taking. Like any data, its value comes from sorting it and acting on the pattern.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

## 2. Practise any topic

Pick a unit and topic from your log below. Questions come in order of difficulty unless you choose shuffled.

<div class="qbank" data-bank="sat" data-exam="sat" data-n="10"></div>

<details class="try"><summary>My log is mostly T (time). What should I do?</summary>

Practise timed single modules ([10.1](/learn/courses/sat/sat-practice-test-1/)) and set a rule: if a question passes two minutes, guess, mark it and move on. Return to marked questions at the end. Often you get more right on the second look.

</details>

<div class="myth">

**"Reading the explanation means I've fixed it."** Understanding someone else's solution feels like learning, but the test is whether you can solve a new one alone. Always retest.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Log every miss and guess with its topic and cause (K, R, T, S).
- Each cause has its own fix.
- Retest K topics with fresh questions a few days later.
- Use the practice set above to work through your log.

</div>
