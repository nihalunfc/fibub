**Module 1 · about 12 minutes · read first: [1.2](/learn/courses/sat/sat-scoring/).** By the end you will have a week-by-week plan and a clear routine for test day.

<div class="bigidea">Scores go up when you practise the skill, test yourself on it, and then fix the exact reason for each mistake. Doing many questions without reviewing them barely helps.</div>

## 1. The 8-week plan

About one hour a day, five or six days a week. Shorter time? Squeeze the lessons and keep the practice tests.

| Week | Math | Reading and Writing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Take practice test 1 (Lesson 10.1) to get a starting score. Read Part A. | |
| 2 | Algebra 2.1 to 2.5 | Information and ideas 6.1 to 6.4 |
| 3 | Advanced math 3.1 to 3.4 | Craft and structure 7.1 to 7.3 |
| 4 | Advanced math 3.5 to 3.7 | Expression of ideas 8.1 and 8.2 |
| 5 | Data analysis 4.1 to 4.6 | Conventions 9.1 to 9.3 |
| 6 | Geometry and trig 5.1 to 5.4 | Conventions 9.4 and 9.5 |
| 7 | Practice test 2 (Lesson 10.2), then review (10.3) | |

Each lesson ends with a three-question check: if you miss one, read that section again before moving on.

## 2. Review that actually raises your score

Psychologists have shown that testing yourself makes learning stick far better than rereading. So after every practice set, sort each mistake into one of three bins:

<div class="steps">
<div class="step"><b>Content gap.</b> You did not know the rule or method. → Go back to that lesson and do its live problems until you get three in a row right.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Misread.</b> You knew how, but answered a different question (solved for x when it asked for 2x, or missed "NOT"). → Before answering, underline what the question asks for.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Time.</b> You ran out of time or rushed. → Practise pacing (below) and skip-and-return.</div>
<div class="step">Keep a <b>mistake log</b>: the question, the bin, and one sentence on what you will do next time. Reread it the week before the test.</div>
</div>

## 3. Pacing inside a module

- Do one pass through the module. Answer every question; if one takes more than about 2 minutes, pick your best guess, **flag it** and move on.
- With the time left, go back to flagged questions.
- With 1 minute left, make sure nothing is blank.
- In Math, the questions roughly get harder through each module, so do not let early ones eat your time.

<div class="myth">

**"Studying the night before helps most."** Sleep fixes memories in place, and a tired brain misreads questions. Do a light review of your mistake log, pack your bag and sleep.

</div>

## 4. Test-day checklist

- **Device**: fully charged laptop or tablet with Bluebook installed and your admission ticket downloaded; a charger.
- **Photo ID** and the admission ticket.
- Pencils or pens for scratch paper (paper is given at the centre).
- An approved calculator if you want one as well as Desmos.
- Water and a snack for the break.
- Arrive early. Check the College Board website for the latest rules and arrival time.

<div class="keypoints">

- Start with a practice test, follow the 8-week plan, and finish with a second test and review.
- Sort every mistake into content gap, misread or time, and fix that cause.
- Pace about 71 seconds per Reading and Writing question and 95 seconds per Math question; flag and return.
- Sleep and a calm morning beat last-minute cramming.

</div>
