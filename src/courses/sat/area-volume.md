<div class="bigidea">Area counts unit squares; volume counts unit cubes. Most formulas are "base times height" in disguise: a box is (base area) × height, a cylinder is (circle area) × height, and a cone or pyramid is one third of that.</div>

## 1. The formulas you need

The SAT shows a reference sheet with these, but knowing them saves time:

| Shape | Area or volume |
|---|---|
| Rectangle | length × width |
| Triangle | ½ × base × height |
| Circle | π<i>r</i>² |
| Box | length × width × height |
| Cylinder | π<i>r</i>²<i>h</i> |
| Cone | ⅓π<i>r</i>²<i>h</i> |
| Sphere | (4/3)π<i>r</i>³ |

## 2. Answers "in terms of π"

When the answer is <i>k</i>π, leave π as a letter and work out <i>k</i>. A cylinder of radius 4 and height 3 has volume π × 16 × 3 = 48π, so <i>k</i> = 48.

## 3. Scaling: lengths, areas, volumes

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">A 1 × 1 square. Double every side: it becomes 2 × 2.</div>
<div class="step">The new square holds 4 of the old squares: area × 2² = 4.</div>
<div class="step">A 1 × 1 × 1 cube doubled becomes 2 × 2 × 2, which holds 8 of the old cubes: volume × 2³ = 8.</div>
<div class="step">Rule: scale lengths by <i>k</i>, areas scale by <i>k</i>², volumes by <i>k</i>³.</div>
</div>

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Area</dt><dd>how much paint covers a surface; measured in square units</dd>
<dt>Volume</dt><dd>how much water fills a container; measured in cubic units</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Scaling explains why big animals have thick legs: double an animal's size and its weight (a volume) rises 8 times while its bones' strength (an area) rises only 4 times.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"A pizza twice as wide is twice as much pizza."** Area scales with the square: a 16-inch pizza is four times an 8-inch one.
