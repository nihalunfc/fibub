**Module 7 · about 15 minutes · read first: [6.1 Central ideas](/learn/courses/sat/central-ideas/).** By the end you will choose precise words from the sentence's own clues.

<div class="bigidea">The sentence always contains the clue. Find it, put your own simple word in the blank, then pick the option closest in meaning. You do not need a huge vocabulary; you need to read the clue.</div>

## 1. Find the clue

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">"Early reports that the material could store twice as much energy proved ______: later tests found only a 10% improvement."</div>
<div class="step">Clue: the colon, then "only a 10% improvement". The reports said far more than the truth.</div>
<div class="step">Your word: "too big" or "overstated".</div>
<div class="step">Closest option: <b>exaggerated</b>.</div>
</div>

Clue words to watch: "although", "but" and "instead" signal a contrast; "and", "also" and a colon signal more of the same or an explanation.

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Precise</dt><dd>the word that fits exactly, not just roughly</dd>
<dt>Context</dt><dd>the words around the blank that limit what it can mean</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Many common words have several meanings ("resolve" a problem, show "resolve"). The SAT tests whether you pick the one the sentence uses.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<details class="try"><summary>Try it: "Critics praised the novel's ______ portrait: every detail rang true." Your word?</summary>

Something like "lifelike" or "vivid". "Inaccurate" contradicts "rang true", and "brief" has no clue behind it.

</details>

<div class="myth">

**"The hardest word is usually right."** Test writers include hard words that do not fit. A plain word that matches the clue beats an impressive word that does not.

</div>
