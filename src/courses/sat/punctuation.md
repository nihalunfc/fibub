**Module 9 · about 18 minutes · read first: [9.1 Sentence boundaries](/learn/courses/sat/sentence-boundaries/).** By the end you will place every mark the SAT tests for a reason you can say aloud.

<div class="bigidea">Punctuation follows the sentence's structure. If you can say what each part of the sentence is (subject, verb, list, extra information), the punctuation follows from a handful of rules.</div>

## 1. Never between subject and verb

"The engineers who designed the first bridges in the region did not understand the wind." However long the subject, no comma goes before "did".

## 2. Extra information gets matching marks

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">"The town's oldest building still grinds flour." Add extra information about the building.</div>
<div class="step">"The town's oldest building—a stone mill finished in 1784—still grinds flour."</div>
<div class="step">Remove the part between the dashes: the sentence still works. That proves it is extra.</div>
<div class="step">Use two dashes, two commas, or two brackets. Never mix a comma and a dash.</div>
</div>

Information that tells you <i>which one</i> is essential and gets no commas: "Students who finish the list receive a certificate."

## 3. Colons and semicolons

A colon follows a complete sentence and introduces a list, an example or an explanation. A semicolon joins two complete sentences.

## 4. Apostrophes

| Meaning | Write |
|---|---|
| one bird owns it | bird's |
| several birds own it | birds' |
| a plural that ends without s (children) | children's |
| "it is" | it's |
| belonging to it | its |

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Essential information</dt><dd>needed to know which thing you mean: no commas</dd>
<dt>Extra information</dt><dd>could be removed without changing which thing you mean: matching commas or dashes</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Commas around a phrase whisper "by the way"; no commas say "this is the one I mean". That changes the sentence's meaning.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Put a comma wherever you would pause."** Pauses in speech are not rules. A long subject makes you pause, but a comma before its verb is still wrong.

</div>
