**Module 4 · about 18 minutes · read first: [2.5 Linear models](/learn/courses/sat/linear-models/), [3.4 Exponential functions](/learn/courses/sat/exponential-functions/).** By the end you will read a scatterplot like a story and use its line of best fit to predict.

<div class="bigidea">A scatterplot shows whether two things move together. A line of best fit is a linear model drawn through the cloud: its slope says how much y changes per unit of x, on average.</div>

## 1. Describing the cloud

- **Direction:** rising left to right is a positive association; falling is negative.
- **Strength:** points hugging a line are strong; a loose cloud is weak.
- **Form:** straight (linear) or curved (often exponential).

## 2. The line of best fit

<figure class="fig" data-fig="scatter"><figcaption>Move the slope and intercept. The dashed gaps are residuals; the line of best fit makes their squared total as small as possible.</figcaption></figure>

Predictions come from putting x into the line's equation. The slope and intercept have meanings, exactly as in a linear model.

<div class="problem" data-problem="tv-g01"></div>

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Slope of the best-fit line</dt><dd>the predicted change in y for each 1-unit increase in x ("about 4 more points per hour studied")</dd>
<dt>Intercept</dt><dd>the prediction at x = 0, which may be meaningless if x = 0 is far outside the data</dd>
<dt>Residual</dt><dd>actual minus predicted: positive means the point is above the line</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>A best-fit line describes a trend in the data you have. Predicting far beyond it (extrapolating) is risky: no child keeps growing 6 cm a year forever.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

## 3. Linear or exponential?

Look at the differences between equally spaced values. Constant differences: linear. Constant <i>ratios</i>: exponential.

## 4. Two-way tables

A two-way table counts people by two categories at once. The key is the denominator: "what percent of grade 9 students" divides by the grade 9 row total; "of the students who walk, what percent" divides by the walkers' column total.

<div class="problem" data-problem="tv-g03"></div>

<div class="myth">

**"A strong association proves that x causes y."** Ice cream sales and sunburns rise together because both follow hot weather. Only an experiment with random assignment shows cause (lesson 4.6).
