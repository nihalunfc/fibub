**Module 9 · about 12 minutes · read first: [9.3 Subject-verb agreement](/learn/courses/sat/subject-verb-agreement/).** By the end you will catch the two pronoun and modifier errors the SAT tests most.

<div class="bigidea">A pronoun must match the noun it replaces (one or many). An opening describing phrase must be followed right away by the thing it describes.</div>

## 1. Pronouns

Find the noun the pronoun replaces. "Sea otters wrap themselves in kelp so that <i>they</i> do not drift": otters, plural, so "they". "The council announced that <i>it</i> would vote": one council, so "it".

## 2. Dangling modifiers

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">"Having studied the ice cores for three years, ______"</div>
<div class="step">Ask: who studied the ice cores? People: the team.</div>
<div class="step">So the words right after the comma must be "the team...".</div>
<div class="step">"the team concluded that the region had been warmer." Any option starting with "the region", "the conclusion" or "evidence" says those things did the studying.</div>
</div>

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Antecedent</dt><dd>the noun a pronoun refers to</dd>
<dt>Modifier</dt><dd>a describing word or phrase; it attaches to the nearest noun</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>"Running for the bus, my bag fell open" says the bag was running. Readers notice, and the SAT does too.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"They" can mean a company.** In everyday speech, yes; on the SAT, a single company, team or council takes "it" and "its".

</div>
