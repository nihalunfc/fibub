**Module 6 · about 15 minutes · read first: [1.1 How the digital SAT works](/learn/courses/sat/how-the-digital-sat-works/).** By the end you will sum up any short passage in one sentence and pick the answer that matches it.

<div class="bigidea">Before you look at the options, say the main idea in your own words in under ten words. Then pick the option that matches it. The wrong options are usually true details, or claims that go further than the text.</div>

## 1. The method

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">Read the whole text once, without stopping on hard words.</div>
<div class="step">Ask: what is the author mostly telling me? Watch for turning words such as "but", "however" and "instead": the main idea often comes after them.</div>
<div class="step">Say it in under ten words, for example "rogue waves were doubted, now proven and studied".</div>
<div class="step">Now read the options. Cross out any that cover only one detail, add something not in the text, or contradict it.</div>
</div>

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Main idea</dt><dd>the one point the whole text supports</dd>
<dt>Detail</dt><dd>a fact, example or number that supports the main idea</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>A good main idea is what you would text a friend about the passage. "It's about rogue waves being real" beats "a wave was 26 metres".</dd>
</dl>

</div>

## 2. The three wrong-answer types

- **Too narrow:** true, but only one example or number.
- **Too broad or extreme:** "all", "never", "should be replaced" when the text is more careful.
- **Not there:** sounds reasonable, but the text never says it.

<details class="try"><summary>Try it: a text says a city's trees cool its streets and also cut its energy bills. Is "trees cool streets" the main idea?</summary>

It is too narrow: it leaves out the energy bills. A better summary is "city trees bring several benefits, such as cooler streets and lower energy bills".

</details>

<div class="myth">

**"The first sentence is always the main idea."** Often the first sentence is the old view the author goes on to correct. Read to the end.

</div>
