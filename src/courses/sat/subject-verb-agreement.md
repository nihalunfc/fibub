**Module 9 · about 15 minutes · read first: [9.2 Punctuation](/learn/courses/sat/punctuation/).** By the end you will find the true subject of any SAT sentence in seconds.

<div class="bigidea">Cross out everything between the subject and the verb, especially phrases starting with "of", "in", "with" and "that". What is left is the true subject, and the verb must match it.</div>

## 1. Cross out the middle

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">"The collection of handwritten letters from the early settlers ______ historians a vivid picture."</div>
<div class="step">Cross out "of handwritten letters from the early settlers".</div>
<div class="step">"The collection ______ historians a vivid picture." One collection: singular.</div>
<div class="step">Verb: <b>gives</b>. "Letters" and "settlers" were decoys.</div>
</div>

## 2. The special cases

- **And** makes a plural: "The mayor and the engineer <i>will present</i>."
- **Each, every, either, neither, one** (alone) are singular: "Each of the trails <i>has</i>."
- **Neither ... nor / either ... or:** match the nearer subject.
- **Inverted order:** "Beneath the floor <i>lies</i> a network of tunnels." Ask "what lies?" The network.
- **Collective nouns** (team, council, flock) are usually singular on the SAT.

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Subject</dt><dd>who or what does the verb</dd>
<dt>Agreement</dt><dd>singular subject with singular verb (it runs), plural with plural (they run)</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Agreement tells the reader which noun is doing the action when a sentence has many nouns in it.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Match the verb to the closest noun."** The closest noun is often inside a phrase. Match the real subject (except with neither/nor).

</div>
