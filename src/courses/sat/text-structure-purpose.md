**Module 7 · about 15 minutes · read first: [7.1 Words in context](/learn/courses/sat/words-in-context/).** By the end you will label the job of every sentence in a short text.

<div class="bigidea">Every sentence in a short text has a job. Label each one in two or three words (old view, new finding, example, explanation) and the purpose and structure questions answer themselves.</div>

## 1. Label the sentences

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">"Some scientists predicted the lizards would vanish." Label: <b>prediction</b>.</div>
<div class="step">"Instead, the lizards moved higher into the trees." Label: <b>what really happened</b>, contradicting the prediction.</div>
<div class="step">"Their legs grew shorter, which helps on thin branches." Label: <b>further change, with a reason</b>.</div>
<div class="step">Structure: a prediction, then evidence that it was wrong. The first sentence's function is "presents a prediction the text shows to be wrong".</div>
</div>

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Purpose</dt><dd>why the author wrote the whole text: to explain, argue, describe, compare or correct</dd>
<dt>Function</dt><dd>what one sentence does for the whole: introduce, support, illustrate, contrast, conclude</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Good writers build like engineers: each sentence carries some of the load. Seeing the frame makes any text easier to read.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<details class="try"><summary>Try it: "Most people know bees make honey. Fewer know they pollinate a third of our crops." What is the purpose?</summary>

To point out a less familiar but important role of bees. The honey sentence is the familiar starting point, not the purpose.

</details>

<div class="myth">

**"Purpose means topic."** The topic is bees; the purpose is what the author does with bees (here, highlighting a less known role). The answer choices are about the doing.

</div>
