**Module 4 · about 15 minutes · read first: [4.1 Ratios, rates and percent](/learn/courses/sat/ratios-rates-percent/).** By the end you will convert any unit without memorising which way to multiply.

<div class="bigidea">A conversion factor is a fraction equal to 1, like 12 inches over 1 foot. Multiplying by 1 never changes an amount, only its units. Line the fractions up so the unwanted units cancel.</div>

## 1. Cancel units like numbers

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">Change 45 miles per hour into feet per second. Write it as a fraction: 45 miles / 1 hour.</div>
<div class="step">Miles must cancel, so put miles on the bottom: × 5,280 feet / 1 mile.</div>
<div class="step">Hours must cancel, so put hours on top: × 1 hour / 3,600 seconds.</div>
<div class="step">45 × 5,280 ÷ 3,600 = <b>66 feet per second</b>. Check the size: there are far more feet than miles and far more seconds than hours, and the answer is sensible for a car.</div>
</div>

<div class="problem" data-problem="uc-g01"></div>

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Conversion factor</dt><dd>a fraction whose top and bottom are the same amount in different units (60 minutes / 1 hour)</dd>
<dt>Square and cubic units</dt><dd>an area has two lengths in it, a volume three, so the length factor is used twice or three times: 1 m² = 100 × 100 cm²</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>Density (mass per volume) tells you whether a thing floats in water (less than 1 g/cm³) and is the same for a pebble or a mountain of the same rock.</dd>
</dl>

## 2. Area and volume units

A floor of 4 m by 3 m is 12 m². In centimetres it is 400 by 300 = 120,000 cm². So 1 m² is 10,000 cm², not 100. For volume, 1 m³ = 100³ = 1,000,000 cm³.

## 3. Rates and density

Rate questions chain several steps: miles to gallons to dollars, or pages to minutes. Density is mass ÷ volume, and the SAT often gives a shape whose volume you must work out first.

<div class="problem" data-problem="uc-g03"></div>

<div class="problem" data-problem="uc-g05"></div>

<div class="myth">

**"To go from a big unit to a small one, always multiply."** That works for single units, but rates flip it: going from per hour to per second makes the number <i>smaller</i>. Let the units cancel and you never need the rule.
