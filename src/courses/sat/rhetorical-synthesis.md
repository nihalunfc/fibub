**Module 8 · about 12 minutes · read first: [8.1 Transitions](/learn/courses/sat/transitions/).** By the end you will answer notes questions in under a minute.

<div class="bigidea">Skip the notes at first. Read the goal at the end of the question, then check which option meets it. Every option is accurate to the notes; only one does what the goal asks.</div>

## 1. The method

<div class="steps">
<div class="step">Read the goal: "emphasise how different the rainfall is in two places in South America".</div>
<div class="step">What must the right sentence contain? Two places, their rainfall, and a contrast.</div>
<div class="step">Check options: "The Atacama is in Chile" has one place and no rain. "Both are in South America" is a similarity.</div>
<div class="step">Only "While some stations in the Atacama have recorded almost no rain in decades, the Amazon receives about 2,300 mm a year" has all three.</div>
</div>

<div class="meaning">

<dl>
<dt>Goal words</dt><dd>"introduce", "emphasise a difference", "present a finding", "explain a method", "compare": each needs particular content</dd>
<dt>Audience</dt><dd>"for readers unfamiliar with" means you must say who or what something is</dd>
<dt>Physical meaning</dt><dd>This is how real writing works: you have more facts than you need, and you choose the ones that serve your purpose.</dd>
</dl>

</div>

<details class="try"><summary>Try it: goal, "introduce Hedy Lamarr to an audience unfamiliar with her". What must the sentence include?</summary>

Who she was (a film actress) and why she matters (she co-invented a radio method used today in Wi-Fi and Bluetooth). Dates alone do not introduce her.

</details>

<div class="myth">

**"Use as many notes as possible."** The best answer uses only the notes that serve the goal.

</div>
