**Module 5 · about 11 minutes · read first: 5.1.** You'll be able to write a summary or email that a manager understands in 30 seconds, and check it as you type.

## 1. Why this matters

Most of an analyst's work reaches managers as a few lines: an email, a Teams message, the top of a report. Many readers stop after the first two sentences. If your finding is in paragraph four, it's lost. Writing answer-first is a habit that makes you look senior quickly.

## 2. The idea in one picture

| Background first (common) | Answer first (better) |
|---|---|
| Hi Sam, I hope you're well. As discussed last week, I pulled the sales data for Q1 and looked at it by store and by month. There were a few interesting patterns. February was lower than expected... | Hi Sam, February sales were $13.4k, 8 % under target and the only month in 2025 below last year. I suggest we check what was different (weather, stock, staffing) before finalising the March plan. Can we discuss Thursday? Details below. |

## 3. The pattern

1. **First sentence: the finding,** with the number.
2. **Second sentence: the so-what or the ask.** What you recommend, or what you need from them, by when.
3. **Then the support:** two or three bullets of evidence.
4. **Then details,** links or attachments for those who want more.

For an executive summary at the top of a report, the same: 3 to 5 sentences or bullets that someone could forward on their own.

<details class="maths" open><summary>Principle</summary>

**Principle:** the inverted pyramid, used in news writing for over a century: most important first, details later. Readers can stop at any point and still have the most important part.

**Analogy:** a doctor who says "it's not broken, just a sprain; rest it for a week" before explaining the X-ray. You relax at once and listen to the rest.
</details>

## 4. Try it

Write the first two sentences of an email about February. The checker looks for a number in the first sentence, an ask, no warm-up and a short length.

## 5. Worked example

*Draft:* "Following our meeting, I analysed the 2025 data across stores and categories using Power BI. I looked at growth, targets and like-for-like. Please see the attached deck, which contains several findings."

*Answer first:* "2025 beat target by 6.6 %, but the older stores grew 14 %, half the 29 % headline, because Forks Market is new. I recommend we report like-for-like growth monthly from January; can you approve the new report page? The three charts below show why."

## 6. Practice

1. Rewrite answer-first: "I've been looking at weekday sales. Sunday is the biggest day, followed by Saturday. Weekdays are lower. Maybe staffing could change."
2. What's the ask in your rewrite?
3. Why put "Details below" at the end of the first paragraph?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. "Weekends bring over a third of our sales in two days, but we staff every day the same. I suggest moving two shifts a week from Tuesday and Wednesday to the weekend; could we try it in one store in January?"
2. To trial the change in one store in January.
3. It tells the reader they can stop there and still have what they need, and that the evidence is available.
</details>
