**Module 3 · about 12 minutes · read first: 3.1.** You'll be able to write titles that tell the reader what the chart shows, and check them as you type.

## 1. Why this matters

Most charts have titles like "Sales by Store". The reader then has to work out the point themselves, and may find a different one from yours, or none. An **action title** (also called a headline or takeaway title) says the point in a sentence. Consultancies use them on every slide; it's one of the quickest upgrades you can make to any report.

## 2. The idea in one picture

| Descriptive title | Action title |
|---|---|
| Sales by Store, 2025 | Four of five stores beat their 2025 target |
| Monthly Sales vs Target | February was the only month below last year, and 8 % under target |
| Sales by Weekday | Weekends bring over a third of sales |
| Margin % by Category | Electronics sells most but keeps the least of each dollar after Grocery |

## 3. How to write one

1. **A full sentence with a verb:** beat, missed, grew, fell, leads.
2. **A number** where possible: makes it specific and checkable.
3. **The thing it's about:** the store, category or month.
4. **Short:** one line, under about 15 words.
5. **True to the chart:** the chart must prove the title at a glance. If it doesn't, change the chart (highlight, sort) or the title.
6. **Subtitle for the facts:** "Sales by store, 2025, $ thousands".

<details class="maths" open><summary>Principle</summary>

**Principle:** readers read the title first and decide from it what to look for. Give them the conclusion, and the chart becomes the evidence. This is the "answer first" idea applied to a single chart.

**Analogy:** a newspaper. "Storm closes 40 schools" is a headline; "Weather report" is not. You'd read the first.
</details>

## 5. Worked example

*Chart: monthly sales 2025 vs target, February highlighted.*

- Draft 1: "Sales vs Target" (no point).
- Draft 2: "February sales were low" (a point, but vague).
- Draft 3: "February was the only month below last year, and 8 % under target" (specific, checkable, matches the highlight).

## 6. Practice

1. Write an action title for: margin % by category (Home 54 %, Outdoor 54 %, Electronics 49 %, Grocery 42 %).
2. What's wrong with "Interesting trends in sales"?
3. Your chart shows no clear difference between stores. What could the title be?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. For example: "Grocery's margin is 12 points below Home and Outdoor".
2. No claim, no number, no subject: it tells the reader nothing.
3. Say that: "All four older stores grew between 9 % and 19 %". "No difference" can be the message.
