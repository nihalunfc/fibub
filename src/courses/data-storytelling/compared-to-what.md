**Module 4 · about 13 minutes · read first: 4.1.** You'll be able to choose the right comparison for a KPI, and see how it changes the story.

## 1. Why this matters

"Sales were $262k." Good or bad? You can't tell without a comparison. The comparison you pick can make the same number look like success or failure, so choosing it honestly, and saying which one you used, is central to data storytelling.

## 2. The idea in one picture

<!--fig:ds-chart like-for-like-->

Both numbers are true. "Up 29 %" answers "is the business bigger?"; "up 14 %" answers "are our stores doing better?".

## 3. The common comparisons

| Compare with | Answers | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **Target / budget** | Are we on plan? | Targets can be set too low or too high |
| **Same period last year** | Are we growing? Removes seasonality | Last year may have been unusual |
| **Previous period** (last month) | Is it getting better right now? | Seasonality: December always beats November |
| **Like-for-like** (same stores) | Is underlying performance improving? | Define which stores count, and stick to it |
| **Average / other units** | How does this store compare? | Different sizes: compare rates, not totals |
| **Year to date** | Is the year on track? | Early in the year, small numbers swing a lot |

<details class="maths" open><summary>Maths and principle</summary>

**Growth** = (this period − comparison) ÷ comparison.
All stores: (262,470 − 203,438) ÷ 203,438 ≈ +29 %.
Like-for-like (the 4 stores open in both years): 2025 = 78,295 + 57,838 + 50,727 + 45,433 = 232,293; (232,293 − 203,438) ÷ 203,438 ≈ +14 %.
**Vs target** = actual ÷ target − 1: 262,470 ÷ 246,300 − 1 ≈ +6.6 %.

**Principle:** a comparison is a choice of question. State it in the label ("vs same month last year", "like-for-like") so the reader knows which question was answered.
</details>

## 5. Worked example

*December 2025 sales: $30,031.*

- vs November 2025 ($27,025): +11 %. But December is always the peak.
- vs December 2024 ($22,313): +35 %. Includes Forks Market.
- vs target ($27,000): +11 %.

The honest headline for a sales review: "December beat target by 11 % and last December by 35 % (including the new store)."

## 6. Practice

1. Rideau: 2024 $42,674, 2025 $50,727, target $45,400. Work out growth and vs target.
2. Why is month-on-month growth misleading for Northbay's Outdoor category?
3. Forks Market had no 2024 sales. What comparison can you use for it?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Growth: 50,727 ÷ 42,674 − 1 ≈ +18.9 %. Vs target: 50,727 ÷ 45,400 − 1 ≈ +11.7 %.
2. Outdoor rises every spring and falls every autumn, so month-on-month change mostly shows the season, not performance.
3. Its target (99.6 % achieved), other stores' first years, or its share of chain sales.
</details>
