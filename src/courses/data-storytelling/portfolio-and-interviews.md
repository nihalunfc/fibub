**Module 6 · about 12 minutes · read first: 6.3.** You'll have a portfolio page plan and answers to the storytelling questions interviewers ask.

## 1. Why this matters

"Communicates insights to non-technical stakeholders" is in almost every data analyst job ad, but it's hard to prove on a CV. A portfolio page that shows a messy chart becoming a clear one, and a short story about a decision you helped make, proves it in a minute.

## 3. The portfolio page

1. **Title and one line:** "Northbay 2025 review: turning a sales dashboard into a 5-slide decision."
2. **The question and audience:** who asked, what they needed to decide.
3. **The big idea:** your one sentence.
4. **Before and after:** two charts, the default and your final version, with a line on each change.
5. **The deck:** a PDF or images of the slides.
6. **The result:** what it led to (for a real project) or what you'd expect it to change.
7. **Honest note:** "Data is a realistic made-up dataset (Northbay Goods, fibub.com)."

## 4. Questions you'll hear

1. **"How do you present findings to non-technical people?"** Answer with your method: audience first, big idea, answer first, one chart per point, plain words. Then a 30-second example.
2. **"What's wrong with this chart?"** Ask what it's for. Then: chart type, clutter, colour, title, honesty (axes), accessibility.
3. **"Tell me about a time you presented data that changed a decision."** Use STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result, with a number.
4. **"How do you choose KPIs for a dashboard?"** Goal, actionable, owned, compared, defined, few (Lesson 4.1).
5. **"What do you do when a stakeholder disputes your numbers?"** The method from Lesson 6.2.

<details class="maths" open><summary>Principle</summary>

**Principle:** show, don't tell. "Strong communication skills" on a CV is a claim; a before and after chart is evidence. The same applies in interviews: one specific story beats three adjectives.

**Analogy:** a chef's portfolio is photos of dishes, not a list of knives they own.
</details>

## 5. Worked example: a STAR answer

*"Tell me about a time you presented data."*

- **Situation:** "In my storytelling capstone, a retail chain reported 29 % growth for 2025."
- **Task:** "I was asked to explain how the year went and what to change."
- **Action:** "I found that a new store made up over half the growth; the older stores grew 14 %. I built five slides with action titles, led with the recommendation, and showed headline vs like-for-like side by side."
- **Result:** "The recommendation was to track like-for-like growth monthly, so a slowdown can't hide behind new stores. I'd measure success by whether the next quarter's targets use it."

Use a real work or study example when you have one; the capstone is a good stand-in until then.

## 6. Practice

1. Write the one-line title for your portfolio page.
2. Prepare a STAR answer from your own experience (work, study or volunteering).
3. Find a chart in the news this week and list three things you'd change.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. For example: "From dashboard to decision: a 5-slide story on a retailer's 2025 growth."
2. Keep it under 90 seconds, with a number in the result.
3. Typical answers: an action title, removing a legend or 3D effect, starting the bar axis at zero, highlighting one series.
</details>
