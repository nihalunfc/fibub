**Module 1 · about 12 minutes · read first: 1.2.** You'll be able to write the main message of any analysis in one sentence, and check it against three rules.

## 1. Why this matters

Ask an analyst "what did you find?" and many answer with a tour of their charts. Ask a good one, and you get one sentence. That sentence, the **big idea**, steers everything after it: which charts to make, which to drop, what the title of each slide says. If you can't write it, you're not ready to present.

## 2. The idea in one picture

A topic is not a message:

| Topic (what it's about) | Big idea (what you want them to think and do) |
|---|---|
| 2025 store performance | Rideau grew fastest in 2025 (+19 %) while Falls Centre grew slowest (+9 %), so let's find out what Rideau does differently before setting 2026 targets. |
| February sales | February was the only month below last year, so we should check what went wrong before planning next February's stock. |

## 3. How to write one

A big idea has three parts, usually in one sentence:

1. **Your point of view:** a claim, with a number. "Rideau grew fastest."
2. **Why it matters (the stakes):** what happens if they act or don't. "...before we set 2026 targets."
3. **The ask:** what you want them to do. "Let's find out what Rideau does differently."

Tests: Could someone disagree with it? (If not, it's too vague.) Does it fit in one breath? Would your audience understand every word?

<details class="maths" open><summary>Principle</summary>

**Principle:** a message must be a complete sentence with a verb. "Sales by store" has no verb, so it can't be true or false. "Rideau grew fastest" can.

**Analogy:** a newspaper headline. "Weather" is a section name; "Storm closes schools on Friday" is news.
</details>

## 5. Worked example

*Draft:* "This presentation covers 2025 performance across our five stores and four categories."
That's a topic. Ask "so what?" three times:

- So what? "Sales grew 29 %." (A finding.)
- So what? "But 15 points of that is the new store; the rest grew 14 %." (A finding with stakes.)
- So what? "So we should track like-for-like growth, or a slowdown could hide behind new stores." (The ask.)

*Big idea:* "2025 sales grew 29 %, but the older stores grew 14 %, so we should report like-for-like growth each month so new stores don't hide a slowdown."

## 6. Practice

1. Turn this topic into a big idea: "Weekday vs weekend sales."
2. What is missing here? "Weekend sales are 36 % of the total."
3. Is "We should improve sales" a good big idea? Why not?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. For example: "Weekends bring 36 % of sales in 2 days, so we should schedule more staff on Saturdays and Sundays."
2. The stakes and the ask: so what should anyone do?
3. No: there's no finding, no number and no specific action. Everyone agrees with it, so it says nothing.
</details>
