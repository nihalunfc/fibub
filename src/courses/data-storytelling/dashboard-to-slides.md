**Module 5 · about 11 minutes · read first: 5.1.** You'll be able to turn a dashboard into a short deck where every slide makes one point.

## 1. Why this matters

Pasting dashboard screenshots into slides is the most common way to lose a room: tiny charts, many numbers, no message. A dashboard is a tool for exploring; a presentation is an argument. Converting one into the other is a skill you'll use every month in a BI job.

## 2. The idea in one picture

Each slide: an action title (the point), one chart that proves it, highlighted where it matters, and nothing else.

<!--fig:declutter lines-->

The last step is a slide: title, subtitle, one chart, one note.

## 3. Steps

1. **Write the big idea** (Lesson 1.3) and the SCR outline (5.1).
2. **List the 3 to 6 points** that support it. Each becomes a slide.
3. **Write each slide's title as a sentence** first. Read the titles in order: do they tell the whole story? (This is the "horizontal logic" test.)
4. **Pick or make one chart per slide** that proves its title. Rebuild it for the slide: bigger text, decluttered, one highlight. Don't screenshot the dashboard.
5. **Add one opening slide** with the answer (the executive summary) and **one closing slide** with the ask.
6. **Move everything else to the appendix.**

<details class="maths" open><summary>Principle</summary>

**Principle:** horizontal and vertical logic. Horizontally, the titles read in order make the argument. Vertically, everything on a slide supports that slide's title. A slide that fails either test needs fixing or cutting.

**Analogy:** chapters in a book. Each has one job, and the chapter titles alone give you the plot.

## 4. Worked example

*Northbay's 2025 dashboard has 4 pages and 18 visuals.* The deck for the regional manager:

1. "2025 beat target by 6.6 %, but growth in our older stores is half the headline" (summary)
2. "Sales grew 29 %, with every month above 2024 except February" (trend chart)
3. "Without Forks Market, growth was 14 %" (headline vs like-for-like bars)
4. "Forks Market reached 99.6 % of target in its first 10 months" (store vs target bars, Forks highlighted)
5. "February missed target by 8 %: we need to find out why" (monthly vs target, February circled)
6. "We recommend monthly like-for-like reporting from January" (the ask, with a mock KPI card)

Read just the titles: the whole story is there.

## 5. Practice

1. Write slide titles for a three-slide deck about weekend sales and staffing.
2. A slide has four charts. What do you do?
3. Your manager will read the deck without you there. What changes?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. For example: "Weekends bring over a third of our sales" · "But we staff every day the same" · "We suggest moving 2 shifts a week from Tuesday and Wednesday to the weekend".
2. Find the one that proves the title; move the others to the appendix or their own slides.
3. Titles and notes must carry the explanation on their own: add a short note to each chart, and write the summary slide as full sentences.
</details>
