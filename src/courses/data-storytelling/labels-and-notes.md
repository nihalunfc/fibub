**Module 3 · about 9 minutes · read first: 3.4.** You'll know which values to label, how to annotate a chart, and how to format numbers so they read at a glance.

## 1. Why this matters

Readers shouldn't have to trace a line to an axis or match a colour to a legend. Labels and notes put the words right where the eye already is. A single note, "Feb: 13.4k vs 14.5k target", can save a minute of explanation in a meeting.

## 3. What to label

- **Few bars (up to ~10):** label every bar, drop the axis.
- **Lines:** label the line name and often the last value at the right end.
- **Many points (24 months):** label only the points you talk about: the peak, the dip, the latest.
- **Annotate events:** "New store opened", "Price rise", "Snowstorm closures". One short line, near the point, in a muted or highlight colour.
- **Source and notes:** a small line under the chart: "Source: Northbay sales system. 2025 includes Forks Market from March."

## 4. Formatting numbers

1. **Round:** $78k, not $78,295.37, on charts. Keep exact values for tables.
2. **Units once:** in the subtitle ("$ thousands") or with the number ("$78k"), not both.
3. **Same precision** across a chart: 12.3 % and 9.0 %, not 12.3 % and 9 %.
4. **Percent vs percentage points:** margin going from 49 % to 54 % is +5 points, or +10 % relative. Say which.

<details class="maths" open><summary>Maths and principle</summary>

**Percentage points vs percent:** change in points = new % − old %; relative change = (new − old) / old.
Margin 49 % → 54 %: +5 percentage points, or (54 − 49) / 49 ≈ +10 % relative. Mixing these up is one of the most common mistakes in business writing.

**Principle:** put the explanation next to the thing it explains. Each glance from the chart to a legend or footnote and back costs attention.
</details>

## 5. Practice

1. Format for a chart label: 57838.21 dollars.
2. Grocery margin went from 41 % to 44 %. Write the change two ways.
3. A line chart of 24 months has a value label on every point. What do you change?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. $57.8k (or $58k).
2. +3 percentage points, or +7 % relative (3 ÷ 41 ≈ 0.073).
3. Remove most labels; keep the last value, the peak and any point you discuss, plus an annotation for any event.
</details>
