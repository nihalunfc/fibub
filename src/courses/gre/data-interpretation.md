**Module 5 · about 12 minutes.** By the end you will answer a three-question data set in about five minutes.

<div class="bigidea">A data set gives you one picture and several questions. Spend thirty seconds learning the picture (title, units, axes, totals) and every question gets faster.</div>

## 1. How a set works

A table or one or two charts, followed by about three questions of the usual formats (one answer, select-all, Numeric Entry). The same skills appear in the GMAT course's [Table Analysis](/learn/courses/gmat/table-analysis/) and [Graphics Interpretation](/learn/courses/gmat/graphics-interpretation/) lessons, which you can use for extra practice.

## 2. Percent of a total versus percent change

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: two kinds of percent">
<div class="step">A table: a firm's costs were 40 (thousand dollars) for staff out of 100 in 2024, and 45 out of 120 in 2025.</div>
<div class="step">Percent of the total in 2025: 45 ÷ 120 = 37.5%. It <b>fell</b> from 40%.</div>
<div class="step">Percent change in staff cost: (45 − 40) ÷ 40 = 12.5%. It <b>rose</b>.</div>
<div class="step">Same table, opposite stories: read which percent the question wants.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Bigger slice, bigger amount."** Only within the same pie. Two pies with different totals cannot be compared by slice size alone.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Read title, units (thousands? percent?), axes and totals before any question.
- Percent of total = part ÷ whole; percent change = change ÷ start.
- Estimate when the choices are far apart; calculate when they are close.
- Two-axis charts: match each series to its axis.

</div>
