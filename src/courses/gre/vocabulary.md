**Module 6 · about 11 minutes, then 15 minutes a day.** By the end you will have a word habit that adds hundreds of useful words over your study plan.

<div class="bigidea">Words stick in families and in sentences. Learn one root and you understand a whole group of words, including ones you have never seen.</div>

## 1. Learn in families

<figure class="fig" data-fig="word-roots"><figcaption>Pick a root to see the family it builds. Each word comes with a short sentence, because a word without a sentence is easily forgotten.</figcaption></figure>

## 2. The daily habit

<div class="steps" data-title="Fifteen minutes a day">
<div class="step">Collect new words from your practice sets and reading.</div>
<div class="step">For each: write its meaning in plain words and one sentence of your own.</div>
<div class="step">Review the words from 3 days ago and from 7 days ago.</div>
<div class="step">Move words you get right twice into a "known" pile; keep the rest cycling.</div>
</div>

## 3. Guessing an unknown word

Use the root for direction (good or bad, more or less) and the sentence for meaning. "The critic's maledictions against the play" → "mal" = bad, "dict" = say: bad things said. That is often enough to pick or rule out a choice.

<div class="myth">

**"I need a 3,000-word list."** Most completions are solved by the sentence's logic. A few hundred well-learned words plus roots go further than a long list skimmed once.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Learn words in root families and always in a sentence.
- Ten new words a day, reviewed after 3 and 7 days.
- Use roots for direction and the sentence for meaning.

</div>
