**Module 4 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will move between radius, diameter, circumference and area in one step, and find any arc or sector.

<div class="bigidea">A circle question usually hides one number, the radius. Find r, and every other measure follows; an arc or a sector is just a fraction of the whole.</div>

## 1. Polygons

- Interior angles of an n-sided polygon add to **(n − 2) × 180°**. A regular polygon has n equal angles.
- Rectangle area = length × width; parallelogram = base × height; trapezoid = ½(sum of the parallel sides) × height.
- A square's diagonal is side × √2 (two 45-45-90 triangles).

## 2. Circles

| From r | Formula |
|---|---|
| diameter | d = 2r |
| circumference | C = 2πr = πd |
| area | A = πr² |

<figure class="fig" data-fig="circle-parts"><figcaption>Change the radius and the central angle. The arc and the sector are the same fraction of the circle as the angle is of 360°.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: from circumference to sector">
<div class="step">A circle has circumference 20π. Find the area of a 72° sector.</div>
<div class="step">2πr = 20π, so r = 10 and the area is 100π.</div>
<div class="step">72 ÷ 360 = 1/5. Sector area = 100π ÷ 5 = <b>20π</b>.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Answers must be decimals."** GRE choices often keep π: 20π, not 62.83. Leave π in until the end, and use the calculator only when a decimal is asked for.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Interior angles: (n − 2) × 180°.
- Circle: find r first; C = 2πr, A = πr².
- Arc and sector = (angle ÷ 360) × the whole.
- Keep π in the answer unless a decimal is asked.

</div>
