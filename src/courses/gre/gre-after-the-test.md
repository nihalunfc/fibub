**Module 9 · about 11 minutes.** By the end you will turn any practice test into a list of exactly what to study next.

<div class="bigidea">Your score tells you where you are. Your error log tells you how to move. Spend as long reviewing a test as taking it.</div>

## 1. Build an error log

For every question you got wrong **or guessed**, write one line: section, question type, topic, what went wrong, and what you will do differently.

## 2. Four kinds of error

| Kind | Sounds like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Content** | "I didn't know the angles in a 30-60-90 triangle." "I didn't know the word *laconic*." | Redo that lesson and its practice set; add the word family to your list. |
| **Method** | "I calculated both quantities fully when plugging in two numbers would do." | Study the worked solution; redo the question a day later. |
| **Careless** | "I picked one answer on a select-all question." | Add a checking step: re-read the instructions (one, two, or all that apply). |
| **Timing** | "I ran out of time on the last three Quant questions." | Pace checkpoints (lesson 9.1); mark and move on earlier. |

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: one test, one plan">
<div class="step">Quant: 7 misses. 3 content (circles, quartiles), 2 Quantitative Comparison method (did not test negatives or fractions), 2 careless (units).</div>
<div class="step">Verbal: 6 misses. 4 Sentence Equivalence vocabulary (content), 2 reading inference (method).</div>
<div class="step">Plan for the next 10 days: redo lessons 4.3 and 5.2; redo lesson 2.2 and always test 0, 1, −1, ½ and a large number; 15 minutes of word families a day (lesson 6.4); redo lesson 7.2.</div>
<div class="step">Retake the practice test after 10 days and compare the logs.</div>

## 3. Spaced review

Redo the questions in your log after a day, then after a week. Getting them right a week later means you have really learned the idea. Spacing out practice and testing yourself are two of the best-supported study methods in learning research.

## 4. When to book the real test

Book it when two practice tests in a row meet your target and your log has few content errors. You can retake the GRE, and ScoreSelect lets you choose which scores schools see, so one test day does not have to be perfect. Check each programme's own score needs and deadlines.

<div class="keypoints">

- Log every wrong and every guessed question.
- Name the kind of error: content, method, careless or timing.
- Fix the biggest kind first; each has its own fix.
- Redo logged questions after a day and after a week.
- Retake a full test after one to two weeks of fixes.

</div>
