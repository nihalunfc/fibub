**Module 2 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will know exactly what each of the four answers claims, and you will stop doing sums the question never needed.

<div class="bigidea">Quantitative Comparison never asks for a value. It asks which of two quantities is bigger, and whether that is always true.</div>

## 1. The format

You see Quantity A and Quantity B, sometimes with a line of information above them. The four answers never change:

| Answer | Means |
|---|---|
| **A** | Quantity A is greater, in every allowed case. |
| **B** | Quantity B is greater, in every allowed case. |
| **C** | The two are equal, in every allowed case. |
| **D** | The relationship cannot be determined: different allowed cases give different results. |

<figure class="fig" data-fig="qc-plug"><figcaption>Choose a comparison and slide x through the allowed values. When the two lines cross inside the allowed range, the answer is D.</figcaption></figure>

## 2. Numbers only: never D

If both quantities are plain numbers, one of A, B or C must be true. You can rule out D at once.

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: compare without finishing">
<div class="step">A: 37 × 41. B: 39 × 39.</div>
<div class="step">Don't multiply. Write A as (39 − 2)(39 + 2) = 39² − 4.</div>
<div class="step">So A is 4 less than B. Answer <b>B</b>, with almost no arithmetic.</div>
</div>

## 3. Unknowns: hunt for a second case

With a variable, test more than one allowed value on purpose. If two values give different results, the answer is D, and you can stop.

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: one case is never enough">
<div class="step">x > 0. A: x. B: x².</div>
<div class="step">x = 2: A = 2, B = 4. B is greater.</div>
<div class="step">x = 0.5: A = 0.5, B = 0.25. A is greater.</div>
<div class="step">Different results: <b>D</b>. If every case had given B, the answer would be B.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"If I can't work it out, choose D."** D is a claim, not a shrug: it says you found (or could find) two cases that disagree. Hard arithmetic with fixed numbers is never D.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- A, B, C mean "always"; D means "it depends on the allowed values".
- Only numbers: the answer is A, B or C. Never D.
- With unknowns, test 0, 1, a negative, a fraction and a big number, where allowed.
- Compare without finishing: simplify both sides the same way.

</div>
