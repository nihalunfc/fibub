**Module 3 · about 11 minutes, plus the linked lessons.** Another map: the algebra is taught in the shared lessons. Follow them in order, then use the GRE notes here.

<div class="bigidea">Every algebra trap on the GRE comes from forgetting a second possibility: a second root, a flipped inequality, or a case where a variable is negative.</div>

## 1. The route

| Step | Lesson | GRE note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Linear equations and systems](/learn/courses/gmat/linear-equations/) | QC often needs only x + y, not x and y |
| 2 | [Quadratics](/learn/courses/gmat/quadratics/) | two roots: a comparison with a root is often D |
| 3 | [Inequalities and absolute value](/learn/courses/gmat/inequalities-absolute-value/) | multiplying by a negative flips the sign |
| 4 | [Functions and sequences](/learn/courses/gmat/functions-sequences/) | made-up symbols (like a ◊ b) are just functions |

## 2. Ask for what is asked

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: don't solve more than needed">
<div class="step">Information: 3x + 3y = 21. A: x + y. B: 7.</div>
<div class="step">Divide the equation by 3: x + y = 7.</div>
<div class="step">Answer <b>C</b>. You never needed x or y on their own.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"A square root symbol gives two answers."** √16 means only the positive root, 4. It is the equation x² = 16 that has two solutions, 4 and −4.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Look for the combination the question wants (x + y, 2x − y) before solving.
- Quadratics and absolute values usually have two solutions.
- Multiplying or dividing an inequality by a negative flips it.
- √ means the non-negative root; x² = k has two roots when k > 0.

</div>
