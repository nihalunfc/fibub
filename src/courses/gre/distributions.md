**Module 5 · about 14 minutes.** By the end you will read a box plot, find quartiles and use the normal curve without a table. The [shared median and spread lesson](/learn/courses/gmat/median-range-sd/) covers the basics first.

<div class="bigidea">Averages tell you the middle; distributions tell you the shape. Quartiles split the data into four equal groups, and the normal curve tells you what share lies within each standard deviation.</div>

## 1. Quartiles and box plots

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: quartiles">
<div class="step">Data: 3, 7, 8, 12, 15, 18, 21, 30 (8 values, already sorted).</div>
<div class="step">Median = average of the 4th and 5th: (12 + 15) ÷ 2 = 13.5.</div>
<div class="step">Q1 = median of the lower half 3, 7, 8, 12 = 7.5. Q3 = median of 15, 18, 21, 30 = 19.5.</div>
<div class="step">IQR = 19.5 − 7.5 = <b>12</b>. A box plot draws a box from 7.5 to 19.5, a line at 13.5, and whiskers to 3 and 30.</div>
</div>

## 2. The normal curve

<figure class="fig" data-fig="normal-curve"><figcaption>Pick a band. In any normal distribution the shares are the same: 68% within 1 SD, 95% within 2, 99.7% within 3.</figcaption></figure>

Half the data is above the mean. Within 1 SD on one side: 34%. Above +1 SD: about 16%. Above +2 SD: about 2.5%.

<div class="myth">

**"A bigger standard deviation means bigger numbers."** It means more spread. Two groups can have the same mean and very different standard deviations.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Sort, find the median, then the medians of each half: Q1 and Q3. IQR = Q3 − Q1.
- Box plot: box from Q1 to Q3, line at the median, whiskers to the extremes.
- Normal: 68%, 95%, 99.7% within 1, 2, 3 SD; half on each side.
- Percentile = share of the group at or below a value.

</div>
