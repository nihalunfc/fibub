**Module 9 · about 12 minutes.** You know the content. This lesson makes sure the clock never takes points you have earned.

<div class="bigidea">Every question is worth the same, a blank costs as much as a wrong answer, and you can move freely within a section. So answer the easy ones first, guess on the rest, and never leave a blank.</div>

## 1. The clock, section by section

| Section | Questions | Minutes | About per question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Analytical Writing (first) | 1 essay | 30 | 30 minutes |
| Verbal 1 | 12 | 18 | 90 seconds |
| Verbal 2 | 15 | 23 | 92 seconds |
| Quant 1 | 12 | 21 | 105 seconds |
| Quant 2 | 15 | 26 | 104 seconds |

The essay comes first; the Verbal and Quant sections follow in any order, with no scheduled break. The whole test takes just under two hours.

## 2. Section-level adaptivity

<figure class="fig" data-fig="adaptive"><figcaption>The idea of adaptivity, shown for a question-by-question test. The GRE adapts less finely: by whole section.</figcaption></figure>

On the GRE, the **first** Verbal and Quant sections are of medium difficulty. Your result on each decides whether the **second** section of that subject is easier or harder. Harder second sections can lead to higher scores, so the first section matters a lot: work it carefully, but do not freeze on any one question.

Within a section, every question counts the same. A hard question you get right is worth no more than an easy one, which is why skipping and returning is a strategy, not a weakness.

## 3. Mark, skip and return

<div class="steps" data-title="Two passes through every section">
<div class="step"><b>First pass:</b> answer every question you can do in about the average time. If one looks long, give your best guess, mark it and move on.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Second pass:</b> use the Review screen to go back to marked questions with the time left.</div>
<div class="step"><b>Last minute:</b> make sure every question has an answer. A guess has a chance; a blank has none.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Guessing lowers your score."** On the GRE there is no penalty for a wrong answer. A guess can only help.

</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Check every answer twice before moving on."** Spend that time on the questions you skipped; re-checking easy questions rarely finds errors.

</div>

## 4. Smart guessing

- **Quantitative Comparison:** if the two quantities have no numbers fixed, test a few values ([lesson 2.2](/learn/courses/gre/qc-strategies/)). If you still cannot tell, D (cannot be determined) is often the answer when variables are involved.
- **Multiple choice:** cross out answers that are clearly too big or too small by estimating, then guess from the rest.
- **Numeric entry:** estimate and type something sensible.
- **Text Completion and Sentence Equivalence:** pick words that fit the signal word's direction ([lesson 6.1](/learn/courses/gre/text-completion/)).
- **Reading:** a choice that agrees with the main idea and uses moderate words is the best guess.

<div class="keypoints">

- About 90 seconds per Verbal question and 105 per Quant question.
- The first section of each subject sets the difficulty of the second.
- Every question counts the same: two passes, mark and return.
- Never leave a blank: there is no penalty for wrong answers.

</div>
