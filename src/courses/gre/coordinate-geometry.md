**Module 4 · about 12 minutes.** By the end you will find a line's slope, equation and distances straight from two points.

<div class="bigidea">On a grid, every distance is a right triangle and every line is a slope plus a starting height. Draw the triangle and the rest is Pythagoras.</div>

## 1. Slope and the line

- **Slope** m = rise ÷ run = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁).
- **Line:** y = mx + b, where b is where it crosses the y-axis.
- Parallel lines: equal slopes. Perpendicular lines: slopes multiply to −1.

## 2. Distance and midpoint

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: distance on the grid">
<div class="step">Find the distance from (−2, 1) to (4, 9).</div>
<div class="step">Across: 4 − (−2) = 6. Up: 9 − 1 = 8.</div>
<div class="step">A 6-8-10 triangle: distance <b>10</b>.</div>
<div class="step">Midpoint: average the coordinates: ((−2 + 4) ÷ 2, (1 + 9) ÷ 2) = (1, 5).</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"A steeper line has a bigger slope."** Only among lines that rise. A line falling steeply has a large negative slope, which is a small number.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Slope = rise ÷ run; y = mx + b.
- Parallel: same slope. Perpendicular: product −1.
- Distance: draw the right triangle and use Pythagoras.
- Midpoint: average the x values and the y values.

</div>
