**Module 6 · about 12 minutes.** By the end you will untangle long sentences with several blanks, one blank at a time.

<div class="bigidea">Multi-blank sentences are chains. Find the link with the clearest clue, fill it, and let it pull the others into place.</div>

## 1. The method

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: three blanks">
<div class="step">"The historian's account, once (i) ______ as the final word on the period, now seems (ii) ______: new archives have shown how much it (iii) ______."</div>
<div class="step">Clearest clue: "once … the final word … now seems" is a contrast. Blank (ii) is the opposite of final: <b>incomplete</b>.</div>
<div class="step">Blank (i): what people once did to a "final word": <b>hailed</b> it.</div>
<div class="step">Blank (iii): the colon explains "incomplete": the archives show how much it <b>omitted</b>. Read the whole sentence again: it holds together.</div>
</div>

<figure class="fig" data-fig="signal-words"><figcaption>The same clue-and-signal habit, one blank at a time.</figcaption></figure>

On this site, multi-blank questions show every blank's words in one list, labelled Blank (i), (ii), (iii): choose exactly one word for each blank.

<div class="myth">

**"Start at blank (i) and go in order."** The first blank is often the hardest, with its clue at the end of the sentence. Start wherever the clue is clearest.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Start with the blank that has the clearest clue.
- Each filled blank becomes a clue for the next.
- Re-read the full sentence at the end.
- All blanks must be right to score.

</div>
