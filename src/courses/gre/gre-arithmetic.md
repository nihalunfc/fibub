## 2. The exceptions that decide comparisons

<figure class="fig" data-fig="number-line"><figcaption>The number line from the shared lessons. The interesting comparisons happen near 0, between 0 and 1, and below −1.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: where the rules bend">
<div class="step">"Squaring makes a number bigger." True for 2 (4), false for 1/2 (1/4), equal for 0 and 1.</div>
<div class="step">"Dividing makes a number smaller." Dividing by 1/2 doubles it.</div>
<div class="step">"Bigger power, bigger number." (−2)³ = −8 is less than (−2)² = 4.</div>
<div class="step">In a comparison with an unknown, these are exactly the cases to try.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"GRE maths is easier than GMAT maths, so I can skip the shared lessons."** The topics overlap almost completely. The GRE is just as strict about exceptions, and its Numeric Entry questions give you no choices to check against.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Follow the nine shared lessons in order; each has its own practice.
- 0 is even; 1 is not prime; 2 is the only even prime.
- Squaring shrinks numbers between 0 and 1; odd powers keep a negative sign.
- Percent change divides by the start; a ratio gives parts, not counts.

</div>
