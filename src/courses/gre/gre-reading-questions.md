**Module 7 · about 10 minutes.** By the end you will handle the two GRE reading formats that have no five-choice list.

<div class="bigidea">Both formats are really true-or-false checks: is this choice supported, or does this sentence do this job? Check each one against the text, separately.</div>

## 1. Select all that apply

Three choices, square boxes, and any number from one to three can be right. Treat each as its own true-or-false question against the passage.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="select-all"><figcaption>The same idea with numbers, from lesson 2.3: each choice is checked on its own, and only the exact set scores.</figcaption></figure>

## 2. Select in passage

You click a sentence in the passage that does what the question says: "states a claim the author rejects", "provides an example". Use your passage map: the job of each paragraph points you to the right area, then the signal words (for example, however, thus) point to the sentence.

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: find the sentence by its job">
<div class="step">Question: select the sentence that gives the explanation the author rejects (street-plan passage).</div>
<div class="step">Map: paragraph 2 holds "the usual explanation", then the author's own.</div>
<div class="step">The sentence "The usual explanation is a lack of money or political will" is the rejected view. Click that one.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"The right sentence repeats words from the question."** Select-in-passage tests the job of a sentence, which is often described in different words.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Select-all: check each choice against the text on its own.
- Select-in-passage: find the paragraph by its job, then the sentence by its signal words.
- No partial credit.

</div>

**Try it:** Tap each paragraph to label its job, then press Check.
