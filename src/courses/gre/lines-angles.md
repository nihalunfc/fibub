**Module 4 · about 11 minutes.** By the end you will find any angle in a diagram of lines from two or three facts.

<div class="bigidea">Almost every angle question uses three facts: a straight line is 180°, a full turn is 360°, and parallel lines make only two different angles with a line that crosses them.</div>

## 1. The basic facts

- Angles on a straight line add to **180°**; angles around a point add to **360°**.
- **Vertical angles** (opposite each other where two lines cross) are equal.

## 2. Parallel lines

<figure class="fig" data-fig="parallel-lines"><figcaption>Turn the crossing line. Whatever its slant, there are only two angle sizes, x and y, and x + y = 180°.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: chase the angles">
<div class="step">Lines k and m are parallel. A transversal makes an angle of 3a − 10 with k and a + 50 with m, in matching positions.</div>
<div class="step">Matching (corresponding) angles are equal: 3a − 10 = a + 50.</div>
<div class="step">2a = 60, so a = 30 and each angle is 80°.</div>
<div class="step">The angle beside either one, on the same line, is 180 − 80 = <b>100°</b>.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"It looks like 90°, so it is."** GRE figures are not necessarily drawn to scale. Use only what is marked or stated: right-angle boxes, equal-length ticks, "parallel" in the text.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Straight line 180°, full turn 360°, vertical angles equal.
- Parallel lines and a transversal: every angle is x or 180° − x.
- Set equal angles equal, or add supplements to 180°, and solve.
- Never read sizes off the picture.

</div>
