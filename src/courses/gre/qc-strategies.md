**Module 2 · about 14 minutes.** By the end you will pick the right strategy for a comparison within a few seconds of reading it.

<div class="bigidea">Treat the two quantities like the two pans of a balance. Anything you do to both pans that keeps the balance honest is allowed; then look which way it tips.</div>

## 1. Do the same to both sides

Add or subtract the same thing, or multiply or divide by the same **positive** number. The comparison stays the same.

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: clear the clutter">
<div class="step">A: 3x + 7. B: 3x + 5.</div>
<div class="step">Subtract 3x from both: A becomes 7, B becomes 5.</div>
<div class="step">A is greater for every x: answer <b>A</b>.</div>
</div>

Never multiply or divide by something that could be negative or zero: a negative flips the balance.

## 2. Plug in smart numbers

When the information leaves an unknown free, test the "weird" numbers: 0, 1, −1, a fraction, a big number. Stop as soon as two cases disagree: that is D.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="qc-plug"><figcaption>The plug-in method as a picture: each quantity is a curve over the allowed values. Look for where they cross.</figcaption></figure>

## 3. Compare piece by piece

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: piece by piece">
<div class="step">A: 1/3 + 2/5. B: 2/5 + 0.34.</div>
<div class="step">Both have 2/5: cancel it. Compare 1/3 ≈ 0.333 with 0.34.</div>
<div class="step">0.34 is bigger: answer <b>B</b>.</div>
</div>

## 4. Use the information first

The line above the quantities often settles the question by itself. "x + y = 10 and x > y" means x is more than 5 before you look at anything else.

<div class="myth">

**"Plugging in two numbers that agree proves the answer."** It does not. Two agreeing cases only suggest it. Try at least one strange value (a negative, a fraction, zero) before you trust A, B or C.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Add, subtract, or multiply and divide by a positive number on both sides.
- Never divide by something that might be negative or zero.
- Plug in 0, 1, −1, a fraction and a big number; two different results mean D.
- Cancel shared pieces and compare what is left.
- Read the given information first: it often decides the question.

</div>
