**Module 6 · about 13 minutes.** By the end you will solve one-blank completions by reading the sentence's logic, not by guessing at words.

<div class="bigidea">Every Text Completion sentence contains its own answer: a clue that says what the blank means, and a signal word that says whether the blank agrees with the clue or goes against it.</div>

## 1. The method

<div class="steps" data-title="Four steps">
<div class="step"><b>Cover the choices.</b></div>
<div class="step"><b>Find the clue:</b> the words that describe the blank (often the longest descriptive phrase).</div>
<div class="step"><b>Find the signal:</b> same direction (and, because, similarly, a colon) or contrast (although, but, despite, far from).</div>
<div class="step"><b>Predict</b> a simple word of your own, then pick the choice closest to it.</div>
</div>

<figure class="fig" data-fig="signal-words"><figcaption>Tap a sentence to see its clue, its signal and a prediction. The prediction can be a plain word: the choice only needs to match its meaning.</figcaption></figure>

## 2. Signal words

| Same direction | Contrast | Cause and result |
|---|---|---|
| and, also, similarly, indeed, a colon or semicolon | although, but, yet, despite, however, far from, rather than | because, so … that, therefore, thus |

<div class="myth">

**"I need to know every word in the choices."** You need to know your prediction and enough of the choices to rule some out. If two unknown words remain, choose the one whose root or feel matches your prediction.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Cover the choices; find the clue and the signal; predict; match.
- Contrast signals flip the clue; same-direction signals repeat it.
- The commonest trap is a word on the right topic pointing the wrong way.

</div>
