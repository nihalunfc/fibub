**Module 2 · about 11 minutes.** By the end you will type answers in exactly the form the box wants, and treat select-all questions as a set of small true-or-false checks.

<div class="bigidea">Without five choices to lean on, the method must carry you: re-read what is asked, check units and rounding, and test every choice on its own.</div>

## 1. Numeric Entry

<div class="steps" data-title="Before you type">
<div class="step">Re-read the last line of the question: what exactly is asked? The area, not the side; the percent, not the decimal.</div>
<div class="step">Check the unit next to the box (cm, %, dollars) and give your number in it.</div>
<div class="step">Check rounding: "to the nearest tenth" means one decimal place. If nothing is said, give the exact value.</div>
<div class="step">Two boxes stacked: a fraction. Type the numerator on top; you need not reduce it.</div>
</div>

## 2. Select-all questions

The question says "Select all that apply" (square boxes, not round ones). Any number of choices can be right, from one to all. You score only if you pick **exactly** the right set.

<figure class="fig" data-fig="select-all"><figcaption>Each choice is its own true-or-false check. Tick the ones that pass and see why partial answers score nothing.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="steps" data-title="Worked example: select all">
<div class="step">Which integers satisfy 1 &lt; 2x − 3 &lt; 9? Choices: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6.</div>
<div class="step">Add 3: 4 &lt; 2x &lt; 12. Halve: 2 &lt; x &lt; 6.</div>
<div class="step">Check each: 1 no, 2 no (strict), 3 yes, 4 yes, 5 yes, 6 no (strict).</div>
<div class="step">Select <b>3, 4 and 5</b>. Missing one, or adding 2 or 6, scores nothing.</div>
</div>

<div class="myth">

**"Usually two are right."** There is no usual number. Judge every choice on its own.

</div>

<div class="keypoints">

- Numeric Entry: re-read the question, match the unit, follow the rounding rule.
- Stacked boxes take a fraction; it need not be in lowest terms.
- Select-all: test each choice separately; exactly the right set scores.
- No partial credit and no penalty for wrong answers: always answer.

</div>
