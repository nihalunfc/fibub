## Part 1: How big is GTA 6, and how long does it take to download?

### The size

Rockstar's own pre-load help page says the game needs **up to 212 GB of free space, depending on your device**. Reported store figures are close to that:

| Platform | Reported size | Where the number comes from |
|---|---|---|
| PlayStation 5 and PS5 Pro | about 209.75 GB to download, about 212 GB installed | RockstarINTEL report, 9 November 2026 |
| Xbox Series X and Series S | 225.34 GB on the store page, about 209.9 GB on the console | Xbox store listing, checked 7 November 2026 by Timesaver.gg |
| PC | not released, no size announced | Take-Two, 2026 |

Why does Xbox show two different numbers for the same file? The store counts in decimal gigabytes (1 GB = 1,000,000,000 bytes) and the console counts in binary gigabytes (1 GiB = 1,073,741,824 bytes). 225.34 billion bytes ÷ 1,073,741,824 = **209.9**. It is the same file measured two ways. You can check this yourself with the [[unit-converter]] (data units).

A day-one patch was not announced as of 9 November 2026, so leave room for one. We suggest keeping **about 250 GB free** so that the game, a patch and the temporary space the console needs while it installs all fit. That buffer is our planning figure, not Rockstar's.

### Will it fit on my console?

- **Xbox Series S, 512 GB:** only about 362 GB of it is free to use (Engadget), so GTA 6 alone takes more than half. Expect to delete other games.
- **PS5 and Xbox Series X:** both have about 1 TB or more; it fits, but check the free space in Settings before you start.
- **Extra storage:** a PS5 needs a compatible M.2 SSD inside the console to play PS5 games; a USB drive can only store them. An Xbox Series console needs an official Storage Expansion Card; an ordinary USB drive cannot run the game.

### How long will it take?

Download time is simple maths: **time = size × 8 ÷ speed**. The 8 turns bytes into bits, because internet speed is sold in megabits per second (Mbps) while game sizes are in gigabytes. For 212 GB at 100 Mbps: 212,000,000,000 × 8 ÷ 100,000,000 = 16,960 seconds, or about **4.7 hours**.

| Internet speed | At the full speed | At 60 % of it (typical Wi-Fi or busy evening) |
|---|---|---|
| 10 Mbps | about 47 hours | about 79 hours |
| 25 Mbps | about 19 hours | about 31 hours |
| 50 Mbps | about 9.4 hours | about 15.7 hours |
| 100 Mbps | about 4.7 hours | about 7.9 hours |
| 300 Mbps | about 1.6 hours | about 2.6 hours |
| 1,000 Mbps | about 28 minutes | about 47 minutes |

Real downloads are often slower than the speed on your plan: Wi-Fi through walls, other people streaming, and game servers that are busy on launch day all cut it down. That is why the table has a second column at 60 %, which is our round assumption, not a measurement.

**Tips that really help**

1. Run the [[internet-speed-test]] on the console's own connection (or a phone next to it) and type 212 GB into its "How long will a download take?" box.
2. Use a network cable if you can. If you cannot, the [[wifi-spot-finder]] helps you find the spot with the best signal.
3. Leave the console in rest mode overnight; both consoles keep downloading in rest mode when it is switched on in settings.
4. Watch your data cap. 212 GB is more than many mobile and some home plans allow in a month.

### How cheats worked in GTA V

GTA V (2013) gave three ways to enter the same cheats:

- **Button sequences** on the controller, typed quickly during play.
- **Phone numbers** dialled on the in-game phone. Each number is **1-999** followed by a word spelled on a phone keypad, for example PAINKILLER → 1-999-7246545537 (P = 7, A = 2, I = 4, N = 6, and so on).
- **Typed words** on PC, using the same word as the phone number.

Using a cheat turned off trophies and achievements for that play session, and cheats did not work in GTA Online. Because the phone is at the centre of GTA 6 too, we think phone-number cheats are the most likely way in. We do not guess button sequences: they were arbitrary in every game, so any guess would be made up.

## Part 3: Console or PC for GTA 6? Costs in India, Canada, the USA and the UK

### The short answer

**For GTA 6 today, you need a console.** It launched only on PlayStation 5 (including PS5 Pro) and Xbox Series X|S. No PC version or PC system requirements have been announced. Take-Two's chief executive has said that "Rockstar always starts on console". GTA V reached PC about 19 months after its first console release, so a PC version may come later, but nobody outside Rockstar knows when. Buying a gaming PC *for GTA 6* is a bet on a future release; buying one for other games is a different question, covered below.

**Which console for GTA 6?** For most people the **PS5 (disc or digital)** is the best value: it runs the game and has room for it. The **PS5 Pro** costs US$250 more for sharper images and steadier frame rates on a good 4K TV; it is worth it only if you have that TV. The **Xbox Series X** works just as well if your friends play on Xbox. The **Xbox Series S 512 GB** is the cheapest way in, but GTA 6 fills more than half its usable space, and the Series S generally runs games at a lower resolution than the Series X.

### Running costs: electricity

A gaming PC draws more power than a console. The chart in the next section shows the yearly cost at 0.15 per kWh, a round price that works in any currency. Put in your own tariff with the [[electricity-cost]] tool; in India use your slab tariff, which the tool also handles. At 3 hours a day, a 200 W console uses about 216 kWh a year (about 32 at 0.15 per kWh); a 400 W PC uses twice that. Either way, electricity is small next to the price of the machine.

Games, online subscriptions and resale value are left out; they can change the picture, and a PC does far more than play games.

**Is GTA 6 on PC?** Not at launch. No PC version or date has been announced. If one is announced, we will update this article.

**Why is my download slower than my internet plan?** Plans promise the speed to your home, not through Wi-Fi to the console, and game servers are crowded on launch day. Test with the [[internet-speed-test]] on the same connection, and try a cable.
