You can apply for Employment Insurance (EI) regular benefits if you lost your job in Canada through no fault of your own, worked enough insurable hours in the last 52 weeks (420 to 700, depending on where you live) and are ready to work. Apply online at canada.ca as soon as you stop working: if you apply more than 4 weeks after your last day of work, you may lose benefits. You do not need to wait for your Record of Employment.

This guide is for anyone working in Canada: students with a part-time job, people on a post-graduation or open work permit, permanent residents and citizens. Before you start, check whether you are likely to qualify and roughly how much you would get with the free [[ei-calculator|EI eligibility and benefit calculator]]; afterwards, plan the months on a lower income with the [[budget-calculator|budget planner]].

## Who can get it

Service Canada lists these conditions for EI regular benefits. You need all of them.

1. **Your job was insurable employment.** Most jobs where an employer pays you and takes EI premiums off your pay are. Your pay stub shows an "EI" deduction.
2. **You lost the job through no fault of your own.** A layoff, shortage of work, or the end of a contract or season counts.
3. **You have been without work and without pay for at least 7 days in a row** in the last 52 weeks.
4. **You worked enough insurable hours** in your qualifying period: between 420 and 700, depending on the unemployment rate in your EI economic region. A violation on an earlier claim raises the number.
5. **Your qualifying period** is usually the 52 weeks before your claim starts, or the time since your last claim started if that is shorter. It can sometimes be stretched to 104 weeks.
6. **You are ready, willing and capable of working each day.**
7. **You are actively looking for work** and keep a written record of the employers you contact and when.

You usually **cannot** get regular benefits if you quit without just cause, lost the job because of your own misconduct, or are out of work because you are directly part of a strike or lockout.

**Students and work-permit holders.** Your immigration status does not change the hours or the maths. If your Social Insurance Number (SIN) starts with a 9, the application also asks for proof of your immigration status and your work permit. If you study, the rule that matters is the same as for everyone: you must be ready and able to work each day. Answer every question about school honestly, and let Service Canada decide.

## What you need

Gather these before you open the form. Service Canada keeps an unfinished form for only 72 hours.

- **Your SIN**, plus your work permit and proof of status if it starts with a 9.
- **The last name at birth of one of your parents** (a security question).
- **Your mailing and home addresses**, with postal codes.
- **Your bank details for direct deposit**: the bank's name, the branch (transit) number and your account number. A void cheque or your banking app shows them.
- **Every employer in the last 52 weeks** (or since your last claim, if shorter): name, address, start and end dates, and why each job ended.
- **Your own account of the facts** if you quit or were let go from any of those jobs.
- **Your best-paid weeks**: the dates (Sunday to Saturday) and earnings of your highest-paid weeks. Your pay stubs show them.
- **Records of Employment (ROEs).** Most employers send them electronically, and you can see them in My Service Canada Account; then you do not need copies. If an employer gave you a paper ROE, keep it to send in.
- If you left with **vacation pay or severance**, have the amounts ready, because the form may ask about them.

## Step by step

1. **Check your hours and region first.** Open the [[ei-calculator|EI calculator]], pick your EI economic region and type your hours and pay, or add your pay stubs. It tells you the hours your region needs, whether you are likely to qualify, and an estimate of your weekly benefit and weeks. Nothing you type leaves your browser.
2. **Apply right away.** Do not wait for your ROE or for your final pay. You can send documents after you apply, and a late application can cost you benefits.
3. **Open the online application** on the canada.ca page "EI regular benefits: Apply" and choose to apply online. It takes about 1 hour. When you start, you get a temporary password so you can save and come back, within 72 hours.
4. **Fill in your personal details**: SIN, name, the parent's last name at birth, addresses, phone and email. Service Canada asks for an email address but will not ask you to send documents by email.
5. **Add every job** from the qualifying period, with dates and the reason each one ended. If you quit or were dismissed, explain what happened in your own words: this is your chance to show just cause.
6. **Give your best weeks of earnings** if the form asks, from your pay stubs.
7. **Sign up for direct deposit** so payments go straight to your bank account.
8. **Submit, and note your confirmation.** Save or print the last page before you close it.
9. **Send any documents** Service Canada asks for, such as a paper ROE or permit, the way the letter or your account tells you to.
10. **Register for My Service Canada Account** if you do not have it. You can check your claim, your ROEs and messages there at any time.

## After you apply

- **Benefit statement and access code.** Service Canada mails you a benefit statement shortly after you apply. It holds your four-digit access code. Keep it safe: you need it with your SIN for every report.
- **Decision and first payment.** The service standard is a decision within 28 days. If you qualify and sent everything, your first payment comes about 28 days after you apply.
- **Waiting week.** Normally the first week of a claim is not paid. A temporary measure waives it for new claims that start between 30 March 2025 and 9 October 2027.
- **Reports every 2 weeks.** You must send a report every 2 weeks, online through the Internet Reporting Service or by phone (Telephone Reporting Service, 1-800-531-7555), for as long as you get benefits. You have 3 weeks from the due date to send each one. With direct deposit, money arrives 2 to 3 business days after the report.
- **Work while on EI.** You may take a part-time or short job. Report your earnings before deductions for every week you work. You keep 50 cents of benefit for each dollar you earn, up to 90% of the weekly earnings your benefit is based on; above that, the benefit drops dollar for dollar.
- **Also report** any time outside Canada and any job offer you turn down.
- **Keep your job search record for 6 years.** Service Canada can ask to see it at any time.
- **Tax.** EI benefits are taxable income, and tax is taken off each payment. See the effect on your year with the [[canada-income-tax|Canada income tax calculator]].

### How much you could get

The basic rate is 55% of your average insurable weekly earnings, up to a maximum. For 2026 the maximum insurable earnings are $68,900 a year, so the most anyone gets is $729 a week. Your average comes from your best 14 to 22 weeks of pay (fewer weeks where unemployment is high), and a claim lasts 14 to 45 weeks. Low-income families with children may get the Family Supplement, which can raise the rate to as much as 80%.

### A worked example

Sam lives in the Niagara EI region, where the rate used for 8 November to 5 December 2026 was 6.8%. Sam was laid off after working 1,100 insurable hours in the last 52 weeks, earning $21,000 in the best 21 weeks. The claim starts on 22 November 2026.

- **Hours needed at 6.8%:** 665. Sam has 1,100, so Sam qualifies on hours.
- **Best weeks at 6.8%:** 21. Average weekly earnings: $21,000 ÷ 21 = **$1,000**.
- **Weekly benefit:** 55% × $1,000 = **$550** before tax (under the $729 cap).
- **Weeks of benefits:** **21**, from the table in the Employment Insurance Act for 1,100 hours at 6.8%.
- **Waiting week:** none, because the claim starts inside the temporary waiver.
- **Total before tax:** $550 × 21 = **$11,550**.
- **If Sam earns $300 in one week** while on the claim: half of it, $150, comes off, so EI pays $400 and Sam has $700 that week, under the 90% limit of $900.

## Common mistakes

- **Waiting for the ROE before applying.** Apply first; your employer's electronic ROE reaches Service Canada on its own. Waiting more than 4 weeks after your last day can cost you benefits.
- **Missing a report.** No report, no payment. Set a reminder every 2 weeks and keep your access code where you can find it.
- **Reporting pay after tax.** Report what you earned before deductions, for the week you did the work, not the week you were paid.
- **No job search record.** Write down every employer you contact, the date and how. A tidy [[ats-resume-checker|résumé that passes job-site screening]] and a short [[cover-letter]] make each application quicker.
- **Leaving Canada without telling Service Canada.** Any absence from Canada has to be reported.

## Questions people ask

**I'm an international student with a part-time job. Can I get EI?**
You may, if your job was insurable, you lost it through no fault of your own and you have enough hours. EI premiums come off your pay like anyone else's. If your SIN starts with a 9 you will need your permit and proof of status, and you must be ready and able to work each day.

**Do I need my ROE to apply?**
No. Apply as soon as you stop working. Electronic ROEs go straight to Service Canada, and you can see them in My Service Canada Account.

**How long does EI take to come?**
Service Canada aims to decide within 28 days, and the first payment usually arrives about 28 days after you apply if you qualify and sent everything.

**I quit my job. Can I still apply?**
You can apply, but you only get regular benefits if you had just cause, meaning you had no reasonable choice but to leave. Explain the facts fully in the application and keep any proof.
