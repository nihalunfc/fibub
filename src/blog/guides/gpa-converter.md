A grade means something only inside the system that gave it. An 8 out of 10 in India, a 3.4 in the US and a 2:1 in the UK can describe similar work, yet the numbers look nothing alike. The [[gpa-converter]] puts one result on several scales side by side, and it works out a credit-weighted average from your own course list. Once your grades are sorted, put them on your CV with the [[resume-builder]] and explain them in your [[cover-letter]]. For marks inside a single course, such as tests worth different shares, the [[weighted-grade-calculator]] is the better fit.

## What it is

Every converter has to pick a bridge between scales. This one uses the percentage as the bridge.

- **From an Indian CGPA (out of 10)**: percentage = CGPA × 9.5, the CBSE rule. Going back, CGPA = percentage ÷ 9.5.
- **From a percentage**: the percentage is used as it is.
- **From a US GPA**: the GPA is matched to the nearest letter on the US college scale, and the middle of that letter's percentage band is used. A 3.4 is nearest to B+ (3.3), whose band runs from 87 to 89, so the bridge is 88%.

From the percentage, the tool reads off the US letter, the OMSAS-style letter used for Ontario medical school applications, and the UK degree class.

For a CGPA or percentage, it also gives two direct ways to reach a US 4.0 figure:

- **Simple scale**: CGPA ÷ 10 × 4.
- **Topper method**: CGPA ÷ (the best CGPA in your batch) × 4. It compares you with the top of your own class, which suits strict grading. It shows only when you type the batch's best CGPA, and it never goes above 4.0.

**Weighted GPA** is a different job. A course with more credits counts for more:

GPA = (g₁ × c₁ + g₂ × c₂ + ...) ÷ (c₁ + c₂ + ...)

where each **g** is a course's grade in points and each **c** is its credits.

## How to use it

1. In **Convert one result**, choose what you have from **I have a**: a CGPA out of 10, a percentage or a GPA out of 4.0.
2. Type the **Value**. The results list fills in straight away.
3. For a CGPA or percentage, type the **Highest CGPA in your batch** if you know it, to see the topper method.
4. In **Weighted GPA from your courses**, pick how your grades are written: US letters or points up to 4.3, OMSAS-style letters, or points out of 10.
5. Fill one row per course with a grade and credits (the name is optional). Use **Add a course** for more rows, the **x** to remove one, **Fill an example** to see it work and **Clear** to start over.
6. Open **The tables used** to see every band behind the results.

## A worked example

Meera has a CGPA of 7.6 out of 10, and the best CGPA in her batch was 9.1.

- Percentage: 7.6 × 9.5 = **72.2%**
- Simple scale: 7.6 ÷ 10 × 4 = **3.04**
- Topper method: 7.6 ÷ 9.1 × 4 = **3.34**
- US college percentage scale: 72.2% is in the 70 to 72 band, **C-, 1.7**
- OMSAS-style table: 72.2% is in the 70 to 72 band, **B-, 2.7**
- UK: 72.2% is above 70, a **First**

The same record gives anything from 1.7 to 3.34 on a 4-point scale. That spread is the honest message: there is no single right answer, which is why you should follow the method the place you apply to asks for.

Now her last term, in points out of 10:

| Course | Grade | Credits | Grade × credits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Physics | 9 | 4 | 36 |
| Maths | 8 | 4 | 32 |
| Chemistry lab | 10 | 2 | 20 |
| English | 7 | 3 | 21 |

Weighted CGPA = (36 + 32 + 20 + 21) ÷ 13 = 109 ÷ 13 = **8.38** out of 10, and 8.3846 × 9.5 ≈ **79.7%**. A plain average of the four grades would give 8.5, because it lets the 2-credit lab count as much as a 4-credit course.

## Where it is used

- **Applications abroad**: a form asks for a GPA out of 4.0 and you studied on a 10-point scale. Give your own grade and the converted one, and say which method you used.
- **Job forms**: some employers ask for a minimum percentage or class. A rough figure helps you see whether you are near the line.
- **Planning a term**: add courses with the grades you expect and see what your weighted GPA would become.

## Common mistakes

1. **Using one method as if it were official.** None of these is. Universities and evaluation services such as WES often study the transcript course by course.
2. **Ignoring your university's own formula.** Many Indian universities publish their own rule instead of × 9.5. If yours has one, use it.
3. **Mixing scales in the weighted part.** Enter every grade in the scale you picked. A row the tool cannot read is skipped, and the result says how many were left out.
4. **Reading a UK class as a GPA.** The class is a band. The tool gives a rough US range for it, not a single figure.

## Questions people ask

**Why does a US GPA turn into a percentage in the middle of a band?** A letter grade covers a range of marks, so the middle of that range is the fairest single guess.

**Can I type A+ on the US scale?** Yes. In the weighted part, A+ counts as 4.0, the same as A.

**Why is my OMSAS-style letter higher than my US letter?** The OMSAS-style table starts its bands lower (A+ from 90, B- from 70), so the same percentage lands on a better letter.

**Is anything stored?** No, nothing is saved.
