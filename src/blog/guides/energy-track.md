When a ball rolls down a slope and up the other side, its energy changes form, but the total does not change. The [[energy-track]] tool lets you shape a track with your finger, let a ball roll and watch that exchange. It then adds friction, so you can see energy turn into heat, and a loop, so you can find the lowest start that gets the ball round. For the forces behind the motion, see [[newtons-laws]]; for friction on a slope, [[friction-ramp]]; and for a swinging version of the same exchange, [[pendulum-analyser]].

## What it is

- **Kinetic energy (KE)** is the energy of motion: KE = ½ × m × v², where m is mass in kg and v is speed in m/s.
- **Potential energy (PE)** is the energy of height: PE = m × g × h, where h is height in m and g = 9.8 m/s².
- **Heat** is energy that friction turns the motion into.

With no friction, KE + PE stays constant. A ball released from rest at height h₀ has only PE, and it returns to the same height on the other side. Its speed at any lower height h is set by the drop alone:

v = √(2g(h₀ − h))

So the shape of the track in between does not matter, and the mass cancels: a heavy ball and a light ball roll in exactly the same way. The mass only changes the energy in joules.

For a vertical loop of radius r, the ball must stay on the track at the top. Gravity alone has to be enough to keep it on its circle, which means v² ≥ g × r at the top. Using the energy at the top, v² = 2g(h − 2r), so the start height must satisfy h ≥ 2.5 × r.

## How to use it

1. Pick a **Level**. Class 9 shows the energy bars and the sum KE + PE + heat. Class 11 adds the work done by friction and, in *Loop the loop*, where and why a ball leaves the track.
2. **Shape a track** by dragging the five round handles up and down. Tap the track to put the ball there. It starts at rest.
3. Press **Play**. Press it again to pause, and **Reset** to put the ball back.
4. Set **friction** (0 is perfectly smooth) and the **mass**. Friction makes the heat bar grow.
5. For **Loop the loop**, set the starting height h and the loop radius r, then press Play. The ball either gets round, falls off part-way, or rolls back.

## A worked example

Start with a **2 kg** ball at rest **8 m** up on the left of the track.

- **At the start:** PE = 2 × 9.8 × 8 = **156.8 J**, KE = 0, total 156.8 J.
- **At 1 m high:** the drop is 7 m, so v = √(2 × 9.8 × 7) = √137.2 ≈ **11.71 m/s**. KE = ½ × 2 × 137.2 = **137.2 J** and PE = 2 × 9.8 × 1 = **19.6 J**. The two add to 156.8 J.
- **At the bottom:** v = √(2 × 9.8 × 8) ≈ **12.52 m/s**, with all 156.8 J as KE. The speeds are the same for a 1 kg or a 10 kg ball.

Now the loop with **r = 1.5 m**. The ball needs h ≥ 2.5 × 1.5 = **3.75 m**, so a start at 8 m is enough. The top of the loop is 2r = 3 m high. There, v² = 2 × 9.8 × (8 − 3) = 98, so v ≈ **9.90 m/s**, well above the minimum of √(9.8 × 1.5) ≈ **3.83 m/s**. At the top, With the same 2 kg ball, KE = ½ × 2 × 98 = **98 J** and PE = 2 × 9.8 × 3 = **58.8 J**, which again add to 156.8 J. If you lower the start to **3 m**, it is below 3.75 m and the ball does not get round.

## Where it is used

- **Roller coasters and skate parks.** Designers choose the height of each hill to give enough speed for the next loop or rise.
- **Brakes.** A car's brakes turn its kinetic energy into heat through friction.
- **Pendulums, springs and hydro power.** Each swaps one form of energy for another, with some losses.
- **Any circular path.** The same idea tells you the least speed a vehicle or object needs at the top of a loop, before friction and spin are added.

## Common mistakes

1. **Saying energy disappears when the ball stops.** The ball's motion energy has become heat in the track and ball, so the total, counting heat, is the same as at the start.
2. **Using different reference heights.** Measure every height from the same level, usually the bottom of the track, so the PE values can be compared.
3. **Expecting a heavier ball to go faster.** Without friction, speed does not depend on mass. Mass changes the joules, not the motion.
4. **Starting the ball at the height of the top of the loop (2r).** The ball would arrive at the top with no speed at all (v² = 2g(h − 2r) = 0), so it cannot follow the circle. Start at 2.5r or higher.
5. **Mixing up units.** Heights in metres, g in m/s² and energy in joules (kg·m²/s²). Keep them consistent in every line of your working.

## Questions people ask

**Why does the ball not return to its start height with friction on?** Friction takes some of its energy as heat, so the ball has less energy to climb back with and stops short.

**Does a heavier ball need a lower start for the loop?** No. In h ≥ 2.5r the mass has cancelled, so a 1 kg and a 10 kg ball need the same height. The heavier one just carries more joules.
