Tiles come in boxes, floors come in odd shapes, and some tiles always break or get cut to scraps. The [[tile-calculator]] turns all of that into one shopping list: the number of tiles, the number of boxes and an estimate of the grout, for any floor or wall you can split into rectangles. A second tab does the same for laminate, vinyl and wood planks sold by area. It suits DIY renovators, small fitters pricing a job, and anyone checking a shop's estimate. When the same room also gets new paint or paper, the [[paint-calculator]] and the [[wallpaper-calculator]] finish the list, and if the floor needs a fresh concrete base first, the [[concrete-calculator]] works out the volume.

## What it is

The count rests on three steps.

**Area.** Most rooms are rectangles, or rectangles joined together. The tool adds the area of each one (length × width) and subtracts any you mark as **Take away**, such as a bath, a shower tray or a kitchen island.

**Tiles.** Divide the area by the area of one tile:

- Exact tiles = A ÷ (l × w), where A is the area to cover and l and w are the tile's length and width.

Then add the waste share and round up once, at the end:

- Tiles to buy = round up (exact × (1 + waste ÷ 100))

Waste covers the cut pieces at the edges and the odd breakage. Diagonal and herringbone patterns leave more offcuts, so they get more.

**Grout.** Grout fills the narrow joints between tiles. Around each tile the joint length per square metre depends on the tile size, so the joint volume is:

- Joint volume = (l + w) ÷ (l × w) × gap × thickness × A

Smaller tiles mean more joints per square metre, and more grout. The tool turns that volume into kilograms with a density figure you can change.

The count uses the tile size alone and ignores the space the joints take up, so it always comes out slightly on the generous side.

## How to use it

1. Choose the **Tiles** tab, or **Planks (laminate, vinyl, wood)** for flooring sold by area.
2. Pick **Units**: metric (metres, centimetres, millimetres) or imperial (feet, inches). Changing it loads a sample job.
3. Type the length and width of the first rectangle. Press **Add a rectangle** for each extra part, and set it to **Take away** for areas you will not cover. **Remove** drops a row.
4. For tiles, type the tile length and width and pick a **Layout**. The layout fills in the **Waste and cuts (%)** box, which you can change.
5. Choose **Box sold by** (tiles per box, or area per box) and type the figure from the label. Planks always use area per box.
6. Leave **Estimate grout** ticked and set the gap, tile thickness and kilograms per litre of joint, or untick it to skip grout.
7. Read the total, the boxes and the working lines. **Reset to the example** starts again.

## A worked example

Jo is tiling a bathroom floor 2.2 m by 1.8 m, but the bath (1.7 m by 0.7 m) stays where it is. The tiles are 60 × 30 cm, laid in herringbone, sold 8 to a box. Joints are 2 mm wide and the tiles are 9 mm thick.

- Floor: 2.2 m × 1.8 m = 3.96 m²
- Bath taken away: 1.7 m × 0.7 m = 1.19 m²
- Area to tile: 3.96 − 1.19 = 2.77 m²
- One tile: 0.6 m × 0.3 m = 0.18 m²
- Exact count: 2.77 ÷ 0.18 = 15.4 tiles
- Herringbone, 15% waste: 15.4 × 1.15 = 17.7, rounded up to **18 tiles**
- Boxes of 8: 18 ÷ 8 = 2.25, so **3 boxes** (24 tiles)
- Grout: about 0.25 L of joint, which at 1.6 kg per litre is **about 0.4 kg**

The six spare tiles are worth keeping. If one cracks in a year, a tile from the same batch will match the shade.

Next door, Jo lays vinyl planks in a living room 5.2 m by 3.9 m with an alcove 1.2 m by 0.9 m. The area is 20.28 + 1.08 = 21.36 m². With 10% waste that is 23.50 m², and boxes of 1.99 m² make 11.8, so **12 boxes**.

## Where it is used

- **Bathrooms and kitchens**, for floors, splashbacks and shower walls (use wall width × height, and take away windows and niches).
- **Flooring fitters**, who need box counts for laminate, vinyl or engineered wood before ordering.
- **Patios and balconies** laid with outdoor pavers sold per square metre: use the planks tab with the area per pack.
- **Quotes and budgets**: multiply the box count by the price per box for the material cost.

## Common mistakes

- **Rounding twice.** Rounding the exact tiles up, then adding waste, then rounding again buys extra tiles for nothing. The tool rounds once, at the end.
- **Using the nominal tile size from the shop name.** A "30 × 60" tile can measure 29.7 × 59.7 cm. Type the real size from the box for the best count.
- **Too little waste in small rooms.** A tiny room has more cut edges for its size. Raise the waste share to 15 or 20%.
- **Forgetting the wall area under a vanity or behind a toilet.** Decide what will be tiled before you measure, then take away only what will truly be hidden.
- **Buying grout to the gram.** The figure is an estimate; buy the next bag size up, as the tool suggests.

## Questions people ask

**Can I mix two tile sizes on one floor?**
Run the tool once for each tile, with the rectangles that each one covers, and add the boxes.

**Does a bigger gap need much more grout?**
Yes, roughly in step. Doubling the gap from 2 mm to 4 mm doubles the grout volume.

**Why does the planks tab hide the tile boxes?**
Planks are sold by area per box, so the tool needs only the floor area, the waste share and what one box covers.
