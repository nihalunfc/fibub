Express Entry is Canada's online system for three economic immigration programs. Everyone in the pool gets a Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score out of 1,200, and the government invites the highest-ranked people in each draw. The [[crs-calculator]] adds up the points grid that IRCC (Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada) published on its CRS criteria page, modified on 3 November 2026 and checked by fibub on 10 November 2026, and shows where each point comes from. Once you have landed, the [[citizenship-days]] counter tracks your days towards citizenship, and [[canada-income-tax]] gives a first idea of tax on a Canadian salary. Rules change often: check the official source.

## What it is

The score has four parts, each with its own cap.

1. **Core human capital** (up to 570 points alone, or 530 with a spouse or partner who comes along): age, education, first and second official language, and either Canadian work experience or a high-wage job offer.
2. **Spouse or partner factors** (up to 40): their education, language and Canadian work. This part exists only if your partner comes with you and is not already a Canadian citizen or permanent resident.
3. **Skill transferability** (up to 100): points for good combinations, such as a degree together with strong language results, or foreign work together with Canadian work.
4. **Additional points** (up to 600): a provincial or territorial nomination (600), strong French (25 or 50), study in Canada (15 or 30) and a brother or sister in Canada (15).

So, in short: **total = core + spouse + transferability + additional**.

Language uses CLB, the Canadian Language Benchmark, from 1 to 12 (NCLC is the French name). Your test result in each of the four skills (reading, writing, speaking, listening) is turned into a CLB level, and each skill earns points on its own. For skill transferability, only your **lowest** skill counts.

The grid also has one choice built in. From 3 November 2026 IRCC groups Canadian work and a job offer into a Labour Market Integration factor: the tool gives you whichever of the two is higher, not both.

## How to use it

1. In **You**, choose your spouse or partner situation, type your age, pick your highest education and your years of skilled work outside Canada. Tick the box if you hold a Canadian certificate of qualification in a trade.
2. In **Languages**, choose English or French as your first official language and set the CLB level for each of the four skills. Tick the box to add results in the other language.
3. In **Work in Canada and job offer**, set your years of skilled Canadian work, whether 12 months of it were in a high-wage occupation, and any high-wage job offer.
4. If a partner is coming, a **Spouse or partner** section appears for their education, Canadian work and optional language results.
5. In **Additional points**, add study in Canada, a sibling in Canada or a nomination.
6. Read the total and the table, which lists every part, its cap and its points. **Reset to the example** brings back the starting profile.

## A worked example

Take a made-up single applicant, 33 years old, with a bachelor's degree, English results of CLB 8, 8, 7 and 8, two years of skilled work in Canada and three years of skilled work abroad. No job offer, no nomination.

**Core human capital**

- Age 33, single: 88 points.
- Bachelor's degree: 120.
- English: 23 + 23 + 17 + 23 = 86 (CLB 8 gives 23 per skill, CLB 7 gives 17).
- Canadian work, 2 years: 53.
- Core: 88 + 120 + 86 + 53 = **347**.

**Skill transferability**

- Education with language: the lowest skill is CLB 7, so 13. Education with Canadian work (2 years): 25. Together 38 (cap 50).
- Foreign work (3 years) with language: 25. Foreign work with Canadian work: 50. Together 75, capped at **50**.
- Transferability: 38 + 50 = **88** (cap 100).

**Total: 347 + 88 = 435 points.**

Now try three changes, one at a time, in the tool:

- **CLB 9 in all four skills**: English rises to 4 × 31 = 124 (+38), and education with language rises from 13 to 25, which fills transferability to its 100 cap (+12). Total **485**.
- **French at NCLC 7 in all four skills as a second language**: 4 × 3 = 12 second-language points, plus 50 additional points for French with English at CLB 5 or higher. Total **497**.
- **A job offer at 1.5 times the median wage** (70 points): it beats the 53 for Canadian work, so the factor becomes 70 (+17). Total **452**.

## Where it is used

- **Planning a language retest**: see what one more CLB level in your weakest skill adds.
- **Couples**: run the profile with each partner as the main applicant and compare.
- **Settlement workers**: explain the parts of the score with a clear table.

## Common mistakes

1. **Adding a partner who is a Canadian citizen or PR.** They are not an accompanying spouse; choose that option and you are scored as single.
2. **Typing test scores instead of CLB levels.** Convert your IELTS, CELPIP, PTE, TEF or TCF results to CLB with IRCC's tables first.
3. **Expecting points for both a job offer and Canadian work.** You get the higher of the two.
4. **Treating the result as a promise.** Draw cut-offs and the grid itself change. Check the official source and IRCC's own tool.

## Questions people ask

**Why do I lose points when my partner comes with me?** Some points move from your own factors to theirs. If your partner has little education, language or Canadian work, the move can lower your total.

**Does age keep costing points?** Yes, the age points fall each year after 30 and reach zero at 45. A birthday can change your score while you wait in the pool.

**Is anything saved?** No, and no ID number is asked for.
