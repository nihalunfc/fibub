If you have a home, car or personal loan, you may have wondered whether paying a bit extra is worth it. The [[loan-payoff-planner]] answers that for your own loan. You enter what you owe, the rate and the time left, then try out one-off lump sums or regular top-ups and see the interest saved and the new end date. This guide is general education, not advice for your situation, and loan rules vary, so check with your lender before you pay extra. To find spare money each month, start with the [[budget-calculator]]; if you have several debts, the [[debt-snowball|debt payoff tool]] helps you choose which to clear first.

## What it is

A normal loan is paid back in equal monthly payments. In India this payment is usually called the **EMI** (equated monthly instalment). Each payment has two parts:

- **interest**, which is the monthly rate times what you still owe;
- **principal**, the rest of the payment, which reduces what you owe.

The monthly payment comes from this formula:

**EMI = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ ÷ ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1)**

Here **P** is the amount owed, **r** is the yearly rate divided by 12 (as a decimal, so 7% a year is 0.07 ÷ 12), and **n** is the number of monthly payments left.

Because interest is charged on the balance, early payments are mostly interest. A **prepayment** is money paid on top of the EMI. It goes straight to the balance, so every later month carries a little less interest. The saving builds up month after month, which is why extra money paid early does more than the same money paid late.

After a prepayment, a lender can do one of two things. It can keep your payment the same so the loan ends sooner, or keep the end date and lower your payment. The first saves more interest, because the balance falls faster.

## How to use it

1. In **Your loan**, pick your currency and enter the **amount still owed**, the **interest rate** (% a year) and the **time left** in years and months. Set **Next payment** to the month your next payment is due.
2. The planner works out your monthly payment and the total interest if you change nothing. If you really pay a different amount, type it into **Monthly payment (EMI)**.
3. Type a sum in **What does one extra payment do?** to see what a single extra payment next month would save, and how much interest each unit of currency you prepay saves.
4. Look at **Ready-made plans**. It works out six common approaches for your loan, such as paying 10% more every month or 10% of the loan once now. Press **Use** on one to copy it into your plan.
5. In **Your prepayment plan**, add rows with **+ Add a prepayment**. Each row has an amount, how often (one time, every month, every 3, 6 or 12 months, or every chosen number of months), a start month and an optional end month. Remove a row with ×.
6. Under **After each prepayment**, pick **Finish sooner** or **Lower my EMI**.
7. Read the results: interest saved, how much sooner you finish (or your new monthly payment), last payment date and total extra paid. The chart and the **Year by year** table compare the two paths, and you can download the full schedule as a CSV file.

## A worked example

Take a car loan of **25,000** (any currency) at **7% a year** with **5 years** (60 months) left.

- Monthly payment: about **495.03**
- Total interest if nothing changes: about **4,701.80**

**Test 1: one extra 2,000 next month.** The planner shows about **780.74** of interest saved, and the loan ends **5 months** sooner. Each 1 prepaid saves about 0.39 in interest.

**Test 2: an extra 100 every month from the first payment, Finish sooner.** The loan is paid off in **49 months** (4 years 1 month) instead of 60, so **11 months** sooner. Total extra paid is **4,800**, and the interest falls to about **3,762.80**, a saving of about **938.99**, or roughly **20%** of the original interest.

Notice that Test 2 saves more in total, but Test 1 saves more for each unit prepaid (0.39 against about 0.20). Money paid at the very start has the longest time to cut interest.

## Where it is used

- **Home loans.** A yearly bonus paid against a 20-year loan can take years off it. Use the planner with a yearly prepayment starting in your bonus month.
- **Comparing with saving.** If your savings earn less than your loan rate, prepaying often makes sense; if they earn more, it may not. The planner gives you the loan side of that comparison.
- **Talking to your lender.** The CSV schedule lets you check your lender's statement month by month.

## Common mistakes

- **Ignoring prepayment fees or limits.** Some loans charge for paying early or cap extra payments. Check with your lender first.
- **Assuming the lender shortens the term.** Many lower the payment instead unless you ask. Tell them which you want, and match the planner's setting.
- **Emptying your emergency fund.** Money paid into a loan is hard to get back. Keep some cash aside.
- **Forgetting a floating rate changes.** The planner assumes a fixed rate. Rerun it when your rate moves.

## Questions people ask

**Is it better to prepay early in the loan or late?** Early. The balance is largest at the start, so each extra payment removes more future interest.

**Should I choose Finish sooner or Lower my EMI?** Finish sooner saves more interest. Lower my EMI frees up money each month. Both are fair choices; it depends on what you need.

**Does it work for an interest-only loan or a credit card?** Not well. It is built for loans with a fixed monthly payment that covers interest and principal. For credit cards and several debts, try the debt payoff tool.
