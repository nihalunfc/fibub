A resume has two readers: software that files your words into boxes, and a busy person who gives it a few seconds. The resume builder turns your details into one clean page for both, scores it as you type and hands you a PDF or Word file. It suits students, career changers and anyone whose old file looks good but reads badly. If you already have a resume, run it through the [[ats-resume-checker]] first, and when the resume is done, write the letter that goes with it in the [[cover-letter]] builder.

## What it is

Most large employers collect applications through an applicant tracking system, or ATS: a database for job applications. It pulls the plain text out of your file, guesses which line is your name, a job title or a date, and lets recruiters search for words from the job ad.

So layout matters more than looks. Two columns can be read straight across, mixing a skill into a job title, and icons can turn into question marks. The builder writes one column, plain section names, contact details in the body and simple bullets.

The ATS score is a checklist turned into a number. Each check is worth some points: readable text, an email and phone near the top, headings such as Experience, Education and Skills, a date range for every job, at least five bullets, numbers in roughly a third of them, bullets that open with an action verb, and a sensible length. The score is

score = points earned ÷ points possible × 100, rounded

Paste a job ad and one more check joins in. The tool picks the terms that the ad repeats, or that name a known tool, and counts how many appear in your resume. That check carries 12 points, so it moves the score a lot.

## How to use it

1. Press **Fill in an example** once to see a finished resume, then **Start over** when you are ready to type your own.
2. Open each card in turn: Contact details, Summary, Skills, Experience, Projects, Education, Certifications and More sections. Add jobs with **+ Add a job** and use the arrows to reorder them. Tick **I work here now** for your current role.
3. Watch the ATS score beside the form. Under it are the three changes that would earn the most points.
4. Open **Match it to a job ad**, paste the advert and read the ticks and crosses. Add the missing terms only where they are true.
5. In **Layout and order**, pick Modern or Classic, Letter or A4, and move your strongest section up.
6. Press **Download PDF** or **Download Word**. To carry on later, tick **Remember my resume on this device** or press **Save a backup file**.

## A worked example

Jordan is a shift supervisor at a regional grocery store. Jordan fills in the contact card (but no link), a two-line summary, two skill lines, two jobs with 8 bullets in total and one school.

- Without the link, the checks earn 91 of 99 points. 91 ÷ 99 × 100 = 91.9, so the score shows **92**.
- The two misses are the LinkedIn link (worth 2 points) and length (worth 6 points): the page has only 115 words.
- Jordan adds a LinkedIn address. Now 93 of 99 points: 93 ÷ 99 × 100 = 93.9, shown as **94**.

Next Jordan pastes a job ad for a store supervisor that mentions stock control, Excel and, twice, inventory counts. The keyword check finds 4 key terms. Two match (stock control, Excel) and two are missing (inventory, inventory counts). That is 2 ÷ 4 = 50%, which earns half of the 12 points. The total is now 99 of 111 points: 99 ÷ 111 × 100 = 89.2, shown as **89**. The score went down because a new check arrived, not because the resume got worse.

Jordan really does run stock counts, so a new bullet goes in: "Managed monthly inventory counts across 12 aisles." All 4 terms now match (100%), giving 105 of 111 points: 105 ÷ 111 × 100 = 94.6, shown as **95**. Only length is still missing.

## Where it is used

- **First jobs and internships.** Students can move Education or Projects above Experience and still get a page that reads well.
- **Career changers.** A teacher moving into training, or a nurse moving into health data, can use the job-ad match to find the words the new field uses for skills they already have.
- **Careers advisers and volunteers.** Helping someone at a library or community centre is easier when the score points to the next fix.

## Common mistakes

- **Copying every word from the job ad.** The match counts terms, but a recruiter reads sentences. Add only what you can talk about in an interview.
- **Bullets that describe duties, not results.** "Responsible for stock" scores lower than "Cut stock write-offs by 18%". Start with a verb and add a number when you have one.
- **Chasing 100.** A score in the 80s or 90s already means the file is easy to read. Time is better spent on the content.
- **Forgetting the dates.** Every job needs a start and an end, or the current-job tick. Mixed date styles make the timeline harder to follow.

## Questions people ask

**Does a higher score mean I will get an interview?** No. It means the file is easy for software and people to read. The interview depends on your experience and how well it fits the job.

**Why did my score drop when I pasted a job ad?** The job-ad match is an extra check. Until your resume uses the advert's key terms, those new points are only partly earned.

**Can I make different versions for different jobs?** Yes. Save a backup file for each version, give each file a clear name, and open the one you need with **Open a backup file**.
