A **kagome lattice** is a net of corner-sharing triangles, named after a Japanese basket weave. Electrons on it can show flat bands and unusual magnetism, so physicists hunt for kagome metals that also superconduct. In a **breathing** kagome lattice the triangles alternate in size, small, large, small. A 2023 paper in *Scientific Reports* showed that three osmium compounds built this way, ScOs₂, YOs₂ and LuOs₂, are superconductors, and characterised them mainly through their **specific heat**, the heat it takes to warm a mole by one kelvin. This article checks their numbers with the [[heat-capacity-fitter]] and the [[superconductor-calculator]]. To see the hexagonal crystal's reciprocal lattice and Brillouin zone, open the [[brillouin-zone]].

## The paper in brief

The authors arc-melted the three compounds, which crystallise as hexagonal **Laves phases** (the MgZn₂ type, where the osmium atoms form the breathing kagome layers), and measured resistance, magnetisation and heat capacity down to about 2 K. All three superconduct: **ScOs₂ at 5.36 K, YOs₂ at 4.55 K and LuOs₂ at 3.47 K** (from the heat-capacity jump).

Above the transition, the heat capacity of a metal at low temperature is the sum of two parts: **γT** from the electrons and **βT³** from the lattice vibrations (phonons). Plotting C/T against T² gives a straight line with intercept γ, the **Sommerfeld coefficient**, and slope β. From β follows the **Debye temperature** θ_D, a measure of how stiff the lattice is: θ_D = (12π⁴nR / 5β)^(1/3), where n is the number of atoms per formula unit (3 here) and R the gas constant. The paper reports γ = 12.7, 11.1 and 14.5 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻², β = 0.18, 0.24 and 0.29 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻⁴ and θ_D = 319, 291 and 270 K. The jump in heat capacity at T_c, the **electron–phonon coupling** λ worked out from T_c and θ_D, and the critical fields all point to **weakly coupled, conventional superconductivity**.

## Checked with the tools

**Step 1: θ_D from β.** The tool's `thetaFromBeta` with n = 3 turns the paper's β into θ_D = **318.8, 289.6 and 271.9 K**, against the stated 319, 291 and 270 K. The small gaps come from β being printed with two figures (0.18 could be anything from 0.175 to 0.185, which alone spans 316 to 322 K). Going the other way, the stated θ_D values need β = 0.1796, 0.2366 and 0.2963 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻⁴, all of which round to the printed β.

**Step 2: a fit on simulated data.** The measured curves are only in the paper's figures, so we **simulated** the normal state of each compound from its γ and θ_D, using the full Debye model (not just T³), from just above T_c up to 10 K in 0.25 K steps with 1 % scatter (**our assumption**). The heat capacity fitter's low-temperature line then gives, for ScOs₂, γ = 12.75 ± 0.16 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻² and θ_D = 318.7 ± 1.4 K; for YOs₂, 11.16 ± 0.16 and 291.8 ± 1.0 K; for LuOs₂, 14.52 ± 0.17 and 270.2 ± 0.9 K. All are within about one standard error of the inputs (Figure 1). LuOs₂, with the lowest T_c, gets the most normal-state points and the tightest θ_D.

**Step 3: how high may the line go?** The T³ law is a low-temperature limit. Fitting exact Debye curves at the paper's θ_D from 1 K upward, the line returns the true θ_D to within 0.1 K up to 20 K, but by 40 K it overstates it by 7 to 13 K and by 60 K by 39 to 57 K (Figure 2). Real solids depart from T³ sooner than the Debye model, so a fit range ending near 10 K, as here, is the safe choice.

**Step 4: coupling and critical fields.** Two checks use simple formulas rather than a fibub tool: the inverted McMillan formula with μ* = 0.13 and the specific-heat T_c gives λ = **0.641, 0.628 and 0.594** (paper 0.64, 0.63, 0.59), and N(E_F) = γ / (2.357(1 + λ)) gives **3.29, 2.89 and 3.87 states eV⁻¹ per formula unit** (paper 3.30, 2.90, 3.86). The jump ΔC/γT_c is 1.06, 0.85 and 0.99 times the BCS value of 1.43, consistent with weak coupling. Then the [[superconductor-calculator]], given the paper's penetration depth λ_GL and coherence length ξ_GL, returns the Ginzburg–Landau fields:

| ScOs₂ / YOs₂ / LuOs₂ | Paper | Superconductor calculator |
|---|---|---|
| κ = λ/ξ | 13 / 20 / 23 | 13.4 / 20.2 / 23.2 |
| Thermodynamic field μ0Hc | 136 / 78 / 50 mT | 136.1 / 78.8 / 50.5 mT |
| Lower critical field Hc1 | 187 / 83 / 48 Oe | 18.6 / 8.3 / 4.8 mT (186 / 83 / 48 Oe) |
| Upper critical field μ0Hc2 | 2.58 / 2.23 / 1.64 T | 2.577 / 2.248 / 1.655 T |
| Pauli limit (BCS gap) | not given | 9.95 / 8.45 / 6.44 T |

Everything agrees to the rounding of λ_GL and ξ_GL. The Pauli limit, the field at which electron spins alone would break the pairs, is about four times Hc2 in each compound, so the upper critical field is set by the orbital motion of the electrons, as expected for a conventional superconductor.

## How we made the graphs

The charts and numbers come from `scripts/blog-figures/breathing-kagome-laves-ros2-superconductors-specific-heat-debye-temperature.mjs` in fibub's public code. It calls `debyeC`, `debyeBeta`, `thetaFromBeta` and `lowT` from `src/lib/sci/phheat.ts`, the maths behind the [[heat-capacity-fitter]], and `glFields` from `src/lib/sci/phsc.ts`, behind the [[superconductor-calculator]]. The McMillan and N(E_F) formulas are written out in the script as hand checks.

All γ, β, θ_D, T_c, λ_GL, ξ_GL and field values come from the paper's text. The simulated heat-capacity points, their 0.25 K spacing, the 10 K upper limit and the 1 % scatter are **our assumptions**; the curves are not the paper's measurements. For the Pauli limit we used the BCS gap 1.764 k_BT_c.

## How we test the maths

On every change to fibub, `tests/sci.mjs` runs `tests/sci-tools/heat-capacity-fitter.mjs`, which pins the fitter against SciPy:

- The Debye heat capacity at θ/30, θ/3, θ and 3.3θ must match SciPy's numerical integral to 10⁻⁹, and the Debye integral to infinity must equal 4π⁴/15.
- On a made-up copper-like data set, the Debye-plus-γ fit must give θ = 340.24 K with the same standard error as SciPy's `curve_fit`, and the C/T-against-T² line up to 15 K must give θ_D = 341.31 K as NumPy does.
- New with this article: β = 0.18, 0.24 and 0.29 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻⁴ with n = 3 must give θ_D = 318.8, 289.6 and 271.9 K, and the five-point ScOs₂ data set below must give γ = 12.70 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻² and θ_D = 319.0 K.

## Check it yourself

On the [[heat-capacity-fitter]]:

## What researchers can use it for

- **Turn a printed β into θ_D**, or back, with the right number of atoms per formula unit, using the [[heat-capacity-fitter]]; a wrong n is the most common slip.
- **Choose the fit range**: simulate your compound's Debye curve and see where the C/T against T² line stops being straight.
- **Cross-check critical fields**: from λ and ξ, the [[superconductor-calculator]] gives Hc, Hc1 and Hc2 together, so a table can be tested for internal consistency in a minute.
- **Screen for unconventional pairing**: an Hc2 near or above the Pauli limit, or a jump far above 1.43, flags a sample worth a closer look.
- **Teach** the electron and phonon parts of a metal's heat capacity with a real, recent superconductor family.

## The fine print

- Steps 2 and 3 use **simulated** data from the paper's stated values; the measured points are only in its figures.
- The paper does not state its fit range; ours (just above T_c to 10 K) is an assumption.
- The McMillan λ and N(E_F) are hand formulas in our script, not part of a fibub tool.
- The paper was published in 2023; the compounds' kagome band physics is beyond what these tools check.
