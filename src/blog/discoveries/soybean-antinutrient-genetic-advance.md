Soybean is a cheap protein for people and for poultry, but the seed also carries **phytate**, which locks up iron and zinc, and **trypsin inhibitors**, which block protein digestion unless the meal is heated. A 2025 paper in *Discover Agriculture* screened 308 Ugandan soybean genotypes for both, and reported the numbers that plant breeders live by: heritability, coefficients of variation and **genetic advance**, the gain expected from one round of selection. This article recomputes those figures with the [[breeders-equation]], recovers the trait mean they imply, and shows two places where the table does not agree with itself. For the trials behind such numbers, see the [[field-trial-designer]] and the [[yield-trial-anova]].

## The paper in brief

The authors grew 308 genotypes, measured seed yield, phytate and total trypsin inhibitors, and found wide variation: phytate from **14.8 to 6,928.8 mg/kg** and trypsin inhibitors from **14.7 to 1,534.8 mg/kg**. Three genotypes combined good yield with low levels of both.

Their genetic parameters are the heart of the paper. **Broad-sense heritability** (the share of the variation between genotypes that is genetic, so passed on) was **0.68 for phytate**, **0.84 for trypsin inhibitors** and only **0.08 for seed yield**. The **phenotypic coefficient of variation** (PCV, the spread as a percentage of the mean) was 203.1 % for phytate against a **genotypic** coefficient (GCV) of 137.6 %; for trypsin inhibitors, 34.6 % and 29.2 %. The reported **genetic advance** was **1,667.6 mg/kg** for phytate and **504.3 mg/kg** for trypsin inhibitors. The conclusion: both anti-nutrients should respond strongly to selection, while yield will not, in this germplasm.

## Checked with the tools

This is a **recomputation from the published parameters**; we do not have the paper's data. A genetic advance is the breeder's equation with truncation selection:

> R = i × h² × σP, with i the selection intensity, h² the heritability and σP the phenotypic standard deviation.

The convention behind a published "genetic advance" is that the best 5 % are kept. The tool's own `intensity` function gives **i = 2.0627** for p = 5 % (the familiar k = 2.06), from the standard normal cut-off z = 1.6449.

**Step 1: what response to expect.** Figure 1 runs one round of selection for each of the paper's three heritabilities, with the response in phenotypic standard deviations so it does not depend on units. At 5 % kept it is **1.73 SD** for trypsin inhibitors, **1.40 SD** for phytate and **0.17 SD** for seed yield. Selecting on yield in this collection moves the mean about **one eighth** as far as selecting on phytate, which is the paper's point, now with a number on it.

**Step 2: recover the mean.** The paper gives the advance in mg/kg but not the trait means, and σP is not printed either. It can be worked back out, because σP = PCV × mean ÷ 100. Putting the paper's advance into R = i h² σP and solving for the mean gives **585 mg/kg of phytate** and **841 mg/kg of trypsin inhibitors**. Both sit sensibly inside the reported ranges, which is a good sign that we have read the authors' convention correctly: feeding σP = 2.031 × 585.4 = 1,188.90 mg/kg and h² = 0.68 back into the tool returns **R = 1,667.60 mg/kg**, the published figure.

**Step 3: the two routes disagree.** The same advance is often written R = i × h × σG, using the genotypic standard deviation; as a percentage of the mean that is i √h² GCV instead of i h² PCV. The two are identical only when **h² = (GCV ÷ PCV)²**. For phytate that squared ratio is **0.459**, not the reported 0.68; for trypsin inhibitors it is **0.712**, not 0.84. Figure 2 shows how far apart the two routes then sit: at h² = 0.68 one gives 285 % of the mean and the other 234 %, so the implied mean moves from 585 to **712 mg/kg** depending on which formula produced the published figure.

Nothing here is a mistake we can prove: a broad-sense heritability estimated from variance components on an entry-mean basis legitimately differs from (GCV/PCV)², which is a single-plot ratio. But a reader cannot tell which was done, and the two give different advances. Printing σP and σG, or the variance components, settles it.

**Step 4: the advance that cannot happen.** Our phytate numbers say the expected gain, 1,668 mg/kg, is **2.8 times the mean** of 585 mg/kg. Selection here runs downwards, so the new mean is 585 − 1,668 = **−1,082 mg/kg**. Phytate cannot be negative. The breeder's equation is a straight-line prediction built on a normal distribution, and a trait with a coefficient of variation of 203 % and a range from 14.8 to 6,928.8 mg/kg is nothing like normal. For a trait like this the honest route is to analyse the logarithm of the concentration, where the advance comes out as a percentage reduction, or to quote the advance for one standard deviation of selection rather than for a 5 % tail.

## How we made the graphs

The charts and every number come from `scripts/blog-figures/soybean-antinutrient-genetic-advance.mjs` in fibub's public code. It calls `intensity` and `response` from `src/lib/sci/breedeq.ts`, the maths behind the [[breeders-equation]].

The heritabilities, coefficients of variation, genetic advances and trait ranges are the paper's published values. The **5 % selected** is **our assumption**, the convention behind a published genetic advance; at 2 % the phytate advance would be 1,957 mg/kg instead. The implied means are **our calculation**, not the paper's.

## How we test the maths

On every change to fibub, `tests/sci.mjs` runs `tests/sci-tools/breeders-equation.mjs`, which pins the tool against textbook values:

- The selection intensity for the best 5 % must be 2.0627 and for the best 20 % 1.3998, as the standard normal gives.
- Response and selection differential must satisfy R = h² S exactly, and halve when only one parent is selected.
- Heritability from an offspring-on-mid-parent regression must match an independent least-squares fit, with its standard error and confidence interval.
- New with this article: with h² = 0.68, σP = 1,188.90 mg/kg and 5 % kept, the response must be 1,667.60 mg/kg with a selection differential of 2,452.36, and with h² = 0.84 and σP = 291.05 mg/kg the response must be 504.30 mg/kg.

## Check it yourself

On the [[breeders-equation]], in the *Response to selection* card:

1. Leave *Selection given as* on **Proportion selected**.
2. Type *Heritability h² (0 to 1)* **0.68**, *Proportion selected (%)* **5**, *Phenotypic SD σP* **1188.90**.
3. Leave *Parents selected* on **Both parents**. The card reads a selection differential S of **2,452.36 mg/kg**, a selection intensity i of **2.0627** and a response **R of 1,667.60 mg/kg**: the paper's figure.
4. Put **585.4** in *Population mean (optional)*. The response reads **284.9 % of the mean**. Selection for less phytate subtracts it, so the new mean would be −1,082 mg/kg. That is the warning sign.
5. Change *Heritability h²* to **0.459**, the value the two coefficients of variation imply. The response falls to **1,125.6 mg/kg**.
6. Now the trypsin inhibitors: h² **0.84**, 5 %, σP **291.05**. The response reads **504.30 mg/kg**, 59.95 % of an 841 mg/kg mean, which is large but possible.

## What researchers can use it for

- **Audit a variability table before citing it.** Three numbers (h², PCV, genetic advance) must fit together; the tool shows in seconds whether they do.
- **Recover a missing standard deviation** from a published advance, so you can compare two papers that report different parameters.
- **Plan a selection round:** how many of your lines to keep for a target gain, with the response in units you measure ([[field-trial-designer]] for the layout, [[yield-trial-anova]] for the analysis).
- **Spot predictions out of range.** If the predicted new mean is negative or beyond the biological limit, switch to a log scale or a per-SD gain.
- **Teach selection intensity.** Figure 1 makes plain that heritability sets the ceiling and intensity only moves you up to it.

## The fine print

- We did not re-analyse the paper's data; everything here follows from its published parameters.
- The 5 % selection convention is our reading. If the authors used another intensity, every implied mean scales with it.
- Broad-sense heritability includes dominance and epistasis, so the response realised by a breeder working with seed from open-pollinated plants will be smaller than these figures.
- The trait means we recover are a reconstruction, and the two routes to them differ by 22 %; the paper's own tables will give the real means.
- Phytate and trypsin inhibitors matter for nutrition, but nothing here is dietary advice: heat treatment and processing change both.
