Famciclovir is a **prodrug**: the cat swallows famciclovir, and the gut and liver turn it into **penciclovir**, the molecule that acts against feline herpesvirus. Vets give a wide range of doses, and the usual assumption is that twice the dose gives twice the drug in the blood. A 2025 study in *Frontiers in Veterinary Science* tested that in 60 healthy cats and found it is not so: the share of each dose that reaches the blood as penciclovir falls from about two thirds to under a fifth as the dose rises. This article recomputes those numbers and rebuilds the paper's curves with the [[pk-simulator]]. To get the same parameters from your own measured concentrations, use [[nca-pk]]; for the prodrug-to-drug mass conversion, the [[molar-mass-calculator]].

## The paper in brief

Groups of ten cats received one oral famciclovir dose of 15.625, 31.25, 62.5 or 93.75 mg/kg. Another group received penciclovir straight into a vein (10 mg/kg over an hour), which gives the 100 % reference. A last group took 62.5 mg/kg every 12 hours for 14 days. Blood was sampled for up to 36 hours and analysed by **non-compartmental analysis (NCA)**: the area under the concentration curve (AUC), the peak (Cmax) and its time (Tmax), and the **apparent** clearance and volume (CL/F and V/F; "apparent" because the fraction absorbed, F, is not known separately).

The headline: a sixfold rise in dose only raised the peak from 2170 to 3290 ng/mL and the AUC from about 21,000 to 35,000 h·ng/mL. The **absolute bioavailability**, the share of the dose that reaches the blood as penciclovir, fell from 67 % to 18 %. Over 14 days of repeated dosing the drug hardly built up. The authors suggest the uptake or conversion step saturates at higher doses. This is general veterinary pharmacology, not dosing advice.

## Checked with the tools

Everything here is a **recomputation or simulation from the group means stated in the paper**. We did not have any cat's measured concentrations.

**Step 1: bioavailability, with the prodrug corrected.** The dose is weighed as famciclovir, but the blood level is measured as penciclovir. One famciclovir molecule gives one penciclovir molecule, so each milligram of famciclovir can give at most 253.26 ÷ 321.34 = **0.7882 mg** of penciclovir (the molar masses from the molar mass calculator's maths). Dividing each group's AUC per milligram of penciclovir-equivalent by the intravenous AUC per milligram gives **67.1, 33.9, 26.4 and 18.4 %**, the paper's 67.12, 33.94, 26.45 and 18.37 % to rounding. Without the molar correction the four values would all be about a fifth too low, so this tells us how the paper defined its bioavailability.

**Step 2: the intravenous reference.** A one-compartment model with the paper's CL 1.21 L/h and volume 3.33 L has a half-life of **1.91 hours** (paper: 2.03 from the terminal slope). The AUC times the clearance gives the amount actually infused: **31.2 mg**, so the cats in that group averaged about **3.1 kg** (our inference, within the paper's 1 to 4 kg). Infusing 31.2 mg over an hour into that model peaks at **7868 ng/mL**, against a measured 7572.

**Step 3: rebuilding the single-dose curves.** For each oral group we used its mean CL/F and Vz/F, worked out the amount from AUC × CL/F, and chose the absorption rate (ka, **our assumption**: the paper does not report one) so the simulated peak falls at the reported Tmax. Figure 1 shows the four curves. The simulated peaks come out at **2275, 2253, 2853 and 3019 ng/mL**, against reported 2170, 2340, 2890 and 3290: within 1 to 8 %. That is a good sign that the paper's summary numbers fit together. The implied doses also give sensible body weights of 2.5 to 3.2 kg.

Look at the curves and the non-linearity is plain: the 15.625 and 31.25 mg/kg curves almost lie on top of each other. The AUC per mg/kg of famciclovir falls from **1366 to 691, 538 and 374** h·ng/mL across the four doses.

**Step 4: does a linear model explain the repeated dosing?** We used the steady-state CL/F (5.91 L/h) and Vss/F (46.71 L), an amount of 149 mg from AUC × CL/F, and a ka of 0.73 per hour to put the day-1 peak at 2.9 hours (Figure 2). By design the model gets the average steady-state level right (2100 ng/mL). It also gets the steady-state peak and trough close: **2979 and 1081 ng/mL**, against reported 3197 and 1047. But it misses day 1. It predicts a first peak of **2209 ng/mL** (reported 3072) and an accumulation ratio of **1.36** (reported 1.09). In plain words, on the first day the cats already had more penciclovir than a simple linear drug would give, and the extra doses added less than expected. That fits the paper's conclusion that this drug does not behave linearly, though a two-phase decline could also explain part of the gap; the summary values cannot tell these apart.

## How we made the graphs

The charts and every number come from `scripts/blog-figures/penciclovir-cats-famciclovir-nonlinear-pharmacokinetics-simulated.mjs` in fibub's public code. It calls `simulate` and `summarise` from `src/lib/sci/pksim.ts`, the maths of the [[pk-simulator]], and `molarMass` and `parseFormula` from `src/lib/sci/chem.ts`, the maths of the [[molar-mass-calculator]].

The doses, clearances, volumes, Tmax, Cmax and AUC values come from the paper's tables (group means). The one-compartment model and the absorption rates (0.30, 0.45, 0.48 and 0.54 per hour for the single doses, 0.73 for the repeated doses, each chosen to match a reported Tmax) are **our assumptions**. The curves are **simulated**, not measured.

## How we test the maths

On every change to fibub, `tests/sci.mjs` runs `tests/sci-tools/pk-simulator.mjs`:

- The general solution was checked against SciPy's ODE solver on 24 random regimens (one and two compartments, bolus, infusion and oral) to within 3 × 10⁻¹¹.
- For an invented drug (CL 5 L/h, V 50 L, 250 mg every 12 hours, ka 1 per hour, F 0.8), the first peak must be **3.097 mg/L** at **2.558 h**, the steady-state peak **4.612 mg/L** and the accumulation ratio **1.503**.
- New with this article: the 1-hour infusion of 31.25 mg with CL 1.21 L/h and V 3.33 L must peak at **7.868 mg/L**, and the repeated-dose model (149 mg every 12 hours, CL 5.91 L/h, V 46.71 L, ka 0.73 per hour) must give a steady-state peak of **2.979 mg/L** and an accumulation ratio of **1.361**, both checked against closed-form formulas written out in the test.

## Check it yourself

On the [[pk-simulator]]:

1. Set *Model* to **One compartment**, *Clearance CL (L/h)* to **5.91** and *Volume V (L)* to **46.71**.
2. Under *Regimen to try*, choose *Route* **By mouth (first-order absorption)**, *Amount each time (mg)* **149**, *Interval τ (h)* **12**, *Number of administrations* **28**, *Absorption rate ka (1/h)* **0.73** and *Fraction absorbed F (0 to 1)* **1** (the clearance and volume are already "apparent", divided by F).
3. The headline reads about **Cmax 2.979 mg/L, Cmin 1.082 mg/L** at steady state (1 mg/L = 1000 ng/mL). *Average at steady state* shows **2.101 mg/L**.
4. *Accumulation ratio* shows **1.361**; the paper measured 1.09. *First administration* shows a peak of **2.209 mg/L** at **2.9 h**, against a reported 3.072.
5. Switch *Route* to **IV infusion**, set *Clearance CL (L/h)* to **1.21**, *Volume V (L)* to **3.33**, *Amount each time (mg)* to **31.25**, *Infusion duration (h)* to **1** and *Number of administrations* to **1**: the first peak is about **7.868 mg/L**, near the measured 7.572.

## What researchers can use it for

- **Sanity-check a PK table**: do the reported CL/F, V/F, Tmax and Cmax describe one consistent curve? Rebuild it in the [[pk-simulator]] and see.
- **Spot non-linearity early**: compare AUC per mg across doses, and a linear model's accumulation ratio against the measured one.
- **Correct prodrug doses** to the active molecule with the [[molar-mass-calculator]] before working out bioavailability.
- **Analyse your own concentration data** with [[nca-pk]] (AUC, λz, half-life, CL/F), then feed the results into the simulator.
- **Teach** why "apparent" clearance changes with dose when absorption saturates, with curves students can move.

## The fine print

- All results are recomputed or simulated from group means. Means of ratios (such as CL/F ÷ V/F) are not the same as ratios of means, which is part of why our half-lives differ from the paper's.
- The one-compartment model and the absorption rates are our assumptions; the paper used model-free NCA.
- The paper reports clearances and volumes per cat (L/h and L), not per kilogram; our body weights are inferences.
- The pk-simulator is a teaching tool for invented drugs. Nothing here is a dose for any animal: dosing is a decision for a veterinarian.
