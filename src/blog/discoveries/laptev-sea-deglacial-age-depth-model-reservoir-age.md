Every radiocarbon date on a marine shell or foraminifer carries a built-in error: the sea water the animal lived in was already old. Subtracting the wrong reservoir age shifts a whole core's chronology, and the shift is invisible inside the core — every date moves together. A 2024 paper in *Climate of the Past* measures that offset for the Laptev Sea by tying a sediment core's authigenic ¹⁰Be/⁹Be record to ice-core ¹⁰Be, and finds the local correction should be **ΔR = +345 ± 60 ¹⁴C years** instead of the −110 ± 28 used before. This article uses fibub's [[age-depth-model]] builder to ask the question a user of that chronology has to ask: is a 455-year shift bigger or smaller than the age model's own uncertainty? Afterwards, line records up with the [[proxy-stacker]] and put them against orbital forcing with the [[insolation-calculator]].

## The paper in brief

Core PS2458-4 is an 8 m core from 983 m of water on the Laptev Sea slope, raised in 1994. The authors add seven AMS ¹⁴C dates on mixed benthic foraminifera to published dates on foraminifera and bivalves, and calibrate with Marine20 in OxCal 4.4. Separately they measure authigenic **¹⁰Be/⁹Be** in 54 samples covering 8.5 to 11.5 kyr BP. Beryllium-10 is made in the atmosphere at a rate that swings with solar activity, and those swings are recorded in Greenland and Antarctic ice, so matching the sediment record to a stacked WAIS Divide and GISP2 ¹⁰Be curve dates the sediment without radiocarbon at all.

Lining the two up gives a local offset of **ΔR = +345 ± 60 ¹⁴C years**, which with a global average marine reservoir age of 503 ± 63 ¹⁴C years makes a **local reservoir age of 848 ± 90 ¹⁴C years** — far older water than the −110 ± 28 ΔR previously used. Sedimentation runs at about **77 cm per thousand years** through the deglacial (16.5 to 9.3 kyr BP) and about **27 cm per thousand years** in the early Holocene.

## Checked with the tools

The paper's dated levels sit in a table published as an image and a spreadsheet, so we cannot rebuild its exact chronology. Three things can still be checked from the text, and the third is the one that matters to anyone using the core.

**Step 1: the reservoir arithmetic.** A local reservoir age is the global average plus the local offset: 503 + 345 = **848 ¹⁴C years**, and the errors add in quadrature: √(63² + 60²) = **87 years**, which is the paper's 90 rounded. The revision itself is 345 − (−110) = **455 ¹⁴C years**, with an uncertainty of √(60² + 28²) = **66 years**. Every ¹⁴C date in the core becomes 455 years younger once it is corrected.

**Step 2: do the two stated sedimentation rates fill an 8 m core?** They do, almost exactly. At 27 cm per thousand years, the 9,300 years of the early Holocene occupy **251 cm**. At 77 cm per thousand years, the 7,200 years from 9.3 to 16.5 kyr BP occupy another 554 cm. Together that is **805 cm** for a core measured at 8 m — a 0.7 % check that the rates, the time span and the core length all describe the same core.

That consistency lets us build the age-depth model the paper implies. We placed **nine dated levels** at 30, 100, 180, 251, 330, 430, 540, 660 and 780 cm, with the ages those two rates give and 1σ errors of 100 years (Holocene) and 120 years (deglacial) — **our construction**, not the paper's table. The tool then returns, with no help from us, exactly **27 cm per thousand years** above 251 cm and **77 cm per thousand years** below it, and its order-preserving Monte Carlo keeps **all 2,000 draws** (Figure 1), because no two dates conflict.

**Step 3: the reservoir revision is bigger than the whole age model's error bar.** The Monte Carlo 95 % envelope on this core is **274 to 585 years wide** — about 390 to 400 years at the Holocene levels, 450 to 470 at the deglacial ones, narrowest between dates and widest where it extrapolates past the top and bottom. The **455-year** reservoir revision is wider than that entire envelope at **148 of the 161 grid depths** (everything between 25 and 775 cm), and only at the deglacial dated levels are the two about equal (Figure 2).

That is the practical point. Doubling the number of radiocarbon dates on this core would tighten the envelope, and change nothing about the 455 years. Anyone comparing this core with an ice core, or with a core from another shelf, has to know which ΔR was used before the error bars are worth drawing.

**One warning the tool hands back.** With the smoothing parameter at its default (χ² equal to the number of dates, here 9), the spline flags the level at **251 cm** as an outlier at **z = 2.54**, because a smooth curve cannot turn a corner. There is nothing wrong with that date: it is the point where sedimentation drops from 77 to 27 cm per thousand years. A real change in accumulation rate looks exactly like a bad date to a smoothing spline, which is why the tool shows the linear and Monte Carlo models beside it.

## How we made the graphs

The charts and every number come from `scripts/blog-figures/laptev-sea-deglacial-age-depth-model-reservoir-age.mjs` in fibub's public code. It calls `parseLevels`, `linearAt`, `linearSlope`, `splineForChi2`, `splineAt`, `monteCarlo`, `flagLevels` and `depthGrid` from `src/lib/sci/agedepth.ts`, the maths behind the [[age-depth-model]] builder.

From the paper: the core length (8 m), the two sedimentation rates (77 and 27 cm per thousand years), the deglacial interval (16.5 to 9.3 kyr BP), the two ΔR values with their errors and the global reservoir age. **Our construction:** the nine dated levels, their depths and their 1σ errors (100 and 120 years), the 5 cm model grid, 2,000 Monte Carlo draws and seed 1. The levels follow the paper's rates but are **not its dates**; the paper's own table is not machine-readable from its page.

## How we test the maths

On every change to fibub, `tests/sci.mjs` runs `tests/sci-tools/age-depth-model.mjs`, which pins the builder against independent references:

- the **smoothing spline** must match SciPy's natural cubic smoothing spline, and `splineForChi2` must hit its target χ² to high precision;
- **linear interpolation and its slope** must match NumPy's `interp` and the exact segment gradients, including extrapolation past the first and last date;
- the **Monte Carlo** must reproduce stored quantiles for a fixed seed, must reject every draw whose ages run backwards, and its median must sit on the linear model when the errors are symmetric;
- **New with this article:** the nine-level Laptev-style core must give accumulation rates of exactly **27.0 and 77.0 cm per thousand years** either side of 251 cm, a Monte Carlo acceptance of **2,000 of 2,000**, an envelope **361 years** wide at 400 cm, and the level at 251 cm must be flagged at **z = 2.54** by the default spline.

## Check it yourself

On the [[age-depth-model]] page:

1. In the dates box (*One date per line: depth, age, 1σ error, and a label if you like*), paste these nine lines: **30, 1111, 100, mbf**; **100, 3704, 100, mbf**; **180, 6667, 100, mbf**; **251, 9296, 100, mbf**; **330, 10325, 120, mb**; **430, 11623, 120, mb**; **540, 13052, 120, mb**; **660, 14610, 120, mb**; **780, 16169, 120, mb**.
2. Leave *Depth unit* on **cm** and *Age unit* on **cal yr BP**. Set *Top depth* **0**, *Bottom depth* **800** and *Step* **5**.
3. Leave *Spline smoothing S*, *Monte Carlo runs* (**2000**), *Random seed* (**1**) and *Flag dates more than (σ)* (**2**) as they are. The headline reads **9 dates, 0 to 800 cm: 1 flagged**, and the note below says **2,000 draws kept of 2,000 (100 %)**, with **χ² = 9**.
4. In the table, the level at **251 cm** carries **z = 2.54** and is marked as a check to look at. That is the change in sedimentation rate, not a bad date.
5. Set *Rates as* to **Accumulation rate (depth per year)** and read the rate panel: it is flat at **27 cm per thousand years** down to 251 cm and **77** below.
6. In *Depths, one per line or separated by commas* type **400**. The table gives Linear **11234**, Spline **11319**, MC median **11233**, MC 2.5 % **11046** and MC 97.5 % **11407** — an envelope 361 years wide, narrower than the paper's 455-year reservoir revision.

## What researchers can use it for

- **Weigh a reservoir correction against your chronology.** Build the model, read the 95 % envelope, and compare: if the correction is wider, more dates will not help, with the [[age-depth-model]] builder.
- **Check a published core for internal consistency.** Sedimentation rates, a time span and a core length have to multiply out; when they do not, something in the table is mislabelled.
- **Report the ΔR you used, next to the ages.** Two cores dated with different offsets cannot be compared, however small their error bars look.
- **Spot rate changes, not outliers.** Compare the spline's flags with the linear model: a flagged date at a known lithology change is usually a real change in accumulation.
- **Propagate the correction into a proxy comparison.** Shift the chronology, restack the records with the [[proxy-stacker]] and see which leads and lags survive.

## The fine print

- Our dated levels are **built from the paper's stated rates**, not taken from its radiocarbon table, which its page publishes only as an image and a spreadsheet. The envelope widths therefore describe a core like this one, not this exact core.
- We do **not** recalibrate anything. Marine20 turns a 455 ¹⁴C-year shift into a slightly different number of calendar years at each age; we treat the shift as about 455 calendar years, which is close over the Holocene but only approximate in the deglacial.
- The 1σ errors (100 and 120 years) are our assumption, chosen to be typical of Marine20-calibrated marine dates with this reservoir uncertainty.
- A reservoir correction is not necessarily constant through time. The paper's own point is that it changed, so a single ΔR for a whole deglacial core is itself an approximation.
