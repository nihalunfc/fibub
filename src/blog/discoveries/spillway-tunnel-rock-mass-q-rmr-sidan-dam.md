Before a tunnel is dug, somebody has to put a number on the rock. There are several competing numbers — Barton's **Q**, Bieniawski's **RMR**, Palmström's **RMi**, Hoek's **GSI** — and a case study from the Sidan Dam spillway tunnel in Bali ran all four at five stations along the same 325 m of pyroclastic breccia. That makes it an unusually complete worked example, and every step of it is arithmetic you can redo yourself. This article redoes it with the [[rock-mass-classification|Rock mass rating tool]], and then asks what the four systems disagree about. For the surveying and core work that feeds these numbers, see [[drill-hole-composites]] and [[stereonet]].

## The paper in brief

The tunnel is about **11 m** across, horseshoe-shaped, driven through tuff and pyroclastic breccia whose intact strength is only **2 to 21 MPa** — weak rock by any standard. The authors logged five stations: the inlet, the outlet, a surface exposure (BPS) and two boreholes. They found **one joint set plus random joints**, dipping 55 to 83°, spaced 0.6 to 2 m, slightly rough, with openings under 1 mm and walls slightly to highly weathered.

Their ratings put every station in the **poor to very poor** range: Q from **0.16** at the inlet to **1.40** in borehole 7, RMR from **35** to **43**. All four systems agree on the big decision — top heading with benching rather than full face, short advances, systematic rock bolts and shotcrete — but they differ widely on how much shotcrete, from 50 mm to 400 mm on the walls depending on which system you believe.

## Checked with the tools

The Rock mass rating tool holds no rating tables on purpose: you read every rating from the published tables and it does the arithmetic, so there is nothing to argue with except the inputs. We typed the paper's five stations in.

**Step 1: Q.** Q is the product of three quotients — block size (RQD/Jn), inter-block shear strength (Jr/Ja) and active stress (Jw/SRF). All five values come back exactly as published:

| Station | RQD/Jn | Jr/Ja | Jw/SRF | Tool Q | Paper Q |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Inlet | 3.17 | 0.25 | 0.20 | 0.158 | 0.16 |
| BPS | 10.67 | 0.25 | 0.40 | 1.067 | 1.07 |
| BHT 6 | 9.67 | 0.25 | 0.40 | 0.967 | 0.97 |
| BHT 7 | 14.00 | 0.25 | 0.40 | 1.400 | 1.40 |
| Outlet | 5.67 | 0.25 | 0.20 | 0.283 | 0.28 |

**Step 2: RMR.** Summing the five ratings and the −12 orientation adjustment reproduces **35, 42, 43, 43, 43** exactly. Worth noting what that adjustment does: the basic (unadjusted) sums are 47 to 55, which would read as *fair* rock. The −12 for unfavourable joint orientation is what pushes the tunnel into *poor*, and it is a judgement, not a measurement.

**Step 3: the two correlations disagree with the data.** The tool carries the editable correlation RMR = A ln Q + B so you can test the published constants against your own site. On these five stations, Barton's **9 ln Q + 44** is **6.2 RMR points** out root-mean-square — it puts the inlet at 27 when the logging says 35, and the outlet at 33 when the logging says 43. Bieniawski's **13.5 log Q + 43** does better at **3.7 points**. Fitting the five points themselves gives **2.70 ln Q + 42.7**, with a residual of 2.1 points, and a slope four times gentler than Barton's. Figure 1 shows why: over this site Q changes by a factor of nine while RMR barely moves eight points, because the things that swing Q here (Jn and SRF) are only lightly weighted in RMR.

**Step 4: SRF is the biggest free choice.** Jr/Ja is 0.25 at every station and Jw is 1 everywhere, so what separates the inlet (Q 0.16) from BPS (Q 1.07) is block size and the stress reduction factor. The paper took SRF as 5 at the portals and 2.5 inside. Figure 2 shows what that choice is worth: with SRF 2.5 the inlet's Q would be **0.32** instead of 0.16, moving it out of Barton's *extremely poor* band. A reviewer who disagreed with one table lookup would change the recommended support.

**Step 5: the GSI routes do not agree, and the paper shows it.** For three stations the paper's GSI matches the 2013 chart formula GSI = 1.5 Jcond89 + RQD/2 exactly: with a joint condition rating of 20, BPS gives 1.5 × 20 + 32/2 = **46**, BHT 6 gives **44.5** (reported 45) and BHT 7 gives **51**. But the same formula gives **47** at the outlet, where the paper reports **35**, and the paper leaves the inlet's RQD and Jcond blank while reporting GSI 25. Meanwhile the tool's own GSI estimate, the older GSI = RMR89 (dry) − 5, gives **50 to 55** for all five stations, 5 to 20 points above the paper. Those differences matter: GSI feeds the Hoek-Brown strength, and 20 points of GSI can halve a rock mass modulus. The honest reading is that in weak, blocky volcanic rock the GSI routes are not interchangeable, and a paper should say which one it used.

## How we made the graphs

Both charts and every number come from `scripts/blog-figures/spillway-tunnel-rock-mass-q-rmr-sidan-dam.mjs` in fibub's public code. It calls `qValue` (Q and its three quotients, with Barton's convention of treating an RQD below 10 as 10), `rmr` (the sum of ratings plus the adjustment), `rmrFromQ` (the editable RMR = A ln Q + B) and `gsiFromRmr` from `src/lib/sci/rockmass.ts`, the maths behind the [[rock-mass-classification|Rock mass rating tool]].

Every input is from the paper's own tables: the RQD, Jn, Jr, Ja, Jw and SRF at each station, the five RMR ratings and the −12 orientation adjustment, and the joint condition rating of 20. **Ours:** the least-squares fit of A and B to the five stations, the dry groundwater rating of 15 used for the tool's GSI estimate, and the SRF sweep in Figure 2. No data are simulated; this is recomputation from published values.

## How we test the maths

On every change to fibub, `tests/sci.mjs` runs `tests/sci-tools/rock-mass-classification.mjs`, which pins the arithmetic:

- Thirty random cases from an independent NumPy script must agree to one part in 10¹² on RQD, Q with and without the RQD floor, Q′, the RMR sum, the correlation both ways and the GSI estimate.
- On an eleven-piece core run, RQD must be 110/150 = 73.3 % with six pieces counted and 93.3 % recovery, and a piece of exactly 10 cm must count.
- The floor convention must apply: an RQD of 4 is used as 10, so Q comes out 10 rather than 4 with every other parameter at 1. (In this tunnel the same rule turns an RQD of 5 at the inlet into Q 0.083 instead of 0.042.)
- RMR = 9 ln Q + 44 must give 44 at Q = 1 and must invert exactly.
- New with this article: the five Sidan Dam stations must give Q 0.158, 1.067, 0.967, 1.400 and 0.283, RMR 35, 42, 43, 43 and 43 from their ratings, and Barton's correlation must put the inlet at 27.4 RMR.

## Check it yourself

On the [[rock-mass-classification|Rock mass rating tool]] page, take the inlet station:

1. In the *Q-system* card, set *RQD from* to **a value I type** and put **19** in *RQD (%)*. Set *Jn* to **6**, *Jr* to **1**, *Ja* to **4**, *Jw* to **1** and *SRF* to **5**.
2. It shows **Q = 0.158**, with the quotients 3.17 (block size), 0.25 (shear) and 0.2 (active stress), and Q′ (without water and stress) of 0.792.
3. Change *SRF* to **2.5**: Q becomes **0.317**. Put it back to 5 and change *RQD* to **5**: with the box *Use 10 when RQD is below 10* ticked, Q is **0.083**; untick it and Q halves to **0.042**.
4. In the *RMR* card, type the ratings **2**, **3**, **15**, **20** and **7** in order (strength of intact rock, RQD, spacing, condition, groundwater) and **−12** in *Orientation adjustment*. The basic RMR is **47** and the total **35**.
5. Set *RMR = A ln Q + B* to **A = 9**, **B = 44**: it reads **27.4** against the logged 35. Change A to **5.86** and B to **43** (Bieniawski's form) and it reads **32.2**.
6. Set *Groundwater rating for dry ground (for GSI)* to **15**: the GSI estimate is **50**, where the paper reports 25 for this station by a different route.

## What researchers can use it for

- **Re-check a published classification table** before building on it: Q and RMR are arithmetic, and a transcription slip in a table is easy to find this way.
- **Test a correlation on your own site.** The editable A and B turn RMR = A ln Q + B from a quoted constant into a hypothesis you can fit and report a residual for.
- **Show how much a single rating is worth**, by sweeping SRF, Jn or the orientation adjustment and watching the class boundary move. This is the fastest honest answer to "how certain is the support design?".
- **Compare GSI routes** side by side before feeding a Hoek-Brown model, and say in the paper which route produced the number.
- **Teach rock mass classification** from a real published tunnel rather than a textbook exercise, with the core-run RQD panel for the logging step.

## The fine print

- Every rating comes from the paper's reading of the published tables. The tool holds no tables and cannot check whether a rating was read correctly.
- The RMi and JSCE systems the paper also uses are outside this tool; only Q, RMR and the GSI estimates were checked.
- The paper's own text gives RQD as 19 to 30 % while its tables reach 42 % at borehole 7; we used the table values, as its Q values do.
- The least-squares fit to five points from one site is an illustration, not a correlation anyone should carry elsewhere.
- Classification is not design. A classification number, however well recomputed, does not replace numerical modelling or monitoring in weak rock.
