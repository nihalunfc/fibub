Wave energy has one quiet advantage over other marine renewables: the sea wall is being built anyway. A 2025 paper in *Frontiers in Energy Research* proposes a **cavity-type revetment**, a coastal defence whose chambers swallow waves and drive turbines, strung 500 units deep along 7.5 km of the Beibu Gulf coast in southern China. Its headline is an electricity cost of **0.422 CNY/kWh**, just under the 0.453 CNY/kWh regional coal benchmark. A levelised cost is a formula, not a measurement, so it can be rebuilt from the inputs — and that is exactly what the [[lcoe-calculator]] is for. For the yield side of a marine or wind project see the [[wind-energy-yield]] tool, and for the storage that a variable source usually needs, [[home-battery-sizing]].

## The paper in brief

The authors model the chambers' hydrodynamics, estimate an overall wave-to-wire efficiency of **12.6 %**, and scale up to an array: **500 units** at 15 m spacing, an average output of **38.7 kW per unit**, and **274 million kWh a year** of electricity. On the money side they state an initial investment of **CNY 1.71 billion**, operation and maintenance at **3.5 % of the investment each year** (about CNY 60 million), a **6 % discount rate** and a **30-year life**, and conclude with the 0.422 CNY/kWh figure and a payback period of 6.8 years. They also report that extending to 500 units with policy support brings the cost down from 0.453 to 0.422 CNY/kWh.

The paper is a feasibility study and is open about its uncertainties. What it does not do is show the levelised-cost arithmetic, and the numbers it gives do not all fit together — the unit rating appears as 50 kW in one table and 150 kW in the design section, and a stated payback of 6.8 years does not follow from a CNY 1.71 billion investment earning tens of millions a year. That makes it a good test case for a tool that shows its working.

## Checked with the tools

This is a **recomputation** from the paper's stated inputs: investment CNY 1.71 billion, O&M 3.5 % a year, 30-year life, no fuel, no output degradation and no cost escalation. We enter the array as 75 MW (500 × 150 kW) with a capacity factor that reproduces each yearly output, since the levelised cost depends only on the yearly energy, not on how the capacity and capacity factor are split between them.

**Step 1: the paper's figure is an undiscounted cost.** With the discount rate set to zero, the tool gives **0.4265 CNY/kWh** — within 1 % of the paper's 0.422. That is simply total lifetime cost over total lifetime energy: CNY 1.71 billion of capital plus 30 years of CNY 59.85 million O&M, over 30 × 274 GWh.

Put the paper's **own 6 % discount rate** in, and the same inputs give **0.6718 CNY/kWh**. The capital recovery factor for 6 % over 30 years is 0.07265, so the capital alone costs CNY 124 million a year rather than the CNY 57 million an undiscounted split implies. Of the 0.6718, **0.4534 CNY/kWh is capital** and **0.2184 is O&M**.

**Step 2: which yearly output?** The paper's own average output per unit, 38.7 kW across 500 units, works out to **169.5 GWh a year**, not 274 GWh. The 274 GWh figure needs an average array power of **31.3 MW**, which is 62.6 kW per unit. The two differ by a factor of **1.62**, and since the levelised cost is inversely proportional to yearly energy, so does the cost.

Only the first line is cheaper than coal. The conclusion that the array beats the benchmark therefore rests on both choices at once: no discounting, and the higher of the paper's two output figures.

**Step 3: what to measure next.** The tool's one-at-a-time sensitivity, swinging each input by ±20 %, ranks the uncertainties for the 6 % case: **capacity factor swings the cost by 0.28 CNY/kWh** (0.56 to 0.84), capital cost by 0.18, the discount rate by 0.12, O&M by 0.09 and the lifetime by 0.07. For a first-of-a-kind device the yearly energy is both the least certain input and the most powerful one, which argues for spending the next research pound on measured output rather than on refining the cost estimate.

**Step 4: the payback period.** CNY 1.71 billion against the paper's stated net profit of about CNY 56 million a year is **30.5 years**, not 6.8. A 6.8-year payback would need about CNY 250 million of net profit a year, which at the 0.453 CNY/kWh benchmark price is more electricity than the array is said to make. We flag it rather than resolve it: one of the figures in that chain must be meant differently.

## How we made the graphs

The charts and every computed number come from `scripts/blog-figures/wave-energy-revetment-beibu-gulf-lcoe-recomputed.mjs` in fibub's public code. It calls `lcoe`, `crf` and `tornado` from `src/lib/sci/eng-lcoe.ts`, the maths behind the [[lcoe-calculator]].

From the paper: the CNY 1.71 billion investment, the 3.5 % yearly O&M share, the 6 % discount rate, the 30-year life, the 274 GWh yearly output, the 500 units and 38.7 kW average per unit, and the 0.453 CNY/kWh benchmark. **Our assumptions**: entering the array as 75 MW with a matching capacity factor (the paper's capacity figures disagree, and only the yearly energy affects the result); no output degradation, no cost escalation and no fuel or variable O&M, because the paper mentions none; and treating the stated O&M share as 3.5 % of the initial investment every year of the 30.

## How we test the maths

On every change to fibub, `tests/sci.mjs` runs `tests/sci-tools/lcoe-calculator.mjs`, which pins the levelised-cost maths against a separate NumPy calculation:

- The three made-up example plants must match NumPy's vectorised discounting to 1 part in 10¹⁰: 52.042, 53.136 and 71.052 per MWh for the solar, wind and gas examples, along with their present-value energy and cost.
- Five random input sets must match the same NumPy code, and in every case the capital, fixed O&M, variable O&M and fuel shares must add back to the LCOE exactly.
- The capital recovery factor must be 0.10185221 for 8 % over 20 years, and with no degradation or escalation the LCOE must equal the plain annuity formula (94.3572 per MWh for a test plant).
- With a zero discount rate the LCOE must equal total cost over total energy, which is the identity this article leans on.
- New with this article: the paper's inputs with a 6 % discount rate must give 671.8 per MWh and 426.5 with no discounting, and the tornado must put the capacity factor first.

## Check it yourself

On the [[lcoe-calculator]] page, in the *Plant* card (currency is whatever you type, here CNY):

1. Set *Capacity (MW)* to **75**, *Capital cost (per kW)* to **22800**, *Fixed O&M (per kW per year)* to **798**, and leave *Variable O&M*, *Fuel price* and *Heat rate* at **0**.
2. Set *Capacity factor (%)* to **41.7**, *Output loss per year (%)* to **0**, *Discount rate (% a year)* to **6**, *Lifetime (years)* to **30** and *O&M and fuel cost rise (% a year)* to **0**.
3. *Levelised cost* reads about **672 per MWh**, that is **0.672 CNY/kWh**. *What it is made of* splits it into roughly 453 capital and 218 O&M.
4. Set *Discount rate* to **0**. The cost falls to about **426 per MWh**, 0.4265 CNY/kWh — the paper's 0.422, to within a percent.
5. Put the rate back to **6** and set *Capacity factor (%)* to **25.8**, the value implied by 500 units averaging 38.7 kW. The cost rises to about **1086 per MWh**, 1.09 CNY/kWh.
6. In *Sensitivity (tornado)* leave *Tornado range (± %)* at **20** and read the order: capacity factor, then capital cost, then discount rate.
7. In the target-price box, set *Target price (per MWh)* to **453** and *Find the value of* to **Capital cost** to see what the investment would have to fall to for the array to match the benchmark at 6 %.

## What researchers can use it for

- **Rebuild a published LCOE** before citing it: if the paper does not state its discount rate, or states one it did not use, the number can be out by 50 %.
- **Say which discounting you used.** An undiscounted cost and a 6 % levelised cost are different quantities; for a 30-year capital-heavy project they differ by more than half.
- **Rank your uncertainties** with the tornado chart, and plan the next measurement campaign around the widest bar.
- **Back-solve a target**: ask what capital cost, capacity factor or lifetime a device needs to reach a given tariff, which is more useful to a developer than a single point estimate.
- **Compare technologies honestly** by putting them through the same tool with the same discount rate, lifetime and currency.

## The fine print

- We did not re-examine the paper's hydrodynamics, its 12.6 % efficiency or its wave resource. Only the economics are recomputed.
- The paper's own figures are not all consistent, and we have not decided which is intended; each row of the table shows what one reading implies.
- A revetment that is built for coastal protection anyway could fairly carry only the extra cost of the power equipment. The paper's CNY 1.71 billion appears to be the whole structure, which makes its levelised cost pessimistic for the energy and optimistic for the sea defence. Splitting that cost is a judgement, not a calculation.
- Levelised cost ignores when the electricity arrives. Wave power is variable, so its market value per kWh is not the benchmark tariff.
- All values are in the paper's own currency and year; no inflation adjustment or exchange rate is applied.
