**Taro** (*Colocasia esculenta*) is a staple root crop across the Pacific, Africa and Asia, grown for its starchy **corm** (the swollen underground stem), yet there is little careful work on how much nitrogen it needs. A 2026 paper in *Frontiers in Plant Science* grew taro in a glasshouse at five nitrogen levels for up to ten months and tracked biomass, leaf area and **relative growth rate** (RGR, the growth per gram of plant per day). This article recomputes those growth rates from the paper's harvest means with the [[plant-growth-analysis]] tool's maths and finds the published values sit almost exactly on base-10 logarithms rather than the natural logarithms the paper's formula names, so every RGR is about 2.3 times too low. For the nitrogen side, the [[fertiliser-blend-calculator]] works out feed recipes, and [[yield-trial-anova]] compares treatments in a field trial.

## The paper in brief

The authors grew the eddoe-type cultivar Bun Long in pots, fed with nutrient solutions holding 2.5, 5, 10, 15 or 20 mM nitrogen (nitrate and ammonium in a 5:1 ratio), and harvested plants at 35 days after planting (before the treatments started), then at about 125, 255 and 301 days. They report total dry mass, leaf area, height, suckers and corm traits, and the nitrogen use efficiency of each level.

Their main result: corm biomass is greatest near **10 mM N**, and more nitrogen than that slows growth, with the 20 mM plants smallest by the last harvest. At about 301 days, plants at 10 mM averaged 168.72 g of dry mass, against 66.49 g at 20 mM.

The paper defines **RGR = (ln W₂ − ln W₁) ÷ (T₂ − T₁)**, where W₁ is the mean dry mass at harvest 1, W₂ each plant's mass at a later harvest and T the day. Natural logarithms are the standard in plant growth analysis because then RGR is the true exponential rate: a plant growing at RGR r per day has mass W₁ × e^(r t) after t days.

## Checked with the tools

This is a **recomputation from the harvest means and days in the paper**. We took the middle of each harvest's stated day range (124.5 and 301.5 days; **our assumption**) and the harvest 1 mean of 4.91 g, and worked out RGR with the tool's maths, using the classical "ln of mean W" form.

**Step 1: the published values are base-10.** For 10 mM N at harvest 4, ln(168.72 ÷ 4.91) ÷ (301.5 − 35) gives **0.0133 g g⁻¹ d⁻¹**, while the paper lists **0.0057**. The base-10 version, log₁₀(168.72 ÷ 4.91) ÷ 266.5, gives **0.00576**. The pattern holds for all ten values we checked (five nitrogen levels at harvests 2 and 4): the published RGR is on average **0.415** times the natural-log value, close to 1 ÷ ln 10 = 0.434 (Figure 1). The small gap below 0.434 is expected, because the paper averages each plant's own RGR, and the mean of logs is a little below the log of the mean when plants vary.

**Step 2: the mass test.** Figure 2 settles it without any formula choice. Starting from the 4.91 g harvest 1 mean, a natural-log rate of 0.0057 per day would grow a plant to only **22.4 g** by day 301.5; the plants actually averaged 168.72 g. Read as a base-10 rate, the same number predicts **162.2 g**.

**What it changes, and what it does not.** Dividing by ln 10 scales every RGR by the same factor, so the ranking of nitrogen levels and the paper's conclusions about the 10 mM optimum are untouched. What changes is comparison with other work: taro's RGR over its first ten months is about 0.013 g g⁻¹ d⁻¹ at 10 mM (13 mg g⁻¹ d⁻¹), not 0.0057. A modeller or a meta-analysis taking the published values at face value would underestimate taro's growth 2.3-fold. The harvest dates matter little: using the ends of the day ranges moves the natural-log values by less than 2 %.

## How we made the graphs

The charts and every number above come from `scripts/blog-figures/taro-nitrogen-relative-growth-rate-natural-log-recomputed.mjs` in fibub's public code. It calls `interval` and `analyse` from `src/lib/sci/plantgrowth.ts`, the maths behind the [[plant-growth-analysis]] tool (Hunt's interval formulas, with RGR from the mean of ln W or the ln of mean W), and `normInv` from `src/lib/sci/stats.ts` to place the simulated plants below.

The harvest 1 mean, its standard error, the harvest 2 and 4 means and the published RGR values come from the paper. The harvest-day midpoints, the six simulated plants per harvest and their leaf areas are **our assumptions**.

## How we test the maths

On every change to fibub, `tests/sci.mjs` runs `tests/sci-tools/plant-growth-analysis.mjs`, which checks the tool against an independent Python script:

- Pure exponential growth, W = 0.1 e^(0.12 t), must give an RGR of exactly 0.12 per day, and Hunt's unit leaf rate must equal the integrated true mean, 6.3606 × 10⁻⁴, found by SciPy's `quad`.
- A made-up 3-harvest example must reproduce RGR, its standard error and 95 % interval, ULR, LAR, SLA and LWR for every interval; for 14 to 21 days, RGR 0.15079 ± 0.00740 per day.
- The classical "ln of mean W" form must give 0.15070 for the same interval.
- New with this article: 4.91 g at day 35 and 168.72 g at day 301.5 must give an RGR of 0.01327 per day, 2.30 times the base-10 figure.

## Check it yourself

On the [[plant-growth-analysis]] tool:

1. In *One row per plant: harvest time, total dry mass, leaf area and (optional) leaf dry mass*, paste six simulated 10 mM plants per harvest (day, dry mass in g, leaf area in cm²), one per line: **35, 2.86, 235**; **35, 3.67, 262**; **35, 4.33, 248**; **35, 5.03, 255**; **35, 5.93, 241**; **35, 7.63, 259**; **301, 126.28, 150**; **301, 145.31, 182**; **301, 159.31, 166**; **301, 173.16, 177**; **301, 189.83, 158**; **301, 218.43, 193**. Their means are the paper's 4.91 g and 168.72 g; the spread follows its standard errors and the leaf areas are made up.
2. Keep *Time in* **days**, *Mass in* **g**, *Leaf area in* **cm²**, *RGR from* **Mean of ln W (with error)**.
3. The headline reads **RGR 35–301 days: 0.0134 g g⁻¹ d⁻¹**, and *Relative growth rate* shows **13.4 mg g⁻¹ d⁻¹ ± 0.61 SE; 95 % CI 12.1 to 14.8**.
4. Switch *RGR from* to **ln of mean W (classical)**: **0.0133**. Divide by 2.303 and you get 0.00578, the paper's 0.0057.
5. The unit leaf rate is not meaningful here: leaf area falls as taro puts its growth into the corm and suckers.

## What researchers can use it for

- **Recheck published growth rates** from harvest means and days before using them in a model or meta-analysis, with the [[plant-growth-analysis]] tool.
- **Report RGR with an error**: the "mean of ln W" option gives a standard error and confidence interval from plant-to-plant spread.
- **Catch unit slips quickly**: if W₁ e^(RGR × t) does not land near the final mass, the logarithm base or the time unit is off.
- **Plan feeding trials**: work out stock solutions or fertiliser mixes for each nitrogen level with the [[fertiliser-blend-calculator]], and analyse a replicated field version with [[yield-trial-anova]].

## The fine print

- We used means, not each plant's mass, so our values differ slightly from a per-plant calculation; the factor near 1 ÷ ln 10 is far bigger than that difference.
- The equation in the paper names the natural logarithm; we cannot see the authors' spreadsheet, so "base-10 logs" is the explanation that fits the numbers, not a confirmed cause.
- The 5 mM plants at harvest 2 varied a lot (a standard error of 8.69 g on 24.52 g), which is why that point sits furthest from the base-10 line in Figure 1.
- The simulated plants in "Check it yourself" share the paper's means and spread but are not its plants.
