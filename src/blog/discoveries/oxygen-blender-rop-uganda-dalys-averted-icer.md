Premature babies given pure oxygen can grow abnormal blood vessels in the retina and go blind. The fix is cheap: a **blender**, a valve that mixes oxygen with air so a baby breathes 30 or 40 % oxygen instead of 100 %. A 2026 paper in *Frontiers in Medicine* puts a price on that valve in Uganda and reports about **$33 per DALY averted**, far cheaper than almost any health programme. Cost-effectiveness papers like this one are built from six or seven numbers and two divisions, so they can be rebuilt from the outside, and that is what this article does with the [[daly-qaly|DALY and QALY calculator]]. For the population side of the same kind of sum, see [[herd-immunity]] and [[epidemic-model]].

## The paper in brief

The authors built a decision tree for Uganda's newborn care. Of 1.7 million live births a year, about 10 % are preterm, about 30 % of those are born before 34 weeks, and about 70 % of that group survive to be at risk of retinopathy of prematurity. Without blended oxygen the authors take **7 %** of those survivors to develop severe retinopathy; with blenders, **3.5 %**. Of untreated severe cases, **62.1 %** end in severe visual impairment or blindness, a figure from the ten-year follow-up of the CRYO-ROP trial.

One case of that blindness carries a **disability weight of 0.184** (Global Burden of Disease 2021) over **58 years** of expected life, discounted at **3 % a year**. The paper reports **5.18 DALYs per case**, **$168.92 per case averted** in the base case, and **$32.60 per DALY averted**, with a range from $4.97 (cheap blender, high turnover) to $104.98 (dear blender, short life, low turnover). For comparison, it quotes about $50 per DALY for insecticide-treated bed nets and a GDP per head of $1,002.30.

## Checked with the tools

Everything below is a **recomputation** from the values the paper states. No new data is involved, and nothing here is the paper's own spreadsheet.

**Step 1: DALYs per case, and a 2.4 % gap.** A case of blindness that lasts 58 years at a weight of 0.184 is worth 0.184 × 58 = **10.67 DALYs** undiscounted. Discounting shrinks that, and *how* you discount matters. The DALY and QALY calculator uses the standard continuous formula, (1 − e^(−rL)) / r, which gives 27.48 discounted years and **5.057 DALYs per case** at 3 %. The paper reports 5.18. The difference is not an error in either: adding up one whole year at a time, with the first year undiscounted, gives 0.184 × Σ 1.03^(−t) for t = 0 to 57 = **5.1798**, which is the paper's number to three figures. Figure 1 shows both conventions across a range of rates.

**Step 2: the cases one blender set prevents.** A base-case set serves 73 at-risk infants a year for five years, so 365 infants. Without it, 365 × 7 % × 62.1 % = **15.87** of them would go blind; with it, half that, **7.93**. So one set averts 7.93 cases, which is exactly the "about 7.94 cases per set" the paper reports, and it tells us something useful: that figure, and the cost per case built on it, do **not** apply the paper's 70 % deployment rate.

**Step 3: every scenario, reproduced.** Dividing each blender price by the cases that scenario averts reproduces the paper's four headline costs to within 0.1 %, including the least favourable one, which turns out to be the dear blender *and* the low turnover *and* the three-year life at once (52 infants a year for 3 years = 156 infants).

| Scenario | Infants served | Cases averted | Paper: $ per case | Tool: $ per case | Paper: $ per DALY | Tool: $ per DALY |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base case (Neotech) | 365 | 7.93 | 168.92 | **169.07** | 32.60 | **33.43** |
| Vayu, no medical air | 365 | 7.93 | 85.33 | **85.40** | 16.52 | **16.89** |
| Most favourable | 1,220 | 26.52 | 25.55 | **25.55** | 4.97 | **5.05** |
| Least favourable | 156 | 3.39 | 543.99 | **543.88** | 104.98 | **107.55** |

The cost per DALY is about 2.4 % higher in every row, and it is the same 2.4 % every time, because it is only the discounting convention from step 1. Feed the paper's own 5.18 DALYs per case into our reproduced cases instead and the four values become $32.64, $16.49, $4.93 and $105.00: the paper's own numbers, to the cent.

**Step 4: what the discount rate is really doing.** The Global Burden of Disease study has not discounted future years since 2010, and many national guidelines use 1.5 % rather than 3 %. The choice moves the headline more than any of the clinical inputs: per case it is 10.67 DALYs at 0 %, 7.13 at 1.5 %, 5.06 at 3 % and 3.48 at 5 %, so the base-case cost per DALY runs from **$15.84** at 0 % to **$48.62** at 5 % (Figure 2). Even the worst corner of the paper's cost range at the harshest rate stays under the bed-net benchmark, which is the paper's real point and survives every convention we tried.

## How we made the graphs

Both charts and every number above come from `scripts/blog-figures/oxygen-blender-rop-uganda-dalys-averted-icer.mjs` in fibub's public code. It calls `disc` (the discounted length of a stretch of future time) and `burden` (YLL + YLD per row) from `src/lib/sci/daly.ts`, the maths behind the [[daly-qaly|DALY and QALY calculator]]. The year-by-year comparison sum in Figure 1 is written out in the script, because the tool only offers the continuous form.

The disability weight, the 58 years, the 3 % rate, the two retinopathy rates, the 62.1 %, the blender prices and the turnover ranges all come from the paper's text. ## How we test the maths

On every change to fibub, `tests/sci.mjs` runs `tests/sci-tools/daly-qaly.mjs`, which pins this maths against SciPy:

- `disc(30, 0.03)` must equal 19.78101134198, the numeric integral of e^(−0.03t) from 0 to 30 computed with SciPy's `quad`, and eight more cases including one that starts part way into the future.
- A two-condition burden table must give YLL 480.2847, YLD 674.6931 and DALY 1154.9778 at 3 %, and the same table at 0 % must come back to the plain products 20 × 30 and 500 × 0.2 × 4.
- An ICER case must give $4,032.68 per QALY gained, $3,871.13 per DALY averted and a net monetary benefit of $64,392.16 at a $30,000 threshold.
- New with this article: a single row of 1 case at weight 0.184 for 58 years must give 5.0568 DALYs at 3 % and 10.672 at 0 %, and the base-case blender scenario must give 7.933 cases and $169.07 per case averted.

## Check it yourself

On the [[daly-qaly|DALY and QALY calculator]]:

1. In *Settings*, set *Discount rate (% a year)* to **3**.
2. In the *Disease burden (DALYs)* box, clear the made-up rows and type one line: **Blindness from ROP, 0, 0, 1, 0.184, 58**. The big line reads **5.06 DALYs (YLL 0.00 + YLD 5.06)**. That is the paper's 5.18, measured with continuous discounting.
3. Change the *Cases* column from 1 to **15.867** (the blind children among 365 infants with no blender) and the total becomes **80.23 DALYs**. Halve the cases to **7.933** and it becomes **40.12**: the set averts **40.1 DALYs**.
4. Divide one set's price, $1,341.25, by those 40.1 DALYs: **$33.43 per DALY averted**.
5. Set *Discount rate (% a year)* to **0** and watch the same table jump to **169.33 DALYs** without a blender: the cost per DALY averted falls to $15.84. Set it to **5** and it rises to $48.62.

## What researchers can use it for

- **Audit a published cost-effectiveness ratio** before citing it: reproduce the DALYs per case from the weight, duration and rate, and see whether the discounting convention explains any gap.
- **Report the convention, not just the rate.** A continuous integral and a year-by-year sum differ by about half the discount rate; at 3 % over a lifetime that is 2.4 %, which is enough to matter in a table of three-significant-figure results.
- **Run the discount rate as a sensitivity axis**, as in Figure 2, instead of quoting one number: the rate usually moves a lifelong-disability result more than the clinical inputs do.
- **Translate a programme into DALYs for a grant case**: type one burden row per condition, with your own weights from a source you name, and keep the CSV with the proposal.
- **Teach the difference between YLL and YLD** with a case like this one, where nobody dies and the whole burden is years lived with disability.

## The fine print

- Everything here is recomputation from the paper's stated values. We did not re-estimate any clinical input, and we have no access to its spreadsheet.
- Disability weights are not ours: 0.184 is the paper's, from the Global Burden of Disease 2021. The tool deliberately ships no weight tables.
- Our reconstruction of the "least favourable" scenario may not be the authors' exact combination, though it reproduces their result.
- The paper's own cost per DALY also folds in screening and treatment scenarios we did not rebuild; our figures are for the blender-only comparison that produces its headline.
- General education, not clinical or policy advice. For a real appraisal, follow the guidelines of the country you are working in.
