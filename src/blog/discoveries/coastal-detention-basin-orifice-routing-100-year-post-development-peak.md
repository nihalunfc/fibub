When farmland or forest becomes roofs and roads, the same rain runs off faster and in greater volume. A **detention basin**, a pond that fills in a storm and drains slowly through a small outlet, is the standard fix. A 2026 study in *Frontiers in Built Environment* modelled one such basin on the Virginia coast against 10-, 50- and 100-year storms and concluded that it needs to be much bigger. We check the runoff arithmetic in its results table and route the 100-year storm through a pond of the same outline with the [[detention-pond]] router. To work out the catchment itself, see the [[watershed-delineation]] tool.

## The paper in brief

The authors built a model in the US EPA's stormwater program (SWMM) of a catchment of about 6 hectares that goes from 5 % to 70 % paved. They applied three **design storms**, each two hours long, and compared runoff **before and after development**. After development the 100-year storm's peak runoff rises from 3.05 to 5.04 m³/s, and the runoff depth from 192.89 to 236.98 mm. The existing pond holds about 17,200 m³ and its outlet is a rectangular opening 3.81 m wide and 1.27 m high.

They then enlarged the pond (to 44,400 m³ for the 50-year storm and 55,800 m³ for the 100-year) and report outlet peaks of 2.81 and 3.51 m³/s, with the aim of bringing peaks back toward the pre-development 10-year value of 1.44 m³/s.

## Checked with the tools

This is a **recomputation plus a simulation** with our own simplifications. We do not have the paper's SWMM files.

**Step 1: the runoff table.** A runoff coefficient is the runoff depth divided by the rainfall depth. For the 50- and 100-year storms the arithmetic works: 160.53 ÷ 226.31 = 70.9 % before (paper 71.0), and 236.98 ÷ 259.08 = 91.5 % after (paper 91.5). In the 50-year row the "after" value is 204.82 ÷ 226.31 = 90.5 %, where the paper prints 90.3. The 10-year row mixes two rainfall depths: the before-development coefficient of 60.5 % matches the table's 162.31 mm (98.30 ÷ 162.31 = 60.6), but the after-development 86.6 % only matches 160.53 mm, the depth in the text (138.94 ÷ 160.53 = 86.6). With 162.31 mm it would be 85.6 %. Since 160.53 is also the 50-year before-development runoff depth, we think the text's 10-year rainfall is a slip. In volume, the 6 ha catchment produces 5,898 m³ before and 8,336 m³ after the 10-year storm, and 11,573 and 14,219 m³ for the 100-year storm.

**Step 2: a pond of the same outline.** The paper's outline of 158.03 × 81.84 m and a depth of 1.40 m would hold 18,100 m³ with vertical walls. Giving the pond sloping sides of about **1.96 horizontal per 1 vertical** (our assumption that the outline is the water's edge at 1.40 m) reproduces the stated 17,200 m³ exactly: a bottom of 152.5 × 76.4 m. The enlarged ponds' storage divided by their outlines gives depths of 2.25 and 2.80 m.

**Step 3: route the storm.** We built the inflow as a triangle that peaks at the paper's post-development peak (5.04 m³/s) one hour into the storm, and whose total volume equals the runoff depth times the catchment (14,219 m³). The shape, and the one-hour peak time, are **our assumptions**; the paper's own hydrograph is only in a figure. We assumed a round orifice at the bottom with a coefficient of 0.6 and asked the tool for the size that holds the peak outflow to 1.44 m³/s.

For the 100-year storm that is an orifice of **1.05 m**. The water rises to **0.92 m**, storing **11,042 m³**, about two thirds of the pond's 17,200 m³, and the peak leaves 24 minutes after the inflow peak. For the 50-year storm the orifice is 1.39 m and the storage 9,408 m³. For the 10-year storm a 2.59 m orifice is enough, with only 5,455 m³ stored.

## How we made the graphs

The script `scripts/blog-figures/coastal-detention-basin-orifice-routing-100-year-post-development-peak.mjs` calls `storageAt` (volume of a sloping-sided pond), `tableHydro` (a pasted hydrograph), `route` (level-pool routing) and `sizeOrifice` (the orifice for a target peak) from `src/lib/sci/pond.ts`, the maths of the [[detention-pond]] router. The runoff depths, peaks, catchment area, outline, depth and stated storage are from the paper. The triangular inflow shape, its one-hour peak, the 1.96:1 side slope, the round orifice with coefficient 0.6 at the bottom and the 1.44 m³/s target are **our assumptions or choices**. The curves are **simulated**, not the paper's.

## How we test the maths

On every change, `tests/sci.mjs` runs `tests/sci-tools/detention-pond.mjs`, new with this article:

- The pond's storage at 1.40 m for a 152.5 × 76.4 m bottom and 1.958:1 sides must be 17,200 m³, and the integral of the surface area must give the same.
- A triangular hydrograph through points (0, 0), (60 min, 5.04) and (94.04 min, 0) must carry 14,219 m³.
- The sized orifice (1.051 m) must route that storm to a peak of 1.44 m³/s with water at 0.92 m.

The older checks in `tests/sci.mjs` compare routing with SciPy's solver to about 1 part in 500,000.

## Check it yourself

On the [[detention-pond]] page:

1. Under *Inflow*, set *Inflow from* to **A hydrograph I paste** and paste three rows: **0, 0**; **60, 5.04**; **94.04, 0** (time in minutes, flow in m³/s).
2. Under *Pond and outlet*, set *Bottom length (m)* to **152.5**, *Bottom width (m)* to **76.4**, *Side slope* to **1.958**, *Depth to the top of the bank (m)* to **1.4**.
3. Set *Orifice diameter (m)* to **1.051**, *Orifice coefficient* to **0.6**, *Overflow weir crest above the bottom (m)* to **1.4**, *Overflow weir length (m)* to **3.81** and *Weir coefficient* to **1.7**.
4. The peak outflow should read about **1.44 m³/s**, the highest water about **0.92 m**, and the storage about **11,000 m³**.
5. Type **1.44** in *Target peak outflow* to see the tool find an orifice of about **1.05 m** by itself, then change the orifice to **0.8** and watch the peak fall and the water rise.

## What researchers can use it for

- **Sanity-checking a pond study**: does the stated storage fit the outline and depth, and does the volume exceed the runoff?
- **Scoping before a full model**: size an outlet with level-pool routing, then confirm in SWMM.
- **Comparing storms**: route the 10-, 50- and 100-year events through the same pond to see which one controls the design.
- **Teaching** the link between runoff coefficient, volume, peak and storage.
- **Checking a table**: divide each runoff depth by its rainfall, as in Step 1, to catch a typo.

## The fine print

- Our inflow is a made-up triangle with the right peak and volume, not the paper's SWMM hydrograph.
- The tool routes level-pool storage with a round orifice and weir. It ignores tidal tailwater, channel losses and the paper's rectangular outlet with coefficient 5.22.
- The 1.96:1 side slope is inferred so that storage matches the paper's 17,200 m³; the paper's own figure 7 gives 18,000 m³.
- The difference in storage we find may come from the model differences above. It is not a claim that the paper is wrong.
