**Lead-212** is a radioactive isotope used in targeted alpha therapy: it is attached to a molecule that seeks out cancer cells, and its decay products deliver short-range alpha particles. Hospitals get it from a **generator**, a column holding radium-224 that is "milked" (eluted) for its lead. But every lead-212 atom that decays on the column ends up as ordinary, stable **lead-208**, and the chemistry cannot tell the two apart: stable lead takes up binding sites that radioactive lead should have. A 2025 paper in *EJNMMI Radiopharmacy and Chemistry* models how fast that stable lead builds up and when to elute. We rebuilt its numbers with the Bateman equations inside the [[decay-chain]] calculator; for single-isotope sums use the [[decay-calculator]], and for counting the eluted activity, [[counting-statistics]].

## The paper in brief

The authors follow a 350 MBq radium-224 generator (and a thorium-228 one) through the whole chain to stable lead-208 with a matrix form of the **Bateman equations**, the textbook solution for parent, daughter, granddaughter and so on, and check it against FISPIN, a UK inventory code. The key quantity is **molar activity**: the radioactivity per mole of *all* lead, radioactive or not. Pure lead-212 would give 10,898 MBq per nanomole; stable lead dilutes it.

Their findings: wait longer and you get more lead-212, but even more stable lead. For a radium-224 generator the best compromise is to elute at about **16.5 h** (61.0 % of the radium activity as lead-212, about 7000 MBq/nmol), while the single-batch maximum of lead-212 comes at **36.7 h**. By 72 h there are 4.55 stable lead atoms for every lead-212 atom. Even so, the molar activities stay well above what preclinical (over 10 MBq/nmol) and clinical work (over 3 MBq/nmol) need, at least for the one chelator with published data.

## Checked with the tools

This is a pure **recomputation**: the paper's inputs are half-lives, and the [[decay-chain]] calculator's maths (`atoms`, `activities` and `peakTime`) takes the same inputs. We ran a six-member chain, radium-224 → radon-220 → polonium-216 → lead-212 → bismuth-212 → stable lead-208, with the paper's 10.64 h for lead-212 and rounded standard values for the rest.

**Step 1: lead-212 growing in.** On a fresh generator, lead-212 activity climbs while radium-224 slowly decays (Figure 1). It reaches **61.0 %** of the starting 350 MBq at 16.5 h and **59.0 %** at 15.4 h, against the paper's 61.0 % and 58.9 %. It peaks at **36.8 h**, at **74.6 %**, exactly the paper's single-batch maximum.

**Step 3: the trade-off.** More lead-212 per elution, or purer lead? The paper's optimum uses its own effective specific activity. As a simple stand-in we scored each time by lead-212 activity times its share of the lead atoms (**our criterion**); that score peaks at **16.2 h**, with 60.4 % of the radium activity and 7050 MBq/nmol, close to the paper's 16.5 h.

**Step 4: the drive to the hospital.** Lead-212 keeps decaying after elution. Taking the eluate at 15.6 h gives 0.518 stable atoms per lead-212 atom and 7177 MBq/nmol (paper: 1:0.519, 7.17 GBq/nmol). After 5 h in transit with its bismuth-212 still present: **1:1.123 and 5132 MBq/nmol** (paper 1:1.124, 5.13 GBq/nmol); with the bismuth removed at elution, **5440 MBq/nmol** (paper 5.44). From a 48 h elution, after 5 h: 1:3.841 and **2251 MBq/nmol**, where the paper prints 1:3.833 and 2.41 GBq/nmol. The ratio agrees, and 1:3.83 itself implies about 2.25 GBq/nmol, so the 2.41 looks like a slip in the paper rather than a difference in the physics.

## How we made the graphs

The charts and numbers come from `scripts/blog-figures/pb-212-generator-stable-pb-208-buildup-optimal-elution-time-bateman.mjs`. It calls `atoms`, `activities`, `peakTime` and `lambdaOf` from `src/lib/sci/phchain.ts`, the Bateman solver behind the [[decay-chain]] calculator. Stable lead-208 is the chain's last member with a decay constant of zero, which the solver's divided-difference method handles without a special case.

The 350 MBq load, the half-lives of lead-212 and radon-220 and every comparison value come from the paper. The other half-lives are rounded standard values. Bismuth-212 sends all its decays straight to lead-208 (**our simplification**: about a third pass through thallium-208, which lives only 3 minutes). "Daughter removal" is our reading of the paper's case: the eluate holds lead only.

## How we test the maths

On every change to fibub, `tests/sci.mjs` runs `tests/sci-tools/decay-chain.mjs`, which pins the solver against a 60-digit matrix exponential:

- Mo-99 → Tc-99m (88 % branching) from 1000 Bq must give **777.20 and 691.83 Bq** after 24 h, and Tc-99m must peak at **22.83 h**, as SciPy finds.
- The stiff six-member radium-226 chain, with half-lives from 1600 years to 164 microseconds, must match the matrix exponential at 2 h and 30 days.
- New with this article: on a fresh radium-224 generator lead-212 must peak at **36.77 h**, and the stable lead-208 per lead-212 atom must be **0.986 at 25 h and 4.554 at 72 h** (a four-member chain), with every atom accounted for.

## Check it yourself

On the [[decay-chain]] calculator:

1. Set *Example chain* to **Your own (edit the members below)**, *Activity unit* to **MBq** and *Time unit* to **h**. Press *Add a daughter* until there are four members.
2. Fill them in: **Ra-224**, *Half-life* **3.632** d, *Starting activity* **350**; **Pb-212**, **10.64** h; **Bi-212**, **60.55** min; and a stand-in for stable lead, **Pb-208**, **1000000000** y. Every *Branching to next (%)* is **100**; daughters start at **0**.
3. Type **16.5** in *Activities at time* and **72** in *Plot up to*. Pb-212 shows about **213.6 MBq** (*Activity ÷ parent* 0.6957; 213.6 of the original 350 is 61.0 %).
4. Read the *Atoms* column: Pb-212 about **1.180 × 10¹³**, Pb-208 about **6.616 × 10¹²**. Divide: 0.561 stable atoms per lead-212 atom, so lead-212 is 64.1 % of the lead and the molar activity is 0.641 × 10,898 ≈ 6990 MBq/nmol.
5. The facts list says **Pb-212 peaks at 36.77 h**, at about 261.3 MBq. Ignore the stand-in's own activity and peak: it only counts atoms.
6. Change the time to **48**: Pb-208 atoms rise to about 3.49 × 10¹³ against 1.41 × 10¹³ for Pb-212, 2.48 to 1.

## What researchers can use it for

- **Schedule elutions** for any generator, Ra-224/Pb-212, Ge-68/Ga-68 or Mo-99/Tc-99m, by adding the stable end product as a long-lived stand-in in the [[decay-chain]] calculator.
- **Estimate carrier from decay alone** before a labelling run, then compare with measured stable lead to see what the column and reagents add.
- **Plan transport**: put the eluate's lead and bismuth in as starting activities and follow molar activity through the journey.
- **Decay-correct and count** the eluted activity with the [[decay-calculator]] and [[counting-statistics]].
- **Teach transient equilibrium**: lead-212 lives shorter than radium-224, so it settles at a fixed ratio above the parent's activity, 1.139 here.

## The fine print

- Our numbers are **recomputed** from half-lives, not taken from the paper's model files. They count only lead made by decay; real eluates also carry stable lead from the column, acids and glassware, which the paper discusses.
- We modelled the radium-224 generator only, not the thorium-228 one.
- The 3-minute thallium-208 step is ignored; it shifts stable lead by a fraction of a percent in the first hours.
- Whether a given molar activity is enough depends on the chelator and how much of it is loaded; the paper's own conclusion rests on one published study.
