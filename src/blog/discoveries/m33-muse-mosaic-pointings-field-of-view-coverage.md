An integral-field spectrograph sees a very small piece of sky. VLT/MUSE in its wide-field mode covers **one arcminute square** — less than a fiftieth of the width of the Triangulum Galaxy, M33. To get a spectrum of every H II region along one arm, you have to tile. A 2026 paper in *Astronomy & Astrophysics* describes the M3D survey, which laid **24 MUSE pointings** over part of M33 in strips of three, tilted 22° east of north, with a 2-arcsecond overlap. That is exactly the arithmetic the [[mosaic-planner]] does for an amateur rig, so we rebuilt the survey's tiling with it — and found that the covered sky is 5 % smaller than the footprint the paper quotes. Work out your own field of view first with the [[telescope-calculator]], then check when the target is high enough with the [[object-altitude|target altitude planner]].

## The paper in brief

The M3D team used MUSE on VLT UT4 in seeing-limited wide-field mode, 4750 to 9350 Å at a resolving power near 3000, with 0.2″ spaxels. They observed between 5 September and 4 October 2022 in observing blocks of three pointings, each block making a 3-arcminute strip at a position angle of 22° east of north; eight such blocks gave **24 pointings** over what they describe as a mostly contiguous **3 × 8 arcmin²** region along M33's southern major axis. Contiguous pointings overlap by **2″**. Each pointing got two 340-second exposures with the instrument rotated 90° between them, and every object block was followed by a 120-second sky block. Seeing was mostly 0.6″ to 0.8″. At M33's distance of about 840 kpc, one arcsecond is **4.0 pc**, so the maps resolve structures of roughly 5 pc — small enough to separate individual H II regions, which is the point of the survey.

## Checked with the tools

Everything here is **geometry recomputed from the paper's stated numbers**: the 1′ field, the 2″ overlap, the 24 pointings, the 3′ strips and the 22° position angle. We used `fovArcmin`, `framesAlong` and `mosaic` from the Mosaic planner's maths.

**Step 1: the overlap as the tool sees it.** Mosaic planners work in overlap *fractions*, not arcseconds. 2″ out of a 1′ field is **3.333 %** — tiny by amateur standards, where 10 to 20 % is normal, because MUSE's pointing and flux calibration are good enough not to need a wide seam.

**Step 2: what 24 panels really cover.** n panels of size f with a fraction o of overlap cover n·f − (n−1)·f·o. Three 1′ fields overlapping by 2″ cover **2.933′**, not 3.000′; eight of them cover **7.767′**, not 8.000′. So the mosaic's real extent is 2.933′ × 7.767′ = **22.78 arcmin²** against the quoted 24 arcmin² — **5.1 % less**. Asked to cover a full 3′ × 8′ at that overlap, `framesAlong` returns **4 × 9 = 36 panels**, half again as many. This is not a criticism of the paper, which says "3 × 8 arcmin²" as a description of the layout; it is the trap every mosaic planner exists to catch, because 12 extra MUSE pointings is about 2.4 hours of extra VLT time.

**Step 4: the scale, in parsecs.** At the paper's 4.0 pc per arcsecond, the covered strip is **704 pc × 1864 pc**, an area of **1.31 kpc²**. And it is a small bite: against M33's catalogued 62.1′ diameter, the mosaic is **0.75 %** of the galaxy's face. Tiling the whole disc at the same overlap would take **65 × 65 = 4225** panels for the bounding square, about **3318** inside the circle — and at 0.2 hours of exposure per panel (two 340 s shots plus a third of a 120 s sky block), roughly **660 hours** of telescope time. That single number explains why integral-field maps of nearby galaxies come one arm at a time.

## How we made the graphs

The charts and every number come from `scripts/blog-figures/m33-muse-mosaic-pointings-field-of-view-coverage.mjs` in fibub's public code. It calls `fovArcmin`, `framesAlong` and `mosaic` from `src/lib/sci/mosaic.ts`, the maths behind the [[mosaic-planner]]. `framesAlong` is a single ceiling: the smallest n with n·f − (n−1)·f·o ≥ the side to cover. `mosaic` turns that grid into tangent-plane offsets and then into RA and Dec around the mosaic centre.

From the paper: the 1′ field, the 2″ overlap, the 24 pointings in strips of 3, the 22° position angle, the exposure times, the 840 kpc distance and the 4.0 pc per arcsecond. From the tool's own OpenNGC data: M33's centre and 62.1′ size. **Our assumptions:** that the mosaic centre is M33's catalogue centre (the real footprint sits south of it, along the major axis), that the panels form a clean rectangle, and that a full map of the disc would be the inscribed circle of the bounding square.

## How we test the maths

On every change to fibub, `tests/sci.mjs` pins the mosaic maths:

- `fovArcmin` must give the textbook small-angle answer: a 23.5 mm APS-C sensor at 480 mm focal length is 168.27′.
- `framesAlong` must be exact at the boundary: 190′ with that field and 15 % overlap needs 2 panels, 100′ needs 1 (not 2), and 300′ with a 100′ field and no overlap needs exactly 3.
- `offsetToRaDec` must be a true gnomonic projection: a 60′ offset east on the equator is 1° of RA, and 60′ north of Dec +30° is Dec +31°, both to 0.001°.
- `mosaic` must put panel 1 on the correct side: at a position angle of 90° the first panel is east of the centre and on the same declination.
- New with this article, in `tests/sci-tools/mosaic-planner.mjs`: the paper's layout must come back as 8 × 3 = 24 panels covering 7.767′ × 2.933′ at a 3.333 % overlap, a full 3′ × 8′ must need 36, and the 24 panel centres must span 4.33′ in RA and sit inside a 9′ box.

## Check it yourself

On the [[mosaic-planner]] page, you can type the survey in. The tool asks for a focal length and a sensor size rather than a field of view, so use the one-arcminute trick: **1 mm at 3438 mm focal length subtends 1 arcminute**.

1. In *Target*, type **M33** and pick "M33 Triangulum Galaxy" from the list; *RA* and *Dec* fill in as **01 33 50.9** and **+30 39 37**.
2. Set *Width to cover (arcmin)* to **7.76**, *Height to cover (arcmin)* to **2.93** and *Angle of the width (° east of north)* to **22**.
3. In *Telescope and camera*, set *Focal length (mm)* to **3438**, choose **Another sensor (type its size)**, and put **1** in both *Sensor width (mm)* and *Sensor height (mm)*.
4. Set *Overlap between panels (%)* to **3.3**.
5. The heading reads **8 × 3 = 24 panels**, *One frame covers* **0.9999′ × 0.9999′**, and *Mosaic covers* **7.768′ × 2.934′** — the paper's mosaic.
6. The *Panel centres (J2000)* table lists all 24, starting near **01h 33m 52.6s, +30° 43′ 07″**. Download them with **Panel centres (CSV)**.
7. Now change *Overlap* to the tool's default **15**. The count jumps to **9 × 4 = 36 panels**: a 50 % longer run for the same patch of sky. Changing the width and height to **8** and **3** at 3.3 % overlap gives the same 36.

## What researchers can use it for

- **Cost a proposal honestly**: turn "we will map 3 × 8 arcmin²" into a panel count before you write the time request, because the covered area is always smaller than the sum of the fields.
- **Choose the overlap on purpose**: Figure 1 is the exchange rate between seam width and telescope time, and it is steep at small fields.
- **Generate pointing lists**: the CSV of RA and Dec per panel goes straight into a phase-2 tool, a mount, or a scheduling script, at whatever position angle the galaxy's major axis needs.
- **Plan an amateur follow-up of a professional field**: enter your own camera and focal length, keep the same target box, and see how many of your frames cover the same ground.
- **Match depth across panels** with the [[sub-exposure-calculator]], since a mosaic is only as deep as its thinnest panel.

## The fine print

- This is **geometry**, not data. We have not touched a MUSE cube, and nothing here checks the paper's astrophysics about H II regions.
- The paper's mosaic is described as "mostly contiguous"; a real schedule loses or shifts panels to weather and visibility, so a tidy 8 × 3 rectangle is our idealisation.
- The tool treats the field as a rectangle on a tangent plane. MUSE's field is square to a good approximation, but the derotation between the two 340 s exposures trims the usable corners slightly.
- The 660-hour estimate for a whole-disc map counts exposure time only: no overheads, acquisition, calibration or standard stars, which in practice roughly double it.
- The 0.75 % share treats M33's 62.1′ catalogue size as a circle. The galaxy is an inclined disc, so the true fraction of its surface depends on the inclination you assume.
