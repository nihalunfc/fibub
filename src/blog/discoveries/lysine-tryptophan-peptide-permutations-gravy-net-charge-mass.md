Short peptides that kill bacteria by breaking their membranes are usually described by three numbers: how positively charged they are, how water-repelling they are, and how much of that water-repelling surface sits on one face of a helix. A 2025 paper in *Antibiotics* took a 13-residue, lysine- and tryptophan-rich peptide and made twelve derivatives by **permuting the same residues**, keeping the composition fixed, and found up to 16-fold differences in the concentration needed to stop bacteria growing, plus one derivative with a much better therapeutic index. That is a clean experiment, because every bulk property stays the same, and it is also an invitation to check: this article recomputes the shared properties with the [[hydropathy-plot]] and the [[protein-calculator]], and weighs each peptide with the [[peptide-mass-calculator]]'s maths.

## The paper in brief

The parent peptide is written in the paper's text as **KKWRKWLKWLAKK**: six lysines, one arginine, three tryptophans, two leucines and one alanine. The authors rearranged it by walking the residues round a helical wheel, which gives twelve derivatives, and measured minimum inhibitory concentrations against Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria plus haemolysis of red blood cells.

Their result is that where the lysine cluster sits matters: derivatives with the lysines at the N-terminus were as potent as the parent or better, while several middle arrangements needed 4 to 16 times more peptide. One derivative had the best therapeutic index (the gap between the dose that kills bacteria and the dose that damages red cells), and a variant with an extra alanine was gentler on red cells while keeping some activity. Circular dichroism did not track activity, so the authors warn against using helicity on its own as a design parameter.

The paper also gives one shared set of calculated properties for all the derivatives: molecular weight 18.4 kDa, net charge +7, hydrophobicity 0.27, aliphatic index 67.69, GRAVY −1.631, computed with HeliQuest and ExPASy.

## Checked with the tools

This is a **recomputation** from the published sequence: no data were simulated. We built the thirteen circular rearrangements of KKWRKWLKWLAKK ourselves (**our construction**; the paper's own twelve are drawn as helical wheels in a figure, not written out) and ran each through the tools' maths.

**Step 1: the shared properties.** The tools confirm that the paper is right to give one set of values for every derivative. All thirteen arrangements give the same net charge at pH 7 (**+6.99, that is +7**, matching the paper), the same isoelectric point (**11.94**), the same GRAVY (**−1.654** with fibub's rounded scale; **−1.631** with the published Kyte–Doolittle values, exactly the paper's figure), the same extinction coefficient at 280 nm (**16,500 M⁻¹ cm⁻¹**, from three tryptophans) and the same mass. The paper's aliphatic index of 67.69 also follows from the composition alone. None of these can distinguish the derivatives, which is what makes the experiment informative: the 16-fold activity differences must come from the arrangement.

**Step 2: the molecular weight is ten times too large.** The tools give **1,799.30 Da** average and **1,798.12 Da** monoisotopic for this sequence, that is 1.80 kDa, not the 18.4 kDa printed in the paper. A 13-residue peptide cannot weigh 18 kDa: that is the size of a small protein of about 160 residues. The derivative with the extra alanine comes to **1,870.37 Da** average. Anyone ordering these peptides, or looking for them on a mass spectrometer, needs the right number; the slip is worth noting because the molecular weight is used to turn a weighed powder into a molar concentration, and so into every minimum inhibitory concentration in µM.

**Step 3: what the rearrangement does change.** Figure 1 plots the sliding-window hydropathy along the peptide for the parent and two rearrangements. The curves are built from the same thirteen values, so their average is identical, but the water-repelling stretch (the leucines and tryptophans) moves from the middle to the end. That is the only thing the paper's permutations alter, and it is exactly what a hydropathy plot shows.

## How we made the graphs

The charts and all the numbers above come from `scripts/blog-figures/lysine-tryptophan-peptide-permutations-gravy-net-charge-mass.mjs` in fibub's public code. It calls `windowAverages` and `gravy` from `src/lib/sci/hydropathy.ts` (the maths of the [[hydropathy-plot]]) and `analyse` and `chargeCurve` from `src/lib/sci/protparam.ts` (the maths of the [[protein-calculator]], and of the [[peptide-mass-calculator]]'s residue masses).

The sequence, the net charge, the GRAVY, the aliphatic index and the molecular weight we compare with come from the paper's text. The thirteen circular rearrangements and the three-residue plot window are **our choices**: a window of three suits a 13-residue peptide, where the tool's default of nine would leave only five points. The single Kyte–Doolittle GRAVY of −1.6308 was worked out in a one-line script with the published values, which fibub's tool does not ship but lets you paste in.

## How we test the maths

On every change to fibub, `tests/sci.mjs` runs `tests/sci-tools/hydropathy-plot.mjs` and the protein checks inside `tests/sci.mjs` itself:

- Five proteins (one of them the tool's example) have their GRAVY, their window-9 and window-19 means and their membrane segments checked against Biopython's `ProteinAnalysis.protein_scale` and a NumPy convolution, to 10⁻¹² .
- A hand-worked window: over "IVK" with a window of 3 the mean is (4.5 + 4 − 4) ÷ 3 = 1.5, drawn at residue 2.
- The protein checks pin the tool's 120-residue example against Biopython: monoisotopic mass 14,130.6520 Da, extinction coefficient 36,440 M⁻¹ cm⁻¹ reduced and 36,690 with its two cystines, isoelectric point 7.38 and net charge +0.68 at pH 7.
- New with this article: all thirteen circular rearrangements of KKWRKWLKWLAKK must give the same average mass of 1799.295 Da, the same isoelectric point of 11.935 and the same GRAVY of −1.653846, and the sequence with an added alanine must weigh 1870.374 Da.

## Check it yourself

First the shared numbers, on the [[protein-calculator]]:

1. Paste **KKWRKWLKWLAKK** into *Paste a protein sequence*.
2. Leave *pH for the charge* at **7.0** and *Disulfide bonds* at **0**.
3. It reads *Average mass* **1,799.30 Da**, *Monoisotopic mass* **1,798.1246 Da**, *Isoelectric point (pI)* **11.93**, *Net charge at pH 7* **+6.99**, *Extinction coefficient at 280 nm* **16,500 M⁻¹ cm⁻¹** and *A280 of 1 mg/mL (1 cm)* **9.170**, with the charge curve crossing zero at the pI.
4. Now paste any rearrangement, for example **WLAKKKKWRKWLK**, and every one of those numbers stays the same.

Then the arrangement, on the [[hydropathy-plot]]:

5. Paste the same sequence into *Protein sequence* and set *Plot window (residues)* to **3**.
6. *GRAVY* reads **−1.65**, and the plot peaks at **+1.67** around residue 10, where WLA sits.
7. Paste **WLAKKKKWRKWLK** instead: GRAVY is unchanged, but the peak moves to residue 2.
8. To match the paper exactly, paste the published Kyte–Doolittle values into *Paste your own scale* and press **Use this scale**: GRAVY becomes **−1.63**.

## What researchers can use it for

- **Check a peptide's mass before ordering it**, and before turning milligrams into µM: a factor-of-ten error in the molecular weight moves every reported MIC by the same factor.
- **Separate composition from arrangement** when designing variants: charge, mass, pI, GRAVY and absorbance are composition-only, so a permutation series isolates the effect of order.
- **Measure what you made**: the extinction coefficient at 280 nm, with three tryptophans, lets you read the concentration from a single A280 instead of trusting the weighing.
- **Compare scales honestly**: GRAVY differs by scale, so report which one you used; the [[hydropathy-plot]] takes any scale you paste.
- **Plan epitope or truncation series** from the same sequence with the [[peptide-overlap-designer]].

## The fine print

- The paper's twelve derivatives are shown as helical wheels in a figure; the thirteen circular rearrangements here are our own construction, used only to show which properties cannot change.
- fibub's hydropathy scale is rounded to the nearest 0.5, so our GRAVY of −1.654 differs from the published-scale value of −1.631 in the second decimal place.
- Our masses are for the free peptide with a free N-terminus and a free acid C-terminus, and no counter-ions. A trifluoroacetate or acetate salt from synthesis weighs more, which matters when you weigh out a powder.
- We did not check any antibacterial or haemolysis result: those are measurements, not calculations. Nothing here is medical advice.
