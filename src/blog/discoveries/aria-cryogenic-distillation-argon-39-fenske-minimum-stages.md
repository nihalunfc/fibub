Argon from the air contains a trace of radioactive argon-39, which would swamp a sensitive dark-matter detector with false signals. Argon from deep underground has far less of it, but it is scarce. The Aria project therefore plans to **clean ordinary argon by distillation**: boil it at about 90 K in a column 350 m tall and let the lighter isotope argon-39 drift one way and argon-40 the other. The catch is that the two isotopes have almost the same boiling behaviour, so a huge number of "stages" is needed. This article recomputes the column's stage counts with the same maths as the [[mccabe-thiele-calculator]]. The vapour pressures behind the volatility are the job of the [[vapour-pressure-calculator]], and the flows of the product streams are a [[mass-balance-solver]] task.

## The paper in brief

A distillation **stage** is one round of boiling and condensing in which the vapour ends up slightly richer in the more volatile component than the liquid it left. The **relative volatility** α is how much richer: the ratio of the two components' vapour pressures. For argon-39 against argon-40 the paper gives the natural logarithm of α as about 1.333 thousandths, with an uncertainty of 0.036 thousandths, at the column's mean temperature of 89.5 K. So α is only about 1.0013: each stage changes the isotope ratio by a little over a tenth of a percent.

To make up for that, the column is packed with structured metal gauze. The authors quote a **height equivalent to a theoretical stage (HETP)** of 10 cm, meaning every 10 cm of packing does the work of one ideal stage. They state that the full column holds 2870 theoretical stages, about 287 m of active packing inside the 350 m shaft. They also state an illustrative number: at **total reflux** (all the condensed vapour returned to the column, the best case for separation) a ten-fold separation needs at least **1727 stages**. The design goal is a ten-fold reduction of argon-39 in each pass.

## Checked with the tools

The tool's Fenske equation gives the fewest stages at total reflux for a constant relative volatility:

N<sub>min</sub> = ln[ (x<sub>D</sub> / (1 − x<sub>D</sub>)) ÷ (x<sub>B</sub> / (1 − x<sub>B</sub>)) ] ÷ ln α

Here x<sub>D</sub> and x<sub>B</sub> are the light-component fractions at the top and bottom. The bracket is the **separation factor** S: the ratio of the two "odds". For isotopes the exact composition does not matter, only S does, so we take (**our assumption**) x<sub>D</sub> = 0.5 and x<sub>B</sub> = 1/11 for S = 10.

| Quantity | Paper says | Tool gives |
|---|---|---|
| α from ln α = 1.333 × 10⁻³ | about 1.0013 | 1.001334 |
| N<sub>min</sub> for S = 10 | 1727 | 1727.4 (stepped on the McCabe-Thiele diagram: 1728) |
| N<sub>min</sub> range for the ± 0.036 × 10⁻³ uncertainty | not stated | 1682 to 1775 |
| Separation reached by 2870 stages at total reflux | not stated | S = 45.9 (41.4 to 50.9 over the uncertainty) |
| Packing height for S = 10 at HETP 0.10 m | not stated | 172.7 m |

So the paper's 1727 is exactly the Fenske number, and the stepped count on the McCabe-Thiele diagram agrees to one stage. The new information is the second chart: because ln S = N ln α, **the separation grows exponentially with height**. The 287 m of packing at total reflux reaches a factor of about 46, comfortably above the factor of 10 that the design asks per pass, which leaves room for the finite reflux, losses and real-world flow limits. A factor of 100 would need about 3455 stages, or about 345 m of packing, which is close to the whole 350 m shaft.

One uncertainty matters more than the rest: the volatility. The one-sigma band on ln α moves the 1727 stages by about ± 50 stages and the reachable separation between 41 and 51, so the column's headroom over the design goal (a factor of at least 4) survives it.

## How we made the graphs

The script `scripts/blog-figures/aria-cryogenic-distillation-argon-39-fenske-minimum-stages.mjs` imports `alphaEq`, `fenske` and `stepStages` from `src/lib/sci/mccabe.ts`, the maths behind the McCabe-Thiele calculator. Inputs from the paper's text: ln α = (1.333 ± 0.036) × 10⁻³, 2870 stages, HETP 10 cm. Our assumptions: x<sub>D</sub> = 0.5 with x<sub>B</sub> = 1/(1 + S) to set a separation factor S, and a feed of x<sub>F</sub> = 0.2 for the stepping (total reflux does not depend on it). Figure 1 is `fenske()` over S; Figure 2 is S = exp(ln α × height ÷ HETP). The stepped count of 1728 comes from `stepStages` at total reflux with a limit of 6000 stages.

## How we test the maths

The checks in `tests/sci-tools/mccabe-thiele-calculator.mjs` run with `node tests/sci.mjs` on every change. They pin Fenske against its closed form for an exact case (α = 2, S = 1 gives N = 0, S = 4 at α = 2 gives N = 2), the stepped total-reflux count to the Fenske value within one stage, and, new with this article, the isotope case: ln α = 1.333 × 10⁻³ and S = 10 give 1727.4 stages, 2870 stages give S = 45.87, and stepping with α = 1.0134192 and S = 10 gives 173 stages, one more than Fenske's 172.74.

## Check it yourself

The page limits stepping to 300 stages, so it cannot take the real isotope α (it would say "More than 300 stages"). Use an α with ten times the log-volatility, which needs one tenth of the stages:

1. Open the [[mccabe-thiele-calculator]]. Under **Equilibrium from**, keep "A constant relative volatility" and type **1.01342** in **Relative volatility α**.
2. Set **Feed, light fraction xF** to 0.2, **Distillate xD** to 0.5 and **Bottoms xB** to 0.090909.
3. Under **Reflux** choose "Total reflux (minimum stages)".
4. You should read "173 stages at total reflux (minimum: 172.73)", and the **Minimum stages (total reflux)** fact shows 172.73 from the Fenske equation.
5. Multiply 172.73 by 10: that is 1727, the paper's number for the real isotope pair, because ln α is ten times smaller in the real case.

## What researchers can use it for

- Sizing a packed column for any difficult separation (isotopes, close-boiling pairs) from a measured α and an HETP, before running a detailed simulation.
- Seeing how a small error in α propagates into the required height, as the shaded band in Figure 1 does.
- Teaching why separation factors multiply stage by stage, and why α close to 1 makes columns tall.
- Cross-checking a published stage count against the Fenske limit: a column with fewer stages than N<sub>min</sub> cannot reach the stated purity.
- Estimating how α depends on temperature by looking up the two vapour pressures in the [[vapour-pressure-calculator]] and taking their ratio.

## The fine print

This is a recomputation from numbers stated in the paper's text; nothing was measured by us. Fenske's equation assumes a constant α and total reflux, so it gives a lower bound; a real column run with finite reflux, feed and withdrawals needs more stages. The paper's own design work (column hydraulics, packing wetting, heat duty, the actual argon-39 depletion) is far richer than this check, and its tables of results were not readable for this article. The split into x<sub>D</sub> = 0.5 and x<sub>B</sub> = 1/11 is our choice to fix a separation factor of 10; the isotope result depends only on that factor. For anything that matters, read the paper and consult the collaboration's published results.
