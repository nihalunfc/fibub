An LC50 is the concentration that kills half the animals in a set time. It is one number, but it comes out of a model: a straight line fitted through transformed mortality proportions, with limits that depend on the slope. That means a published LC50 can be checked by anybody who has the mortality counts, and the check often answers questions the paper left open. A 2026 preprint (**not yet peer reviewed**) exposed sub-adult African catfish, *Clarias gariepinus*, to fixed-ratio mixtures of cadmium chloride and glyphosate potassium and reported a 96-hour LC50 of 28.100 mg/L. Its five mortality percentages are in its text, so the whole fit can be rebuilt with the [[lc50-probit|LC50 probit analysis tool]]. Put several species' LC50 values together with the [[species-sensitivity]] tool, and follow the metal into the fish with the [[bioaccumulation-factor]] calculator.

## The paper in brief

Cadmium reaches African rivers from mining and industry, glyphosate from farming, and fish meet both at once. The authors ran a 96-hour static test to OECD Test No. 203: six groups of **30 sub-adult catfish** (three replicates of ten), one control and five mixtures in a fixed **1:2 ratio** of cadmium chloride to glyphosate potassium, from 6.18/12.36 up to 30.90/61.80 mg/L. Deaths were counted at 24, 48, 72 and 96 hours. No fish died in the control.

By 96 hours the cumulative mortality was **13.33, 16.67, 33.33, 43.33 and 60.00 %** across the five treatments. Probit analysis gave LC50 values of **90.744, 73.906, 46.153 and 28.100 mg/L** at 24, 48, 72 and 96 hours, with slopes rising from 1.640 to **2.027**. The authors then derived "safe" levels of **2.81 mg/L** (one tenth of the 96-hour LC50, after Sprague) and **0.28 mg/L** (one hundredth, after NAS/NAE).

One thing the preprint never states is **which concentration those LC50 values are measured in**: the cadmium chloride alone, the glyphosate alone, or the two added together. In a fixed-ratio mixture study that choice changes every number by a factor of three.

## Checked with the tools

This is a **full refit** of the paper's 96-hour result from the five mortality percentages in its text. Nothing was simulated.

**Step 1: the fit comes back exactly.** Thirty fish per group turn those percentages into whole deaths: 4, 5, 10, 13 and 18. Entered against the cadmium chloride concentrations (6.18 to 30.90 mg/L) with a 0-of-30 control, the tool's maximum-likelihood probit fit gives:

| Check | Paper | The tool gives |
|---|---|---|
| 96 h LC50 | 28.100 mg/L | 28.100 mg/L |
| Probit slope | 2.027 ± 0.496 | 2.0275 ± 0.5044 |
| 95 % limits | 21.628 to 47.653 mg/L | 21.644 to 47.970 mg/L |
| Pearson χ² | 2.085 (p = 0.555) | 2.0849 on 3 df (p = 0.5550) |
| Safe level, Sprague (0.1 × LC50) | 2.81 mg/L | 2.810 mg/L |
| Safe level, NAS/NAE (0.01 × LC50) | 0.28 mg/L | 0.281 mg/L |

The LC50, the slope, the chi-square and its p-value all match to the digits printed. The small differences in the limits and the slope's standard error are what you expect between two implementations of Fieller's theorem: the tool reports the fiducial interval from the maximum-likelihood information matrix, and statistical packages differ in whether they widen it for heterogeneity (here they should not: p = 0.555, so the scatter is ordinary chance). **Step 2: the LC50 is on the cadmium scale.** Refit the same deaths against the **total** mixture concentration, 18.54 to 92.70 mg/L, and the slope is identical (the slope does not care what you multiply the x-axis by) but the LC50 becomes **84.300 mg/L**, three times larger. Since the paper reports 28.100, its LC50 values are **concentrations of cadmium chloride within the mixture**, not of the mixture as a whole. Anyone comparing these numbers with a single-chemical cadmium study needs to know that, and anyone comparing them with a mixture study needs to multiply by three.

**Step 3: how far the result can be trusted.** The tool reports Finney's **g = 0.238** at LC50. Below 0.4 the slope is well enough determined for the fiducial limits to mean something, so the 96-hour result is sound. The other mortality levels are not: the same fit gives **LC10 = 6.56 mg/L** (limits 2.35 to 9.70) and **LC90 = 120.4 mg/L** (limits 62.7 to 755). The LC90 and its limits sit far above the highest concentration tested, 30.90 mg/L (Figure 2). The same objection applies to the preprint's own **24-hour and 48-hour LC50 values of 90.744 and 73.906 mg/L**: both are above every concentration in the experiment, so they are extrapolations from a model, not measurements. The tool says so itself, printing a *Warning: LC50 is outside the tested range* fact whenever that happens.

**Step 4: a shallow slope is a wide answer.** A probit slope of 2.03 means the concentration has to rise about 18-fold to take mortality from 10 % to 90 %. That is a shallow dose-response curve for an acute fish test, and it is why the limits are so lopsided: 21.6 to 48.0 mg/L around 28.1. Reporting an LC50 without its slope hides that.

## How we made the graphs

The charts and every number come from `scripts/blog-figures/cadmium-glyphosate-catfish-96h-lc50-probit-slope-reproduced.mjs` in fibub's public code. It calls `fitQuantal` (Fisher scoring with step halving, the maximum-likelihood probit fit), `lcp` (any LC level with Fieller fiducial limits and Finney's g) and `predict` from `src/lib/sci/probit.ts`, the maths behind the [[lc50-probit]] tool.

The inputs are the preprint's: five concentrations, 30 fish per group, no control deaths, and the five 96-hour mortality percentages from its running text, turned into whole numbers of fish (13.33 % of 30 is exactly 4). **Our assumption** is only the obvious one: that those percentages are of the 30 fish in the treatment, which the preprint's design states. The second x-axis in step 2, the total mixture concentration, is the sum of the stated 1:2 pair.

## How we test the maths

On every change to fibub, the probit section of `tests/sci.mjs` and `tests/sci-tools/lc50-probit.mjs` pin the tool:

- A six-concentration quantal data set must give intercept −1.71559108 and slope 2.68434559, with the exact covariance matrix and χ² 0.08951500, as `statsmodels` 0.15 gives for a binomial GLM with a probit link.
- The same data with a fixed natural response of 10 % must match an independent SciPy maximum-likelihood fit, and the fiducial limits must match a SciPy solution of Fieller's quadratic.
- A deliberately scattered data set must be flagged as heterogeneous, and its limits must then use the heterogeneity factor and Student's t.
- New with this article: the catfish deaths (4, 5, 10, 13, 18 of 30 at 6.18 to 30.90 mg/L, control 0 of 30) must give slope 2.0274518, intercept −2.9371778, LC50 28.0998587 mg/L, 95 % limits 21.6440784 to 47.9700347, χ² 2.0849485 on 3 df, p 0.5549638 and g 0.2377659; and the same deaths on the three-times-larger total-mixture scale must give the same slope and an LC50 of 84.2995762 mg/L.

## Check it yourself

On the [[lc50-probit|LC50 / EC50 calculator]]:

1. In *Test results*, paste six rows of concentration, number exposed and number that responded: **0, 30, 0**; **6.18, 30, 4**; **12.36, 30, 5**; **18.54, 30, 10**; **24.72, 30, 13**; **30.9, 30, 18**.
2. Leave *Model* on **Probit (normal)**, *Response* on **Death (LC)**, *Natural response* on **From the control (Abbott)** and *Limits* on **95 %**. Set *Concentration unit* to **mg/L**.
3. The *Result* line reads **LC50 = 28.1 mg/L (95 % limits 21.6 to 48)**.
4. The facts below give *Slope* **2.027 ± 0.50**, *Natural response* **0 %**, *Goodness of fit (Pearson χ²)* **2.08 on 3 df, p = 0.55** with the note "scatter is consistent with chance", and *g (Finney)* **0.24** with the note that the limits are reliable.
5. The table under it lists **LC10 6.555**, **LC20 10.8**, **LC50 28.1** and **LC90 120.4** with their limits: watch the upper limit for LC90 run to the hundreds.
6. Now multiply every concentration by three (**18.54, 37.08, 55.62, 74.16, 92.7**) to put the whole mixture on the axis. The slope stays **2.027** and the LC50 becomes **84.3 mg/L**.

## What researchers can use it for

- **Reproduce a published LC50** from the mortality table before you cite it, and see its slope, χ² and g.
- **Find out what a mixture LC50 is measured in**, by refitting on each candidate scale and seeing which one gives the published number.
- **Spot extrapolated values**: the tool warns when an LC50 or LC90 falls outside the concentrations tested.
- **Plan the next test**: shift concentrations so that two or three groups land between 20 % and 80 % mortality, where the slope is determined and g stays small.
- **Carry the result forward**: feed LC50 values for several species into the [[species-sensitivity]] tool for an HC5, rather than relying on a single application factor.

## The fine print

- This is a **preprint and has not been peer reviewed**. Reproducing its arithmetic says nothing about its biology.
- We refit only the 96-hour data, because only the 96-hour mortality percentages appear in the preprint's running text. The 24, 48 and 72-hour counts are in its table, which we do not reproduce.
- The 24-hour and 48-hour LC50 values are above the highest concentration tested, so they are model extrapolations.
- Dissolved oxygen fell from 6.79 to 3.74 mg/L across the treatments. Part of the mortality may be low oxygen rather than the chemicals, which no probit fit can separate.
- Application factors of 0.1 and 0.01 are rules of thumb, not regulatory limits. For a real limit, check the official standard for the water body in question.
- An LC50 from a fixed-ratio mixture cannot be split into the two chemicals' separate contributions without more designs, such as a full factorial or an isobole study.
