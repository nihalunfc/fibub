How fast does a bacterium divide? The textbook answer for *E. coli* in rich broth is about 20 to 30 minutes. A small team measured *Enterobacter* sp. SM3, a soil bacterium that swarms quickly across agar, and found it divides every **21 ± 3 minutes**, clearly faster than the *E. coli* strain they grew beside it (29 minutes). What makes the paper useful to anyone with a spectrophotometer is that they got the same answer four different ways, and say exactly how. To fit your own readings, see the [[growth-curve-od600|OD600 growth curve tool]]; for a classroom picture of doubling, try the [[microbe-growth]].

## The paper in brief

The cultures grew in LB broth at 37 °C, shaken at 160 rpm. The four methods:

1. **OD600**: the cloudiness of the culture at 600 nm, read every so often, with a straight line fitted to the logarithm of OD in the fast-growth stretch. Result: a rate constant of **1.99 per hour**.
2. **Relative density (RD)**: OD is not proportional to the number of cells once the culture gets cloudy, because light scattered by one cell is scattered again by others. The team converted OD to RD with a calibration curve of the form RD = a·OD/(b − OD), measured for each strain, and fitted again: **2.08 per hour**.
3. **Colony counts (CFU)**: diluting samples, spreading them on agar and counting colonies the next day: **1.96 per hour**, with a lag of about an hour before growth starts.
4. **Shift analysis**: start a series of cultures from ever smaller inocula. Each one reaches a set OD (here 0.1) later than the one before. Plot that onset time against the logarithm of the dilution and the slope is the doubling time, with no calibration at all: **19.5 minutes**.

They also tried the Gompertz and Baranyi models on the colony counts and found neither followed the whole curve; a modified logistic did better.

## Checked with the tools

**Step 1: rate constants to doubling times (recomputation).** A rate constant k (per hour) becomes a doubling time with Td = ln 2 / k, the same line the growth-curve tool uses. From the paper's rates:

| Method | Paper says | Tool maths gives |
|---|---|---|
| OD600 fit, SM3 | 1.99 per h, 21 ± 3 min | 20.9 min |
| Relative density, SM3 | 2.08 per h, 20 ± 3 min | 20.0 min |
| Colony counts, SM3 | 1.96 per h, 21 min | 21.2 min |
| Shift analysis, SM3 | slope 1.079 h per tenfold, 19.5 min | 1.079 × log10 2 = 0.325 h = 19.5 min |
| OD600 fit, E. coli RP437 | 1.40 per h, about 30 min | 29.7 min |

All five agree to rounding. The shift method needs one extra idea: a tenfold dilution costs log2(10) = 3.32 doublings, so the doubling time is the slope times log10 2.

**Step 2: does the tool find the right rate? (simulation).** We built our own culture in the script, not the paper's data: true growth at **2.0 per hour** after a 1-hour lag, levelling off, read every 15 minutes, with 2 % reading noise. The "meter" OD bends below the true density using the paper's SM3 calibration turned round. Fed to the tool's maths with *Find it* and 5 readings in the window, the steepest stretch (1.5 to 2.5 h, OD 0.01 to 0.04) gives **1.97 per hour**, a doubling time of **21.1 minutes**, within 2 % of the truth. The same readings converted to RD give **1.98 per hour**.

**Step 3: why the paper bothered with RD.** If you instead take a later window, 3.75 to 4.75 h (OD 0.7 to 2.0 in the simulation), the OD gives **1.08 per hour** but RD gives **1.36 per hour**: the bend in the OD scale alone knocks a fifth off the rate, on top of the real slowing as food runs out. The tool warns when readings in the window go above OD 1 for this reason. Keep the window low, or calibrate.

**Step 4: the shift method (simulation).** We started seven simulated cultures, each from half the cells of the one before, and timed when each reached OD 0.1. Every halving delays the culture by one doubling time, so the points fall on a line: **1.152 hours per tenfold dilution**, a doubling time of **20.8 minutes**, against the true 20.8 (ln 2 / 2.0 h). This is the same arithmetic as Step 1, so the method checks out on data where we know the answer.

**Step 5: models.** On the simulated culture the tool's Gompertz fit gives µm = 2.25 per hour and λ = 1.66 h (R² 0.998): close, but the model's steepest slope is not the same as the straight-line rate, and the smooth end of our lag pulls it about. That matches the paper's warning that Gompertz-type curves do not always follow a real culture end to end; treat model rates as a second opinion next to the straight-line fit.

## How we made the graphs

Every number and both charts come from `scripts/blog-figures/enterobacter-sm3-doubling-time-od600-cfu.mjs` in fibub's public code. It calls `analyse` and `LN2` from `src/lib/sci/growthcurve.ts`, the maths behind the [[growth-curve-od600]], so the window search, tangent lag and Gompertz fit are exactly what the tool page does.

Where the inputs come from: the rate constants, the shift slope and the calibration constants a = 1.34 and b = 6.17 are from the paper. The simulated culture (2.0 per hour, 1 h lag, plateau, 15-minute readings, 2 % noise, a fixed random seed) is our own; it is **simulated**, not the paper's measurements. The shift-method onsets are found by bisection on the same simulated growth law.

## How we test the maths

On every change to fibub, `tests/sci.mjs` runs the growth-curve checks in `tests/sci-tools/growth-curve-od600.mjs` and, new with this article, `tests/sci-tools/growth-curve-od600-sm3.mjs`:

- A hand-worked culture that doubles every 30 minutes must give µ = 1.386 per hour, Td = 0.5 h and a lag of exactly 1 h.
- The tool's window search, confidence limits and Gompertz and logistic fits must match an independent NumPy script on a worked example and six random cultures (for example Strain A: 0.8088 per hour, 51.4 minutes, lag 1.32 h).
- New: the paper's rates must turn into its doubling times (1.99, 2.08, 1.96 and 1.40 per hour to 21, 20, 21 and 30 minutes), the shift slope 1.079 h to 0.325 h (19.5 min), and the table below must give 1.990 per hour, 20.9 minutes and a 1.00-hour lag.

## What researchers can use it for

- **Turn a plate-reader export into rates**: paste many wells at once into the [[growth-curve-od600]] and get rate, doubling time and lag for each, with confidence limits and a CSV.
- **Check the window**: switch between *Find it* and *Choose by hand* to see how sensitive a published rate is to the stretch of the curve that was fitted.
- **Compare straight-line and model rates**: fit Gompertz or logistic beside the tangent, as the paper did, and report both when they differ.
- **Plan a shift experiment**: a culture at 20 minutes per doubling will be about 3.3 doublings, or 66 minutes, later for every tenfold dilution, which tells you how long to keep reading.
- **Teach** exponential growth and the meaning of a doubling time with the [[microbe-growth]], then move to real readings.

## The fine print

- Our cultures in Figures 1 and 2 are simulated with our own numbers; only the rates, slope and calibration constants come from the paper.
- The simulated OD reaches about 2.9. A real spectrophotometer reading that high would need diluting; we left it to show the bend.
- The RD calibration is specific to SM3 and the authors' instrument; measure your own before using it on another strain or meter.
- The tool's lag is where the fitted line meets the starting OD. With a gradual end to the lag it can differ from a "first sign of growth" lag by a fraction of an hour.
