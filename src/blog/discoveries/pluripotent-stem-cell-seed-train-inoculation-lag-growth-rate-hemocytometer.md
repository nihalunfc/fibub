Making enough human pluripotent stem cells for research or therapy means growing them from a few million to many billions without losing their quality. A 2024 paper in *Stem Cell Research & Therapy* grows them as floating clumps in stirred-tank bioreactors and passes them on every 3 or 4 days instead of every 7. This article checks the paper's inoculation, growth and yield numbers with the maths of the [[hemocytometer]] and the [[cell-doubling-time]] tool. For storing the vials such a process needs, see the [[cryovial-inventory]] tool.

## The paper in brief

The authors start a 150 mL stirred bioreactor at **5 × 10⁵ viable cells/mL** and passage it every 3 days (the D3 process) or every 4 days (D4), against an earlier 7-day process that reached about 35 million cells/mL. The D4 passages peaked between 6.6 and 9.7 million cells/mL and the D3 passages between 3.7 and 5.2. They report a specific growth rate of about **1.0 per day** (a doubling time of 16.6 hours) on days 2 to 3, and viability above 93 % from day 3. Cell loss on day 1 was 15 to 45 % depending on the freezing density. Cells were frozen at 20 to 100 million per mL in 1 mL vials with 80 to 100 % recovered. Over 5 passages the D4 route gave a cumulative 1.29 × 10¹⁴ cells in 20 days and the D3 route 3.76 × 10¹² cells in 15 days.

## Checked with the tools

This is a **recomputation** from the numbers stated in the paper's text. The paper's per-day counts are in its supplement, which we did not use. Any count example below is made up and marked as such.

**Step 1: ideal growth against what was seen.** At a rate of 1.0 per day the doubling time is **16.6 hours**, so a culture growing from 5 × 10⁵ cells/mL with no pause for 4 days would multiply by **54.6** and reach 27 million cells/mL. The first D4 passage peaked at 8.1 million, a 16.2-fold gain. Taken over the whole passage, that is an effective doubling time of **23.9 hours** (the doubling-time tool's two-count formula), a mean rate of 0.70 per day instead of 1.0. The 3-day passages tell the same story: 20.1-fold at 1.0 per day, 10.4 seen for the first one (21.3 hours).

**Step 2: how many hours are missing.** If growth runs at 1.0 per day once it starts, the passage must have lost time somewhere. Solving for the hours with no net growth gives **29, 34, 30, 25 and 26 hours** for the five D4 passages (mean 28.8) and **16, 21, 20, 24 and 22 hours** for the D3 ones (mean 20.6; Figure 2). So each passage seems to start growing about a day late. That fits the paper's own account of a day 1 with 75 to 85 % viability and cell loss of 15 to 45 %: cells are recovering from being split, not dividing (Figure 1). It also fits a rate slowing after day 3 (the paper reports about 0.76 per day from day 4 in the old 7-day process); our model cannot tell the two apart.

**Step 3: the 7-day process agrees.** The earlier process reached about 35 million cells/mL, which is 70-fold, a mean rate of **0.607 per day** over 7 days. That is lower than the 4-day passages' 0.70, as expected when slower late growth is added to the same early delay.

**Step 4: how many cells to seed the reactor.** A 150 mL reactor at 5 × 10⁵ cells/mL needs **7.5 × 10⁷ viable cells**. From a 1 mL vial frozen at 100 million cells/mL, with 80 % recovered and thawed into 20 mL (**our assumption** for the numbers; the paper thaws into 10 mL then 20 mL), the live density is 4 million cells/mL. The hemocytometer's seeding calculation then gives **18.75 mL of suspension and 131.25 mL of fresh medium**, less than one vial's worth (0.94 vials). A vial frozen at 20 million per mL gives 0.8 million live cells/mL after the same thaw, which needs **93.8 mL** of suspension: **4.7 vials**. The freezing density decides how many vials a seed train uses.

**Step 5: how well can you set the inoculum?** Suppose you count a peak culture at 8.1 million cells/mL after a 1 : 10 dilution and 1 : 1 trypan blue (a total factor of 20), with 40, 41, 39 and 42 live cells in four large squares (made-up numbers, **our assumption**). The tool gives 8.1 million live cells/mL, 98 % viability and a Poisson counting error of **7.9 %** from the 162 live cells. The five D4 peaks (mean 8.3, SD 1.2 million) vary by **14.6 %**, the D3 peaks by 13.2 %. Counting error alone is therefore about half of the observed variation; the rest is real passage-to-passage difference (or errors in the other steps). More squares or a larger count lowers the counting part.

**Step 6: do the cumulative yields add up?** Multiply each passage's fold gain (peak ÷ 5 × 10⁵) over the five passages: D4 gives a combined **1.21 × 10⁶-fold**, D3 gives **44,477-fold**. The paper's cumulative yields then imply starting inocula of 1.07 × 10⁸ cells (D4) and 0.85 × 10⁸ (D3), equal to 214 and 169 mL at 5 × 10⁵ cells/mL, against the 150 mL (7.5 × 10⁷ cells) described. They are 1.4 and 1.1 times too high, so the cumulative figures are broadly consistent with a 150 mL start, with some extra from the 500 mL larger reactor the paper also uses. We cannot resolve the difference without its supplementary tables.

## How we made the graphs

The script `scripts/blog-figures/pluripotent-stem-cell-seed-train-inoculation-lag-growth-rate-hemocytometer.mjs` calls `cellCount` and `seeding` from `src/lib/sci/cells.ts` (the [[hemocytometer]]) and `twoPoint`, `growthFactor` and `tdFromRate` from `src/lib/sci/doubling.ts` (the [[cell-doubling-time]] tool). The inoculation density, volume, growth rate, peak densities, freezing densities, recovery and cumulative yields come from the paper's text. The thaw into 20 mL, the 80 % recovery used in the vial example, and the count example are **our assumptions**. Nothing is fitted to the paper's raw counts.

## How we test the maths

On every change to fibub, `tests/sci.mjs` runs `tests/sci-tools/hemocytometer.mjs`, a new file added with this article. It pins the chamber maths to hand calculations: four squares with 40, 41, 39 and 42 live cells at a factor of 20 must give 8.1 × 10⁶ cells/mL, and the seeding calculation for 7.5 × 10⁷ cells in 150 mL from 8.1 × 10⁶ cells/mL must give 9.26 mL of suspension and 140.74 mL of medium. The existing `tests/sci-tools/cell-doubling-time.mjs` pins the two-count doubling time (4-fold in 48 h is 24 h); the new file also checks a 16.2-fold gain in 96 h is 23.9 h.

## Check it yourself

On the [[hemocytometer]] page:

1. Under *Chamber and dilution*, keep **4 corner squares**, keep **1 : 1 (×2)** for *Cells : trypan blue*, and set *Earlier dilution (×)* to **10**.
2. Under *Count*, enter the four squares: live **40, 41, 39, 42** and dead **1, 0, 1, 1**. The *Result* shows about **8.1 million live cells per mL** and a viability near **98 %**.
3. Under *Seed the cells*, set *Live cells per well* to **7.5e7**, *Wells* to **1**, *Medium per well (mL)* to **150** and *Spare (%)* to **0**: about **9.26 mL** of suspension and **140.7 mL** of medium.

On the [[cell-doubling-time]] page, use *Add a count* for **0 h, 5e5** and **96 h, 8.1e6**: the doubling time is about **23.9 hours**.

## What researchers can use it for

- **Seed train planning**: work out cells needed, vials needed and volumes for each passage before a run, with the [[hemocytometer]] seeding box.
- **Finding lost growth time**: compare a passage's effective doubling time with the exponential rate to see how long the cells needed to recover.
- **Setting counting precision**: choose how many squares to count so that counting error stays below the variation you care about.
- **Comparing freezing densities**: convert a density and recovery into live cells per vial and number of vials.
- **Teaching** why a 4-day passage does not give 55-fold growth even at a 1.0 per day rate.

## The fine print

- The paper's day-by-day counts are in its supplement, which we did not use; our lag hours assume a constant 1.0 per day rate, so they mix an early delay with any later slowing.
- The vial thaw example uses our own round numbers, not the paper's protocol.
- Cumulative yields in the paper may be defined differently (for example, which passages are summed); our product-of-folds check is approximate.
- This article describes cell-culture arithmetic only, not a procedure to follow.
