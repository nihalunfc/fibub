How hot weather **feels** depends on more than the thermometer. In humid air, sweat dries slowly, so the body cools less well. **Heat stress indices** fold temperature and humidity into one number: the US **heat index**, Canada's **humidex** and the older **Thom discomfort index**. A team at the National Observatory of Athens worked out all three for every hour since 1901 at one of Europe's longest-running weather stations. This article shows how the [[wind-chill-heat-index]] tool lets anyone check their thresholds; for humidity in other forms see [[psychrometric-calculator]], and for heating and cooling demand [[degree-days]].

## The paper in brief

The Thissio station sits on a hill in central Athens, 107 m up and about 5 km from the sea. Its hourly records run from 1901 to 2024, over a million readings. The authors computed the three indices hourly and counted when each crossed a **hazard threshold**: heat index 32 °C, humidex 38, Thom index 27 °C.

The heat-stress season now starts earlier and lasts longer: by about **2.2 days per decade** for the heat index, **2.5** for the Thom index and **3.5** for humidex. In 1991-2024, humidex reached 38 on more than 70 days a year on average, against about 40 in the early 1900s. The total number of hours with a heat index of 32 °C or more was about twenty times higher in 2001-2024 than in 1901-1920. The highest values, about 54 °C for the heat index and 49 for humidex, came in 2001-2010. The spread of relative humidity narrowed over the century while the spread of the heat index widened, so the rise comes from heat more than from damp.

## Checked with the tools

**Do the three thresholds mean the same thing?** We used the tool's heat index and humidex to find, for each humidity, the air temperature where each index reaches its threshold, and added Thom's formula from the paper for comparison (it is not in a fibub tool). Figure 1 shows the answer: almost yes. In dry Athens summer air of 30 % humidity, the heat index reaches 32 °C at **33.0 °C** air temperature, humidex 38 at **34.4 °C** and Thom 27 at **34.8 °C**. At 60 % humidity all three sit within one degree, between 29.6 and 30.5 °C. That explains why the paper finds such similar trends for the three indices: they flag nearly the same hours.

**A typical hot afternoon.** For 35 °C and 30 % humidity (dew point 14.9 °C), the tool gives a heat index of **34.7 °C** ("extreme caution" in the US bands) and a humidex of **38.9**: both just past the paper's thresholds. Thom's formula gives 27.1 °C, also just past. At 33 °C and 35 %, all three sit just below.

**Which heat index?** The paper uses the Rothfusz regression in its °C form above 20 °C. The tool uses the US National Weather Service method: Rothfusz from about 27 °C (80 °F) up, with small corrections for very dry or very humid air, and Steadman's simple formula below. We checked that the two give the **same number to 0.01 °C** wherever Rothfusz applies (for example 32.80 °C at 33 °C and 35 %). Below 27 °C they part (Figure 2): at 22 °C and 50 %, the raw regression gives 24.9 °C, the NWS method 21.6 °C. That gap does not touch the 32 °C threshold, which lies well above it, but it matters for any average or median of the index over cool hours.

## How we made the graphs

Both charts and every number above come from `scripts/blog-figures/athens-heat-index-humidex-thermal-discomfort-1901-2024.mjs` in fibub's public code. It calls `heatIndex`, `humidexC` and `dewPoint` from `src/lib/sci/feelslike.ts`, the maths behind the [[wind-chill-heat-index]] tool, and finds each threshold temperature by halving the interval 50 times. Thom's index and the raw °C Rothfusz formula are written out in the script itself, for comparison only. The thresholds (32, 38 and 27) come from the paper; the humidities and temperatures in the charts are a plain grid of our choosing.

## How we test the maths

On every change to fibub, `tests/sci-tools/wind-chill-heat-index.mjs` (run by `tests/sci.mjs`) checks the tool:

- The heat index at 30 °C and 70 % must be **35.04 °C** and at 35 °C and 50 % **40.68 °C**, the values MetPy 1.7.1 gives; 90 °F at 60 % must read **100 °F**, as on the NWS chart.
- Humidex at 30 °C with a 15 °C dew point must be **34.0**, from Environment Canada's formula.
- New with this article: the heat index at 33 °C and 35 % must be **32.80 °C** by both the tool and the °C Rothfusz formula the paper uses, 35 °C and 30 % **34.67 °C** by both, and 22 °C and 50 % must use the simple formula (**21.56 °C**) where raw Rothfusz says 24.94 °C.

## Check it yourself

On the [[wind-chill-heat-index]] tool:

1. Set *Temperatures in* to **°C** and *Humidity as* to **Relative humidity (%)**.
2. Type **35** in *Air temperature*, **30** in *Relative humidity (%)* and **10** in *Wind speed*.
3. Under *Feels like*, *Heat index* reads **34.7 °C** (extreme caution, Rothfusz regression) and *Humidex* **38.9**; *Dew point* reads **14.9 °C**.
4. Change *Air temperature* to **33** and *Relative humidity (%)* to **35**: the heat index falls to **32.8 °C** and humidex to **37.3**, just under the paper's humidex threshold.
5. In *Table for a range*, pick *Heat index (temperature × humidity)* in *Index*, set the temperatures from **28** to **40** step **1** and the humidities to **20, 30, 40, 50, 60**, to see where 32 °C is crossed at each humidity.

## What researchers can use it for

- **Turn station temperature and humidity into heat index and humidex** for a day, or a whole table, with the [[wind-chill-heat-index]] tool.
- **Compare thresholds between indices** before choosing one, as in Figure 1, so trends from different indices can be read side by side.
- **Check which heat index formula a dataset used**: the NWS method and raw Rothfusz agree in hot weather but not in mild weather.
- **Convert humidity measures** (relative humidity, dew point) with the [[psychrometric-calculator]] before working an index.
- **Teach** why humid heat is harder on the body than dry heat of the same temperature.

## The fine print

- Heat stress indices are for shade and light wind; sunshine can add several degrees to how hot it feels.
- The thresholds are guides for healthy adults; children, older people and outdoor workers are at risk sooner. For warnings, check your national weather service.
- Thom's discomfort index is not in a fibub tool; we worked it in the script only.
- An urban station like Thissio also records the city's own warming, which adds to the climate signal.
