Uranium-lead zircon dating gives you two clocks in one mineral, and the gap between them is where the information hides. A 2025 petrochronology paper in the *European Journal of Mineralogy* dates the zircon cores of eclogites from the Léon domain in Brittany at **385.7 Ma** and reports two goodness-of-fit numbers side by side: a **concordia MSWD of 8.7**, which is a bad fit, and a **weighted mean MSWD of 1.14**, which is a very good one. Those look contradictory, and they are not: they are the signature of a specific, common problem. This article uses the [[concordia-plot]] tool to show which problem, and how big it has to be. For related jobs, the [[radiometric-age-calculator]] handles single-pair ages and the [[uncertainty-calculator]] propagates the errors.

## The paper in brief

The authors combine zircon uranium-lead dating, zircon oxygen isotopes and garnet lutetium-hafnium dating on eclogites from the Léon domain of the Armorican Massif, and on a nearby felsic intrusion. Their chronology, as the abstract gives it:

| What was dated | Age | MSWD |
|---|---|---|
| Eclogite zircon cores, concordia age | 385.7 ± 2.9 Ma | 8.7 |
| The same cores, weighted mean ²⁰⁶Pb/²³⁸U | 385.7 ± 1.6 Ma | 1.14 |
| Garnet Lu-Hf, Fe-Ti-rich eclogite | 346.5 ± 0.8 Ma | 0.6 |
| Garnet Lu-Hf, kyanite-bearing eclogite | 349.1 ± 1.6 Ma | 0.3 |
| Felsic intrusion, older population | 387.3 ± 5.2 Ma (n = 9) | 0.25 |
| Felsic intrusion, younger population | 321.6 ± 2.7 Ma (n = 13) | 0.76 |

The story is a Middle Devonian protolith, with eclogite-facies metamorphism and then exhumation recorded by garnet some 37 million years later. Median zircon δ¹⁸O of 4.32 ‰ points to a mantle-like or slightly altered source.

The paper's isotope ratios are in supplementary tables we could not read, so nothing below is a refit of its data. What we can do is reproduce the *structure* of its two MSWDs, which is a question about the method, not about Brittany.

## Checked with the tools

**Step 1: where is concordia at 385.7 Ma?** The tool's Wetherill curve, with the Jaffey et al. (1971) decay constants, puts a perfectly concordant zircon of that age at

> ²⁰⁷Pb/²³⁵U = **0.46207**, ²⁰⁶Pb/²³⁸U = **0.061658**, ²⁰⁷Pb/²⁰⁶Pb = **0.054377**

which in Tera-Wasserburg coordinates is ²³⁸U/²⁰⁶Pb = **16.219** against the same ²⁰⁷Pb/²⁰⁶Pb. Those four numbers are the anchor for everything else, and anyone can check them in one line.

**Step 2: what the two MSWDs actually measure.** Ludwig's concordia age reports three of them, and the tool shows all three:

- **MSWD of concordance** (1 degree of freedom): does the group's mean point sit *on* the concordia curve?
- **MSWD of equivalence** (2n − 2): are the analyses consistent with *each other*?
- **MSWD of both** (2n − 1): the two together.

A weighted mean ²⁰⁶Pb/²³⁸U age, by contrast, uses only the ²⁰⁶Pb/²³⁸U axis. It is blind to the concordia curve. So a group can scatter nicely along one axis (weighted mean MSWD near 1) and still sit well off the curve (concordance MSWD large). That is exactly the paper's pair, and it means the trouble is in the **²⁰⁷Pb/²³⁵U** direction.

**Step 3: how big does the ²⁰⁷Pb problem have to be?** We simulated twelve analyses on concordia at 385.7 Ma with 2σ relative errors of 2.0 % on ²⁰⁷Pb/²³⁵U and 1.4 % on ²⁰⁶Pb/²³⁸U and a correlation of 0.5, the sort of precision a laser-ablation session gives, scattered so that the weighted mean's MSWD comes out at the paper's **1.14**. Run through the tool, that clean set gives

> concordia age **385.64 ± 1.42 Ma** (2σ), MSWD of concordance **0.00** (probability 0.99), equivalence **1.16**, both **1.11**; weighted mean **385.66 ± 1.51 Ma** (2σ), MSWD **1.14**.

Now add a *systematic* excess to every analysis's ²⁰⁷Pb/²³⁵U and nothing else. The concordance MSWD climbs steeply; the weighted mean does not move at all (Figure 2). It reaches the paper's **8.7** at an excess of **0.79 %**. A sub-one-per-cent systematic in one channel is enough to turn a perfect concordia fit into a failing one while the ²⁰⁶Pb/²³⁸U age stays untouched.

**Step 4: what that looks like analysis by analysis.** Put one of the biased analyses through the tool's three-age readout and it gives ²⁰⁶Pb/²³⁸U **385.7 Ma**, ²⁰⁷Pb/²³⁵U **388.6 Ma** and ²⁰⁷Pb/²⁰⁶Pb **405.5 Ma**, with **4.9 %** discordance. This is the standard fingerprint of **unsupported ²⁰⁷Pb**: a trace of common lead that was not fully corrected, or a slightly mis-set background on mass 207. It is also why Devonian zircon is usually dated on ²⁰⁶Pb/²³⁸U and the ²⁰⁷Pb/²⁰⁶Pb age is only a discordance check.

**Step 5: the error bar convention.** When the concordance MSWD is 8.7, the uncertainty on the concordia age should normally be expanded by √MSWD = **2.95**. Our simulated 2σ of 1.42 Ma becomes **4.20 Ma** after that expansion. The paper reports **± 2.9 Ma** on its concordia age and **± 1.6 Ma** on its weighted mean, so its concordia uncertainty is roughly twice its weighted-mean one, which is what partial expansion of a similar unexpanded error would look like. Our simulation is not the paper's data, so this is a reading of the convention rather than a check of a number; the point to take away is that **a concordia age with MSWD 8.7 should never be quoted with an unexpanded error bar**, and the tool shows you the MSWD so you can tell.

## How we made the graphs

The charts and every number come from `scripts/blog-figures/leon-eclogite-zircon-concordia-age-mswd.mjs` in fibub's public code. It calls `cW`, `cTW`, `ellipse`, `ages`, `concordiaAge` and `weightedMean68` from `src/lib/sci/concordia.ts`, the maths behind the [[concordia-plot]] tool.

Where each input comes from:

- The concordia age, the weighted mean age and both MSWDs: **the paper's abstract**.
- The decay constants (λ²³⁸ = 1.55125 × 10⁻¹⁰, λ²³⁵ = 9.8485 × 10⁻¹⁰ yr⁻¹) and ²³⁸U/²³⁵U = 137.818: **Jaffey et al. (1971)** and **Hiess et al. (2012)**, built into the tool.
- The twelve analyses, their 2σ errors of 2.0 % and 1.4 %, the error correlation of 0.5 and the twelve random draws: **ours**. They are a simulation, not the paper's measurements, and are described as such throughout.
- The scatter on ²⁰⁶Pb/²³⁸U: **chosen by us** so that the weighted mean's MSWD equals the paper's 1.14, which is the only way to make the comparison in Step 3 fair.
- The 0.79 % excess in ²⁰⁷Pb/²³⁵U: **found by the tool**, by searching for the value that reproduces the paper's concordance MSWD of 8.7.

## How we test the maths

Every change to fibub runs `tests/sci.mjs`, which loads `tests/sci-tools/concordia-plot.mjs`. It pins the tool against an independent NumPy and SciPy implementation on two made-up zircon sets:

- On a discordant set, the York fit's intercept, slope, their errors, their covariance and the MSWD must match NumPy, and the discordia line must cross concordia at 306.88 ± 6.35 Ma and 1852.44 ± 3.46 Ma, recovering the 300 and 1850 Ma the data were built from.
- On a concordant set, the Ludwig concordia age must be 418.015 ± 1.191 Ma with a concordance MSWD of 0.00060, an equivalence MSWD of 0.748 and the matching probabilities, all from SciPy's `minimize_scalar` and `chi2.sf`.
- The weighted mean ²⁰⁶Pb/²³⁸U age of the same set must be 418.020 ± 1.223 Ma with MSWD 1.146, and the Wetherill to Tera-Wasserburg conversion must match a numerical Jacobian for the ratios, both errors and the correlation, and round-trip back exactly.
- A concordant 1 Ga analysis must give all three ages within 0.5 Ma of 1000 Ma with less than 0.1 % discordance.
- New with this article: the concordia point at 385.7 Ma must be ²⁰⁷Pb/²³⁵U 0.46207, ²⁰⁶Pb/²³⁸U 0.061658 and ²⁰⁷Pb/²⁰⁶Pb 0.054377; our twelve simulated analyses must give a weighted mean of 385.66 Ma with MSWD 1.14 and a concordia age of 385.64 Ma with a concordance MSWD below 0.01; and the same twelve with every ²⁰⁷Pb/²³⁵U raised by 0.79 % must give a concordance MSWD of 8.70 with the weighted mean completely unchanged.

## What researchers can use it for

- **Diagnose a published MSWD pair.** A large concordance MSWD with a small weighted-mean MSWD points at the ²⁰⁷ channel: common lead, a background, or the ²³⁸U/²³⁵U value used. The reverse pattern points at real age scatter.
- **Work out how big a systematic would have to be** to produce a reported misfit, as in Step 3. If the answer is a fraction of a per cent, the data are probably fine and the correction is the problem.
- **Decide whether to expand an error bar**, and by how much: the tool prints the MSWD beside the age, so √MSWD is one keystroke away in the [[uncertainty-calculator]].
- **Plan a session's precision.** Simulate the analyses you expect to get and see whether a concordia age or a weighted mean will resolve the difference you care about, before paying for beam time.
- **Teach discordance** with one editable column: raise ²⁰⁷Pb/²³⁵U by a per cent and watch the ²⁰⁷Pb/²⁰⁶Pb age run away while the ²⁰⁶Pb/²³⁸U age sits still.
- **Fit a discordia line** when the analyses really are spread along one, and read its upper and lower intercepts with proper errors from the York regression.

## The fine print

- The twelve analyses here are **simulated by fibub**, not the paper's data; its isotope ratios are in supplementary tables we could not read. Nothing above refits the Léon zircons.
- Our simulation matches the paper's weighted mean MSWD by construction, so the agreement in that row is not evidence of anything. The result that matters is the 0.79 % figure in Step 3, which follows from the paper's own pair of MSWDs and the shape of concordia at 386 Ma, not from our random draws.
- A ²⁰⁷Pb excess is one explanation for the paper's MSWD pattern, and the simplest. Real data can also break concordance through lead loss, mixed age domains inside one spot, or an inappropriate common-lead correction, and those leave different patterns.
- Decay-constant uncertainties are left out of the concordia age's error, as is usual, so comparisons with ages from other decay schemes (the paper's garnet Lu-Hf ages, for instance) carry an extra systematic.
- The tool does not implement Lu-Hf or oxygen isotopes, so the paper's 346 to 349 Ma garnet ages and its δ¹⁸O values are not checked here.
