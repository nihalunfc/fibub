To cite a website in APA 7, write the author, the date in brackets, the page title in italics, the site name and the web address, in that order. The [[citation-generator]] builds it for you: choose **Web page** and **APA 7**, type what you can see on the page, and copy the finished reference with its italics.

## What you need

Open the page you want to cite and look for five things. Most sit at the top or bottom of the article.

- **Who wrote it.** A person, or an organisation if no person is named.
- **When it was published or last updated.** Year, and the month and day if shown.
- **The title of the page.** The headline of the article, not the name of the whole site.
- **The site name.** For example the name of the charity, magazine or council that runs the site.
- **The full web address (URL).** Copy it from the address bar.

If one is missing, that is normal; see below for what APA does.

## Step by step

The pattern for a web page in APA 7 is:

**Family name, Initials. (Year, Month Day). *Title of the page*. Site Name. URL**

- **Family name, Initials**: "Chidi Daniel Okafor" becomes "Okafor, C. D.". Two authors are joined with "&"; up to 20 authors are all listed.
- **(Year, Month Day)**: the date goes in round brackets straight after the author, followed by a full stop.
- ***Title of the page***: in italics and in sentence case, which means only the first word and names get capitals.
- **Site Name**: plain text. Leave it out when it is the same as the author, so a page by a weather office on its own site does not name the office twice.
- **URL**: the link goes last, with no full stop after it, so nobody copies a stray dot into their browser.

In the citation generator:

1. Set **What is it?** to **Web page** and **Style** to **APA 7**.
2. In **Authors, one per line**, type each person as "Family, Given", for example "Okafor, Chidi Daniel". For an organisation, type its name on one line with no comma.
3. Type the **Title of the page** in sentence case. The tool keeps your capitals as you typed them.
4. Fill in **Website name**, **Year**, **Month** and **Day**.
5. Paste the address into **Web address**.
6. Leave **Date you looked at it** empty for a normal article. Fill it only for a page that is meant to change, such as a live data table; APA then writes "Retrieved [date], from" before the link.
7. Read the reference under **2. Your citation**, then press **Copy** to paste it into Word or Google Docs with the italics kept, or **Add to my list** to build a reference list.

## A worked example

You used an online article called "Why city trees cool streets at night", written by Chidi Daniel Okafor and posted on a site called Urban Green Notes on 18 June 2025, at https://example.org/trees-cool-streets. (All of these are made up.)

The five parts are:

- Author: Okafor, C. D.
- Date: (2025, June 18)
- Title: *Why city trees cool streets at night*
- Site: Urban Green Notes
- URL: https://example.org/trees-cool-streets

Put together, the APA 7 reference reads:

Okafor, C. D. (2025, June 18). *Why city trees cool streets at night*. Urban Green Notes. https://example.org/trees-cool-streets

In your essay, the matching in-text citation is (Okafor, 2025), or "Okafor (2025) found that..." when the name is part of the sentence.

Now a page with no named person and no date: "Heat safety tips for students" on the World Weather Office's own site. Type "World Weather Office" as the author, leave **Year** empty, and the tool writes "(n.d.)", short for "no date". Because the site and the author are the same, the site name is dropped:

World Weather Office. (n.d.). *Heat safety tips for students*. https://example.org/heat-tips

If your teacher asks for a different style, switch **Style** to MLA 9 or Harvard and the same details come out in that layout, with nothing to type again.

## Check your answer

- **Read it left to right.** Who, when, what, where: author, date, title, site, link. If the title comes before the author, you probably left the author box empty.
- **Only the title is italic.** In APA, the site name of a web page is plain text.
- **Initials, not first names.** If you see "Chidi Daniel Okafor" written out in full at the start of an APA reference, the name was typed without a comma, so the tool treated it as an organisation. Retype it as "Okafor, Chidi Daniel".
- **The in-text citation matches.** The family name and year in your essay should be the first two things in the reference list entry, so a reader can find it.
- **Count the cost.** Many courses leave the reference list out of the word limit. Paste only the body of your essay into the [[word-counter]] if that is your rule.

## Questions people ask

**What if the page has no author?** Use the organisation that published it. If there is truly no one, leave the author box empty and the title moves to the front, followed by the date.

**Do I need "Retrieved from" in APA 7?** Usually not. APA 7 dropped it for most pages. Add the date you looked only for pages that are designed to change over time.

**How do I put my references into Zotero or EndNote?** Add each source to the list, then press **Download .ris** or **Download .bib**. If you already have a messy BibTeX file, tidy it first with the [[bibtex-cleaner]].
