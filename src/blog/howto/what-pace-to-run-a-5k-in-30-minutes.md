To finish 5 km in 30 minutes you need to average 6:00 per kilometre, which is 9:39 per mile or a steady 10 km/h. The [[pace-calculator]] works this out for any goal time, gives you the split time at each kilometre or mile marker, and estimates what the same fitness could mean over 10 km.

## What you need

- **Your goal time**, here 30:00.
- **The race distance**, 5 km (3.11 miles). A parkrun or most charity 5Ks are measured to this length.
- **The unit you think in.** If your watch shows kilometres, use pace per km; if it shows miles, use pace per mile.
- **A recent run time, if you have one.** It tells you how far you are from the goal.

## Step by step

Pace is time divided by distance:

**Pace = total time ÷ distance**

For 5 km in 30 minutes: 30 min ÷ 5 km = 6 minutes per km. To get the pace per mile, multiply by 1.609344 (the kilometres in a mile). Speed is the same idea turned upside down: **speed = distance ÷ time**.

1. Open [[pace-calculator]] and leave **What do you want to find?** on **Pace (from distance and time)**.
2. Press the **5 km** button. It fills the distance box for you.
3. Type the goal time in the Time box as **30:00** (minutes and seconds). A plain 30 also works and is read as minutes.
4. Read the big line: the pace per km and per mile. The sentence below it gives the speed in km/h and mph.
5. Scroll to **Splits** to see the time you should see on your watch at each marker. Change the km / miles switch next to the distance if you want mile markers instead.
6. The **Race time predictor** below shows what a 30-minute 5K suggests for other distances.

Training with a heart rate monitor? [[heart-rate-zones]] shows what effort level to train at. If you walk part of the course or count steps, [[steps-to-distance]] turns a step count into kilometres.

## A worked example

Priya ran her last 5K in 32:30 and wants to go under 30 minutes.

- Her current pace: 1,950 s ÷ 5 km = 390 s, or **6:30 per km**.
- Goal pace: 1,800 s ÷ 5 km = 360 s, or **6:00 per km**. That is 30 seconds faster for every kilometre.
- Per mile: 360 × 1.609344 = 579 s, so **9:39 per mile**.
- Speed: 5 km ÷ 0.5 h = **10 km/h**, or about 6.21 mph.

On a kilometre watch her splits read 6:00, 12:00, 18:00, 24:00 and 30:00. On a mile watch the splits table shows 9:39, 19:19 and 28:58 at miles 1, 2 and 3, then a short last row of 0.11 miles that takes about 1:02.

To give herself a safety margin she types 5:55 as the pace and switches the tool to find **Time**. It shows 29:35, so holding 5:55 per km leaves 25 seconds in hand for a crowded start or a slow drinks stop. At 5:50 per km she would finish in 29:10.

The race predictor, using the 30:00 goal, gives about **1:02:33 for 10 km** (6:15 per km). It assumes the same fitness and proper training for the longer race, so read it as a rough guide.

## Check your answer

- **Round numbers:** 10 km/h means 1 km every 6 minutes, so 5 km takes 30 minutes. If your pace and speed do not agree like this, one of the boxes has the wrong unit.
- **Mile pace must be slower than km pace.** A mile is longer than a kilometre, so 9:39 per mile against 6:00 per km makes sense. If the mile figure is the smaller one, the pace unit is switched the wrong way.
- **Add the splits.** Five kilometres at 6:00 each is 30:00 exactly.

## Questions people ask

**Is 6:00 per km fast?**
It is a steady jog for many regular runners and a real challenge for many newer ones. What matters is the gap between your current pace and the goal: going from 6:30 to 6:00 per km usually takes a few weeks of consistent training, not one hard run.

**Should I run the first kilometre faster to bank time?**
Most coaches say no. Starting too fast tends to cost more time later than it saved. Aim for even splits, or a slightly quicker last kilometre.

**My watch said 5.15 km for a 5K. Which pace counts?**
GPS often measures a little long, especially around trees and tight corners. Your official time over the official 5 km is the one that counts, so it can help to aim a few seconds per km faster than the goal on your watch.
